## CKB–BBJ candidate and exact-pair prior art — 2026-10-10

The CKB paper describes broad snoring as a self-reported symptom (frequently or sometimes versus no/don't know) and reports 100,626 participants; habitual snoring is a separate frequent-only phenotype. The GVM accession currently labels the deposited source “Controlled access” and lists GRCh37 files, which conflicts with a simple public-use assumption. BBJ PheWeb identifies 9,305 cholelithiasis cases and 168,253 controls under ICD-10 K. These two East Asian biobanks make an independent-cohort transport test plausible, but neither source page certifies zero participant overlap or the complete pairwise data/LD contract.

Exact sleep–biliary genetic testing is not wholly absent from prior art: the 2023 Research Square preprint *Sleep Disorders and Biliary Tract Disease: A Mendelian Randomization* includes snoring among sleep exposures and cholelithiasis/biliary outcomes. It is an unreviewed Mendelian-randomization analysis, not the same estimand as cross-cohort LDSC genetic correlation; therefore it limits first-genetic-test language but does not answer whether CKB–BBJ genetic sharing transports across Chinese and Japanese cohorts. No pairwise BBJ–CKB correlation has been run here. Source access, exact phenotype versions, BBJ precision and human clinical relevance remain insufficient for admission. The candidate is an optional validation route, not evidence of a new discovery.

The primary preprint text explicitly lists seven sleep traits including snoring and seven biliary outcomes including cholelithiasis; its main results emphasize daytime napping and refer all individual exposure–outcome calculations to Supplementary Table S1. I could not retrieve S1 from the accessible preprint copy. Therefore, this audit verifies that the exact pair was tested but does **not** classify the snoring–cholelithiasis MR estimate as positive, null, or statistically inconclusive. The pair's genetic-correlation novelty remains unresolved. Citation: [Research Square v1 PDF](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-2731162/v1).

## Newer OSA meta-analysis and pain prior art — source-level check

The 2025 medRxiv preprint *Genome-wide analysis in over 1.6 million participants uncovers 147 loci associated with obstructive sleep apnoea* reports a European OSA meta-analysis (230,657 cases; 1,377,442 controls) and putative causal relationships including multisite chronic pain. The meta-analysis combines UK Biobank, FinnGen, MVP, MGB and other cohorts. Because the aggregate includes project source cohorts, it is not an independent replication input for the historical OSA–abdominal-pain signal. The pain result is related prior evidence, not a direct genome-wide genetic-correlation comparator for abdominal pain. The public data statement points to aggregate METAL summary statistics; no cohort-excluded summary was found in this bounded source screen, and All of Us individual-level data are controlled. Do not present this literature result as new evidence from the present project or as independent confirmation. Source: [medRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.08.25339824).

## Conditional 23andMe sleep-apnea source and prior art — 2026-10-10

Campos et al. (SLEEP 2023, zsac308) describe a 23andMe sleep-apnea replication GWAS in 1,477,352 participants (175,522 self-reported cases; 1,301,830 controls). The survey item asks whether participants were ever diagnosed with or treated for sleep apnea; the paper describes BMI-adjusted logistic regression. Full summary statistics require a qualified-researcher agreement with 23andMe. This is a potential independent sleep source, not a currently accessible project input. Its article reports genetic correlations with multiple traits, including multisite chronic pain, so broad OSA–pain novelty is already constrained. That is related prior evidence, not an exact OSA–abdominal-pain genetic-correlation comparator. The source is not admitted here: no file, pair protocol, outcome source, overlap contract, or power assessment has been verified, and no estimator was run. Citation: [Campos et al.](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/46/3/zsac308/6918774).

## MVP OSA GWAS and access-gated independent sleep-source lead — 2026-10-10

Sofer et al. report a 568,576-participant MVP EHR-based OSA GWAS with both BMI-adjusted and unadjusted models, sex/ancestry subgroup analyses, and a 916,696-participant MVP+FinnGen+MGB meta-analysis. The MVP phenotype uses an EHR multimodal automated algorithm; it is clinically ascertained but not a universal sleep-test phenotype. Full MVP summary statistics are routed through dbGaP (phs001672), for which the official MVP page currently specifies an application for full downloads. The MVP-only GWAS is a potential sleep-side replacement for the project’s FinnGen R9 OSA source; the aggregate meta is not cohort-independent because it includes FinnGen. This source adds prior art for OSA genetics, sex heterogeneity and OSA cross-trait sharing, and it does not create a new project finding. No data were accessed or analyzed. [Sofer et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10065974/); [MVP summary-statistics access](https://www.mvp.va.gov/pwa/discover-mvp-data).
