## Independence, overlap and dependence

All 41 historical estimates reuse discovery sleep GWAS. Therefore none is a fully independent two-trait replication. Distinct outcome sources can provide useful outcome-side corroboration under the estimator assumptions. Source reuse does not by itself invalidate the individual correlation; it changes the replication label and covariance needed to compare correlations. All four discovery–validation cross-stage intersections, both within-stage intersections, and reuse across validation waves matter. The sealed cohort matrix records these separately. Its stronger old `NON_OVERLAPPING_CONFIRMED` labels must be shown beside the current qualification: named cohorts distinct, individual intersections unknown. An unconstrained cross-trait intercept is not an individual membership audit or a between-rg covariance estimate. The [official LDSC instructions](https://github.com/bulik/ldsc/wiki/Heritability-and-Genetic-Correlation) explain overlap-related intercept modeling; provenance independence remains a separate design property.

FinnGen R9 and R13 cannot be treated as separate disjoint cohorts. Exact release totals do not determine the membership intersection or prove strict nesting. Core R9 apnea versus R9 MS/asthma/T2D/CAD/melanoma is within-source discovery, and R13 sleep alternatives share FinnGen information with the earlier FinnGen outcome-validation wave. A new label, later freeze or different endpoint does not confer independence. Related individuals across cohorts can also create dependence; participant disjointness alone would not prove absence of shared relatives, but no numerical relatedness bound is available here. Retain the known-source and unknown-person distinctions without inventing overlap fractions.

The frozen MVP insomnia × ten FinnGen-outcome route changes both named discovery sources, while reusing the earlier FinnGen outcomes. The reverse FinnGen R13 insomnia × MVP abdominal-pain route changes both named discovery sources, while reusing the historical MVP outcome. These are possible clinical transport designs relative to original discovery, not two mutually independent corroboration waves. The same MVP outcome used with several sleep traits produces correlated tests; counting every positive as an independent confirmation would exaggerate evidence. No pooling or calibrated discovery–validation difference is admitted until joint sampling covariance and selection are addressed.

## Ascertainment, target population and measurement

The target is source-specific common-variant genetic sharing. It is not a patient's risk difference, diagnosis, treatment effect or causal direction. UKB usual sleep-initiation/night-waking complaints, MVP coded PheCode 327.4, and FinnGen F5_INSOMNIA represent related constructs with different inclusion rules and controls. The MVP public GIA definition gap is resolved at the documented rule level: at least two mapped ICD instances, zero-instance controls, no related-PheCode exclusions, EHR through September 2020. Distinct-date counting, spacing, lookback and executable historical extraction remain unresolved. Those precise questions replace the obsolete blanket HARE-only objection. FinnGen R13 uses F51.0/G47.0 in primary outpatient, hospital and death registers, no minimum-event requirement, and controls excluding F5_SLEEP. Neither endpoint proves chronic symptom duration, impairment, clinical adjudication or questionnaire equivalence. FinnGen's documented broader-control exclusions change the estimand; they must not be replaced with MVP's zero-code controls or imported from F5_SLEEP's own parent-control rule.

Control exclusion can alter genetic contrast and select on comorbidity or health-care contact. Register cases and uncoded controls differ in opportunity to receive a code. Health-care contact, age, survival, veteran eligibility and volunteer/device participation are potential selection mechanisms. This review infers vulnerabilities from designs; it has not measured the direction or magnitude of a project bias. The primary [UKB participation analysis by Schoeler et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01579-9) demonstrates that participation can change genetic correlations in other traits. It does not establish that these sleep correlations are biased by a particular amount or justify importing that study's weights.

MVP's primary resource reports an older, predominantly male overall sample and standardized EHR phenotyping; these are cohort-level facts, not accession-specific sex proportions. The [MVP primary publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12857194/) does not authorize extrapolation to all women, younger people, non-veterans or other ancestry groups. FinnGen's insomnia case counts are sex-imbalanced, but those case fractions do not establish sex-specific genetic effects. A difference between cohorts is jointly confounded by definition, ascertainment, age/sex composition, selection, ancestry and analysis pipeline; it cannot be attributed specifically to biological transport or measurement error.

Device main-period duration and questionnaire 24-hour duration including naps differ in construct, transformation and assessment time. The device GWAS is a later selected UKB subset. Their disease correlations can be compared descriptively only with valid joint uncertainty and a stated target. More significant questionnaire pairs may simply reflect sample information. Under an ideal single latent trait with independent measurement noise, rescaling/added noise can lower heritability and precision without necessarily changing genetic correlation; therefore neither differing h2 nor presumed device reliability alone explains a difference in rg. Exact-source reliability coefficients and selection effects are not verified. Shared controls for short/long sleep and exclusion of the opposite tail likewise preclude independent-tail counts or a U-shaped causal conclusion.

## Ancestry, reference LD, effect and N semantics

The analyses concern specific European-ancestry inputs, including a Finnish population source. Broad EUR labels do not certify identical LD, allele-frequency distributions, genetic effects or environments. Reference-LD mismatch is a possible estimator issue distinct from phenotype transport. Before a new source is eligible, pin release/body identity, signed tested allele, build conversion, retained variant universe, imputation-information policy, per-variant versus constant N and reference compatibility. Do not substitute a multi-population meta-analysis for the pinned EUR accession. A low intercept does not rule out all selection/LD/model bias; a high intercept does not uniquely diagnose its cause.

The companion N table derives metadata-only `N_eff=4*Ncase*Ncontrol/(Ncase+Ncontrol)`. FinnGen R13 insomnia has 51,643 cases and 446,273 controls; MVP clinical insomnia has 78,566/329,572; MVP abdominal pain has 89,262/299,053; MVP drug-allergy history has 535/448,351. The last total is 448,886 but its balanced case/control information is only about 2,137. This arithmetic is not a GWAS-reported per-SNP N, h2 estimate or power calculation. The [official GenomicSEM guidance](https://github.com/GenomicSEM/GenomicSEM/wiki/2.1-Calculating-Sum-of-Effective-Sample-Size-and-Preparing-GWAS-Summary-Statistics) distinguishes single-cohort N conventions from the sum of cohort-specific effective N needed in heterogeneous meta-analyses. Proxy/clinical mixtures, covariate-adjusted tests and source-specific per-variant missingness need their own contracts; a pooled case count cannot establish every meta-analysis N.

MVP insomnia preprocessing establishes a receipt-verified 19,703,815→826,027 chain, not the upstream CI coverage/test construction or EBI conversion. Historical MVP abdominal-pain/drug-allergy outcomes also use the CI-derived conversion and must not inherit uncertainty validation from another accession. Discrepant P versus CI-Z values cannot be filtered after seeing pair results. FinnGen supplies explicit log-OR SE in its documented schema, but the F5_INSOMNIA body/header/finite-row chain has not been verified here. Source P and beta/SE need not encode the same Wald test when correction/test methods differ; do not force equality or filter discrepancies without the source's test contract. INFO and per-variant N are absent from that documented schema. Missing INFO is not INFO=1 and source-level imputation QC does not certify the inherited per-variant INFO>0.9 rule. That gate needs documented release-specific equivalence or a new result-free protocol choice; it cannot be relaxed silently to enable this source.

The v4 statistical reviewer demonstrates inconsistent effective-N/sample-fraction liability presentation for MS/melanoma and reports fixed-fit sensitivities. This review accepts the arithmetic scope, not a validated epidemiologic prevalence. Age/time/population risk targets and the melanoma exclusion of all cancers remain unresolved. A bounded rescaled h2 does not make the source representative or pass melanoma's Z<4 gate. Constant positive rescaling can cancel in rg while absolute h2/covariance/power proxies remain wrong. Lipid source recovery and the identified two-step control-flow difference likewise do not clear the 36 flagged pair models. No unqualified lipid or absolute liability claim is admitted by preprocessing, algebra or native reproducibility alone.

## Prospective eligibility, power and null claims

Passing h2 Z≥4/intercept≤1.2 is estimator eligibility, not 80% power for an rg target. The frozen transport target is |rg|=.15, two-sided alpha=.05/217, 80% power. The companion script computes the exact normal-model planning SE ceiling and a declared-SE grid without using any candidate rg. The approximately .0332 ceiling is conditional on calibrated normal uncertainty and a prespecified effect; it is not achieved power. Source h2, SNP information, balanced case/control information, overlap, reference matching and uncertainty in the rg denominators must inform planning. The [LDSC FAQ](https://github.com/bulik/ldsc/wiki/FAQ) identifies low h2, small sample size and SNP coverage as causes of imprecision; the metadata count alone is insufficient.

For every eligible future test report estimate/SE/CI, corrected P, source QC and the minimum detectable magnitude based on the chosen planning/precision convention. Freeze a scientifically meaningful magnitude before outcomes; .15 is an existing research-design target, not a validated clinical threshold. Do not use discovery winners to set the alternative. Do not call an observed-estimate-derived post-hoc power a validation success. A CI containing zero is not absence; a precise null needs a declared equivalence/bounded-effect procedure and an interval inside a predeclared meaningful region. Missing, source-QC failed, numerically failed and unavailable associations remain unestimated/ineligible. A genuinely eligible but imprecise test is inconclusive, and a precise transport null concerns those constructs/populations only.

The existing 217-family correction remains frozen. Its candidate selection followed discovery magnitude/FDR/QC/novelty screening, so selection affects magnitudes and naive cross-stage differences. The 41 directionally concordant rows are correlated and selected; no binomial independent-trial evidence is admitted. Lower validation magnitudes do not isolate winner's curse, demographic heterogeneity or ascertainment. Calibrated covariance is necessary for difference tests but is not a selection correction. Discovery outcome–sleep sample sharing and reuse of the sleep h2 denominator must both enter joint uncertainty.

Keep the 396 and 1,200 discovery corrections separate and named. Neither supplies an overall study-wide false-discovery guarantee for their union plus new contrast/model/transport tests. The extension's prospective 100-outcome selection does not make it a representative phenome, and correlated outcome categories or nested disease codes do not yield independent biological confirmations. Discovery-density comparisons between families or sleep modalities require matched eligibility, precision and taxonomy; 603 FDR-positive rows alone do not measure greater biological importance. Do not silently recompute an effective family size or remove unfavorable/QC-excluded rows to improve that story.

**New multiplicity objection:** the reverse FinnGen-insomnia source route is not a source-specific experiment already frozen by the MVP-insomnia ten-outcome protocol. It needs an amendment before its h2/rg outcomes, defining the target estimand, source role, all attempted alternatives, stopping rule and interpretation. Reusing a historical pair ID and .05/217 does not allocate unlimited tests over alternate GWAS, releases, adjusted models, SNP universes or measurement definitions. If several sources are tried for one scientific target, freeze a primary-source rule or an explicit within-hypothesis alpha budget (for example an allocated .05/217 budget with correction over the prespecified alternatives), or treat the new family as separately exploratory. This is a recommendation, not an implemented new family. Preserve historical 396/1,200/217 results and their corrections byte for byte. No test admitted by this review.
