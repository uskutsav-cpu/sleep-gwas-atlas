The pinned `regressions.py:107` implements

`C(P,K) = K²(1−K)² / [P(1−P) φ(Φ⁻¹(1−K))²]`, with `h²_liability=C·h²_input`.

`Hsq.summary` lines 444–454 multiplies h2 and its SE after fitting. For a single pooled case/control cohort, effective N with P=.5 is the balanced-N convention; total N with actual sample fraction is the corresponding alternative. This is supported by the [official GenomicSEM effective-N guidance](https://github.com/GenomicSEM/GenomicSEM/wiki/2.1-Calculating-Sum-of-Effective-Sample-Size-and-Preparing-GWAS-Summary-Statistics). Meta-analyses need cohort-specific effective-N reasoning; pooled case counts cannot simply be generalized to every source.

Holding the original fit and K fixed, `C(.5,K)/C(P_logged,K)=4P_logged(1−P_logged)`, independent of K. The script uses standard-library NormalDist and independently checks this cancellation. It inverts the original printed conversion to recover the **N_eff-input scale**, which is not ordinary actual-sample observed-scale h2, and applies the balanced presentation:
