# Public-data assessment of the eight proposed extensions

**Reviewed:** 15 October 2026
**Scope:** the eight extensions in `EIGHT_EXTENSION_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.tsv`. No manuscript text was written, no frozen result was altered, and no participant data were accessed.

## Decision

The current atlas already supports the broad resource and global-correlation parts of this work. The most important remaining scientific gaps are cohort-independent sleep replication, ancestry-specific transportability, a calibrated phenotype-measurement contrast, and independent evidence of researcher utility. The newly published device-sleep GWAS is genuinely relevant public data, but it is UK Biobank European data and its authors have already published the primary sleep–health, sex, locus, and molecular follow-ups. Re-running that work across the entire Atlas panel would increase output volume while adding little independent evidence.

Accordingly, this assessment does **not** download the four full association files (1.255 GB total), does not rerun published LDSC/MR/colocalization, and does not add another broad testing family. The GWAS Catalog REST records, per-study metadata, and source MD5 manifests were saved and hashed. The raw statistics remain downloadable under the Catalog-reported CC0 terms; no hash of the full data files was computed because they were not transferred.

## What the public evidence adds

Portas et al. (2026) analyze four accelerometer-derived traits in 80,013 UK Biobank participants of European ancestry. Their models provide a finer phenotype axis than the Atlas's earlier actigraphy traits: night-time duration, efficiency, REM, and NREM. The authors report 20 autosomal loci, 12 not previously reported for the corresponding device phenotypes, and a REM/NREM contrast at MEIS1. They report genetic correlations with ten health traits, locus and eQTL follow-up, and bidirectional MR. They also report only moderate PSG concordance for inferred sleep staging (κ=0.32), which is a substantive measurement limitation. These are published findings, not new findings from the Atlas.

The Catalog confirms four full-statistics records, each with N=80,013, GRCh37, combined-sex summary statistics, and a CC0 license. The files are 312, 319, 312, and 312 MB as listed by the FTP server. The article's sex results therefore cannot be reconstructed from the four combined-sex Catalog files. The metadata calls out adjusted age, but does not provide age-stratified GWAS. Neither the source release nor its same-UKB cohort constitutes independent replication of older UKB sleep GWAS.

The public prior literature already includes multi-ancestry self-reported sleep-duration GWAS, and a 2026 four-cohort evaluation of sleep-phenotype definitions using PRS. Yale's landing page links six EUR, AFR, and cross-population short/long sleep summary files; HTTP HEAD requests returned 200 for each (about 925 MB combined). These are promising inputs for a future ancestry-specific sleep-source audit, but do not supply ancestry-matched disease outcomes or ancestry-specific LD for the Atlas. The data-use terms and file schema/checksums also remain unaudited. Population differences in sample size and ascertainment make counts of significant loci an invalid transferability test.

## Scientific priority by extension

1. **Sleep phenotype comparison:** retain as the strongest extension. The new device results make the construct distinction clearer, while existing Atlas sources cover self-report and older actigraphy. A valid next study needs a preregistered estimand and joint uncertainty for comparing same-outcome genetic correlations. Separate point estimates or overlapping UKB statistics do not establish a difference.
2. **Disease-system comparison:** the frozen 1,200-pair family already supplies broad coverage. A new 4-by-100 scan overlaps published device-sleep follow-up and would need a newly defined testing family; it is not justified as an unselected expansion.
3. **Ancestry comparison:** high scientific value, but no valid Atlas-level portability analysis is presently admitted. It requires ancestry-matched, cohort/overlap-resolved sources and LD references. Existing multi-ancestry papers are prior evidence, not a substitute.
4. **Age and sex:** the 2026 paper reports sex results, while its Catalog full-statistics releases are combined sex. It gives age-adjusted overall estimates, not a young-versus-old comparison. The paper itself calls its sex-specific locus results exploratory and in need of replication.
5. **Global vs local:** global rg is already available. Local analyses remain out of scope until a concrete, falsifiable locus/region question and method-compatible regional inputs are established.
6. **Molecular mechanisms:** published device-sleep loci/eQTL analyses already cover this source. No new mechanism should be claimed from Atlas-wide enrichment or proximity without a new admissible locus and independent molecular support.
7. **Replication and reliability:** the essential blocker remains. Existing 23 validation positives are outcome-side validation because discovery sleep is reused; the UKB device source does not repair that dependence. Seek genuinely cohort-disjoint sleep sources before calling any result independent two-trait replication.
8. **Existing-resource comparison:** keep the V7 benchmark's comparator snapshots and denominator fixed. The device-sleep article is a study, not a comparable general-purpose resource export. It adds important prior art but does not change the measured resource-coverage result.

## Critical path and stopping rule

The critical path is (i) authenticate at least one cohort-disjoint sleep GWAS with compatible phenotype and complete source metadata; (ii) for ancestry work, audit the Yale file schema, checksums and rights, then establish ancestry/build/allele/effective-N/overlap compatibility with matched disease outcomes and LD; (iii) freeze a narrow phenotype or transportability estimand and its multiplicity family; (iv) execute only that admitted comparison with calibrated uncertainty; and (v) report it against the existing frozen Atlas and named prior resources. If no independent compatible sleep source or calibrated covariance model is found, retain a source-aware descriptive resource claim and do not present comparative or local results as inferential evidence.

The large public 2026 files should be downloaded only if a human investigator approves a particular analysis question that differs from the published ten-outcome/device follow-up or if a reproducible source-integration test becomes necessary. Raw-data transfer is not itself a scientific milestone.
