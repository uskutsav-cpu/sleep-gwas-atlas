# Targeted audit of prior genetic sleep–frailty studies

Audit date: 2026-09-23. This focused audit covers eight directly relevant genetic studies in the
frozen PubMed corpus (four from 2024, three from 2025, and one from 2026),
plus one targeted 2026 publisher follow-up absent from the frozen corpus. All eight captured queue rows remain unscreened; the 2026 record is separately
logged as an external follow-up and is not added to frozen PRISMA counts. This is not a completed systematic review, and licensed database
exports remain absent.

## Findings

### Song et al. (2024): insomnia and Frailty Index shared architecture

PubMed PMID 38425786; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:38425786:97252b6b220a` (reviewer decisions blank;
retrieved in two PubMed batches).

The study paired the Atkins et al. Frailty Index GWAS (GCST90020053; N=175,226,
UK Biobank plus Swedish TwinGene) with an insomnia GWAS identified as
`ukb-b-3957` (N=462,341; UK Biobank). It ran bivariate LDSC with unconstrained
intercepts and a constrained-intercept sensitivity analysis, local genetic
correlation with rho-HESS, cross-trait analyses with MTAG/CPASSOC, and GTEx
SMR. It reported rg=1.14 under the unconstrained model and rg=0.61 under the
constrained-intercept sensitivity model, a local region at 3p21.31, and
cross-trait/functionally annotated findings. The unconstrained point estimate
is outside the theoretical correlation range; its change under the constrained
sensitivity analysis makes the reported magnitude especially sensitive to
intercept assumptions. Treat this as evidence that prior work examined global
and local overlap, not as a plausible quantitative benchmark for comparison.

This is direct prior evidence for insomnia–FI genetic overlap and explicitly
precludes presenting that link, a local-sharing result, or QTL follow-up as a
first. Its FI input is the same registered GCST90020053 summary source used
here. The insomnia source is UK Biobank based; both source descriptions
therefore indicate cohort-level UKB overlap, while exact participant
intersection is unknown. The two LDSC estimates are not independent
replications of the current atlas extract.

### Li et al. (2024): sleep duration and napping with Frailty Index

PubMed PMID 38368347; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:38368347:22f5b0c19626` (reviewer decisions blank).
This study combined two-sample univariable and multivariable MR with LDSC for
leisure sedentary behaviours, sleep duration, daytime napping, and FI. It
reported FI genetic correlations for sleep duration (rg=-0.20) and daytime
napping (rg=0.25), alongside MR associations. It is prior evidence for both
global sleep–FI genetic correlation and MR; its rg estimates and MR results
are distinct estimands and do not establish independent replication here.
Exact cohort intersections with the project inputs require source-level
verification.

### Lu et al. (2024): insomnia, sleep duration, sleepiness, and frailty

PubMed PMID 38502408; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:38502408:b44430107842` (reviewer decisions blank).
This two-sample MR study examined insomnia, sleep duration, daytime sleepiness,
and frailty, with heterogeneity, pleiotropy, and outlier sensitivity methods.
It is prior sleep–frailty MR evidence, not LDSC replication. Its PubMed abstract
contains internally inconsistent reported confidence intervals and P values
for at least some estimates (for example, an interval crossing the null paired
with P=0.02); we therefore do not reproduce its numerical estimates here.
Those abstract-level inconsistencies should be checked against the full report
before quantitative synthesis. Cohort overlap with the current sources remains
unresolved.

### Deng et al. (2024): seven sleep traits and Frailty Index

PubMed PMID 38784581; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:38784581:97f289205197` (reviewer decisions blank;
retrieved in two PubMed batches).

This study used the same Atkins FI GWAS (GCST90020053; N=175,226) with seven
self-reported sleep traits, including insomnia, sleep duration, chronotype,
napping, sleep apnea and snoring. It reported bidirectional univariable and
multivariable MR, sensitivity methods, MRlap overlap correction, and LDSC
genetic correlations between sleep and FI. The paper reports a significant
LDSC correlation only for chronotype (rg=-0.03, P=0.011); it also reports MR
estimates, which target a different estimand from global LDSC rg.

This work already covers multiple sleep traits in relation to FI and is not an
independent replication source for our UKB-based analyses: the FI source is
the same, the sleep sources are UKB-based, and exact participant intersections
are not supplied. The published MR estimates should not be conflated with
replication of genetic-correlation estimates or interpreted as causal proof.

### Che et al. (2025): two frailty definitions and sleep traits

PubMed PMID 39936043; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:39936043:28078d381cf3` (reviewer decisions blank;
also retrieved by the genetic query and deduplicated into this queue row).

This bidirectional two-sample MR study included FI (N=175,226), Fried Frailty
Score (FFS; N=386,565), insomnia (N=283,595), sleep duration (N=445,966), and
sleep apnea (N=523,366). It reported associations in both directions: FI with
shorter sleep duration and sleep apnea; FFS with insomnia, shorter sleep, and
sleep apnea; and sleep liability to insomnia and short or long sleep with FI,
with short or long sleep also associated with FFS. It used IVW with MR-Egger,
weighted median/mode, MR-PRESSO, and heterogeneity checks. The abstract reports
MR results, not LDSC genetic correlations, so this is prior evidence for
sleep–frailty MR across FI and Fried constructs, not replication of global rg.
The article methods specify that its FI meta-analysis combines UK Biobank
(n=164,610) and Swedish TwinGene (n=10,616), while its sleep-duration meta-analysis
combines UK Biobank (n=278,003) and the Million Veteran Program (n=167,963).
This establishes cohort-level UKB participation in both inputs, although exact
participant intersections are not reported. The FFS summary-source identity
and the catalog accessions for insomnia and sleep apnea still require
source-level verification. This study cannot be treated as independent
replication for the project’s UKB-based comparisons.

### Zhang et al. (2025): sleep disorders and multiple aging outcomes

PubMed PMID 40738335; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:40738335:04799ea9936b` (reviewer decisions blank).
This two-sample MR analysis tested daytime sleepiness, insomnia, and sleep
apnea against several aging outcomes, including FI, and applied FDR correction.
The abstract reports associations of all three sleep traits with FI. This is
prior MR evidence, not global genetic-correlation replication; exact input
study identities and cohort overlap remain to be reconciled.

### Gao et al. (2025): sleep traits and Frailty Index

PubMed PMID 41398875; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:41398875:73608cce138c` (reviewer decisions blank).
This bidirectional MR study included sleep duration, short and long sleep,
napping, morning preference, chronotype, and FI. The abstract reports a
bidirectional association for short sleep and FI, with no significant result
for other sleep traits. It does not estimate LDSC rg, and exact GWAS-source
identity and cohort overlap are unresolved.

### Zhang et al. (2026): sleep behaviours and functional/biological aging

PubMed PMID 41821113; queue row
`primary_sleep_frailty:41821113:c7536774509f` (reviewer decisions blank).
This univariable and multivariable MR study included FI among multiple aging
outcomes and examined chronotype, daytime napping, and sleep duration. Its
abstract reports associations of napping and sleep duration with FI. It is
relevant adjacent evidence for the sleep–frailty question, but does not
replicate global LDSC rg; cohort overlap and exact GWAS-source identity remain
unresolved.

### Huang et al. (2026): obstructive sleep apnea and frailty traits

PMID 41692760; DOI `10.1186/s12890-026-04178-2`. It is in the frozen
2026-09-22 PubMed snapshot in three query XML sources, all matching their
recorded byte counts and SHA-256 values. Its PubMed Entrez and publication
dates (2026-02-15 and 2026-02-16) are within the registered query limits. The
three source occurrences deduplicate to one screening-queue row
(`primary_sleep_frailty:41692760:11f58fecd8d0`); its reviewer decisions and
adjudication remain blank. It is already counted in the frozen snapshot and
must be screened through the registered dual-review workflow. Detailed source
verification and correction of prior notes:
`frailty_paper/analysis/pubmed_record_41692760_snapshot_verification_2026-09-24.md`.

Huang et al. used bidirectional two-sample MR, with FinnGen OSA summary data
(N=217,955; 16,761 cases) and FI summary data of N=175,226 from the Atkins
source also used by this project. Their forward OSA-to-FI IVW result was null
(OR=1.013, 95% CI 0.943–1.088, P=0.73; three OSA instruments). Reverse FI-to-OSA
IVW was positive (OR=1.777, 95% CI 1.137–2.777, P=0.01; 14 instruments), with
heterogeneity reported for the FI instruments. These MR estimates address a
different question from LDSC genetic correlation and do not replicate a global
rg estimate. The local project’s registered FinnGen R9 sleep-apnea dataset has
a different reported sample size; release identity and participant overlap
with Huang et al.’s OSA input have not been established. This study therefore
adds relevant prior MR evidence but is not independent replication of the
project’s genetic-correlation analysis.

Interpret the directional findings cautiously. The article says only three
OSA-associated instruments were available for the forward analyses and that
MR-PRESSO could not be run because of this limited instrument count. Its
OSA-to-appendicular-lean-mass estimate was significant only in an additional
analysis including two BMI-related variants (OR=1.166, 95% CI 1.062–1.279,
P<0.01); after their exclusion, the estimate was null (OR=1.026, 95% CI
0.985–1.068, P=0.22). The authors also report heterogeneity for reverse FI,
appendicular-lean-mass and walking-pace instruments. Their term “frailty
phenotype” is operationalized as sarcopenia-related appendicular lean mass,
grip strength and walking pace, rather than the five-component Fried score.
These design and sensitivity details limit the strength and scope of this
study’s MR claims; they do not change the prespecified LDSC estimand or the
study’s unscreened status. [Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12890-026-04178-2)

## Positioning consequence

The manuscript must state that multiple prior genetic studies of sleep and
frailty exist, including prior global FI genetic-correlation estimates and
MR analyses across FI and Fried definitions. It must not claim the first insomnia–frailty genetic link, first
genome-wide or local overlap, or first molecular follow-up. The defensible
distinction is a broader phenotype-specific map and an explicitly gated
attempt to assess replication and follow-up across frailty dimensions. The
current results do not yet establish independent replication or novel local,
variant, gene, tissue or cell-type findings. The introduction and reviewer
audit were updated accordingly.
