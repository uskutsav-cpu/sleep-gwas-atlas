# Limitations, corrections, and publication boundaries

## What this analysis can support

These are cross-sectional observational associations in NHANES 2011–2014, with a separate prognostic construct check against public vital status through 2019. They support the existence of an association between a lifetime disclosure question and an adapted health-deficit score under the stated models. They do not test whether sleep difficulty causes frailty, whether removing items reduces a genetic correlation, whether one score predicts frailty better, or whether the candidate scores work in individuals. No journal acceptance, clinical utility, genetic validation, or causal interpretation is implied. The estimates and analysis plan were developed retrospectively without a locked independent preregistration.

## Reconstruction and systematic error

* The 49-item reference paper leaves some mapping and scoring ambiguous. This project selects KIQ005 for urinary leakage; treats BMI ≥30 as the printed obesity condition; averages observed dominant-hand grip trials and infers H1 is the first tested hand; and assigns walking/steps limitation to those who need special walking equipment and were skipped. The latter does not establish actual difficulty on each specific task. A no-walk/steps sensitivity is provided. Neither the FI nor the Fried-like count is an exact recreation of its source instrument.
* More than 5% of MEC participants lack 21 reference items in 2011–2012 and 13 in 2013–2014, including many PHQ-9 and performance measures. The 2011–2012 visit item is ambiguous for the 4–9-visit category; the two cycles also ask about different care locations. The 80% rule means participants may have different items in their denominators. The care-free, all-complete, and response-weighted analyses address aspects of this problem but do not establish missing-at-random or remove selection bias.
* The FI includes correlated consequences of illness and medical care. No pair had |Pearson r|>0.8 in the reported observed-pair audit, but this screen does not rule out redundancy, local dependence, or differential measurement. Several clinically used lab reference ranges create high deficit prevalence; excluding all six laboratory components is reported. The age signal, sex comparison, right-skewed distribution and mortality association are construct checks, not instrument validation; no predictive discrimination, calibration or external validation is claimed.
* FI items other than DPQ030/040 do not directly ask about sleep, fatigue or exhaustion, but sleep-disorder treatment or indirect sleep-related disability may still contribute to medication counts, functional limitations, diseases and care utilization. Mood- and care-excluded versions probe, rather than eliminate, this possibility.
* SLQ050 captures ever disclosing trouble sleeping, which depends on recall, healthcare access, help-seeking and clinician contact, not necessarily current or clinically defined insomnia. DPQ030 includes sleeping too much and its two-week timing differs from the lifetime SLQ measure. Survey models do not remove confounding by illness, socioeconomic factors or healthcare-seeking; added covariates can also be mediators or colliders. Reverse causation and common-method bias remain plausible. Age is topcoded at 80.
* Different FI versions and old/new physical composites contain different items and use different denominators. The paired contrast tests a difference between scale-specific slopes, not a mechanistic decomposition. The 28-item missingness-screened version is below the frequently recommended ≥30 deficits; the >0.21 cutoff and ≥3 adapted physical criteria are not validated diagnostic thresholds for these adaptations.
* R `survey` and Python produce the same point estimates and Taylor SEs, which is a software cross-check rather than a second independent cohort. The design-based jackknife response-weight sensitivity refits the response model in each replicate. It cannot recover unmeasured missingness drivers. The 2011–2012 physical-four score's previously observed nonsignificant association remains a genuine negative finding; a multidomain score is not the same endpoint.
* The sex interaction has an unadjusted p=0.043 but Holm p≈0.259 across the exploratory family. The cycle interaction and the paired new-versus-old score association contrast were not statistically clear; these should not be presented as confirmed subgroup differences or evidence of equivalence. Small numbers of masked variance units limit inferential precision. Confidence intervals are based on design degrees of freedom, and all families were chosen retrospectively.
* Public-use mortality status is not perturbed, but source linkage can have errors and six older MEC participants were ineligible. Some public follow-up times and cause-of-death values are synthetic by CDC design; they are excluded here. Age/sex/cycle adjustment does not make variable follow-up time a fixed time horizon. The mortality association is in the same cohort and overlaps the reference-study setting, so it is not independent predictive validation.

## Corrections and updates relative to the earlier project output

1. The earlier four-component report stated too broadly that weight-loss intention was unavailable. WHQ060 records intention after a qualifying weight change. The new adapted shrinking component uses it for >10-lb loss. The earlier score is retained without modification and was independently matched exactly for its nonmissing rows.
2. This final package adds linked 2019 mortality status, a separate R `survey` implementation, alternate recent-symptom and index definitions, an interaction test, missingness response weighting with replicate-refitted propensity models, item-by-exposure missingness tables, and vector figures. None replaces or edits the earlier files.
3. The seven-primary-test Holm family is retained to preserve comparability with the earlier index reconstruction. The expanded 33-test exploratory family is separate, so adjusted p-values for exploratory analyses can differ from earlier reports. Unadjusted coefficients, Taylor SEs and their pooled CIs match the earlier implementation.
4. The earlier 15/22 marker package reproduced saved prototype values and cached audits. This package additionally checks all rows against three actual project GWAS summary-statistic files and directly queries Ensembl GRCh37 records. This is source and reference consistency, not an independent confirmation of every original effect allele, study build or multiallelic contrast.

## Remaining external requirements

1. **Genetic overlap question:** obtain approved linkable genotype data and phenotype data, with consent, sample QC, ancestry/relatedness adjustment, appropriate replication, and sufficiently powered GWAS/heritability before LDSC comparisons. CDC's information sheet lists NHANES 2011–2012 repository variants as forthcoming; individual-marker coverage is currently unknown. User-provided candidate source files and reference checks alone do not make it available.
2. **Score validation:** an independent held-out cohort with reliably measured frailty and insomnia outcomes, adequate genotyping/imputation and identical variant contrasts is required for the 15- and 22-marker score calibration, discrimination and portability. Original GWAS data curators may need to confirm FFS A1 semantics and multiallelic encoding.
3. **Definitive phenotype validation:** an independent cohort with original 15-foot walking times, CES-D exhaustion items, matching grip protocol, validated physical activity and longitudinal unintentional weight loss would be needed to evaluate the exact original Fried phenotype. Independent outcome and measurement validation would also be required before calling this adapted FI validated.
4. **Unperturbed mortality timing/longer follow-up:** the CDC 2022-linked mortality files and unaltered time-to-event variables require approved NCHS RDC access. Whether an approved project may use any particular resource must be verified with CDC; the public binary-vital-status check does not substitute for this.
5. **Editorial readiness:** external expert review of the clinical phenotype, causal assumptions and target-journal requirements remains necessary. These drafts are intended for review and integration, not automatic insertion into the main manuscript.
