# Independent replication resource audit

**Scope:** source-feasibility review for Phase 8. This is not a replication analysis. No new frailty-specific sleep–frailty estimate is available yet, so no association is labeled replicated or non-replicated. The candidate/source assessment is tabulated in `replication_resource_audit.tsv`. It covers all seven requested priority branches, including conditional sleep-apnea/long-sleep pairs, a generic component/factor branch, and the HFRS alternate-definition candidate.

## Evidence and interpretation

- The Atkins Frailty Index GWAS combines UK Biobank and Swedish TwinGene participants. Mekli et al. studied a cumulative-deficit Frailty Index in HRS and ELSA, which are distinct cohort sources. Their report provides selected association results; this audit did not establish a complete genome-wide summary-statistics release suitable for LDSC or paired sleep–FI analysis. This is a comparable FI construct, not an identical operationalization. [Mekli et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0207824)

### Mekli source-availability recheck (2026-09-23)

Rechecked the primary article record and its supporting-information descriptions. The paper labels S3 “GenAssoc” as the **most significant results** of the genome-wide scan, rather than a complete genome-wide summary-statistics file. Its data-availability statement points to HRS genetics through dbGaP (`phs000428.v2.p2`) and ELSA genetics through EGA (`EGAS00001001036`); these individual-level resource routes do not supply the paired genome-wide sleep and FI summary statistics needed for the planned LDSC replication. Mekli therefore remains a comparable FI outcome-side source only; the exact HRS/ELSA phenotype/sample possibilities do not establish pairwise replication. No protected data were requested or accessed. [Primary article and supporting-information record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258126/).
- A separate Fried Frailty Score GWAS used 386,565 UK Biobank discovery participants and 9,720 HRS validation participants. In HRS, 18/22 eligible or well-proxied UKB lead variants had concordant direction (binomial sign-test p=0.00042); the UKB-derived polygenic score was associated with binary FFS (OR per SD=1.27, 95% CI 1.22–1.31, p=1.3×10⁻¹¹). This supports transportability of frailty-score genetic prediction, not replication of a sleep–frailty genetic correlation: no paired independent sleep GWAS result is reported. The paper states that HRS data are available through dbGaP accession phs000428.v2.p2. [Kim et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/)
- The Dashti sleep-duration study tested sleep-duration signals and risk scores in CHARGE (18 studies; 47,180 participants). This is independent sleep-trait-side evidence relative to UK Biobank. It is not an exact short-sleep phenotype replication, and it does not by itself replicate a sleep–frailty or sleep–lifespan association. [Dashti et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-08917-4)
### UKB–MVP extreme-short-sleep source recheck (2026-09-23)

The primary Austin-Zimmerman et al. paper reports an EUR short-sleep GWAS meta-analysis with 445,966 participants: 278,003 from UK Biobank and 167,963 from MVP. Its phenotype is ≤5 hours versus 7–8 hours, which is comparable to, but not the same threshold as, the locked short-sleep trait. The official Yale lab summary-statistics page links the EUR short-sleep meta-analysis file alongside cross-population and AFR files; it does not link an MVP-only EUR file. The meta-analysis therefore contains UKB participants also represented in the FI source and cannot serve as independent replication for the UKB+TwinGene FI result. [Austin-Zimmerman et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41249-y), [Yale lab summary statistics](https://medicine.yale.edu/lab/gelernter/stats/).

The article states that the GWAS summary statistics are deposited in dbGaP `phs001672` and available through the lab's public page. The current public dbGaP study record presents an authorized-access route for MVP study data; it does not establish that an MVP-only version of this specific sleep GWAS is openly downloadable. No access request was submitted. An MVP-only EUR file, if independently made available under authorized terms, could be reassessed for comparable sleep-trait-side support, but would still need h²/QC, build, allele, ancestry/LD and overlap review and would not by itself replicate a paired sleep–FI estimate. [dbGaP `phs001672.v1.p1`](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs001672.v1.p1).

**Decision:** Record the public UKB–MVP meta-analysis as ineligible for independent replication because UKB is shared with the FI discovery. Do not infer the MVP-only estimate from the meta-analysis or label any sleep-side signal as pairwise replication.

### MVP debility-code endpoint feasibility check (2026-09-24)

The public Million Veteran Program (MVP) PheWAS data dictionary lists `Phe_799` as a binary “Debility unspecified” phenotype in the Symptoms group. The current dictionary reports 618,973 participants overall (19,654 cases) and 441,123 participants in the European ancestry group (13,556 cases, 427,567 controls). The official MVP gwPheWAS information says cases require at least two phecode-mapped ICD-9/ICD-10 codes and controls have no such code; only population-specific results with at least 500 cases were uploaded to dbGaP. The paper and dbGaP documentation describe the `phs002453.v1.p1` summary-statistics release as publicly browsable/downloadable. The direct data-dictionary and methods references are [dbGaP data dictionary, phd008759.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetPdf.cgi?id=phd008759.1), [MVP gwPheWAS Basic Information, phd008760.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetPdf.cgi?id=phd008760.1), and [Verma et al., Science (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adj1182).

This is an independent cohort source relative to the UK Biobank/TwinGene FI source, but “Debility unspecified” is an EHR-coded, nonspecific symptom phenotype, not a validated Frailty Index, Fried phenotype, or HFRS. The locked analysis plan does not include it. The exact GIA association file and variant-level schema were not downloaded or verified in this audit, and no compatible independent MVP sleep GWAS paired with it was established. **Decision:** retain as a ruled-out, lower-specificity resource lead; do not add it to a primary or confirmatory replication family, alter the frozen plan, or treat its case count as a frailty measure. Reconsider only under an explicitly approved exploratory amendment with the precise code definition, file provenance, ancestry/build, sleep phenotype, and sample-overlap implications verified first. No file was downloaded and no access route was bypassed.

### CHARGE detailed summary-statistics access recheck (2026-09-23)

The primary Dashti et al. paper reports an adult CHARGE sleep-duration study (47,180 participants from 18 studies) and evaluates selected lead-locus associations and a sleep-duration risk score in CHARGE. Its Data Availability statement points to the Sleep Disorder Knowledge Portal and UK Biobank for summary GWAS statistics; it does not identify an openly downloadable full, all-variant CHARGE sleep-duration file. The paper therefore provides sleep-side locus/score validation, not the genome-wide input needed to estimate a new CHARGE sleep-duration–frailty LDSC correlation. [Dashti et al., Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-08917-4)

The official CHARGE results page points to dbGaP `phs000930` for full summary statistics. The current public dbGaP record (`phs000930.v11.p1`) states that the open query exposes rsID and P-value, while detailed statistics including effect size require an approved Data Access Request. The public record does not enumerate this specific sleep-duration analysis, so its inclusion and exact file coverage remain unverified. No Data Access Request was submitted, and no protected data were accessed. [CHARGE Results](https://www.chargeconsortium.com/main/results), [dbGaP `phs000930.v11.p1`](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000930.v11.p1)

**Decision:** Keep the CHARGE row as candidate sleep-trait-side evidence only. Do not infer sleep–frailty replication from sleep-locus replication, and do not run LDSC using a top-locus subset. If an authorized investigator later verifies that the exact CHARGE phenotype is present and obtains detailed data, classify any new analysis separately and assess phenotype and cohort overlap before use.

### CLSA ordinal Fried-phenotype GWAS recheck (2026-09-23)

Borhan et al. report a 2026 ordinal GWAS in 23,105 European-ancestry CLSA participants. The phenotype uses non-frail/prefrail/frail categories derived from five Fried-style components, but with study-specific operational definitions. The paper reports one genome-wide significant lead SNP and explicitly reports no validation sample, no effect estimates/95% confidence intervals, and no SNP-heritability estimate. Its data-availability statement directs researchers to CLSA access; no public full summary-statistics file or accession is identified in the article. The study does not test sleep and cannot itself estimate or replicate a sleep–frailty relationship. Exact overlap with any paired sleep dataset is UNKNOWN. See `analysis/clsa_ordinal_frailty_gwas_resource_audit_2026-09-23.md` for details, including the cytoband inconsistency in the source. [Borhan et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00363-z)

**Decision:** Retain only as contextual phenotype-side evidence; classify it `NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE` for pairwise replication. Do not use the reported lead SNP as a substitute for genome-wide statistics or infer a locus-level replication. Reassess only if complete, accessible summary statistics with effects, standard errors, build and alleles are verified alongside a compatible independent sleep dataset and overlap review. No CLSA application was submitted and no controlled data were accessed.

- FinnGen R9 G6_SLEEPAPNO is a distinct hospital-record sleep-apnea phenotype. The registered primary FI GWAS describes UK Biobank and TwinGene. No shared cohort is identified from these source descriptions, but exact participant intersection remains unknown. A distinct cohort for the sleep exposure alone does not establish pairwise replication. [FinnGen R9 documentation](https://finngen.gitbook.io/documentation/r9/data-download)
- The 2025 HFRS GWAS used FinnGen R12 discovery (N=500,737; Finnish ancestry) and UK Biobank replication (N=407,463; white British). HFRS is a continuous sum of 109 weighted ICD-10 codes; the dementia-excluded score is a separate sensitivity phenotype. The article directs FinnGen summary-statistics access through the consortium route and does not identify a directly downloadable HFRS endpoint in the checked materials. FinnGen could offer an alternative frailty definition, but the ancestry/LD-reference match and exact overlap need review; its UKB replication cohort is shared with UKB sleep sources. No sleep–HFRS estimate is reported, so HFRS is not a pairwise replication result. The publisher workbook provides only thresholded FinnGen hits. In its cross-reference, the workbook yields 1,261/1,588 HFRS hits with UKB beta/SE/P and 13/36 nominally significant independent leads, versus 1,262 and 14 reported in the article; HFRS-without-dementia counts reproduce the article. These are source-study variant checks and do not establish a sleep–HFRS replication. See `hfrs_published_lead_variant_replication_2026-09-24.md`. [Mak et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y)

## Replication decision

Current status for all prioritized pairs is `NOT_YET_ASSESSABLE`: no headline new pairwise estimate has been produced. Candidate evidence is useful for planning, but current sources do not establish a complete independent paired dataset for those relationships. The replication classification must be assigned only after (1) an eligible headline association exists, (2) exact phenotype and cohort overlap are verified, and (3) paired summary statistics or an appropriate independent cohort analysis are available. Use the labels `EXACT PHENOTYPE REPLICATION`, `COMPARABLE PHENOTYPE REPLICATION`, or `NO INDEPENDENT REPLICATION AVAILABLE` only at that stage.

For HRS/ELSA and CHARGE, retain the distinction between cohort-level independence and the participant-level intersections relevant to each analysis. Do not interpret unknown overlap as zero. FinnGen summary-statistics access rules and other controlled data access requirements remain in force; no forms were submitted and no access controls were bypassed.
