# British Regional Heart Study: feasibility for genetic-score validation

Reviewed 29 October 2026. **Status: no-fee controlled access to the BRHS phenotype deposit, but the separate potential genetic route lists cost recovery and no linkage; not yet an executable or confirmed free score-validation dataset.** The cohort has sleep questions, physical-frailty assessments and genotyping, but the public documentation reviewed does not establish that individual-level genotype dosages are included in the UK Data Service deposit or that they can be linked to the deposited phenotypes. No independent score validation has been run.

## What is documented

- The study enrolled 7,735 men in 1978–80, predominantly white, with repeated follow-up. The study describes extensive genotyping, and the data guide identifies a pseudonymised UK Data Service deposit (study 9402). The original cohort count is not the number available for analysis. [Cohort website](https://research.ncl.ac.uk/brhs/); [version 4 guide, pp. 5–6](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/9402/mrdoc/pdf/9402_brhs_data_user_guide_version_4_ukds.pdf); [catalogue](https://datacatalogue.ukdataservice.ac.uk/studies/study/9402).
- Questionnaire inventory includes difficulty falling asleep, early awakening, trouble maintaining sleep, sleep quality and duration. These items have appeared at multiple waves; ascertain which specific waves and codings align with each frailty assessment before defining insomnia. The published sleep-and-mortality analysis illustrates use of the cohort's sleep measurements, not genetic-score validation. [Guide, p. 31](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/9402/mrdoc/pdf/9402_brhs_data_user_guide_version_4_ukds.pdf); [sleep study](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10177891/1/Lennon_Sleep%20patterns%20and%20mortality%20april%2012th%20finalgoyaclean.pdf).
- The guide lists derived frailty score categories and components including no energy, weight loss, activity, slow walk and grip for later examinations. This is a physical-frailty phenotype; its exact thresholds, missing-data rules and relation to the project's FFS must be checked before calling it the same outcome. It is not established that a deficit-accumulation FI is provided. [Guide, p. 19](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/9402/mrdoc/pdf/9402_brhs_data_user_guide_version_4_ukds.pdf).
- There were 1,722 and 667 physical examination attendees at approximately 30 and 40 years respectively; these are examination counts, not counts with linked genotypes, sleep and frailty. Loss to follow-up, survivor selection and a male, predominantly white cohort limit generalizability. [Cohort profile](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyac122); [guide](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/9402/mrdoc/pdf/9402_brhs_data_user_guide_version_4_ukds.pdf).

## Genetic-data route and array coverage

UCLEB lists BRHS among its studies and describes a steering-group application for consortium data. A published BRHS UCLEB sample used a cardiometabolic chip and cardiovascular case-control selection; the historical sample is not a representative count for our target analysis. A [public manufacturer-manifest screen](brhs_metabochip_score_marker_screen.md) found only 1/15 and 2/22 coordinate matches on the chip, respectively. This does not count imputed variants or other genotyping datasets, and actual cohort coverage and quality remain unknown. The UCLEB catalogue lists cost recovery and a no-linkage data-use limitation. Its fee amount and whether the restriction permits use of existing linked genotype and phenotype data remain unverified; the no-fee SecureLab statement for the BRHS phenotype deposit must not be extended to UCLEB genetic access. [UCLEB catalogue](https://healthdatagateway.org/en/dataset/831); [study design](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0071345).

## Access and unresolved feasibility

The released dataset is **controlled access**, not an unrestricted public download. The guide requires data-owner approval, a secure-access application, training and UK-based access. UK Data Service says SecureLab currently charges no researcher access fee; institution, eligibility and any other project-specific costs still need confirmation. It also lists UK organisation affiliation requirements and limited international arrangements for some non-ONS data; the BRHS guide specifically states users must be based in the UK. No BRHS-specific international exception has been established. [Guide, p. 5](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/9402/mrdoc/pdf/9402_brhs_data_user_guide_version_4_ukds.pdf); [SecureLab fees and conditions](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/help/secure-lab/securelab-faqs/); [eligibility](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/help/secure-lab/am-i-eligible-to-apply-to-access-securelab/).

The guide's introduction explicitly mentions genotyping; its phenotype and derived-variable inventories reviewed do not establish a genotype file, genomic build, variant content, dosage quality, or a permitted linkage pathway. Absence of such detail is not evidence that genotypes do not exist or cannot be requested. Before treating BRHS as a feasible validation cohort, obtain documentation establishing:

1. Whether individual-level genome-wide genotypes or imputed dosages can be accessed for approved research, in the UKDS deposit or separately, and whether participant-level linkage is permitted.
2. Whether our exact 15 and 22 variants, counted alleles, build, missingness and imputation quality are available; assess alternate alleles, duplicates and ambiguous variants.
3. Counts at the person-level intersection of genotype QC, sleep items, frailty components and covariates, per wave; and whether genotype PCs are available or can be computed.
4. Whether BRHS participants contributed to either discovery GWAS or other sources used for selecting or weighting our markers. Resolve any overlap before describing the analysis as independent.
5. The eligibility, training, legal agreement, any additional costs and whether the team's actual access location and institution satisfy the dataset-specific conditions.

## Analysis plan if those gates are met

Freeze the two existing scores, including each score's allele orientation and weights, before inspecting associations. Use the wave with the largest verified joint complete sample and prespecify a later-wave analysis if sufficient. Report marker coverage and score calculation; do not silently substitute a proxy, change the weights or equate the differently coded 15- and 22-marker files. Regress physical-frailty status and, where adequately supported, each sleep phenotype on each standardized score with age and ancestry PCs plus relevant technical covariates; report sample sizes, effect estimates and confidence intervals. Use sex-specific interpretation because this cohort contains men. Frailty includes exhaustion, and some sleep items include tiredness, so repeat with a prespecified exhaustion-excluded frailty sensitivity outcome and separately evaluate sleep-initiation/maintenance items. If an FI can be constructed from documented items, document the exact denominator and separately test a sleep- and tiredness-excluded FI; do not call this equivalent to FFS. Treat a small sample and broad confidence intervals as inconclusive rather than proof of no association. These are proposed analyses, not results.
