# Public allele-specific reporter evidence for the insomnia–frailty queue

## Main finding

The public primary CD4 T-cell MPRA of [Ho et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02301-3) contains exact GRCh38 chromosome, position, REF and ALT matches for 88 of the 488 queued variants. Their Supplementary Table 1 classifies one of those, **rs9814873 (chr3:49416679:A>G)**, as an expression-modulating variant (`Enhancer_Skew`). The reported log2 allelic skew is +1.5574 and the reported -log10 FDR is 5.9376 (FDR approximately 1.15 × 10⁻⁶). This is strong evidence of allele-dependent activity in that reporter system; the table's A/B-to-REF/ALT direction has not yet been independently confirmed from the primary analysis, so do not assign the skew's sign to a particular allele.

In the project data, this variant is in a skeletal-muscle **INKA1** eQTL credible set (molecular PIP 0.00496), with Mak FI p = 3.03 × 10⁻¹⁰ and Atkins FI p = 2.21 × 10⁻⁸. Jansen insomnia p = 0.110; FinnGen insomnia p = 0.0064, an uncorrected, postselected result. These associations use distinct phenotype scales and possible overlapping cohorts. The PIP is for the molecular signal, not a disease causal probability. The MPRA does not establish that this element regulates INKA1, that it acts in muscle, or that it causes or mediates insomnia or frailty. INKA1 is identified from the Ensembl identifier using [NCBI Gene](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/389119/).

For this allele the live AlphaGenome scores include CD4 ATAC -0.0130, naïve CD4 ATAC -0.0122, CD4 DNase -0.00240, naïve CD4 DNase +0.00220, and two naïve CD4 CAGE rows +0.05999 and +0.01403. The two CAGE rows have the same visible track name but opposite strands. The API calls their biosample naïve CD4, whereas the matching published source-file label identifies memory conventional CD4 cells; the live/paper track-version equivalence remains to be confirmed. See `stage5_alphagenome_track_independence_audit.md`. These are ALT-minus-REF model outputs on their own assay scales. An activated-primary-cell reporter construct is not the same measurement as native chromatin accessibility or CAGE in the model's annotated cells, and its allele direction is not yet verified; the observed differences should not be labeled concordant or discordant. See `stage5_ho2025_mpra_model_comparison.csv` for the matching tracks and other variants.

## Broader search and limitations

The UCSC [MPRAVarDB hg38 track](https://genome.ucsc.edu/cgi-bin/hgTrackUi?db=hg38&g=mpraVarDb), queried across all 488 reference-checked allele positions, contains 113 rows with exact REF/ALT labels for 89 variants: 88 Jurkat measurements from the Mouri et al. 2022 study, 24 from Griesemer et al. across six cell lines, and one mouse N2A neuroblastoma result from Mulvey and Dougherty. The 24 six-cell-line rows cover four variants, and are not 24 independent variants. The N2A match, chr5:88416702:C>A, has reported p = 0.3645 and FDR = 0.4470; the track lacks its log2FC. No track record is not evidence the variant was tested and had no effect. The track is a curated subset of all public assays, and source allele orientation and liftOver must be verified before directional use.

Ho et al.'s primary T-cell and reanalyzed Jurkat tables each match the same 88 queue alleles. Of these, the authors classify one primary-cell result and three Jurkat results as `Enhancer_Skew` under their stated criteria; this is not the same as using a simple nominal-p cutoff. The Jurkat source experiments overlap the older MPRAVarDB collection and are not an independent replication of it. The primary-cell assay is a separately measured experiment, but **independence from every component of AlphaGenome's training and benchmarking has not been demonstrated**; the model's genomic training folds and related input data may overlap. Reporter assays test an engineered construct rather than endogenous variant editing. Nonsignificant or inactive calls do not prove absence of effect in other contexts.

The strongest rs323509, FOXP2 insertion, and SMAD5 marker do not have an exact allele match in either of these two queried reporter sources. This finding is limited to the searched datasets. A targeted endogenous perturbation or allele-specific experiment in a relevant cell type, plus stronger colocalization/independent phenotype evidence, remains necessary to substantiate a shared disease mechanism.

## Files and reproducibility

- `stage5_reporter_validation_coverage.csv`: all 488 inputs and whether either source had exact allele labels.
- `stage5_mpravardb_exact_allele_records.csv`: 113 matched database rows; `stage5_mpravardb_query_responses.zip` and `stage5_mpravardb_query_receipt.json` preserve 46 window queries, source responses and hashes. UCSC responses containing nonstandard lowercase `nan` were normalized to null during parsing, with original bytes retained.
- `stage5_ho2025_mpra_exact_alleles.csv`: 176 source rows (88 each in primary T-cell and Jurkat tables) with workbook sheet and row references. The workbook is openly downloadable from the paper and is identified and hashed in `stage5_ho2025_mpra_source_receipt.json`; individual-level data availability has separate restrictions.
- `stage5_ho2025_mpra_model_comparison.csv`: same-variant candidate track-level predictions, source assay results and disease p-values. Rows are not independent tests.

From the project root, run `python3 outputs/stage5_query_mpravardb.py`; run `stage5_extract_ho_primary_tcell_mpra.py --workbook PATH` using a Python environment with openpyxl; then run `python3 outputs/stage5_compare_ho_mpra_alphagenome.py --store PATH_TO_LIVE_RESULTS`. These are selected, post hoc findings; no calibrated model-performance test or independent cohort replication has been established. Source-specific multiple-testing criteria, differing cell states, inherited GWAS allele/build assumptions, and model-data overlap remain material limitations.

## Additional neuronal-cell-line evidence

A second public dataset, [Siraj et al. (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10121-6), provides SK-N-SH reporter measurements. The project matched 52 variants (66 library rows) among 433 reference-checked GRCh37 mappings; 55 variants remain unassessed in this cross-build screen. Six variants pass an exploratory reconstruction of the activity and active-set FDR criteria; five are also identified by the authors' selected-library analysis. The SMAD5 alternative is not in that selected subset. One is the SMAD5-region alternative chr5:136207208:G>A, with strong allelic skew in the SK-N-SH reporter. The original rs323509 marker was assayed but did not pass that screen. See `stage5_siraj2026_neural_reporter_review.md` and `stage5_siraj2026_neural_mpra_model_comparison.csv` for source rows, model scores and limitations. These are engineered reporter measurements in a neuroblastoma line, not proof of a causal target or normal-brain effect. The 488-variant coverage table now includes this source.

A newer neuron-derived MPRA resource, IGVFDS1419ZPHD, was also inspected. The portal metadata marked two relevant variant-effect files as revoked and denied access to two others; no result from those files was used. This does not establish that the variants were absent or negative.

## Selected reporter results across cell lines

The author's selected-library processed results were subsequently examined across SK-N-SH, K562, HepG2, HCT116, A549 and GM12878. They cover 42 distinct reference-mapped project variants, and five meet the authors' activity-and-skew criteria in at least one line; the same variant can recur across lines and data sources. Matched-label AlphaGenome DNase scores are linked in `stage5_siraj2026_allcell_reporter_model_comparison.csv`; these are different assay readouts and their signs are not yet interpreted as concordance. See `stage5_siraj2026_allcell_reporter_review.md` and `stage5_siraj2026_allcell_coverage.csv` for the full counts and exclusions. Cell-line reporter data are distinct experimental measurements, but fully independent model evaluation has not been established.

For five selected SK-N-SH candidates, a check of author-labeled counts supports the sign of the reported reporter skew; four share the sign of a matched-label AlphaGenome DNase score and one differs. These are different assays and a postselected set, so this is not a model-accuracy estimate. See `stage5_siraj2026_reporter_direction_review.md`.
