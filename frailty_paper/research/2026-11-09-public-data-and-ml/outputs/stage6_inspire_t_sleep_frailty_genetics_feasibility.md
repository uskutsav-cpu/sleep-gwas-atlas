# INSPIRE-T Toulouse: sleep–frailty cohort; score genotypes unverified

Checked 29 October 2026. INSPIRE-T is a longitudinal cohort in Toulouse, France, spanning adulthood into old age; its [cohort description](https://ihuhealthage.fr/en/inspire-t-human-cohort/) reports repeated sleep questionnaires and Fried physical-frailty assessments. The custodian's [2025 data-access application, Appendix 2](https://ihuhealthage.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/INSPIRE-T-Data-Access-Request-Form_V3.0_160525clean.pdf), lists sleep and Fried phenotype at baseline and annual visits, with collection intended for all participants unless otherwise indicated. These are catalog descriptions, not a count of complete paired responses or proof of a clinical insomnia diagnosis. Exact sleep questions, frailty cutoffs, visit completeness, ages and event counts need verification.

The application lists baseline APOE genotyping for n = 946, as well as 595 baseline accelerometry records whose processing was ongoing when that form was prepared; those are separate counts and do not establish overlap or current availability. APOE-only genotyping does **not** establish coverage of our 33 distinct candidate markers. The [original cohort protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7352084/) describes collecting baseline samples for genotyping; stored biosamples are not variant calls. No usable genome-wide genotype or sequence release, marker coverage, mapped individual-level genotypes, or no-cost genotyping route was found in this bounded search. The 2025 form mentions genetic analyses of requested biological samples, which is not evidence that participant-wide genotypes already exist or would be free.

According to the [cohort's published data-availability statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12448789/), de-identified individual data require a research proposal approved by the data-access committee and a signed data-use agreement. The access form asks for funding information; no clear project-specific fee or waiver was established. Neither this application nor the linked material was submitted or accessed by the project. With about a thousand participants across all ages, relevant older participants, frailty cases, score-marker quality and linked-sample power would need careful assessment. The cohort is a **phenotype-rich controlled-access lead**, not a verified free linked genetic validation dataset.

If the custodian later documents existing genome-wide genotypes covering both scores and a no-cost access route, establish sleep and FI/FFS definitions, consent and permitted linkage, complete-case sample size, genetic ancestry, relatedness, sample or relative overlap with discovery studies, and allowed computing before drawing conclusions. Until then, [Framingham's published no-data-service-fee route](framingham_no_fee_validation_route.md) and [NHATS](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md) remain more concrete access leads. No individual score has been calculated.

## Published sleep measure and missingness

The [baseline cohort paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12448789/) identifies the self-reported sleep measure used in its published baseline table as **sleep duration**, not a diagnosed insomnia phenotype. In the 1,109-person baseline cohort, the table reports 13 missing sleep-duration values and 387 short (<7 hours), 630 intermediate (≥7 to <9 hours), and 79 long (≥9 hours), for 1,096 nonmissing values. These are whole-cohort aggregate counts across ages 20 and older, not counts with complete Fried components or genotypes; they cannot establish the project's relevant analysis sample. The paper also describes PROMIS fatigue and Fried physical frailty, which raises potential overlap of fatigue/exhaustion constructs. Its measures and age distribution must be compared with our discovery phenotypes before using the cohort.

The presence of a reported duration item makes sleep-related analysis more concrete than the generic application label but does not prove that insomnia onset/maintenance items exist. Search of the inspected baseline paper did not identify a validated insomnia instrument; a complete instrument inventory has not been established. Current sleep–Fried–genotype intersection, access fee and availability of genome-wide variants remain unknown, so this evidence does not change the cohort's validation status.
