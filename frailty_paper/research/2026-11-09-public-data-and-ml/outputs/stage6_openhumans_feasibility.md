# Open Humans: public genetic and wearable data feasibility

The [Open Humans public-data API](https://www.openhumans.org/public-data-api/) exposes metadata for data voluntarily shared by members. Its [public-data guidelines](https://www.openhumans.org/community-guidelines/) ask researchers not to attempt re-identification, to cite sources, and to recognize that participants can change or withdraw sharing. This check used only aggregate metadata and did not download personal data, inspect participant profiles, or attempt to identify anyone.

A preliminary pagination scan on October 28, 2026 observed **38 distinct file owners** with at least one file labeled as one of four genetic-source projects and at least one file labeled as one of four wearable-source projects. These labels do not show that any person has a usable genotype file covering the score variants, a valid insomnia assessment, a frailty outcome, or appropriate demographics. They also do not establish that the owner sets are independent of the PGP or other cohorts. Wearable source labels do not establish that a sleep measurement is present.

**The API scan is incomplete and must not be treated as a census.** The API reported 15,670 records consistently, but the 16 requested pages produced only 12,427 unique record IDs and 3,243 repeated IDs. The documented `source_project_id` filter returned mixed project sources when tested. No cause was established for the pagination duplication; changes to the live list or unstable ordering are possibilities, not verified explanations. Thus the 38 observed owners are not a reliable estimate of the full eligible sample, and missing people cannot be treated as nonparticipants or controls. `stage6_openhumans_inventory.json` gives the selected-source counts and quality diagnostics; `stage6_openhumans_inventory.py` reproduces this metadata-only attempt.

A later check used the eight individual project file endpoints rather than the unreliable general listing. At the time of retrieval, each returned the same number of unique file IDs as its reported count, with no duplicates or incorrect project labels. Within the three genetic-upload projects (23andMe, AncestryDNA and genome/exome uploads), **32 distinct owners** also had a file from at least one of the four wearable projects checked. One of those 32 had a wearable-file name, tag or description containing the word “sleep”. This is a keyword scan of metadata, not file contents: other files may contain sleep data without mentioning it. Separately, 17 owners had a PGP-source file and a wearable-source file; PGP-source files can contain traits as well as genomic data, and these people may overlap the 32. Public sharing can change, and even these cleaner endpoint counts do not establish usable genotype, insomnia or frailty outcomes. The checks cover only the specified projects and one retrieval period, not all Open Humans data. [Project-specific counts and diagnostics](stage6_openhumans_project_inventory.json).

A further content-feasibility check examined the 11 publicly shared PGP-source JSON files whose metadata suggested health or trait information among the 32 owners above. All 11 parsed successfully, representing nine distinct owners. An exact-term search in these JSON contents found none of the selected terms for insomnia, frailty, sleep, fatigue, walking, grip, weight loss or physical activity. This is **not** a clinical assessment: synonyms, numerical codes, linked external documents, differently structured data or other projects may contain relevant information. It does not establish that participants lack those conditions or measurements. The script retained only aggregate counts, with no individual records or identifiers. [Structured-file feasibility counts](stage6_openhumans_structured_feasibility.json).

The associated source data remain a possible lead for examining measured sleep, but the present scan finds no validated frailty data or usable joint individual-level outcome dataset. A follow-up would require reliable pagination or an official export, checking current public-sharing status, inspecting only necessary data with the platform's guidelines, defining relevant outcomes and genotype quality, and verifying sample size and discovery overlap. The earlier [Harvard PGP snapshot check](stage6_pgp_validation_feasibility.md) found too little established insomnia and genotype coverage for a reliable score evaluation and did not establish FI or Fried frailty outcomes.

For other leads, [ELSA](https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/genetic-data-access) explicitly requires an application to link its genetic and phenotype data. The separate [openSNP service](https://opensnp.github.io/) reports that it shut down and deleted its hosted genetic and phenotype data; this does not establish whether any independently shared Open Humans files remain available or should be combined with other sources. Neither observation supplies an immediately executable freely public validation cohort.

No score accuracy, calibration or discrimination was computed. The independent validation objective remains open until an appropriate participant-level outcome and genotype dataset is accessible or a genuinely equivalent validated evaluation design is established.
