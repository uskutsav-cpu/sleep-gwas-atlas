# Public sleep–disease genetic evidence: 2026 PheWAS

Checked 29 October 2026. [Hernandez and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70411) report a phenome-wide Mendelian randomization study of seven sleep or circadian traits and 702 health-related phenotypes. The paper reports that genetic instruments for insomnia symptoms were associated with coronary artery disease, major depression and osteoarticular disease. These findings are relevant to the mentor's request to connect sleep to disease phenotypes and possible pathways affecting frailty. They are **published MR findings, not evidence that our 15- or 22-variant scores predict any phenotype**, and MR associations depend on instrumental-variable assumptions.

The publication identifies its insomnia exposure as the [Lane UK Biobank GWAS summary statistics, GCST007387](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST007001-GCST008000/GCST007387/), and gives OpenGWAS IDs for outcome datasets. Several outcome discovery IDs are explicitly UK Biobank (`ukb-*`), and its follow-up list includes FinnGen-coded outcomes. UK Biobank contributes to project discovery data, so the exposure and those outcomes must not be described as independent of this project without a dataset-specific overlap audit. The source describes its own discovery and follow-up procedures; this does not establish independence from our scores. The accessible article text does not identify a frailty outcome, and osteoarticular disease is not frailty.

Useful next step if this direction is prioritized: choose one clinically interpretable disease outcome in advance, check its exact definition, ancestry, sample membership, and complete signed summary statistics, then explicitly test each fixed selected marker after allele/build harmonization and a documented multiple-testing plan. Even a clean locus-level follow-up would not measure a composite score's discrimination, calibration or incremental clinical value. Avoid repeating the published MR as though it were a new project result, and avoid inferring causation or assigning a causal gene from proximity.
