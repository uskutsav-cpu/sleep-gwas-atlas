# Manchester longitudinal aging cohort: sleep and genotype lead

The [University of Manchester Longitudinal Study of Cognition in Normal Healthy Old Age](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41606-025-00135-w) (UMLCHA) is a secondary sleep-genetics lead, **not an established free public score-validation dataset**. A published analysis describes repeated self-reported sleep questions including difficulty falling asleep, nighttime awakenings and sleep medication. It reports genome-wide array data for 1,558 volunteers and imputation for the study's 13 narcolepsy candidates. Those 1,558 are not a verified complete-case count for the project scores and frailty outcomes. Recruitment and repeat survey counts differ, and attrition and sample selection need attention. Sleep questions are not a clinical insomnia diagnosis.

The article's data-availability statement says no datasets were generated or analyzed, despite describing secondary analysis of participant data; this does not provide an accessible data route. A separate [2025 UMLCHA analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01035-x) explicitly says its Manchester cohort data are not publicly available. Neither statement establishes whether any specially approved project could obtain the relevant genotypes and sleep data, whether there would be a fee, or the current conditions of access. No request or external contact was made.

We have not established that UMLCHA has an operationalized deficit FI or five-component physical-frailty phenotype, nor verified exact candidate alleles, imputation quality, score-marker coverage, sample or relative overlap with discovery data, or usable participant counts. It could at most be a **conditional sleep-outcome** route until those issues are resolved; a sleep-only test would not validate frailty prediction or support the intended FI-versus-FFS comparison. Do not prioritize this above a documented no-fee linked frailty/genotype route on the present evidence.
