# Candidate mapping for NCDS age-50 frailty index

The accompanying [mapping](ncds_age50_frailty_candidate_questionnaire_map.csv) matches 28 of the 34 published deficits to candidate age-50 questionnaire fields by their descriptions. It is a preparation aid, not a verified executable phenotype definition. The remaining deficits require mapping to the original derived measures, clinical-history questions, or cognitive test variables. In particular, check the exact release, question wording, response codes, missing-value codes and derived variables against the study documentation before calculating a score. The published paper describes deficit thresholds and partial scores; raw category codes must not be assumed to equal those scores.

Two additional candidate variables are `n8HlthGn` (self-rated general health) and `n8khlstt` (health compared with a year ago), whose descriptions appear in the [UK Data Archive longitudinal guide](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/app/uploads/userguide-socialparticipationidentity.pdf). The original age-50 guide includes both in its SF-36 derivation. The published FI assigns intermediate values to these items, so treating them as simple binary responses would fail to reproduce its scoring. Check the full original coding and whether these fields are included in the approved linked release before use.

Correction, 29 October 2026: an earlier version misassigned three fatigue labels. The [age-50 guide](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6137/mrdoc/pdf/ncds_2008_user_guide_to_the_dataset.pdf) identifies `n8scq10a` as full of life, `n8scq10e` as energy, and `n8scq10g` as worn out; the CSV now reflects this. No participant-level results were produced from the earlier map.

Sources: [published index and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8327203/); [age-50 dataset user guide](https://sp.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6137/mrdoc/pdf/ncds_2008_user_guide_to_the_dataset.pdf); [CLS data access](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/data-access-training/data-access/accessing-data-directly-from-cls/). This is a potential no-data-fee, controlled-access route; it is not an openly downloadable genotype–phenotype dataset, and approval and secure access requirements apply.

The two published sleep deficits are sleep-onset latency and feeling rested on waking. The index also contains four fatigue deficits. An insomnia association with the original index can therefore reflect direct item overlap. A prespecified sensitivity analysis excluding sleep would have at most 32 deficits; excluding sleep and fatigue leaves at most 28 before participant-level missingness. The latter is an exploratory modified index, not the published 34-item index, and falls below the commonly used 30-item convention. Sleep questions about duration and nighttime waking may be useful additional exposures, but are not these two published sleep deficits.

The candidate map does not establish that genetic and phenotype data are linkable for the exact participants, that the current score variants and alleles are available, or that the cohort is independent of discovery samples. Those checks are required before interpreting a score association as validation. No participant-level analysis has been performed here.

## Additional interview and cognitive mappings

The original [age-50 mainstage instrument](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCDS_8_FINAL_MAINSTAGE_DOCUMENTATION.pdf) makes the remaining information request more specific. The candidate map now includes three cognitive deficits: immediate word recall `n8cflisn`, delayed recall `n8cflisd`, and animal naming `n8cfani`, as named in the [age-50 data guide](https://sp.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6137/mrdoc/pdf/ncds_2008_user_guide_to_the_dataset.pdf). At that stage 22 of 34 published deficit descriptions had candidate fields; see the subsequent update below for six more. These are candidate mappings, not a validated replication of the index. The published study marks the lowest quartile as a deficit for each cognitive test; the article does not supply the exact integer cutoffs or enough information here to reproduce tie handling and which sample defined the quartiles. Obtain the study code or a fully specified rule before calculation; do not infer cutoffs from the genotyped analysis sample.

The fourth cognitive deficit counts **correctly crossed-out** P and W targets. The guide distinguishes the `n8cfrc` total letters scanned (speed) from `n8cfmis` targets missed (errors); neither alone is proven to be the exact correct-target count in the published FI. The guide lists `n8cfcor` (also spelled `n8cfor` in another version), `n8cftot`, `n8cfmis`, `n8cfrc`, `n8cfrow` and `n8cfcol` among the letter-cancellation fields. Obtain the variable dictionary and original construction before choosing one. In 151 cases the dataset uses `-6` for missing/unscorable letter-test forms; 57 delayed-recall scores were coded `-6` after a test-administration problem. Those are missing, not poor performance. [Data guide](https://sp.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6137/mrdoc/pdf/ncds_2008_user_guide_to_the_dataset.pdf).

The mainstage instrument's `KHLPRB` question is a multiple-response list covering asthma, diabetes, recurrent back problems, hearing, eyesight, high blood pressure, stomach problems, kidney/bladder problems, and persistent cough. Several deficits require follow-up definitions: the published FI's stomach and sensory categories are narrower than the initial checklist; its diabetes variable specifies type 2. The `MHPROBS` and repeating `MHSTILL` questions concern care for low mood or anxiety and whether each remains symptomatic. The instrument's question names do not by themselves establish exact delivered dataset variable names, repeated-response reshaping, missingness or subtype rules. Request the full corresponding age-50 fields and original FI code rather than scoring a checklist response without those details. [Instrument, health module](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCDS_8_FINAL_MAINSTAGE_DOCUMENTATION.pdf); [published FI Table 1](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/190/8/1550/6140871).

## Dictionary update: 28 candidate fields

The [genetic-sharing dictionary](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/NCDS_Longitudinal_Data_Dictionary.xlsx) identifies `n8cfcor` as the number of P and W targets correctly marked, which matches the fourth cognitive test description. It also identifies candidate `n8khpb01`, `n8khpb05`, `n8khpb09`, `n8khpb15`, and `n8khpb16` for asthma/wheezy bronchitis, back problems, high blood pressure, kidney/bladder problems and persistent cough/phlegm. The health-checklist responses are 0 = not mentioned and 1 = mentioned, with negative codes for nonresponse and applicability; not-mentioned should not be imputed for missing or unasked cases. These six additions bring the candidate map to 28 of 34, not a validated reconstruction. Verify the cognitive quartile threshold, tie handling, disease definitions, nonresponse, and actual authorized release.

For the remaining six, diabetes is listed as sugar diabetes in the checklist but the published index specifies type 2; stomach and sensory deficits have narrower subtype definitions than the initial checklist; and low mood and anxiety depend on both care-seeking and continued symptoms (`n8mhprb1`/`n8mhstl1`, `n8mhprb2`/`n8mhstl2`). These stems are leads, not final coded deficits. The published paper and scoring code, rather than label similarity alone, must resolve all six. [Published FI](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/190/8/1550/6140871); [age-50 main interview](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCDS_8_FINAL_MAINSTAGE_DOCUMENTATION.pdf).

The [remaining-six field audit](ncds_age50_unresolved_deficits_field_audit.md) identifies candidate subtype and follow-up fields for each unresolved deficit and records ambiguities in disease definitions and question routing. They remain groups of candidate inputs, not fully verified scoring rules.
