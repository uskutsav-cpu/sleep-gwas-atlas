# COFITAGE: open-data validation lead

The 2026 [COFITAGE dataset preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746635) describes 101 community-dwelling adults aged 50–69 with sleep and neuropsychological measures, brain imaging and genotyping, and states that its BIDS-formatted data are released openly through EBRAINS. A [University of Liège record](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/348160) describes genome-wide analysis. The preprint is not peer reviewed as of this assessment. Its abstract describes the dataset at a high level; it does not establish which individual genetic files or score variants are included in the public release, exact sleep measurements or usable FI/FFS outcomes.

The abstract did not supply a dataset-specific EBRAINS link, and targeted web and DataCite searches did not identify a matching dataset record. The paper's PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403 in this environment. Thus open access is **author-described but file-level access, permissions, formats, identity linkage, score-variant coverage, frailty outcomes and sample/relative independence remain unverified**; no participant data were downloaded. The preprint webpage specifies CC BY-NC 4.0 for the preprint; the actual dataset license and access terms require separate verification.

At a maximum baseline sample of 101, with an apparently healthy sample and potentially fewer complete participants or outcome events, this is unlikely to yield a reliable assessment of calibration, discrimination or incremental performance. A separate [COFITAGE sleep paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10619502/) describes exclusion of sleep disorders, which may substantially limit insomnia applicability; whether the released sample and protocol are identical needs verification. This is an interesting open multimodal resource to investigate, not a demonstrated independent genetic-score validation cohort. No conclusion about its complete contents follows from the limited search.

## Full-text follow-up

The university-hosted [full preprint](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/348160/1/2026.08.24.746635v1.full.pdf) was accessible and gives a more precise account. The authors say genome-wide SNPs were assayed using an OmniExpress array on GRCh37 and imputed using HRC. Their described curated genetic variables, however, are **rs429358 and rs7412**, APOE genotype and derived carrier/risk categories; Table 1 lists APOE genotyping for 84 participants. Neither named SNP appears by rsID in the project's 15- or 22-marker lists. This does not prove the raw genome-wide data are absent from the release, but the manuscript does not establish that the 33 distinct project variants or whole-genome individual files are publicly available. The data-location field in this preprint is a placeholder, `https://....`, rather than a usable repository URL. The study reports polysomnography and actimetry and screening exclusions including apnea and parasomnia; the manuscript does not identify an FI or FFS.

This narrows the lead: the documented APOE-derived variables alone cannot calculate either project score. No genome-wide participant file, outcome table or dataset license was inspected and no validation was performed. The exact rsID comparison used the two existing score CSVs. The preprint's file-level availability and final dataset publication can change.
