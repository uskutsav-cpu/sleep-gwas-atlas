# Sleep genetics, grip strength and disease: 2026 phenome-wide MR review

Checked 29 October 2026. [Hernandez et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70411) screened genetic instruments for seven self-reported sleep or chronobiology phenotypes against 702 health-related outcomes. The openly hosted [accepted manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10230416/1/Hernandez%20MH%20-%20PWMR%20%26%20sleep-chronobiology.pdf), pp. 7–8 and 12–14, defines insomnia symptoms using trouble falling asleep or waking in the night and reports retained associations with cardiovascular, psychiatric and musculoskeletal outcomes. Its Figure 3 shows negative associations with handgrip strength in both paper-labelled discovery and validation analyses. Grip is a component of some physical frailty definitions, but it is not itself FI or a Fried frailty score. The plotted values are Wald Z-scores in standard-error units, not effect estimates in kilograms, and cannot be used to quantify a clinical grip difference.

This is a potentially useful background example for the mentor's sleep–disease connection, with three important limits. First, the authors used a genome-wide insomnia instrument, not either project's 15- or 22-marker score; findings do not validate these scores or identify a shared causal variant or gene. Second, Mendelian randomization depends on relevance, independence and exclusion restrictions; sensitivity analyses and significance cannot prove that those assumptions hold or establish a treatment effect. Third, the authors list the [Lane UK Biobank insomnia GWAS](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST007387) as their insomnia exposure, while their insomnia outcome identifiers include `ukb-b-10215` and `ukb-b-7478`. The [OpenGWAS grip metadata](https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/datasets/ukb-b-7478/) identify these as right and left hand UK Biobank-derived analyses, respectively. This raises a material participant-overlap concern for the handgrip example, both with the exposure and between the two grip analyses if these identifiers correspond to the figure's two labels. The inaccessible supplementary table is needed to verify the mapping and sample intersection; two labels alone do not establish independent cohorts. Both insomnia and frailty source datasets in the current project also use UK Biobank, so overlap with this paper cannot be assumed absent.

The paper reports analyses in European populations; portability to other ancestries is untested here. Its authors state that full exposure GWAS and outcome-source identifiers are available, but GWAS summary data alone cannot establish individual-level discrimination, calibration, or incremental predictive value. The publisher's instrument supplement is listed but direct download returned HTTP 403 during this check; no instrument-by-instrument comparison was performed. Do not infer that unlisted variants were absent or failed. No new effect sizes were computed.

For a future follow-up, request or obtain the study's detailed insomnia-to-grip results and exact dataset pairing, then quantify participant overlap. Prioritize an independently collected grip or frailty cohort with linked genotype and phenotype data for actual score evaluation. The separately documented [Japanese knee-strength screen](stage6_japanese_knee_candidate_review.md) is a different physical phenotype and remains an exploratory variant comparison, not a substitute for the required validation.
