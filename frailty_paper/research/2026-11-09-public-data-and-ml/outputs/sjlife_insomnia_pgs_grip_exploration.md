# Exploratory SJLIFE insomnia score and grip strength

On 29 October 2026, the free interactive [St. Jude Survivorship Portal](https://viz.stjude.cloud/cancer-survivorship/visualization/st-jude-survivorship-portal~18) returned a linear model in the SJLIFE cohort (portal's December 2018 data freeze). The outcome was `Grip Strength: Right 1 (kg)`. The predictor was the existing `PGS001932 insomnia (Derived from European ancestry; Computed for European ancestry)`, modeled continuously at the portal's original scale. Covariates were sex (male reference), age in years at baseline assessment, and EUR SJLIFE PC1, all with default continuous/categorical settings as appropriate. The portal reported **3,203 complete cases**.

| Portal term | Beta | 95% CI | P |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PGS001932, per one unscaled score unit | -2.133 kg | -6.341 to 2.074 | 0.3202 |
| Female versus male | -17.42 kg | -18.12 to -16.72 | Displayed as 0 |
| Baseline assessment age, per year | 0.316 kg | 0.2802 to 0.3518 | 2.872e-64 |
| EUR SJLIFE PC1, per unit | -20.71 kg | -42.01 to 0.5857 | 0.05664 |

The insomnia-score interval includes both directions. Its coefficient is **per one unit of the raw score**, not per standard deviation; the portal display did not supply the complete-case score SD, so a per-SD conversion is not supported. `0` is the portal's display for extremely small p-values, not an exact p-value. Total model R² was 0.4622, driven by all included terms, and is not the incremental prediction from the insomnia score.

This is an exploratory, post-selection analysis, not validation of the project's 15- or 22-marker scores. Grip strength is one frailty-related measure rather than FI or FFS, and the sample consists of childhood-cancer survivors with a wide age range rather than a representative older-adult cohort. Only **one** genetic principal component was included, and whether grip was measured at the same visit as the baseline-age variable was not established. Selection, relatedness, potential discovery overlap, score calculation and residual confounding remain unverified. The striking positive age coefficient may reflect the cohort's developmental age range, timing or other factors; it should not be generalized as an aging effect. The result does not establish or exclude a meaningful biological association or clinical utility.

The external [PGS Catalog entry](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS001932/) reports 37,712 variants and UK Biobank European-ancestry training. Portal displayed 4,557 nonmissing grip measures and 3,554 nonmissing score values in the SJLIFE cohort before complete-case intersection; separate marginal counts must not be interpreted as matched sample sizes. The model output is transcribed from the portal's public aggregate interface; no individual data were downloaded. Further work should verify visit timing and eligibility, use an adequate set of ancestry PCs and relatedness handling, prespecify outcome and score scaling, and test the actual project scores in a separate authorized dataset.

## Two-PC sensitivity

In a subsequent post hoc portal fit, I added `EUR SJLIFE PC2` as a continuous covariate to the same model. The portal again reported 3,203 complete cases and the PGS001932 coefficient was **−2.135 kg per raw score unit** (95% CI −6.343 to 2.073; p=0.3199). The point estimate is essentially unchanged compared with the one-PC model, but two PCs do not guarantee adequate control of population structure. This sensitivity was prompted by inspection of the first model and is not independent replication. Grip visit timing versus baseline age remains unverified.

**Timing clarification:** the portal's [release documentation](https://github.com/stjude/proteinpaint/wiki/St.-Jude-Survivorship-Portal-Data-Releases) says its version 1 phenotypes are baseline data except longitudinal chronic health conditions; version 2 retains the December 2018 SJLIFE freeze. The separately published [SJLIFE clinical data dictionary](https://sjlife.stjude.org/content/dam/sjlife/en-us/documents/dd-clinical-20200430.pdf) identifies the right first grip trial as `GRIP_RT1` in functional assessment data, alongside assessment-date and visit-type fields. Together these support interpreting the portal's grip as a baseline measure, but do not independently verify exact matching of each participant's grip date to the portal's age variable or identify release-specific transformation rules. Individual visit-level verification remains needed. The dictionary describes a later 2020 freeze and should not be used to infer this portal's individual counts.

## Adult baseline-age sensitivity

Because the portal's baseline-age distribution includes minors, a further post hoc fit restricted baseline assessment age to **18 years or older**, retaining sex, continuous baseline age and both continuous PCs. The portal reported 2,827 complete cases: the score coefficient was **−2.667 kg per raw score unit** (95% CI −6.700 to 1.367; p=0.195). The age coefficient was 0.04978 kg/year (95% CI 0.01083 to 0.08872), compared with 0.3161 in the all-age two-PC fit. This illustrates that the age relationship changes with the analyzed age range; the two age coefficients also come from different samples and do not isolate the reason for the difference. The score confidence interval again spans zero. This is a post hoc sensitivity within the same cohort, not replication; two PCs, uncertain correspondence of grip and baseline assessment, selection and unknown relatedness remain limitations. Age at baseline is not necessarily age at the grip test.
