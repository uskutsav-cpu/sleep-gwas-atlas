# Circadian clock expression, insomnia and frailty: 2026 study context

[Ortiz-Vázquez et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13662892/) is relevant to the mentor's proposed sleep–circadian–frailty bridge. In a cross-sectional study of 751 community-dwelling Mexican older adults, the authors assessed chronotype, insomnia and frailty. Extreme morning preference was associated with lower reported insomnia than intermediate preference in their adjusted model (OR 0.393, 95% CI 0.169–0.827); very few participants were evening types, so this is not a well-powered morning-versus-evening comparison.

The authors measured eight clock genes by RT-qPCR in peripheral blood mononuclear cells from a small subset; the article reports 82 participants for its PCA. Frailty groups differed in a principal component summarizing morning gene expression; after adjustment, the authors report a lower CLOCK expression association for prefrailty. The supplementary caption lists group counts 8, 39 and 36 (83 in total), inconsistent with the 82-person description, so exact denominators should be checked before reuse. Expression at a single morning time point is not a measure of circadian phase, amplitude, rhythmicity or cell-type-specific expression. Cross-sectional association does not identify direction or causal mechanism.

This is **biological context, not genetic-score validation**: gene expression is not genotype, PBMCs are a mixture of immune cells, and the article does not establish that our 33 candidate variants were measured or linked to these outcomes. The authors say participant data are not publicly available and are available on request; no request was sent, and cost and conditions are unknown.

A defensible next hypothesis is to ask whether independently prioritized sleep–frailty loci colocalize with regulatory or expression effects in relevant immune and neural cell populations, then test direction, cell composition, age, and time-of-day sensitivity in independent datasets. Neither proximity to CLOCK nor a model prediction would by itself establish that a variant affects this expression pattern or causes frailty. A suitable repeated-sampling or timed-expression dataset would be required to test an actual rhythm hypothesis.
