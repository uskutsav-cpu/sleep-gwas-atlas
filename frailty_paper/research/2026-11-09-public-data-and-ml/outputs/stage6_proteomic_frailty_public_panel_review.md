# Public proteomic frailty panel: evidence and feasibility

The open-access [frailty proteomics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12419846/) supplies a published, 25-protein plasma score trained with elastic net in LonGenity. It reports correlation with concurrently measured frailty index in ARIC (n=5,195, r=0.61) and BLSA (n=654, r=0.45), as well as associations with physical frailty categories. Those are external evaluations of **that protein score**, not of the project's insomnia–frailty genetic scores. The associations are not evidence that proteins cause frailty or that sleep and frailty share genetic risk. The development sample had 440 training and 440 validation participants, and selection of 143 eligible proteins preceded fitting the 25-protein model; check the original study when interpreting selection and performance.

The paper provides a downloadable supplementary table with the 25 individual-protein–FI associations in ARIC and BLSA. The underlying LonGenity, ARIC and BLSA participant data are request-based according to its data statement; public paper tables do not supply linked genotypes, insomnia, FI and FFS for the same people. Thus the published material is freely usable for background and future molecular target triage, but is not an executable independent test of either project genetic score. The apparent absence of sleep from the main article's text is not proof that sleep was absent from every cohort's FI; review cohort-specific supplementary deficit lists before using a sleep-free interpretation.

## Exploratory cross-reference

As a limited name-level check, we manually translated the Table 2 assay labels to 26 possible gene symbols (GDF-11/8 counted as both GDF11 and GDF8) and intersected them with the 98 gene-symbol values in `stage5_variant_gene_tissue_phenotype_integrated.csv`. No exact symbols matched. This is **not** a gene-set enrichment test, evidence of no biological link, or a direct protein–variant analysis: the project's inventory is targeted and incomplete, circulating proteins need not share a name with a regulatory target, and manual assay-to-gene mappings are provisional. In particular a protein assay can recognize multiple molecular targets, so future use needs authoritative assay mapping and specificity checks. Without a justified molecular bridge and pQTL signal-sharing analysis, this panel should not be added as a validated variant-to-gene result.

A future properly designed integration would obtain signed cis-pQTL summary statistics for each well-mapped panel protein, identify variant coverage and cohort overlap, and test locus-specific colocalization against independent sleep and FI/FFS GWAS using consistent alleles and ancestry-matched LD. It would be an additional hypothesis-generating biological analysis, not participant-level validation of the proposed score. The project's existing external frailty validation remains outstanding.

## FI item audit from the public supplement

We retrieved the supplementary archive from the [Europe PMC supplementary-files endpoint](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12419846/supplementaryFiles) and examined Tables S4–S6. None explicitly names sleep or insomnia among the listed LonGenity, ARIC or BLSA FI items. All three do list “everything is an effort” and difficulty getting going, which can overlap conceptually with fatigue, plus depression and physical function items. Thus these are useful contextual measures for the sleep–frailty project, but the absence of a named sleep item does not prove the absence of all symptom or measurement overlap. The protein panel was derived from an FI containing those items, and the published FI correlations are concurrent associations rather than prospective validation of genetic scores. ## Published individual-protein association check

We read the 25-protein association table (supplementary Table 8) and compared the reported directions across LonGenity, ARIC and BLSA. Twenty-four of 25 reported protein–FI slopes had the same sign in all three; GLRX3 was the exception and had imprecise external associations (ARIC p=0.70; BLSA p=0.49). Sixteen had the same direction and p<0.002 in both external cohorts, a descriptive Bonferroni threshold for 25 proteins applied separately per cohort. These are post hoc checks on a preselected protein panel, not evidence that the 16 proteins are causal or independent genetic sleep–frailty mechanisms. Cohorts differ in sample size, composition and covariates; their effect scales and assays require source review before direct comparisons. In particular, the GDF-11/8 assay lists two targets. Individual protein slope signs differ in interpretation from coefficients in the multivariable protein-prediction model.

## Check against a public hospital-frailty pQTL analysis

The 25-protein panel's assay labeled WFKN2 has UniProt Q8TEU8 (WFIKKN2). In the published [Mak et al. supplementary tables](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y), the matching gene label WFIKKN2 appears in the full-HFRS pQTL colocalization table, with reported posterior percentages H3=76.4 and H4=23.5 across 7,916 SNPs. Under that paper's single-causal-variant model and priors, this comparison favors distinct over shared signals and does not meet its reported H4>80% criterion. It concerns UK Biobank Olink pQTL and FinnGen hospital frailty, whereas the protein panel was measured on SomaScan; assay identity and cross-platform comparability need checking. WFIKKN2 is absent from their dementia-removed pQTL table, which does not show the association is absent. Neither comparison includes insomnia or validates a genetic score, and a conditional posterior cannot exclude other signals or mechanisms. The paper's analysis uses a ±1 Mb window and default priors; differences in assays, populations, available variants and multiple causal signals limit interpretation.
