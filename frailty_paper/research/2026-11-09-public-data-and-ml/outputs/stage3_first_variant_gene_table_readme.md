# Initial variant-to-gene table: GTEx v10

The [first variant–gene table](stage3_initial_variant_gene_table.csv) covers all 22 prior exploratory candidates plus the earlier candidate rs323509. It contains the requested columns: variant, locus, insomnia/FI/FFS effects, candidate gene, tissue, eQTL, sQTL, colocalization and evidence status. Rows describe a **variant–gene–tissue combination**, so one marker can appear many times. The initial table had 565 gene–tissue rows derived from 795 portal association records; it now has 719 rows after adding 147 candidate–target summaries from LD-proxy regulatory predictions, and two separately labeled OneK1K cell-QTL matches, one published MMRN1 insomnia TWAS gene-model row, two predicted ABC regulatory-target rows, and two explicitly labeled positional fallback rows. The proxy-based target names are model predictions; 110 of 147 candidate–target pairs have a symbol, chromosome and TSS match among tested Ensembl v92 coding genes, while the remainder have unresolved IDs. These predictions do not establish QTL associations or proven causal genes; see [the proxy regulatory review](stage3_abc_ld_proxy_regulatory_review.md). Three markers had no returned GTEx associations and retain their positional-gene labels as placeholders. This remains a provisional inventory; the later region-specific colocalization comparisons and their limitations are linked in the relevant rows and companion reports.

The GWAS effects in this table are aligned to the explicitly listed counted allele and use UK Biobank insomnia, Atkins FI and Ye FFS summary inputs. They are source-specific effect scales, not directly comparable quantities or absolute risks; the FFS effect-allele convention and the assumed build require further verification. The marker set was post-selected and some studies share participants. A coordinate and matching allele pair do not establish causal association.

## Initial molecular observations

- rs12536335 returned FOXP2 eQTL associations in pituitary, subcutaneous adipose and adrenal gland, plus associations with MDFIC in atrial heart tissue and sun-exposed skin. For example, the pituitary FOXP2 row has p = 4.70 × 10⁻⁸. No sQTL was returned by this exact-marker query.
- rs7734045 returned associations involving SMAD5 and SMIM32 in several tissues; in skeletal muscle the portal returned an SMIM32 eQTL (p = 9.92 × 10⁻³³) and multiple SMAD5 sQTL rows, including p = 1.19 × 10⁻⁷⁵ for a specified intron excision event. This does not establish that either gene mediates frailty or insomnia.
- rs323509 returned sQTL rows for `ENSG00000251574.8`; these are reported as the portal's identifier rather than assigned an unverified gene name. Its MVP insomnia result was not nominal (p = 0.05089), and it is included because the user requested coverage, not because it passed the later 22-marker screen.
- rs2838788 returned both ADARB1 eQTL and sQTL associations in multiple tissues. Tissue-specific molecular association is a gene-prioritization lead, not evidence of shared causal variants.

The portal's precomputed significant-QTL endpoint is selective. **No returned association** means none was returned for the queried pair and release; it does not prove that the variant has no molecular effect, was measured in every tissue, or lacks a causal proxy. The p-values above are molecular-association p-values, not probabilities of causality or evidence of colocalization. The smallest splicing p-value is one intron event, not necessarily an independent gene-level test. The table preserves all gene–tissue groups instead of choosing a single gene by smallest p-value. Full variant/event-level output is in [the molecular rows](stage3_gtex_v10_candidate_qtls.csv).

## Source and technical checks

GTEx Portal API dataset **`gtex_v10`**, accessed 27 October 2026, endpoints `singleTissueEqtl` and `singleTissueSqtl`. The [GTEx portal](https://m.gtexportal.org/news) notes that v11 open downloads exist but portal visualizations continue to display v10 pending integration; this analysis explicitly uses v10, not v11. The current [API specification](https://gtexportal.org/api/v2/redoc) describes these as precomputed single-tissue QTL endpoints. Each query used the exact candidate GRCh38 coordinate and queried both possible orders of the two alleles, without interpreting the returned normalized effect as aligned to the GWAS allele. Returned variant IDs, dataset IDs and, where supplied, rsIDs were checked; all 92 queries succeeded and declared pagination totals were reconciled. The assumed GRCh37→GRCh38 lift and original GWAS multiallelic representation are not independently verified by a portal match. Per-query URLs, complete response contents and SHA-256 hashes are in [the query receipts](stage3_gtex_v10_query_receipts.json). Local scripts are `work/stage3_gtex_v10_lookup.py` and `work/stage3_first_variant_gene_table.py`.

The [eQTL Catalogue data-access page](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/eqtl/Data_access/) says its old REST API is discontinued; release 7 and pre-release 8 summary files and fine-mapping files remain available by FTP. The original GTEx lookup did not use the Catalogue; subsequent region-level analyses use Catalogue release 7, as detailed in the linked reports. The page advises rate-limiting remote tabix queries, and explains that ALT is the effect allele and multiallelic variants are split into distinct variant IDs. These considerations must be handled in subsequent Catalogue and colocalization work.

Subsequent exploratory region-level SMIM32 colocalizations were added for [insomnia](stage3_smim32_skeletal_finngen_coloc.md) and [FI and physical frailty](stage3_smim32_skeletal_mak_coloc.md); and [SMAD5 splicing](stage3_smad5_splice_coloc.md); subsequent exploratory comparisons cover all 19 provisional regions, with substantial differences in coverage and reliability; see [the region inventory](stage3_region_evidence_inventory.md). Published TWAS context, molecular credible sets, predicted regulatory links, and exploratory MAGMA and scDRS analyses were added separately. No experimental validation was performed. The source annotations are bulk-tissue results, not single-cell assignments. More complete region-level analyses, distinct from these exact-marker lookups, remain necessary.

Two [naïve CD4 T-cell molecular-QTL credible-set results](stage4_onek1k_cell_qtl_first_results.md) were added after the bulk GTEx lookup. They are not cell-specific GWAS colocalizations or enrichment results.

The [published TWAS comparison](stage3_published_fi_insomnia_twas_review.md) is gene-level context; its MMRN1 result is not an rs7691487-specific association.

The **GWAS gene evidence** column now includes available LD-aware MAGMA gene associations from the standard Ye FFS and Mak FI gene analyses and approximate Brown-1df Mak FI, Atkins FI, Ye FFS and Jansen insomnia analyses. Each q-value is corrected across that model’s tested genes. These are gene-level tests and do not establish that the row’s variant affects the gene or that the gene mediates either phenotype. See [FI cell-type analysis](stage4_mak_fi_brown_magma_cell_type_review.md) and [FFS cell-type analysis](stage4_ye_ffs_magma_cell_type_review.md).

The **Published FI TWAS evidence** column matches source gene symbols against the 36 cross-tissue and 107 whole-blood significant genes in [Lin et al. supplementary tables](stage3_published_fi_twas_supplement_review.md). These are published gene-model associations, not tests of the candidate marker or causality; absence from a significant-only table is not a negative TWAS result.
