# TwinGene allele-frequency direction: new source consistency check

A genome-wide sample from the original FI cohort file shows that the reported TwinGene frequency column is generally closer to the **complement** of the UK Biobank frequency column than to the reported UK Biobank frequency. For 101,233 unique sampled, non-palindromic SNPs with valid frequencies, the mean absolute UKB–TwinGene frequency difference was **0.657** as reported, versus **0.0116** after replacing TwinGene frequency with 1 minus that frequency. The complemented frequency was closer in 100,174 of 101,233 rows (99.0%). For the 89,805 sampled SNPs with UKB frequency ≤0.4 or ≥0.6, where the orientations are easier to distinguish, the complemented value was closer in 89,797 rows.

A separate check of the 22 project candidates used their counted-allele frequencies in the 503-person 1000 Genomes EUR reference. After accounting for whether the source EA matched the candidate allele, the mean absolute difference from the reference was **0.0211** for UKB, **0.4351** for TwinGene under the source EA convention, and **0.0259** for TwinGene if the frequency is complemented. The complemented TwinGene frequency was closer in 21 of 22 candidates. These are approximate cross-population comparisons, not allele-frequency equality tests.

Together with [the separate beta reconstruction check](twingene_direction_genomewide_audit.md), this is strong evidence that the released TwinGene frequency and effect columns may be oriented to the opposite allele from the file's shared EA field, as used by the current analysis. It does **not** establish why, prove that frequency and beta necessarily use the same orientation, authenticate the original GWAS coding, or replace confirmation by the original investigators. UKB, TwinGene and the 1000 Genomes panel differ in ancestry, sample size, imputation and ascertainment. The complement operation assumes a two-allele contrast; multiallelic source representation remains a separate concern. The sampled SNPs are correlated and these descriptive counts are not independent hypothesis tests.

The [source README](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Genome-Wide_Association_Study_of_the_Frailty_Index_-_Atkins_et_al_2021/9204998) labels EA as the effect allele and calls both columns effect-allele frequencies; the empirical pattern conflicts with treating both cohort frequency columns as referring to that same allele. Consequently the previously reported negative correlation of UKB- and TwinGene-weighted 22-variant sums remains **conditional on the inferred adapter**, but is supported by two different internal consistency checks. It still does not measure genetic correlation or clinical prediction.

## Methods and files

The source scan used a deterministic rsID-only 1/64 BLAKE2b hash sample, not variant significance or effect direction. It considered single-base non-palindromic pairs with finite, bounded frequency values, removing sampled IDs with an additional source row after an eligible record. The genome-wide sample is from the cohort archive and is not the identical matched set used for the beta reconstruction. For candidates, exact rsID, assumed GRCh37 position and unordered alleles matched uniquely; frequency is expressed on the candidate counted allele under each hypothesis and compared with the EUR reference frequencies from [the score variance diagnostic](reference_22_score_variance_review.md). A [subgroup sensitivity](22_candidate_frequency_subgroup_followup.md) identifies variants whose frequencies are less informative about orientation. No individual-level data were retained.
