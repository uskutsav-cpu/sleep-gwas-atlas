# MrOS: a documented sleep–physical-frailty validation candidate

Checked 29 October 2026. **Scientifically strong, but controlled access; no completed score validation and no established total free-of-cost route.**

The Osteoporotic Fractures in Men (MrOS) Sleep Study collected subjective and objective sleep measures in older men. A [cross-sectional study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3024909/) assessed sleep and a Cardiovascular Health Study (CHS)/Fried-style frailty phenotype in 3,133 men; 437 were classified frail. A [prospective study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3449012/) examined 2,505 initially non-frail participants, with frailty and vital status followed an average of 3.4 years; associations with the combined follow-up outcome attenuated after adjustment for baseline frailty. Those results are phenotypic, not validation of the project's scores. The prospective paper's non-frail category includes prefrail participants; do not call it restricted to baseline robust men. Neither published count is a genetically linked validation sample.

A separate [sleep genetic study](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/6304/files/journal.pgen.1007739.pdf) reports 2,178 European-ancestry MrOS participants with polysomnography and genotype data. This documents real overlap of some sleep and genotypes, but not the intersection with the frailty analyses, complete genotype calls for our markers, participant independence, or usable follow-up outcomes. A [separate insomnia-genetics analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8807892/) used MrOS and other cohorts and found limited correspondence between subjective-insomnia-derived genetic risk and most objective polysomnographic phenotypes. Such objective measurements should be assessed as distinct endpoints, not automatically equated with the project's insomnia definition.

## Access and phenotype caveats

- [NSRR](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/mros/files) lists MrOS sleep files for access request; its [data-security policy](https://sleepdata.org/about/data-security) says data are provided at no cost to users completing an online request. The relevant subject IDs, phenotype fields, approval conditions, genotype linkage and any computing expenses need checking.
- [dbGaP phs000373](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000373.v1.p1) describes the MrOS parent and sleep studies and controlled individual-level data. Its [study-specific data-use addendum](https://dbgap.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/aa/wga.cgi?stacc=phs000373.v1.p1&view_pdf=) has three different consent groups: group 1 covers aging disorders including sleep and physical-function decline; group 3 explicitly includes sleep and physical-function decline including frailty; group 2 is limited to osteoporosis/fracture, osteoarthritis and obesity. Our sleep–frailty project cannot assume all groups are eligible. The wording does not preapprove this project, authorize cross-repository linkage, prove exact genotype availability or waive institutional requirements.
- The [NIA AgingResearchBiobank FAQ](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/faq/) says data packages are generally no-cost, but lists an institutional IRB approval or waiver, proof of funding, a signed institutional agreement and other request materials. Its general fee statement is not a quote for the exact MrOS linked files; additional genotype or computing costs and cross-repository permissions remain unknown. No request was submitted.
- The [MrOS analyst guide](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/studies/mros/documents/download/Visit_Information.pdf/) uses the name “Frailty Index” for a CHS-like measure and says calculated measures are not released in its described package because they depend on dataset distributions. This is not evidence that a deficit-accumulation FI is available. Verify the five component definitions, thresholding, study release date and whether the original components are available for reconstruction; do not substitute it for the project's FI.

MrOS enrolled men aged 65 and older, and the sleep substudy had eligibility restrictions; transport to women, younger populations and diverse ancestries cannot be assumed. Before any score test, verify study and participant overlap with discovery and all candidate-selection datasets, their relatives, outcome completeness, allele build and imputation quality, and the current eligibility and data use terms. Select held-out outcomes and freeze the score before testing. Survival, loss to follow-up, competing mortality, and selection into the sleep study matter for a longitudinal analysis. Neither the 15- nor 22-marker score has been tested in MrOS here.


## Genotype release and cross-repository linkage detail

The [MrOS dbGaP record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000373.v1.p1) says that its controlled release supplies QC-cleaned PLINK files, whereas raw GWAS data are held by the coordinating center. It reports 5,130 consented subjects overall, not 5,130 eligible participants with the relevant genotype, sleep and frailty measures. [NCBI educational material](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/ncbi/workshops/2023-05_genetic-disorder-mechanisms/supplemental-material/Flyer_Pharmocogenetics_CaseStudy.pdf) describes dbGaP data as no-cost, subject to controlled-access approval. This does not establish the costs of linked phenotype datasets or computing. The record does not establish coverage, imputation, target alleles or quality for our selected variants; scoring must be gated on those checks. Because group 2's permitted uses are narrower than groups 1 and 3, group-level eligibility can further reduce the sample.

A public [dbGaP–NSRR linkage document](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/document.cgi?phd=5259&study_id=phs000287.v6.p1), hosted with a superseded CHS release, describes a proposed subject-ID lookup system across dbGaP and NSRR and mentions MrOS among NSRR studies. It supports the existence of a general linkage workflow, but it is not a current MrOS-specific crosswalk or proof that one is available for our consent groups. Verify the actual current MrOS lookup, its access controls, allowed linkage and unique identifiers in both approved datasets before relying on the path.


## Existing project analysis and visit-order check

The earlier [MrOS access and analysis brief](mros_access_analysis_brief.md) documents that MrOS contributed to the CHARGE gait-speed results already screened during this project. This is a possible overlap with **post-selection** evidence and requires a decision-history audit; it does not on its own prove that these participants influenced marker selection. Avoid calling the cohort fully untouched. The same brief identifies sleep visit 2 insomnia severity and a possible later visit 4 frailty assessment; the published earlier visit 3 predates sleep visit 2, so visit 3 must not be treated as a later outcome for that sleep measure. Whether all later visit 4 components can be accessed and linked is unresolved.

That brief already audits public dbGaP phenotype summaries: consent groups 1 and 3 have reported sleep-efficiency and grip observations, and their marginal counts imply 2,441–2,543 people with both measurements under a shared-universe assumption. This is a mathematical bound, not the actual genotype–insomnia–frailty overlap or score validation. It also records that the release dictionary did not include the exact names for the later insomnia severity variables. Consult the brief for sources, assumptions, and missing-file limitations.


### What the recorded selection history does and does not show

The project [15-marker selection audit](candidate_selection_and_sleep_score_context.md) attributes selection to the published Song cross-trait ranking followed by availability in the FI, insomnia and FFS inputs. The [22-marker guide](shared22_score_research_guide.md) attributes its selection to nominal results in MVP insomnia and FinnGen HFRS. The [CHARGE gait lookup](stage6_charge_gait_full_candidate_review.md) explicitly calls the 22 candidates previously selected and describes itself as post-selection. Thus the recorded selection rationale does **not** identify that gait screen as the original source of either candidate list, and the lookup states a post-selection order for the 22. This is reassuring about initial selection, but the files do not establish the timing of every later change, score weight, sensitivity analysis or outcome choice. Nor do they prove that MrOS participants or relatives are absent from all other sources. Retain the specific prior exposure to gait evidence in any validation report, freeze all score choices before further outcome review, and do not describe MrOS as fully untouched without a complete provenance and overlap audit.
