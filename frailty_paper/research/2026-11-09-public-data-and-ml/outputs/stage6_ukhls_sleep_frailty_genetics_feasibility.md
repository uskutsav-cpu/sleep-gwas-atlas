# Understanding Society: sleep, frailty and genetic-data lead

Checked 29 October 2026. Understanding Society (UKHLS) is a household cohort with genetic data, longitudinal health data and sleep questionnaires. It is **not a verified unrestricted free genetic-validation dataset**: participant-level genetics require controlled access and project-specific eligibility and total costs remain unverified.

## Evidence for the relevant components

* [EGA accession EGAD00010002876](https://www.ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00010002876), listed on 19 October 2026, identifies **9,920 samples** genotyped on the Illumina HumanCoreExome 12v1-0 array. EGA lists PLINK BED/BIM/FAM files under a data-access committee. These are sample counts in that genetic dataset, not people with complete sleep and frailty, and the existence of files does not establish coverage or quality for our candidate variants.
* A [published study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492181/) reports UKHLS genome-wide imputed data and self-reported sleep-duration analyses. An [associated thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10053572/1/Thesis_VG_27.07.18_FINAL.pdf) reports **8,668** UKHLS participants in a sleep-duration GWAS. This is evidence that sleep and genetic data were jointly analyzed in an earlier selected sample. The number is not the number with the 36-item FI, insomnia, our 33 variants or the EGA release, and the study assessed sleep duration rather than validating our score.
* The [cohort's early findings report](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/9115/1/Understanding-Society-Early-Findings.pdf) describes seven wave-1 sleep questions that mirror some aspects of the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index; they are not the complete PSQI and do not necessarily constitute clinical insomnia. Later sleep-question timing and eligible sample need checking.
* The [2024 FI technical report](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10203000/) describes a 36-item deficit index for respondents aged 50 and over, using waves 1–9 and requiring complete items. The published item list includes an SF-12 energy question but no explicitly named insomnia or sleep item. The energy question can still overlap conceptually with fatigue or daytime sleep symptoms; other definitions of FI may differ. A no-energy version would need a prespecified definition and validation. The report excludes some cohorts because the needed information is unavailable. The availability and distribution of a Fried five-component phenotype in the same people remain unverified.

## Access and next checks

The [UK Data Service](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/help/new-users/faqs-for-new-users/) says its data collections can be downloaded free for non-commercial purposes, but this general survey-data statement does **not** establish free access to the EGA genetic files or their linkage. [METADAC's current notice](https://www.metadac.ac.uk/data-access-through-metadac/) says it closed in December 2020 and that access decisions are now managed by the relevant studies; the older UKHLS-specific METADAC page retains historical language. EGA also requires a request; contact the Understanding Society team through its current process for linkage requirements. Exact eligibility, legal conditions, processing charges, permitted country/institution, data transfer costs and consent need direct verification. No application or communication has been submitted.

For a prospective request, verify linkage and the joint sample by sleep-question wave, FI wave, age, genotype QC and the exact 33 marker contrasts; ascertain participant and family overlap with all discovery GWASs. Genotyping of a relative or participation in a separate sleep GWAS is not proof of independent, usable data. Consider selection, household relatedness, survey design, ancestry and sampling weights. This resource has not been used to validate either prototype, and no prediction or clinical claim follows.

## Earlier sleep GWAS and independence caveat

The [published sleep-duration analysis](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/23614/1/Garfield_et_al-2018-Genetic_Epidemiology.pdf) says its ELSA and UKHLS genome-wide analyses used 1000 Genomes-imputed genotypes, and that the authors combined those estimates with **UK Biobank** sleep-duration summary statistics by fixed-effect meta-analysis. This supports the historical feasibility of computing genotype-based associations for a sleep phenotype in UKHLS. It does not establish that the imputed files in that analysis are available in the current EGA dataset, that the project variants passed QC, or that the participants have a complete FI.

The combined ELSA–UKHLS–UK Biobank meta-analysis is **not an independent target dataset** for this project's scores, whose development or candidate selection used UK Biobank results. Do not treat a reported meta-analysis effect as a UKHLS-specific replication or extract its pooled effect to validate these scores. The paper describes removing closely related UKHLS participants using an estimated relatedness threshold for that specific GWAS; a future analysis must perform its own cohort-specific relatedness and household-design checks. Whether UKHLS itself overlaps any other source in the project remains to be verified.

## Imputation and biomarker availability

A 2025 [Understanding Society/UK Data Service presentation](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/app/uploads/kumari2025-06-23.pdf) reports 9,944 genotyped individuals who also have biomarker data, more than 500,000 array SNPs and more than 8 million imputed SNPs. The [study metadata](https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/854237/) describes nurse assessments that included grip strength and other physical measures. These are relevant to a later physical-frailty analysis and suggest that imputed genetic data exist, but they do **not** establish that each participant has a usable grip measure, that the imputation files are included in the specific 9,920-sample EGA release, or that our 33 variants meet quality thresholds. The 9,944 and 9,920 figures come from differently dated descriptions and must not be combined or assumed to be identical.

The research team should request the precise genetic file and imputation version, build, variant allele contrasts and quality, as well as the authorized joint sample and access cost. The existence of millions of imputed sites makes a direct-array-only screen insufficient for deciding whether the prototypes can be calculated, but does not itself confirm score coverage. No data were downloaded from the controlled archive.

## Specific imputed-genotype archive identified

The controlled [EGA HRC-imputed release EGAD00010002875](https://www.ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00010002875) is separately listed with **9,920 samples** and 24 compressed VCF files totaling **130.9 GB**. It was listed on 19 October 2026 under the same study and data-access committee as the 9,920-sample direct-array release. Matching displayed counts do not prove identical individuals or successful linkage, and the public listing does not establish genome build, per-variant imputation quality or score-marker coverage. Those must be checked in the authorized file metadata and actual records. A different [older EGA imputation release](https://ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00010000918) describes UK10K + 1000 Genomes imputation and a different displayed sample count; do not add the datasets' counts or assume independence.

The HRC release is a more concrete candidate for obtaining missing alleles than the direct array alone, but EGA still requires a data request, and linkage to survey data requires confirmation and approval through the study’s current access process. Total costs, permitted research use, overlap with discovery, and the linked sample remain unverified. The large listed files also exceed current local free disk space; file selection or an approved secure compute environment would need planning after access. No restricted files were downloaded.

## Correction to access guidance

An earlier version of this review incorrectly treated a historical METADAC committee instruction as current. The committee’s [closure notice](https://www.metadac.ac.uk/data-access-through-metadac/) says METADAC closed in December 2020 and directs data-sharing enquiries to the relevant studies. The UKHLS-specific page retains historical language; its continued visibility does not establish an active committee review. The [EGA](https://ega-archive.org/about/ega/) describes its archiving and secure-access service as free, but this does not by itself verify the costs of cohort-specific linkage, file transfer, computing, or institutional compliance. The total no-fee status and current application route remain to be checked.
