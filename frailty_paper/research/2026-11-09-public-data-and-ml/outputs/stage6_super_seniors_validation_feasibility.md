# Super Seniors healthy-aging GWAS: validation feasibility

Checked 29 October 2026. [Hoque and colleagues' 2026 Canadian study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-026-02229-4) performed a GWAS comparing 597 adults aged 85 or older who had no history of several major diseases with 420 middle-aged population controls. It reports about eight million analyzed variants. This is a potentially useful *healthy survival* phenotype, but it is neither the frailty index nor the Fried frailty score; the extreme age and health-based sampling create a different outcome and possible survival-selection effects. Its relatively small, selected sample also limits precision, especially for low-frequency variants. Whether insomnia was measured, how many participants have relevant measurements and whether this cohort overlaps the project's source samples or relatives were not established.

The paper's data-availability statement says Super Seniors data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request, subject to institutional and ethical approvals. It does not establish an unrestricted, free download of full signed GWAS results or individual-level genotype–phenotype records. The paper has a public supplementary PDF, but no complete candidate-specific allele and effect extraction was performed. Therefore no missing-variant or null-effect conclusion follows. The study is not currently an actionable open score-validation cohort and no request or contact was made.

Another search result, [Hu and colleagues' 2026 frailty study](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1005045), mentions a UK Biobank-excluded GWAS meta-analysis. Reading the methods shows that this is for **suicide-attempt genetic risk**, not an independent frailty GWAS. Its frailty instruments come from UK Biobank; the article says UK Biobank access has fees. It should not be mistaken for a free UKB-excluded frailty dataset or validation of the current scores.

Neither source supplies the independently verified linked genotype and sleep/frailty outcomes necessary to assess the score's prediction, nor has a new candidate-level score test been run here.
