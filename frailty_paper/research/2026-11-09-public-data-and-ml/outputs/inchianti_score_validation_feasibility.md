# InCHIANTI: lower-priority controlled-access validation lead

Checked 29 October 2026. InCHIANTI has published sleep, aging and genetic research, but it is **not a verified free, publicly downloadable individual-level validation dataset**. Its relatively small sample, unclear fee and prior project use of cohort-containing gait summary data make it a secondary lead.

* A [genomics methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5388176/) reports 1,210 participants genotyped with the HumanHap550 array. This historical count does not establish currently available data, sample consent, quality or 33-marker score coverage.
* A [published study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3198213/) reports self-reported sleep duration and time in bed alongside walking speed and physical function; 751 participants entered its longitudinal analysis. These measures are not themselves an insomnia diagnosis, and 751 is not a genotype–sleep–frailty intersection. Exact insomnia symptom variables remain unverified.
* A [separate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7260260/) describes a 42-item frailty index, while another [study](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/bc1940adfd9a57dfedf91fb69fdb8501/PMC7362211.pdf) describes Fried-type frailty. Neither establishes that the exact definitions and needed input variables are in the genetic release or in the same eligible participants.
  **FI item check:** The published 42-item inventory has no explicitly named sleep item, but includes two CES-D exhaustion questions (effort and difficulty getting going), weight loss, physical activity, gait speed and grip strength. Those are related to the five Fried components and can create shared measurement content; whether the separate Fried construction uses identical source items and thresholds must be checked. It is therefore reasonable to call the documented inventory *without an explicitly listed sleep item*, not proven free of all sleep-related effects. The paper scores some deficits on multiple levels between 0 and 1, not purely binary, and requires fewer than 20% missing items. With 42 items that means at most eight missing and at least 34 observed. A fatigue-excluded 40-item sensitivity needs its own prespecified missingness rule, and is not the published FI. The published FI's mortality analysis included 1,129 individuals aged 65 or older; this is not the linked genetics/insomnia sample.
* The [NIA access policy](https://www.nia.nih.gov/inchianti-study) requires a detailed proposal, committee approval before analysis, a sponsoring study member for an outside first author, and agreement to publication and sharing conditions. The policy does not specify whether a proposed genetics request is free. An older [repository directory](https://www.re3data.org/repository/r3d100011834) reports a fee but also labels the old repository offline; it is not reliable evidence of current charges. Do not assume no fee or submit a proposal without the research team's details.
* InCHIANTI contributed to the previously inspected [CHARGE gait GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5310665/). This creates cohort overlap with prior exploratory evidence, but not proof that those results selected the prototypes or that the same people would enter a future analysis. Audit the selection history and participant/relative overlap before calling any future test held out.

Next checks, if this route becomes worthwhile: confirm the cost and release of imputed genotype dosages, insomnia symptoms rather than only sleep duration, linked FI and Fried inputs, consent, participant intersection, exact variants and discovery overlap. No communication or application was sent and no score was calculated.
