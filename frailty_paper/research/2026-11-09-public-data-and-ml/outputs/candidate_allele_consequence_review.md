# Allele-specific functional annotation of the 15 candidate variants

I queried the Ensembl GRCh37 VEP ID endpoint for each of the 15 exploratory markers, checked the returned chromosome and position against the previously audited GRCh37 position, and retained transcript consequences for the alternate allele corresponding to the candidate's two-allele contrast. All 15 returned exactly one coordinate-matching VEP record and contained the candidate's reported reference–alternate pair. The complete extracted results, query URLs and SHA-256 response hashes are in `candidate_vep_allele_consequences.csv`; the extraction script is `work/vep_candidate_consequences.py`.

**What this adds:** this is an allele-specific computational annotation, not evidence that a variant causes either disease, changes gene expression, acts in a particular cell, or predicts personal risk. The study's effect allele can be the reference allele: VEP describes the alternate allele relative to reference, while the score weights are oriented to the counted allele. The presence of the pair in the reference does not resolve all the project’s original study-specific multiallelic coding concerns.

- **rs1032524**: the candidate contrast is T/C, with T as GRCh37 reference and C as alternate. VEP annotates C as a 3′ UTR variant in multiple NAV1 transcripts and as intronic/noncoding for other transcript records. A UTR annotation is a regulatory hypothesis, not demonstrated altered NAV1 expression or shared causation. The study's counted allele is T, so it must not be relabeled as the annotated alternate without changing its weight's orientation.
- **rs12536335**: the G alternate is annotated as intronic/noncoding in FOXP2 transcripts, including an NMD-transcript annotation. An NMD transcript annotation describes a transcript category; it does not establish that the variant triggers nonsense-mediated decay or alters FOXP2 expression. FI estimates in UKB and TwinGene had opposite directions under the project’s inferred allele adapter, so this remains a sensitivity candidate.
- **rs113851554**: the T alternate has intronic and noncoding-transcript annotations at MEIS1. The source rsID is multiallelic; the annotation is for T against G, not every allele at that rsID.
- **rs35881094 and rs17550821**: each alternate is annotated in LINC01122 transcripts; the former includes a noncoding transcript exon term. These variants are in the same region and have reference-panel r² ≈ 0.137; they are not automatically independent score markers.
- **rs10865954**: the C alternate is annotated against multiple transcripts including KLHDC8B and C3orf84; the candidate's counted allele is T, the reference. This illustrates why one nearest-gene label is not a proven effector gene.

Four of the 15 contrasts returned no transcript consequence in the selected coordinate and alternate-allele records: rs1909650, rs78206187, rs1942262 and rs7734045. A blank in this table does not mean the allele has no regulatory effect; distal/intergenic or unreported annotations can be relevant. No protein-altering transcript consequence appeared in this set of returned allele-specific transcript terms, but that absence is limited to this VEP annotation and its represented transcripts, not a proof of biological inactivity.

The 15 candidates are selected from a prior cross-trait list and have not been independently validated as a joint score. Genotype-level allele QC, LD-aware development and independent outcome validation remain necessary before estimating an individual's risk. The project’s failed prespecified local-correlation QC gates remain failed.
