# Proxy-score arithmetic in the 1000 Genomes European reference

This is a genotype-only feasibility simulation, not a validation of insomnia, frailty or any clinical prediction. We compared four existing research score definitions with experimental sums that retain markers identified by position in the related Omni5 report and replace the other markers with their previously selected nearby reference tags. We also compared full scores with sums restricted to the position-matched markers. All 503 samples in the project's 1000 Genomes European PLINK reference had complete calls for these constructed sums. [Aggregate results](stage6_framingham_proxy_score_reference_audit.csv); [reproducible script](../work/framingham_proxy_score_reference_audit.py).

| Research sum | Position-matched markers | Substituted markers | Correlation with full sum: substituted | Correlation with full sum: position-matched only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15-marker FI weights | 9 | 6 | 0.984 | 0.699 |
| 15-marker FFS weights | 9 | 6 | 0.998 | 0.774 |
| 15-marker insomnia weights | 9 | 6 | 0.990 | 0.831 |
| 22-marker shared-direction count | 12 | 10 | 0.991 | 0.741 |

The tag dosages were oriented using their sign in the same reference, and each reconstructed absolute marker-level correlation was checked against the saved signed value. We applied the original target's weight to each tag directly; the proxy sums can have different means and scales, so raw values and thresholds cannot be exchanged. Correlation is not agreement in clinical classification, calibration or disease prediction. The 15-marker FFS weight direction is inferred, and the 22-marker count is distinct from the alternative 22-marker weighted scores.

These correlations are **optimistic reference-panel descriptions**: the same 503 people were used both to select the best tags and to evaluate score similarity. They are not out-of-sample results, FHS participant data, or evidence of performance in other ancestries. The 1000 Genomes biallelic PLINK records are treated as the modelled allele pairs; this does not establish the exact source GWAS contrast or valid complementing in a multiallelic target. The related Omni5 report's genome build and exact FHS chip revision are unverified, and same-position probes are not proof of allelic identity or QC-passing calls. Proxy effects, strand and LD must be checked in authorized target data, and an independently defined evaluation is required before considering any altered score. No individual scores or genotypes were saved. See [tag selection](stage6_framingham_omni5_reference_tags_review.md) and [signed direction audit](stage6_framingham_proxy_reference_direction.md).

## European reference subgroup sensitivity

The [subgroup table](stage6_framingham_proxy_score_eur_subgroups.csv), generated by `work/framingham_proxy_score_eur_subgroups.py`, partitions the same 503 people into the five 1000 Genomes European reference labels (91–107 per group). Across those groups, correlations between the full and experimental tag-substituted sums ranged 0.979–0.990 for 15-marker FI weights, 0.997–0.999 for FFS weights, 0.985–0.994 for insomnia weights, and 0.986–0.994 for the 22-marker count. Direct-only correlations and subgroup standard-deviation ratios are also recorded.

These groups were all part of the reference used for tag selection, so this is **not cross-validation** or independent replication. Subgroups are small, no confidence intervals were estimated, and apparent differences should not be interpreted as reliable population differences. This does not test non-European transfer, actual Framingham genotypes, multiallelic contrasts, or prediction of outcomes. All the primary simulation limitations above remain.
