# A diagnosis-and-laboratory FI GWAS as an alternative sensitivity lead

[Tseng et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-023-00874-7) reported a UK Biobank GWAS of a 52-item frailty index in **178,566 participants**. Its FI was constructed mainly from diagnoses and laboratory measures, alongside physiological and self-reported items. The published [supplementary item list](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-023-00874-7/MediaObjects/11357_2023_874_MOESM2_ESM.docx) does not explicitly name insomnia, sleep, fatigue or tiredness. This is a promising **different-construct sensitivity** for the project's direct-item-overlap concern, not a clean deletion from the Atkins or Mak FI.

There are important limits. Broad diagnosis groups and general health or stress items may capture sleep-related information indirectly; the supplement does not give complete ICD code lists for every group, so the index cannot yet be called definitively sleep-free. Age range, selection, source items, laboratory inclusion, denominator, phenotype transformation and GWAS model differ from the project's FI. The cohort is UK Biobank and can overlap the original frailty and UKB insomnia GWAS. Even if full summary statistics are obtained and an LDSC estimate is precise, differences in genetic correlation cannot be attributed solely to removing insomnia or fatigue.

The FI source list is not identical across the published descriptions: the article text describes **38 ICD-coded deficits, 10 blood tests and four physiological status reports**, while the supplement numbers **36 named diagnosis-type deficits, 12 biomarker/anthropometric measures and four other measures**. Both total 52, but that discrepancy, the actual code mappings, units, cutoffs and implementation need clarification before reconstructing the phenotype or labeling exact exclusions. Several supplementary thresholds or units also warrant verification against the original code. The paper reports a separate Fried-type physical phenotype, but definitions and case selection differ from Ye's 0–5 GWAS; it is not a direct substitute for FFS.

The paper also describes FI as a proportion of nonmissing scored deficits, yet reports a range of **0–29.67** in the text and **0–29.6** in Table 2 alongside mean 0.104. If each available deficit is scored from zero to one and the denominator is the number of available items, the proportion must lie between zero and one. A percentage versus proportion reporting mix is a possible explanation, but it is not confirmed and should not be silently corrected. Establish the actual phenotype and beta scales from the original code or authors before interpreting the FI coefficients.

The FP descriptions also require verification: the main text says **“more than three”** criteria for cases, whereas the supplementary definition says **three or more**; the results text reports 164,292 robust participants, while Table 2B lists 216,744. These are source-report discrepancies, not evidence that either number or cutoff was actually used in the GWAS. Obtain the analytic code and final case/control counts before comparing this FP with other frailty phenotypes or using the reported models as benchmarks.

## Availability and use

The article's data-availability statement identifies UK Biobank project 54423 but does not provide a link to full FI GWAS summary statistics. Targeted searches located the paper and supplementary material but did **not establish a full downloadable GWAS file**; this is not proof that none exists. The paper prints selected significant variants, which are insufficient for genome-wide LDSC. The GWAS Catalog API attempts in this environment returned HTTP 410, so no claim is made that its catalog was exhaustively checked. No new genetic correlation was calculated.

The publisher's first supplementary DOCX contains figures rather than a dense SNP-level table; its second contains the item lists described above. Searches by article PMID and title did not reveal a linked full-statistics release. A [data request draft](tseng_fi_data_request_draft.md) is prepared for the corresponding authors; it has not been sent.

## Published score and machine-learning comparison

The paper also reports an XGBoost AUC of **0.85** for a physical-frailty classification model using serum markers and demographics, and **0.82** for its model labeled FI plus PRS. The authors state that including the genetic score did not improve performance, but their figure and Table 4 describe **different phenotype populations/models** for those displayed comparisons. The two AUCs therefore do not by themselves quantify the incremental value of adding PRS to the same baseline model for the same outcome and participants. The physical-frailty analysis also excludes prefrail individuals, which changes the classification problem. The paper says its models were not externally validated. These reported AUCs do not validate the project's 22-marker score, predict incident frailty, or establish that genetics cannot improve a properly paired model. [Paper's Table 4 and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10828416/).

## Exact overlap with the published lead list

The complete 31 FI rsIDs in the paper's Table 3A were subsequently recovered across two indexed excerpts of the [article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10828416/) and checked against the 22 candidate rsIDs. **None match exactly**. The [lead-ID comparison](tseng_published_fi_lead_id_overlap.csv) records all 31 checked names. This is an ID-level comparison to a selected lead table, not a comparison of the complete GWAS or of independent loci. Different rsIDs can tag the same signal through LD, and a candidate not listed among selected leads may still have an association. LD between the sets has not been comprehensively evaluated, and not all genomic builds and alleles are verified. The paper states a p < 10⁻⁸ threshold for 31 FI loci but its printed table includes entries above that threshold; use the listed variants as the table's published selections, not as 31 independently verified p < 10⁻⁸ results.

The discrepancy is quantifiable from the **printed** values: 20 of 31 are below 10⁻⁸, 10 are strictly below 10⁻⁹, one is printed as exactly 1.00 × 10⁻⁹, and all 31 are below 5 × 10⁻⁸. The text instead says 31 were below 10⁻⁸ and 21 below 10⁻⁹. Rounding prevents determining which side of the strict 10⁻⁹ cutoff the exactly printed value falls on, but cannot explain the other large count discrepancies. We do not know whether the text, table, analysis or another unreported criterion explains them. The [transcribed p-values](tseng_fi_published_lead_pvalues.csv) allow the counts to be checked; the data-request draft can be amended to ask the authors for clarification.

An exploratory positional check used the 31 [published coordinates and allele pairs](tseng_fi_lead_positions_reported.csv) as if GRCh37, matching the assumed build of the 22 candidates. Of the 682 possible cross-set pairs, one lies on the same chromosome within 1.5 Mb: rs34290943 (LAMB2-labeled) and the published FI lead rs3774745 (SEMA3F-labeled), **1,043,085 bp apart**. This arbitrary distance rule was chosen post hoc, and most Tseng coordinates and their genome build have not been individually authenticated; it does not define biological loci or exclude longer-range LD.

In 503 EUR-labeled 1000 Genomes phase 3 individuals, the specific rs34290943 T/C and rs3774745 C/T biallelic reference records matched those assumed positions, with phased r² = **0.022** (r = 0.149) between the listed first alleles. This is low pairwise reference LD, not evidence of conditional independence or of no other shared variants. The paper's effect-allele coding has not been independently confirmed. [Reference receipt](tseng_shared22_nearby_ld.json); calculation: `work/tseng_lead_rs342_reference_ld.py`.

## Exploratory clinical-insomnia lookup for the FI leads

I looked up the 31 selected Tseng FI lead rsIDs in the full MVP and FinnGen insomnia files. On an **assumed GRCh37-to-GRCh38 mapping**, 25 were uniquely matched to the same nonpalindromic SNP and allele pair in both files; six failed these eligibility criteria and are not negative findings. Two of the 25 have unadjusted p < 0.05 in both clinical insomnia sources:

| Variant; aligned allele | Tseng FI p | MVP insomnia p | FinnGen insomnia p | Insomnia direction on aligned allele |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| rs429358 C / T, APOE | 7.82 × 10⁻¹² | 0.000582 | 0.00353 | Negative point estimates in both sources. |
| rs9277988 C / T, MHC region | 3.62 × 10⁻⁹ | 0.0350 | 0.0182 | Positive point estimates in both sources. |

The Tseng published FI coefficient for rs429358 is positive for its reported A1 allele C, while the two insomnia estimates aligned to C are negative. The Tseng A1 effect convention and rounded FI coefficient have not been independently verified, so this potential **opposite direction** must not be described as a concordant shared-risk allele. The rs9277988 FI coefficient is positive on the reported C allele, matching the positive insomnia point estimates under that same unverified source convention; its position is in the MHC, where extended LD and complex allele representation make locus interpretation particularly difficult. Neither row establishes shared causality. APOE haplotypes cannot be inferred from rs429358 alone.

The paper reports using PLINK 2; the current [PLINK 2 format documentation](https://www.cog-genomics.org/plink/2.0/formats) defines A1 in a standard linear association report as the counted allele for BETA. That supports the assumed interpretation if Table 3 retains the standard output convention, but the published table is formatted and the actual output/code was not available, so author confirmation remains necessary.

For context, a direct lookup in the separate Mak UKB full-FI and physical-frailty files, aligned to **C versus T**, gives:

| Variant | Mak FI beta, p | Mak physical-frailty beta, p |
|---|---:|---:|
| rs429358 | +0.0180, 0.424 | −0.00328, 0.193 |
| rs9277988 | +0.1057, 2.86 × 10⁻⁷ | +0.00802, 0.000505 |

These are post hoc marginal results on two different phenotype scales; neither estimate for rs429358 is statistically precise. They cannot establish agreement or disagreement with Tseng's effects, and the Mak and Tseng studies both use UK Biobank. The MHC result remains a signal requiring region-specific analysis, not evidence for one independent causal allele. Mak A1 is T in all four source rows, so the beta sign was reversed to align to C.

This is a post-selected, uncorrected screen of FI leads, not a validated replication or a test of all variants. Multiplying the larger of the two insomnia p-values by 31 gives approximately 0.109 for rs429358 and >1 before capping for rs9277988; neither meets a 0.05 Bonferroni-style benchmark across the printed 31, even before addressing selection and dependence. Failure of a nominal threshold does not establish no effect. The GWAS use different insomnia ascertainments; Finnish and MVP populations differ, UKB sources may overlap the Tseng sample, and the FI could have indirect sleep overlap. The [full lookup](tseng_lead_insomnia_followup.csv) preserves eligibility, exact matches, p-values, source frequency and MVP imputation r². Script: `work/tseng_lead_insomnia_lookup.py`; no participant data were analyzed.

If the authors or a permitted repository supply the complete FI summary statistics, obtain the sample definition, genome build, effect-allele convention, beta and SE, per-variant N and quality, plus the original 52-item definitions and code. Then apply the same predeclared heritability-precision gate, source QC, appropriate LD reference and matched-support covariance-aware comparisons used for the existing analysis. Do not assume a non-significant result proves no shared risk. A same-person full FI versus FI with only insomnia and fatigue removed remains the cleaner test.
