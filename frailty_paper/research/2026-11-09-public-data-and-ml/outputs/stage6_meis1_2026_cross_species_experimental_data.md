# MEIS1: 2026 cross-species experiments and open behavioral data

Checked 29 October 2026. [Zimmerman et al., Genome Research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532834/) used prior human cell-based mapping to nominate insomnia candidate genes, then screened zebrafish orthologs with CRISPR. The abstract reports that perturbation of **meis1b** impaired nighttime sleep maintenance and increased sleep latency. Developmental profiling identified meis1b in cerebellar granule progenitors; the authors report comparable MEIS1 expression in humans. This is gene-level, cross-species experimental support for a sleep-related MEIS1 hypothesis—not a human insomnia–frailty study.

A separate [Joo et al. study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13392613/) reports altered locomotion and cerebellar development after zebrafish meis1b loss, with Purkinje-cell manipulation supporting a circuit hypothesis. The study notes that zebrafish mutants do not model the full human restless-legs syndrome phenotype. Together these studies provide a reason to consider cerebellar progenitors and circuits alongside prior GABAergic-model evidence, but they do not show that the project variant **rs113851554** causes these effects, nor that the locus causes frailty. Gene knockout and human noncoding-allele effects can differ substantially. Do not assume the two experiments provide independent human cohorts or transfer effect directions to our score.

## Available data and what can be done

The [Dryad deposit](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.wpzgmsc07) advertises individual larval sleep and activity measures, outlier flags, statistical results, and Sanger sequencing files. Its README says results average days 6–7 post-fertilization, and each manipulated gene is compared with its negative-control-injected siblings; 11 endpoints were analyzed per gene with a gene-specific Hochberg procedure. These are public **animal functional data**, not human genotype–frailty data. The author-identified outlier flags are based on visual inspection, and fish from the same clutch may not be independent; use the paper's detailed experimental design before attempting any reanalysis or treating fish as independent biological replicates.

Attempted 29 October 2026 to download the listed sleep workbook through the Dryad `file_stream/4781328` link using this environment; it returned HTTP 403. This is an observed local download failure, not evidence that the deposit is inaccessible elsewhere. No workbook contents were inspected and no independent numerical reanalysis was performed. The deposit landing page and README were readable. Follow-up should obtain the workbook through an accessible route and check experimental replicates, exclusions, endpoint definitions and the original multiplicity procedure. The project scores remain unvalidated and the search for no-cost linked participant genotype and sleep/frailty outcomes continues.

## Accessible supplementary file and replication structure

The Dryad page links an open [Zenodo supplement](https://zenodo.org/records/20406938), separate from the blocked raw workbooks. Its `Zimmermanetal-SupplementaryBehaviorData.docx` downloaded successfully on 29 October 2026; its MD5 `b6912cbd8652c493f9c5a25ede3cbc7a` matched Zenodo's displayed checksum. The document contains 18 embedded figures and describes their format; it is not the individual-fish data workbook. Its text states that plots present each independent clutch separately, while the reported statistical analyses combined both clutches after averaging measurements over the two days. Zenodo's methods say at least two clutches were run for each gene with matched injected siblings within each clutch and alternating plate placement.

These details make clutch and day important design features for any independent reanalysis; the source description alone does not establish the exact statistical model or whether it accounts for clustering. The accessible document does not resolve the missing workbook or demonstrate validation of the genetic scores. The raw workbook remains uninspected. Local archived supplement: `work/zimmerman_2026/supplement.docx`.

## Human neural-progenitor variant-to-gene link

The [preceding human-cell study by Palermo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821868/) offers a more specific link than physical proximity: its promoter-focused Capture C and ATAC-seq analysis in iPSC-derived neural progenitor cells places rs13033745 within an open region contacting the MEIS1 promoter. The authors report r² = 0.84 between this proxy and their insomnia sentinel rs1519102; that is **the paper's reported pair**, not a measured correlation with our rs113851554. A chromatin contact is physical/regulatory evidence, not proof of variant causality, and neural progenitors are not mature human sleep circuitry. The 2026 zebrafish screen builds on this mapping, so the two studies should not be counted as fully independent variant-to-gene evidence. A subsequent European reference-panel check found very low LD with our scored variants, as detailed below. The study does not test frailty or our score.

## Reference LD check of the neural-progenitor proxy

A new allele-specific check of [1000 Genomes phase 3 EUR phased genotypes](https://www.internationalgenome.org/data) finds very low reference LD between the paper's contacted proxy **rs13033745 T/G** and our score marker **rs113851554 T/G**: haplotype r² = 0.00043, based on 503 individuals (1,006 phased haplotypes). The paper's proxy–sentinel pair rs13033745 T/G and rs1519102 C/G gives r² = 0.84082 in the same sample, matching the reported r² of 0.84 as a useful positive control. The sentinel–score pair gives r² = 0.00046. Positions and allele contrasts come from the cited GRCh37 reference VCF; rs113851554 and rs1519102 have additional alleles listed by Ensembl outside the VCF's biallelic representations. This check does not establish the original GWAS or experimental allele contrasts.

Thus the reported contact at rs13033745 should **not be presented as direct proxy evidence for rs113851554** on the basis of this EUR reference. These observations do not exclude another regulatory contact, other causal signals, or different LD in other populations, and neither establish nor refute a frailty mechanism. They do not validate a score. [Reproducible script](../work/meis1_proxy_reference_ld.py) and [machine-readable results](stage6_meis1_npc_proxy_reference_ld.json).

The same reference calculation was extended to the other chromosome 2 variant in the 15-marker prototype, **rs78206187 G/A**. Its r² with the neural-progenitor proxy rs13033745 T/G is 0.00030; r² with the paper's sentinel rs1519102 C/G is 0.00031. The two project variants have r² = 0.07291 with each other. All three pairs use the same 503 EUR individuals and 1,006 phased haplotypes. This supports keeping the reported proxy's chromatin-contact evidence separate from both scored variants in this reference population; it does not establish independence in another ancestry or rule out other connections.

## Exploratory association lookup for the separate proxy signal

The project scanned the full local copies of the Atkins FI, Ye FFS and Jansen insomnia files for rs13033745 and rs1519102 by rsID. Both matched the GRCh37 positions and reference-VCF allele pairs above. These are the same or overlapping discovery sources used elsewhere in this project, and these two highly correlated variants were selected after the chromatin finding; the results are **not independent replication**. All p values below are unadjusted and none reaches the conventional genome-wide threshold.

| rs13033745 G vs T | +0.0104 (0.0036), 0.0036 | +0.00416 (0.00199), 0.035 | 1.017, 0.002443 |
| rs1519102 G vs C | +0.0121 (0.0036), 0.000693 | +0.00475 (0.00198), 0.018 | 1.020, 0.0002746 |

The FI source reports the opposite effect allele (T or C); the displayed FI coefficients have been sign-flipped **under the assumed biallelic diploid contrast**. FFS uses the source-supported A1 effect convention, while insomnia OR is the reported A1 effect; the traits' scales differ. Ensembl lists an additional allele for rs1519102 outside the GRCh37 1000 Genomes VCF record, so the source's treatment of that allele requires verification. The FI file's two cohort direction strings differ (`-+` in the original effect orientation); the TwinGene sign-convention inconsistency documented in the main project limits interpreting those strings. These selected, nominally associated variants can motivate independent fine-mapping and cell-specific follow-up, not demonstrate a causal shared mechanism or validate the 15- or 22-marker scores.

## Follow-up in additional public GWAS files

The full existing FinnGen R13 insomnia, MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital frailty risk score (HFRS) summary files were scanned for these two selected rsIDs. Both GRCh38 positions matched unique `hg19ToHg38` chain conversions from the GRCh37 positions, and the listed allele pairs matched the reference above. These are exploratory single-marker lookups, not score validation. MVP/FinnGen were already used elsewhere in the project's follow-up and selection; target-study independence, participant/relative overlap and cohort-level independence are not fully established. HFRS is a hospital-code-based outcome distinct from FI and Fried physical frailty.

- For rs13033745 G, the MVP insomnia OR was 1.010 (95% CI 0.997–1.024, p = 0.1289); for rs1519102 G it was 1.013 (0.9995–1.027, p = 0.05973). These p values do not meet a conventional 0.05 threshold, and rounded estimates should not be treated as proof of no effect.
- In FinnGen insomnia, the effects were reported for the opposite allele: rs13033745 T beta +0.00131 (SE 0.00725, p = 0.857) and rs1519102 C beta +0.00241 (SE 0.00714, p = 0.736). Under the biallelic contrast the corresponding G effects have negative signs but are very imprecise; apparent sign differences do not establish heterogeneity or contradictory biology.
- In FinnGen HFRS the reported T and C effects were −0.0396 (SE 0.0137, p = 0.00379) and −0.0416 (SE 0.0135, p = 0.00199), respectively; assuming the matched biallelic contrasts, the G effects have positive signs. Both are unadjusted, postselected and below genome-wide significance. High LD means these two rows are not two independent discoveries. HFRS and FinnGen insomnia analyses may share participants.

The full scan receipts are [FinnGen insomnia](stage6_meis1_proxy_FinnGen_insomnia_lookup.json), [MVP insomnia](stage6_meis1_proxy_MVP_insomnia_lookup.json), and [FinnGen HFRS](stage6_meis1_proxy_FinnGen_HFRS_lookup.json). Reproduce with `python3 work/meis1_proxy_external_lookup.py STUDY`. Source allele frequencies and representation support the stated matches but do not resolve alternate alleles or prove a shared causal variant. An appropriately powered independent analysis and locus-level fine-mapping would be required to evaluate sharing.

## Public GTEx v10 molecular follow-up

The public GTEx v10 exact-variant queries returned 14 single-tissue eQTL rows for rs13033745 (`chr2_66433271_G_T_b38`) and 13 for rs1519102 (`chr2_66450684_G_C_b38`). The eQTL response is a filtered set of reported associations, not all attempted variant–gene–tissue tests. Both variants returned associations with **MEIS1-AS3**, an antisense transcript, in bulk cerebellum and cerebellar hemisphere. For rs13033745, cerebellum normalized effect size (NES) is −0.879 with p = 8.48×10⁻²²; for rs1519102 it is −0.784 with p = 4.96×10⁻¹⁷. These are reported marginal nominal p values; the response does not by itself provide a joint disease–molecular test. Other returned rows include MEIS1-AS3 and MEIS1-AS2 in non-brain tissues; neither response contains a row for the protein-coding **MEIS1** gene. GTEx's [FAQ](https://www.gtexportal.org/home/faq) defines NES relative to the ALT allele: T for rs13033745 and C for rs1519102. NES is on a normalized expression scale, not a percentage or fold change.

This strengthens a specific transcript-and-tissue hypothesis for **the proxy signal**, not the scored rs113851554 variant, which has low EUR reference LD with the proxy. MEIS1-AS3 expression is not the same as MEIS1 expression, bulk tissue does not identify a cell type, and associations at the two high-LD variants are not independent. The nearby chromatin contact, expression associations and GWAS associations may be driven by distinct linked variants; no colocalization was performed. rs1519102's additional reference alleles and source coding remain unresolved. Each sQTL query returned zero rows; that is not proof that splicing is unaffected or adequately tested in every tissue. This does not establish a sleep or frailty mechanism or validate a score.

[Query receipt with URLs and response hashes](stage6_meis1_proxy_gtex_v10_receipt.json); raw responses reside in `work/meis1_gtex/`; run `python3 work/meis1_gtex/query_proxy.py` for a live repeat.

## eQTL Catalogue: regional cerebellum data

A public [eQTL Catalogue](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/eqtl/Data_access/) tabix lookup retrieved 372 MEIS1-AS3 gene-expression association rows in a limited GRCh38 interval (chr2:66,400,000–66,530,000) from dataset `QTD000166`, a separately processed GTEx cerebellum dataset whose release-7 metadata lists 209 samples. This is a different processing release from GTEx v10 and may share donors; it is not an independent cohort. The source uses ALT as the effect allele. At rs13033745 G/T, the ALT T beta is −0.9166 (SE 0.0733; nominal p = 1.04×10⁻²⁶). At our scored rs113851554 G/T, ALT T beta is +0.1268 (SE 0.2120; nominal p = 0.551). The latter is an imprecise marginal association, **not** proof of no regulatory effect. Both rows list 418 called alleles; source-specific analysis/sample filters and allele representations still require checks.

This limited 130-kb extract includes nonsignificant tests and is a potential starting point for a regional analysis, but it is not the full cis window or an established complete variant universe. It does not provide study-matched LD, fine-mapping, colocalization, cell-type resolution, causal evidence or genetic-score validation. A valid shared-signal analysis would require full comparable cis coverage, matched alleles and variants, adequately specified sample sizes, credible signal models and suitable LD; do not run coloc on just these selected rows. [Retrieval receipt](stage6_meis1_catalogue_cerebellum_receipt.json), [script](../work/meis1_gtex/catalogue_region.py), and local regional extract `work/meis1_gtex/QTD000166_MEIS1AS3_66400000_66530000.tsv`.

## Wider regional coverage and GWAS matching

The eQTL Catalogue cerebellum extract was expanded to GRCh38 chr2:65,400,000–67,500,000, yielding **7,095 rows** for MEIS1-AS3, including nonsignificant tests. This interval spans more than 1 Mb on each side of the [NCBI-reported MEIS1-AS3 coordinates](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/730198), but the catalogue's precise source annotation and its full cis-test boundaries have not been independently reconciled, so complete source coverage is not assumed. [Retrieval receipt](stage6_meis1_catalogue_cerebellum_wide_receipt.json) and [script](../work/meis1_gtex/catalogue_wide.py).

A full scan of the locally held MVP insomnia and FinnGen HFRS GWAS files matched **6,379** and **6,112** unique eQTL rows, respectively, by GRCh38 position and unordered allele pair; duplicate GWAS matches were excluded. The strongest eQTL in the retrieved interval, rs6738144 C/A, is present in each matched set. The counts are pairwise, not a three-way common set or credible-set coverage, and exact position/pair equality does not settle multiallelic semantics. The full MVP file supplies rounded odds ratios and `#NA` standard errors for these matched rows, complicating signed-effect reconstruction. HFRS is distinct from the target FI and FFS and may share participants with other FinnGen results. Source sample sizes, QC, variant coverage and genetic LD remain to be evaluated before fitting a regional sharing model; matching alone cannot establish colocalization. The input and matching procedures are documented in the [MVP receipt](stage6_meis1_MVP_eqtl_wide_receipt.json), [HFRS receipt](stage6_meis1_HFRS_eqtl_wide_receipt.json), and [script](../work/meis1_gtex/match_wide_gwas.py).

The same eQTL region was also matched to the project's GRCh37 Atkins FI, Ye FFS and Jansen insomnia files after public-chain GRCh38-to-GRCh37 conversion. All 7,095 eQTL rows had a unique plus-strand chromosome-2 mapping in that conversion. Position and unordered allele-pair matching produced 5,363 unique FI rows, 5,818 FFS rows and 5,462 insomnia rows; their three-way intersection contains **5,165 allele-defined positions**. The strongest eQTL in the retrieved interval was present in all three pairwise matches. No allelic sign was flipped or causal model fitted in this matching step. The source build and original effect-allele semantics, multiallelic contrasts, ancestry, frequency mismatches, cis completeness, genotype LD and trait overlap remain to be audited. In particular, these counts include strand-palindromic pairs, which require special handling before signed analysis. Shared UK Biobank participants mean these datasets are not independent replication.

## Reference LD among regional marginal leads

A postselected check compared the most significant MEIS1-AS3 cerebellum eQTL in the retrieved window, rs6738144 C/A, with the strongest Jansen insomnia association among its matched eQTL rows, rs11693221 C/T. In 503 1000 Genomes EUR participants (phased haplotypes), r² = 0.000084. The eQTL lead's r² with the scored rs113851554 G/T is 0.00036; with the strongest FI matched row rs2194667 A/G, 0.0031; and with the strongest FinnGen HFRS matched row rs111736174 C/A, 0.0165. These are reference-population LD estimates between marginal lead variants selected inside this particular window. FI and HFRS leads are not genome-wide significant in these source files. GRCh38-to-GRCh37 coordinates and biallelic reference VCF records were checked; original study multiallelic coding and other populations remain unverified.

The low lead-pair LD warns against calling these particular top associations one shared signal. It does **not** exclude a secondary shared signal, condition on other variants, or substitute for multi-signal colocalization with suitable study-matched LD and adequate coverage. Nor does it validate a genetic score. [Reference-LD receipt](stage6_meis1_lead_reference_ld.json), [script](../work/meis1_gtex/lead_reference_ld.py).

A subsequent [275-variant SuSiE-coloc sensitivity](stage6_meis1_cerebellum_susie_sensitivity.md) favored distinct eQTL and Jansen insomnia signals under its restricted model, but its limited window, excluded top eQTL variants, external rank-deficient LD, and source and overlap uncertainties preclude a definitive no-sharing conclusion. FI and FFS produced no credible sets in that analysis.
