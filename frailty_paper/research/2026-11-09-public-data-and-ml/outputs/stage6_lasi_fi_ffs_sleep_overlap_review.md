# LASI: published deficit index, physical frailty and sleep overlap

The parent LASI wave 1 has published constructions of both a 40-deficit frailty index (FI) and a five-component physical-frailty phenotype, making it a promising setting for the mentor's FI-versus-FFS question if linked LASI-DAD genotypes can be accessed. These published analyses do not demonstrate that a given participant, or any particular number of participants, has both scores, sleep measures and usable project-marker genotypes.

| Feature | 40-deficit FI | Five-component physical phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| Source | [LASI FI study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10391831/) and its [supplement, Table S1](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC10391831.1/bmjopen-2023-071842supp001.pdf) | [LASI sleep/physical-frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10339638/) |
| Construction | Fraction of 40 health deficits including diseases, symptoms, function, BMI, measured grip and walking time | Five domains: exhaustion, grip, physical activity, weight loss and walking time; the study uses a food-insufficiency weight-loss proxy |
| Sleep overlap | **One deficit directly combines four sleep questions:** trouble falling asleep, waking during the night, waking early and daytime unrestedness; a frequent response to any is coded as the deficit | No identical sleep question is documented among the five domains, but self-reported exhaustion overlaps conceptually with sleep and includes tiredness |
| Relationship to project | Different from the discovery FI; original contains sleep. Grip and walk are shared with physical frailty. | Adapted phenotype, not identical to the discovery Fried frailty score or the original Fried operationalization |

The sleep/physical-frailty study's sleep-symptom composite uses **three of the same questions** as the FI sleep deficit, but its positive cutoff includes occasional or frequent symptoms (three or more nights per week), whereas the FI supplement uses frequent symptoms (five or more nights per week) on any of four questions. The constructs directly overlap even though the cutoffs and fourth question differ. Neither composite is a clinical insomnia diagnosis. Treating the original FI as an independent sleep-free endpoint would be incorrect.

The FI paper's primary construction requires all 40 deficits to be observed, defines FI as the sum divided by 40, and labels FI ≥0.25 as frail. Its reported sensitivity allows up to three deficits missing and divides by the number observed. If the explicit sleep deficit is removed, a 39-item ratio would be a **new, unvalidated adaptation**, not the published FI. Its missingness rule, denominator and any frailty cutoff require prespecification; the published 0.25 cutoff must not automatically be assumed valid for the changed index. The FI also includes physical-function, mental-health and other potentially sleep-related concepts. Excluding the explicit sleep deficit reduces direct item reuse but does not make constructs independent or eliminate shared measurement and selection effects.

## Shared measures use different rules

Both constructions use measured grip and a 4-m walk, but their published operationalizations differ. The FI supplement describes grip as `(left + right) / 2` with sex- and BMI-specific thresholds and describes the timed-walk deficit as a mean walk time greater than 10 seconds. The physical-phenotype paper describes the average of two dominant-hand grip trials, with sex/BMI stratification, and a mean timed walk with sex/height stratification. Its article does not give enough detail in those passages to reconstruct all cutoffs. The FI supplement says two readings were collected per hand but does not make the `left + right` trial aggregation fully explicit; it also states threshold rules only for BMI up to 40 before saying otherwise coded zero. Resolve those details against study data/code, including handling of BMI above 40, missing trials, inability to walk and questionnaire skips, before reproducing either phenotype.

Consequently, the two measures can overlap even when they classify the same physical observation differently. A useful prespecified paired comparison would analyze the original FI, the explicitly sleep-excluded 39-item adaptation and the separately reconstructed five-domain phenotype in the **same genotyped participants**, then report each grip/walk coding and the shared-component sensitivity. Removing shared physical items as a further sensitivity would change the FI and its meaning again; it would not estimate the independent causal contribution of sleep or create a validated score. The sample sizes and consented participant intersection remain unknown.

The [wave-1 instrument map](stage6_lasi_wave1_instrument_field_map.md) identifies the source question IDs for sleep and the physical phenotype, including a `993` sentinel used for both inability to complete a measurement and equipment problems. These question IDs are not yet verified variable names in the linked release.

A separate [harmonized 30-item FI](stage6_harmonized_global_fi_sleep_overlap.md) has been published for LASI and seven other aging surveys. It also includes sleep, so it is not a ready-made sleep-excluded endpoint; its item coding and mortality evidence differ from the LASI 40-item index.

The original paper reports 65,562 people aged 45+ in its FI cohort and 64,331 with the alternative allowing up to three missing deficits; these are parent-study analysis counts, not LASI-DAD genotype-linked counts. The separate physical-phenotype paper reports 31,902 older people, likewise not a shared genetic analysis sample. A valid same-person comparison must construct both outcomes in the same eligible genotyped respondents, verify the separate grip/walk definitions and missingness, and compare score effects with their paired uncertainty. The free-access routes, dataset linkage, appropriate ancestry/relatedness, sample and consent restrictions, marker quality and discovery overlap still need verification; see the [LASI-DAD feasibility review](stage6_lasi_dad_validation_feasibility.md). No individual data were accessed or scored.
