# CLSA frailty index: sleep and exhaustion overlap

Checked 29 October 2026 against [Pasquet et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40520-024-02706-w) and their [supplementary tables 2 and 3](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs40520-024-02706-w/MediaObjects/40520_2024_2706_MOESM1_ESM.docx). This is an inspection of public documentation, not an analysis of participant data.

The paper describes a 93-deficit baseline FI. Its supplementary inventory explicitly includes `DEP_RSTLS_COM` (restless sleep). That is not identical to the separate insomnia-onset and maintenance questions, but it is a sleep-related deficit and can mechanically contribute to observed overlap. The inventory also includes `DEP_FFRT_COM` (everything felt like an effort) and `DEP_GTGO_COM` (difficulty getting going); these two variables also define the exhaustion component of the paper's Fried-type phenotype. Thus FI and physical frailty share those two inputs, independently of any genetic correlation. Neither score association should be called sleep-independent without a sensitivity analysis.

The shared inputs extend beyond exhaustion: the two tables also list `GS_EXAM_AVG_COM` for grip weakness and `WLK_TIME_COM` for walking time in both the FI and phenotype. Their weight-loss and physical-activity definitions use related measures but are not demonstrably identical: the phenotype's weight-loss definition adds a BMI restriction, and the physical-activity phenotype uses a total PASE score while the FI lists several PASE-related deficits. This overlap limits interpretation of same-person agreement as evidence of separate constructs.

There is a source inconsistency to resolve before reproducing slowness: supplementary table 2 describes the phenotype cutoff as the lowest quintile of `WLK_TIME_COM` (a time in seconds), whereas table 3 describes the FI walking-time deficit as the highest quintile. Do not silently substitute one definition for the other. Check the underlying cited construction or authors' code and document any correction. Different definitions can still share the same raw input.

Pasquet et al. cite [Verschoor and Tamim](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6477649/) for their physical-phenotype construction. The cited study's main text directs readers to its supplement for the component thresholds; the original threshold was not independently retrieved in this check. Conceptually, for a fixed distance, a longer time means a slower walk, and the highest time quintile corresponds to the lowest speed quintile, aside from tie and boundary handling. That supports a candidate interpretation, but **does not prove which cutoff the Pasquet analysis actually used**. Obtain the original component table or analysis code before claiming exact replication.

The cited earlier study reports treating the 93-item FI as missing when 14 or more items were missing (thus requiring at least 80 observed), and treating its Fried phenotype as missing if any component was missing. These are that study's rules, not automatically Pasquet et al.'s: Pasquet reports multiple imputation. For modified 92- and 90-item FIs, specify a separate missingness rule in advance rather than transferring the absolute count without justification. A [published erratum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6477642/) corrects a smoking-covariate coding error in the earlier study; it is not a correction of the walking cutoff.

An automated structural check of supplementary table 3 found **93 distinct listed item labels** across nine categories, including the three sleep/exhaustion items. Thus removing those labels leaves maximum counts of 92 and 90, respectively; this verifies the table inventory, not availability, calculation or per-person nonmissing denominators. Reproduce with [the inventory checker](../work/clsa_fi_inventory_check.py) and the public supplement (downloaded DOCX SHA-256 `67678af8f288151a854651df56e5f36c20e2e85997f52c93149a3f3c1c259754`). Several labels are derived or composite, so their individual source variables still require mapping.

The supplement describes its binary thresholds for these negative CES-D items as all the time or occasionally versus some of the time or rarely/never. The [CLSA depression derived-variable document](https://www.clsa-elcv.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/dv_dep_17dec2018.pdf) distinguishes these as 5–7, 3–4, 1–2 and less than 1 day, respectively, while its standardized CES-D scoring uses a different numerical direction. Verify exact baseline raw variable coding and the published construction before applying any numeric threshold. Do not substitute a derived depression total containing restless sleep for an audited item-level FI.

## Proposed comparisons after authorized data access

1. Reconstruct the published FI and five-component physical phenotype first, including the publication's imputation and sample rules where feasible; compare distributions with the publication without assuming the project cohort matches its sample.
2. Prespecify an FI excluding the restless-sleep deficit and another excluding restless sleep plus the two exhaustion-related deficits. Recalculate each person's numerator and denominator from the retained observed items, with an explicit minimum-item and missingness rule. The table inventory has 93 distinct item labels, giving maximum counts of 92 and 90, but source data and composite construction remain unverified. Results for changed FIs are sensitivity analyses, not the published FI.
3. Keep the published physical-frailty phenotype intact for the primary comparison. If removing its exhaustion component is scientifically justified, describe the result as a separate modified four-component measure, not standard Fried frailty; do not reuse the original 3-of-5 threshold without a separately justified definition.
4. Use the same participants and prespecified covariates when comparing outcomes or scores; report changes in estimates with uncertainty, not proof that residual associations are independent or causal. Remaining indirect overlap, phenotype differences and selection bias can persist.

Exact variable mapping, source sample overlap, eligibility and fee coverage are not yet verified. No project score has been validated in CLSA.
