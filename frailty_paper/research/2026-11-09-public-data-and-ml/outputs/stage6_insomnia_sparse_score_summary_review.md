# Fifteen-marker weights against two external insomnia GWAS: exploratory check

The project's three existing 15-marker weight sets give positive reference-LD-adjusted weighted-Z statistics against both the MVP clinical-insomnia and FinnGen R13 insomnia summary statistics. This is a **post hoc, approximate association check**, not independent validation of a score or evidence that a score predicts a person's insomnia or frailty.

| Target and reference LD | FI weights | FFS weights (direction inferred) | Insomnia weights |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MVP insomnia, 503 European 1000 Genomes reference samples | 8.54 | 4.67 | 9.19 |
| FinnGen R13 insomnia, 99 Finnish 1000 Genomes reference samples | 6.05 | 4.21 | 7.67 |

The figures are weighted Z statistics, not odds ratios, risk probabilities, accuracy measures or a comparison of predictive performance. The small reference panels and approximated assumptions prevent asserting that nominal p values are well calibrated. Candidates and target associations were examined before this calculation; discovery and selection overlap or relatedness has not been fully excluded, and multiple loci and models were explored. Consequently this is not a prespecified replication. The FI discovery phenotype includes insomnia, and the FFS allele directions in the prototype remain inferred. Different source ascertainment and target ancestry also matter.

In an unplanned sensitivity, removing rs113851554 changed the MVP figures to 7.90, 5.52 and 6.89 and the FinnGen figures to 5.43, 5.04 and 5.57, respectively. The increases for the FFS weight reflect cancellation between differently signed components; they do not establish that removing the variant improves prediction. The marker is in the MEIS1 region and has prior links to restless-legs or periodic-limb-movement phenotypes; those studies do not prove the mechanism in either insomnia cohort. These omissions are exploratory sensitivity analyses, not independent tests or confidence intervals.

## Calculation and limitations

For each target, the project's 15 exact position-and-allele lookups were aligned to the prototype's counted alleles. Target allele frequency was oriented to the same allele and converted to an approximate genotype standard deviation under Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, `sqrt(2p(1-p))`. For weights `w`, target marginal Z scores `z`, scaled weights `a`, and external signed reference LD `R`, the statistic is `a'z / sqrt(a'Ra)`. Cross-chromosome reference correlations were set to zero; within-chromosome dosage correlations used the previously checked 1000 Genomes matrices. This approximation assumes compatible target samples and covariates, suitable imputation and effect scales, and that the covariance of target Z statistics is adequately approximated by external LD. It is not equivalent to fitting the genetic score in the actual participants.

For MVP, source odds ratios are rounded and standard errors unavailable; we reconstructed signed Z from the two-sided p value and aligned log-odds direction. For FinnGen R13, Z is aligned beta divided by reported SE. Matched 15-variant source rows and allele caveats are in `prototype_15_variants_full_external_insomnia_lookup.csv`. Some reference rsIDs are multiallelic in broader databases even when the reference contrast used here was biallelic, and the exact study representation needs verification. The 503-person reference and 99-person Finnish subset are related, not independent validation panels. As a reference sensitivity, swapping them changed the MVP FI/FFS/insomnia statistics to 8.46/4.67/9.07 and the R13 figures to 6.11/4.22/7.76. This stability does not resolve possible target-LD mismatch or selection bias.

`stage6_insomnia_sparse_score_summary.py` writes all target/reference/weight combinations, leave-one-variant-out sensitivities and component details. The source and weight hashes and assumptions are in `stage6_insomnia_sparse_score_summary_receipt.json`; prior reference genotype checks are documented in `stage6_hfrs_sparse_score_summary_review.md`. That prior hospital-frailty analysis and these insomnia lookups are different targets but not independent patient-level tests of a common clinical score.

## Reference sampling sensitivity

A further resampling check drew 5,000 bootstrap samples of the reference people, holding the target GWAS statistics and weights fixed. For MVP/EUR503, the central 95% reference-only ranges were 8.44–8.65, 4.63–4.72 and 9.02–9.37 for FI, FFS and insomnia weights. For R13/FIN99, they were 5.88–6.24, 4.13–4.32 and 7.44–7.95; 27 of 5,000 Finnish draws were excluded because at least one reference marker had no variation, leaving 4,973 draws. These are **not confidence intervals for an association, an effect size or predictive performance**: target GWAS uncertainty, sample overlap, post-selection, reference/population mismatch and measurement differences were held fixed or unmodeled. Resampling reference individuals assumes exchangeability and does not eliminate relatedness or reference ascertainment concerns. Similar values across these draws do not validate the score. `stage6_insomnia_sparse_score_ld_bootstrap.py`, the summary CSV and receipt retain the seed, counts and method.

The TwinGene FI cohort cannot serve as a clean independent target here: it contributed to the original FI discovery meta-analysis, and the source's TwinGene allele-direction convention is unresolved. See `candidate_twingene_sensitivity.md`.

To validate the actual 15- or 22-marker score, freeze a definition and evaluate it against prespecified individual outcomes in an adequately sized non-overlapping cohort with genotype QC, baseline covariates and an explicit performance comparison. Public summary data cannot establish calibration, discrimination, incremental utility or a joint sleep–frailty endpoint.
