# CKB proteomic aging models: AI design context

[Bennett, Wang and colleagues (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106401) studied machine-learning-derived proteomic age measures in the China Kadoorie Biobank (CKB). Their main models use blood proteins and LightGBM, with Boruta feature selection within a 70% training split and a 30% held-out test split. The authors report 3,976 participants in the overall descriptive plot and 1,193 in the holdout plot. They also describe an external application of CKB-trained models to approximately 50,000 UK Biobank participants using Olink proteins. Those are sample counts for specific analyses, not the sample size for a sleep–frailty–genotype validation.

Their brain-associated proteomic age gap was positively associated with FI and slow walking in baseline adjusted analyses; the overall gap was also associated with FI. The paper also analyzed sleep duration in lifestyle models, which is distinct from clinical insomnia. These are observational associations involving model-derived protein measures, not evidence that the project score variants cause changes in brain aging or frailty. The model predicts chronological age from proteins; it is **not** a genetic embedding or a predictor trained on our insomnia–frailty outcomes. The authors' external model assessment should not be represented as validation of our scores, and reported associations with a gap after adjustment do not prove prediction of subsequent frailty.

The authors additionally ran GWAS of their proteomic age gaps with East Asian reference LD, and provide [analysis code](https://github.com/ckbiobank/Bennett-Wang-2026-proteomic-ageing-clocks). Their data-sharing statement describes request-based access to CKB non-genetic data, but does not supply an individual-level linked genotype/phenotype download or a full age-gap GWAS file. Such a release may exist elsewhere, but availability, access cost, score-marker coverage and participant overlap were not established here. The separate [CKB browser audit](stage6_ckb_catalog_screen.md) did not identify an immediately usable FI/insomnia GWAS by the tested phenotype codes. Neither result means the underlying CKB cohort lacks relevant data.

For our AI work, this study motivates a testable **multi-modal hypothesis**: evaluate whether a fixed genotype-derived score plus independently measured protein-derived age measures improves a prespecified frailty outcome beyond demographic and conventional baseline models. That analysis requires an appropriate cohort with permitted linked data, sample flow, independent outcome assessment and enough events. Feature selection and model fitting must be restricted to training data; use untouched participants and, where possible, an independent cohort, report calibration and uncertainty, and compare a simple model before claiming added value from embeddings. A protein age clock can capture health status and treatment effects; its association with frailty is not proof of a genotype-mediated mechanism. AlphaGenome sequence annotations, if available and permitted, would be a separate feature class, not measured clinical outcomes.
