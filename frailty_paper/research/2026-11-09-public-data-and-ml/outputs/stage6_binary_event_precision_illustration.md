# Frailty case counts and a future genetic-score test

Published MESA work reports 32 participants classified as frail among 1,743 in an imaging subgroup. For an illustration only, suppose all 1,743 had complete, independent genotype and outcome data and we compared the 32 frail people with the other 1,711, including prefrail people. Under a simplified near-null, unadjusted logistic model with a standardized score, the approximate 95% interval around an odds ratio of 1 would span **0.70–1.42**. An odds ratio of roughly **1.65 per score SD** would be needed for approximately 80% power at a two-sided 0.05 threshold under the same approximation.

These are **not observed score effects or confidence intervals**. The MESA paper's imaging group is not the verified intersection of genotypes, exam-5 sleep and exam-6 frailty, and it uses a modified FRAIL scale rather than the discovery FFS. The actual number of eligible frail participants could be different. The numbers are an illustration of the information carried by a small number of cases, not a formal MESA power calculation.

For comparison, purely hypothetical groups of 100 cases and 3,906 noncases, and of 500 cases and 3,506 noncases, produce null-centered approximate intervals of 0.82–1.22 and 0.91–1.10 respectively. They are not estimates of the number of frail participants in NHATS or any other cohort. The [calculation](../work/score_validation_binary_event_illustration.py) uses `SE(log OR) ≈ sqrt(1/cases + 1/noncases)` for a standardized score, with equal score variance in cases and controls and independent observations, plus a normal-shift power approximation. The approximation is most credible near the null; covariates, sample selection, relatedness, missing variants, separation, multiple testing and score distribution can change precision. Small samples may require different methods.

This addresses only a single score-association test. It does not determine sample size for calibration, discrimination, incremental AUC, subgroup analysis, or a combined insomnia–frailty endpoint. Before choosing a cohort, obtain actual linked genotype and phenotype counts, define the outcome and comparator, and calculate precision for the prespecified model using realistic assumptions.
