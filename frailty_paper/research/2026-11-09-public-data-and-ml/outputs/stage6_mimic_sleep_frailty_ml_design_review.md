# Sleep–frailty machine learning: MIMIC-IV preprint and design lessons

Checked 29 October 2026. An unreviewed [May 2026 preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202605.0889) reports predicting coded sleep-disorder diagnoses using hospital frailty, comorbidities and first-day variables in MIMIC-IV. It describes an XGBoost held-out AUROC of 0.871. This is an inpatient clinical-code model, not a genetic score, not a population insomnia–frailty model, and not validation of our prototypes. It reports no external dataset validation.

Two details limit the reported claim of calibrated admission-time prediction:

1. The authors state they randomly downsampled controls before creating an 80/20 train–test split, yielding 4,841 positive and 4,841 negative examples. Its hold-out test set therefore has an artificially balanced case fraction. Sensitivity and specificity can be evaluated under suitable sampling assumptions, but positive predictive value, precision–recall curves and unadjusted probability calibration depend on prevalence; the reported calibration cannot automatically be transferred to the original hospital population. Preserve a naturally prevalent untouched test set, and calibrate and evaluate there.
2. The paper says predictors include HFRS and diagnoses constructed from `diagnoses_icd` during the index hospitalization and describes them as admission-available. [MIMIC-IV documentation](https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/iv/modules/hosp/diagnoses_icd.html) says that table contains billed hospital-stay diagnoses determined from signed notes at discharge; its documented fields have no diagnosis-time column. Therefore the description alone does not establish that all index-stay diagnosis features were known at admission. It is a potential temporal leakage problem, not proof that a particular feature leaked: confirm the actual source code, temporal availability and any independently timestamped present-on-admission information. Likewise a variable gathered during the first 24 hours supports a 24-hour prediction landmark, not automatically a time-of-admission prediction.

The preprint additionally describes first ICU admission per person, which can reduce repeated-patient leakage, but the source text does not replace code-level verification. Its outcome is an inpatient diagnostic code and may reflect documentation and recognition, not newly incident clinical insomnia. SHAP values explain model output under a specified setup; they do not establish biological causation.

## Application to this project

For a future permitted sleep–frailty cohort: define the decision time and outcomes first, use only features available by that time, separate people and close relatives before feature selection and tuning, and leave an untouched naturally prevalent independent cohort or temporal test set. Compare age/sex/ancestry and clinical baseline models with and without the frozen research scores on identical cases; assess calibration, discrimination and uncertainty. Any embedding or ML model should be compared with simple models under the same split, and genetic-feature generation, imputation and normalization must not learn from held-out outcomes. A single-cell or AlphaGenome feature is a research annotation, not outcome validation. This is a design review of a preprint, not a reproduction; no MIMIC-IV participant data or model outputs were accessed.
