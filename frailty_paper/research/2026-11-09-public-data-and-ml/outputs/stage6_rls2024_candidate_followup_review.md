# Public RLS GWAS follow-up of the project candidate variants

**Important update:** a [cross-study follow-up](stage6_rls_cross_study_direction_discrepancy.md) found that two other public RLS GWAS report a positive rs78206187 G effect, opposite to the negative effect in this file. The reason is unresolved; do not generalize this file’s sign as a robust cross-study finding.

[Akçimen et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.27040) report a restless-legs syndrome (RLS) meta-analysis of 9,851 cases and 38,957 controls, including All of Us, CLSA and CARTaGENE. Their [GWAS Catalog release GCST90435387](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST90435387) provides free summary statistics. We queried the harmonized, indexed GRCh38 file by position and unordered allele pair for the 33 distinct candidates, then checked the reported rsID. The query is an exploratory variant-level sleep/movement follow-up, not an individual-level genetic-score evaluation or a frailty test. All of Us contributed to this meta-analysis, so it must not be counted as independent of the All of Us screens already used in this project; participant and relative overlap with every other discovery and selection dataset is unresolved.

**Coverage and results.** Exact coordinate-and-allele-pair results were returned for 21/33 candidates, and the source rsIDs agree for those matches. Twelve had no exact result; this is not a zero association. Four of 21 have nominal p < 0.05. Two pass Benjamini–Hochberg at q < 0.05 in a 33-candidate family that assigns p=1 to missing rows:

| Variant | Study effect / other allele | RLS beta (SE) | p | BH q across 33 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rs113851554 | T / G | +0.5044 (0.0319) | 2.36 × 10⁻⁵⁶ | 7.79 × 10⁻⁵⁵ |
| rs78206187 | G / A | −0.1584 (0.0350) | 6.19 × 10⁻⁶ | 0.000102 |

The reported effect allele for each is the 15-marker prototype's counted allele; exponentiating these log-odds coefficients gives approximately 1.66 and 0.85, respectively. In the project's original discovery-derived prototype, both counted alleles have positive insomnia and FI weights. Thus the two exact variants have **opposite RLS association directions**, with rs78206187 G associated with lower RLS odds in this meta-analysis despite a positive discovery insomnia weight. This comparison is across different traits, samples, models and ascertainment; it is not an estimate of an individual score's effect, a causal interaction, or proof that RLS mediates insomnia or frailty. Source allele orientation, multiallelic handling and remaining cohort overlap require confirmation for formal comparisons. The source metadata describes a 0.005 lower minor-allele-frequency limit; other filters, imputation, representations or harmonization can also affect missingness.

The two variants are about 272 kb apart on chromosome 2 and should not be assumed to be independent. An earlier 1000 Genomes EUR reference check reported r² = 0.073 for their exact prototype alleles in 503 reference participants; it is not study-specific LD or a conditional RLS analysis. Multiple variant–trait comparisons and earlier selection were not accounted for by the within-33 correction. The striking MEIS1-region association supports examining movement symptoms as a sensitivity analysis, but neither variant result establishes shared frailty causality, a cell type, or an improved risk score.

The [lookup table](stage6_rls2024_candidate_lookup.csv) and [receipt](stage6_rls2024_candidate_lookup_receipt.json) preserve the observed summary rows, allele direction and hashes. Reproduce with `python3 work/rls2024/query_candidates.py`. The script uses the public remote tabix index and checks exact coordinate/pair matches; it reads the published file header and metadata for column definitions. Because it downloads only indexed ranges, the full published compressed-file checksum was **not** verified. No participant data were accessed. True validation still requires the project score to be computed in an independent cohort with measured sleep and FI/FFS outcomes.
