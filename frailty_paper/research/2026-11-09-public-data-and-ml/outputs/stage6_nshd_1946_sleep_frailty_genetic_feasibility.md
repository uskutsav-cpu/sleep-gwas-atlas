# NSHD 1946 birth cohort: sleep–frailty genetic score feasibility

Checked 29 October 2026. **Status: controlled-access lead; cost and linked eligibility unconfirmed. No score validation was performed.** This is the 1946 National Survey of Health and Development (NSHD), a different cohort from the 1958 National Child Development Study (NCDS).

## Evidence

* The publicly browsable [sleep field catalogue](https://datashare.ndph.ox.ac.uk/nshd46/label.cgi?id=530) lists questions on sleep duration, quality, tiredness and trouble sleeping. For example, field [8824](https://datashare.ndph.ox.ac.uk/nshd46/field.cgi?id=8824) asks about difficulty falling asleep within 30 minutes, field [8825](https://datashare.ndph.ox.ac.uk/nshd46/field.cgi?id=8825) asks about waking during the night or early morning, and field [32726](https://datashare.ndph.ox.ac.uk/nshd46/field.cgi?id=32726) asks at age 43 whether the person has ever had trouble sleeping. Questions are insomnia-related symptoms, not necessarily an insomnia diagnosis; confirm exact sweep, coding and missingness before analysis.
* A [published study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-85435-8) constructed a 45-deficit frailty index (FI) at ages 0–16, 19–44, 45–54 and 60–64; 1,375 were included in its heart-function analysis. The 45-item FI is a deficit-accumulation measure, not the Fried physical frailty phenotype or a validated equivalent to this project's discovery FI. The 1,375 figure is not the available score-validation sample. The authors’ [supplementary tables](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-021-85435-8/MediaObjects/41598_2021_85435_MOESM1_ESM.doc) identify sleep disturbance as an included deficit, scored 0, 0.5 or 1. Table S3 lists sleep-trouble questions at several waves, plus lost sleep over worry and a PSQI total score in 2009. Therefore a sleep-excluded FI is an essential prespecified sensitivity analysis, with the denominator and missingness recalculated; which exact exposure and FI questions overlap at each wave still needs a field-level mapping.
* In a [published polygenic-score study](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1010233), the investigators retained imputed genetic data for 2,794 individuals after their QC. That paper successfully constructed BMI scores and used a European-ancestry analytical sample. This demonstrates that genetic score calculation was possible in the cohort, but not that our particular score markers are available or that those 2,794 participants have usable sleep and FI data. A [2025 genomic resource profile](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/54/5/dyaf141/8237599) likewise reports 2,794 imputed/QC genetic records.
* [NSHD's access process](https://skylark.ucl.ac.uk/NSHD/access/process/) requires an application; students must include their supervisor as a co-applicant. The [HDR UK catalogue](https://web.prod.hdruk.cloud/dataset/832) describes a trusted research environment and institutional data-sharing agreement. Its access-request cost field says “N/A,” which does not establish that all genotype, environment or project costs are zero. Current fees and eligibility for the requested linked genotype data must be confirmed. The dictionary is open; individual records are not established as openly downloadable.

## What must be checked before use

1. Confirm permission and total costs for imputed genotypes, sleep questions, FI variables, covariates and secure-environment use together. Identify any allowed jurisdiction/institution requirements.
2. Establish the participant intersection for each proposed sleep assessment, FI and genotype release, and report attrition and missingness. Assess candidate marker coverage, allele orientation, imputation quality and ancestry. Establish discovery-cohort overlap before claiming independent validation.
3. Choose an age-consistent analysis: an age-68 sleep measure occurs after the published age-60–64 FI and cannot be used as a prospective predictor of that earlier FI. Earlier sleep questions may permit a different temporal comparison, but available follow-up and overlap must be checked.
4. Freeze the existing candidate weights before evaluation. Evaluate each score against a prespecified phenotype with covariate-only comparison and uncertainty; evaluate FI and physical-frailty measures separately where both can actually be constructed. This cohort cannot yet be treated as evidence of score accuracy or causality.

The cohort is scientifically attractive because it combines life-course sleep and frailty phenotyping with demonstrated score calculation. However, the published FI directly incorporates sleep disturbance, and it is **not yet a confirmed free or independently usable validation dataset**.
