# Can we test the insomnia–frailty association without sleep in the FI?

**Update:** Mak et al. 2023 report a GWAS of a 41-item FI excluding all eight mental-wellbeing items, including insomnia and fatigue. The specific released Figshare inventory examined does not list its full summary statistics, so it is not yet ready for LDSC. See [the source audit](mak2023_sleep_excluded_fi_lead.md).

Search update: 27 October 2026. The targeted sources reviewed did **not establish an available, ready-to-run GWAS of an FI with all sleep and fatigue items removed**. That is a limitation of this search, not proof that none exists. The best newly identified candidate is an electronic-health-record FI with no explicitly named sleep or fatigue item in its published component list; its exact underlying coding and full summary-statistics access still need to be verified.

## Most promising alternative FI

[Tseng et al., GeroScience (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-023-00874-7) performed a continuous FI GWAS in 178,566 UK Biobank participants, ages 60–73. Their FI comprises 52 items: 38 ICD-10 health deficits, 10 blood biochemistry tests and four physiological-status reports. Its composition differs substantially from Atkins' questionnaire-rich FI; this makes it worth examining for a sensitivity analysis, but it also changes the frailty construct.

The published FI SNP-heritability estimate is 0.12 (SE 0.0051), approximately Z = 23.5. This suggests a well-powered *published* heritability result, not automatic passage of our project's quality gate: the full statistics must first be acquired, harmonized and checked with the project's own LDSC settings. We obtained and inspected [Supplementary Table S1](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-023-00874-7/MediaObjects/11357_2023_874_MOESM2_ESM.docx) and extracted its 52 named items into `tseng_fi_supplement_components.csv` (source DOCX SHA-256 `1a873e7d42745f0c25011e04a2bbd5fbe6f5f028f8522c6764ad36314b90d3aa`). None explicitly names sleep, insomnia, fatigue or exhaustion. However, broad categories such as self-reported health and chronic pain may capture related burdens indirectly, and the supplement does not specify the complete underlying diagnosis codes. Thus absence of explicit named items is supported, but absence of all sleep-related coding and indirect overlap is not established. There is also a source discrepancy: the article describes 38 ICD-coded deficits plus 10 blood tests plus four other indicators; the supplement enumerates 36 diagnostic deficits, 12 measures (including two BMI measures), and four other indicators. Both sum to 52. The supplement’s 12 measures consist of two BMI indicators and ten laboratory measures; the article’s count of 38 may therefore be grouping those two BMI indicators with the 36 diagnoses, but that interpretation does not match its wording “ICD10-coded” and needs confirmation. Use the item-level source with caution until authors or code resolve the categorization. The supplement also prints an upper calcium threshold of 8.2 mmol/L, exactly the value printed for urea; this unusual threshold should be checked against the authors’ code before reconstructing the FI, not silently corrected. The [data-availability statement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-023-00874-7) points to UK Biobank under application 54423; it does not identify a downloadable full GWAS file. The article prints lead hits, which are insufficient for LDSC. Full summary-statistics availability remains unverified.

An alternative FI based on the same UK Biobank population also cannot be treated as an independent replication of the project's Atkins UK Biobank FI. Differences in ascertainment, item content and overlap with the insomnia GWAS must be evaluated before interpreting differences in genetic correlations. Its Fried phenotype includes self-reported exhaustion and is a separate phenotype, not a sleep-excluded FI.

## Other leads checked

- [Ma et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11979021/) studied a Fried-style physical frailty phenotype with exhaustion, not a sleep-excluded deficit FI. Its discovery and validation work used UK Biobank; the paper's data statement says UKB individual-level data are restricted. Its statement about limited GWAS beyond UK Biobank should not be read as showing that its own samples came from separate external cohorts. Availability of usable full GWAS statistics has not been established.
- [Blood pressure and frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11258474/) used a modified FI excluding cardiovascular deficits. This does not establish exclusion of insomnia or fatigue. The paper points to licensed UK Biobank data rather than providing evidence here of an immediately usable sleep-excluded GWAS.
- [Zhu et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-022-02557-0) removed insomnia from an FI for a prospective observational analysis. This directly addresses measurement overlap at the phenotype level, but the study is not evidence of a sleep-excluded FI GWAS or of a genetic correlation.

## Decision-ready next step

1. Obtain the underlying ICD code lists and phenotype-construction code for Tseng’s 52 items, and resolve the discrepancy in the reported category counts. Check insomnia, sleep disorders, tiredness and exhaustion at the code level; the inspected named-item list does not settle indirect overlap.
2. Locate the complete FI GWAS summary statistics or request them from the authors. Confirm genome build, alleles, sample size, ancestry and model. A list of genome-wide significant hits cannot replace genome-wide statistics.
3. If both checks are satisfied, harmonize and run the same predefined QC and LDSC settings as the project. Check h²/SE against the existing ≥4 gate, and run correlations against the same insomnia GWAS. Estimate uncertainty in any FI comparison with an appropriate paired or block-jackknife procedure where possible; do not compare significance labels alone.
4. Interpret any change as a combined effect of item composition, phenotype construction and possible differences in participants and ascertainment. A more decisive design would recompute the original FI in the **same** participants with sleep and fatigue items removed, rerun the GWAS and compare with the original using the same model. This requires appropriate individual-level access and remains unperformed.

No new LDSC result or validated risk score is claimed here.
