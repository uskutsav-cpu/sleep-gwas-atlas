# Genodex and historical openSNP: distinguish genetic estimates from observed outcomes

The [Genodex insomnia page](https://genodex.org/phenotypes/insomnia) displays thousands of public genome-linked results, many labeled openSNP. This may look like a free validation cohort, but the site's [methodology](https://genodex.org/methodology) explains that it maps genomic variants to a model and reports values such as scores, z-scores, percentiles and coverage. These displayed calculated results are **not evidence that the corresponding people have measured insomnia**. Comparing one genetic score to another calculated genetic score could reflect shared input variants or training data and would not validate our score against observed insomnia. The page's displayed count is not a case count; none of these records has been shown here to have a measured FI or Fried phenotype.

The [openSNP maintainers announced](https://opensnp.wordpress.com/2025/03/31/sunsetting-opensnp-a-personal-retrospective/) that the original service would close and delete its stored data on 30 April 2025. Genodex's display of openSNP-labeled material does not by itself establish current rehosting provenance, participant choices, available phenotype data, genotype quality, cohort independence or suitable controls. Its [terms](https://genodex.org/terms) distinguish direct Genodex contributions from external data and recognize external datasets' verified licenses. Those details must be checked before using participant-level material. We did not retrieve or analyze identifiable records.

**Decision:** do not treat a Genodex z-score, model percentile or site phenotype label as an observed diagnosis or as confirmation of score performance. This is not an established individual-level insomnia–frailty validation source. The general possibility that another public release contains suitable observed phenotypes is not ruled out; such a release would need data provenance, current access conditions, measured outcomes, adequate events, nonoverlap, marker coverage and a prespecified analysis.
