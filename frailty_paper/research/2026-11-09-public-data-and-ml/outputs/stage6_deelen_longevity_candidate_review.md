# Public longevity GWAS: exploratory candidate follow-up

We obtained the publicly downloadable [Deelen et al. 90th-percentile longevity GWAS](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST008001-GCST009000/GCST008598/), a meta-analysis of European-ancestry cohorts with 11,262 cases and 25,483 controls in the discovery analysis. The [published study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11558-2) defines cases by survival at or beyond the age corresponding to the 90th percentile and controls by age at death or last contact at or below the 60th percentile. Longevity is an aging-related outcome, **not** FI, Fried frailty or insomnia, and this analysis does not measure a participant-level score, prediction, discrimination or calibration.

We matched the two existing, postselected score lists against the source file by GRCh37 position and unordered allele pair. All 15/15 variants in the weighted prototype and 22/22 in the equal-count prototype matched (33 distinct variant IDs; four are shared). The reported effect allele was aligned to each list's counted allele. All candidate allele pairs are non-palindromic. Genome-build and allele matches alone do not prove that all source harmonization, rare/multiallelic representation or reference-strand issues are resolved. Source effect and p-value rounding is retained, and `Effective_N` is the author's per-variant field rather than a count independently reconstructed by us.

No individual candidate had nominal p<0.05. The smallest p-values were 0.07943 among the 15 and 0.09572 among the 22. These exploratory comparisons were selected after earlier analyses and involve correlated variants and overlapping lists; lack of a nominal result does not establish absence of effects. Neither the aggregate scores nor differences in their prediction were tested. The cohorts have different demographic and survival-selection features; the discovery includes HRS, which has contributed other project follow-up, and full participant/relative overlap with all discovery, target and selection resources has not been established. The discovery file is separate from the paper's UK Biobank validation, but separation by cohort label does not prove complete independence.

## Combined-variant sensitivity

As a post hoc sensitivity check, we combined the signed marginal Z statistics for all available candidates, using the existing 503-person European reference. This is an approximation to a weighted association statistic, not the result of scoring the longevity participants. For the 15-variant prototype, empirical reference standard deviations and within-chromosome correlations (with cross-chromosome correlations set to zero) yielded Z≈−0.93 with FI weights, +0.43 with inferred FFS weights and −0.77 with insomnia weights. For the 22-variant equal-count prototype, reference-frequency Hardy–Weinberg standard deviations and the empirical reference score standard deviation yielded Z≈−0.87. Nominal two-sided p-values were 0.35, 0.67, 0.44 and 0.38, respectively.

These statistics are uncertain and not a formal replication: variant-level effective sample sizes differ; cohort composition and LD may vary by variant; the target genotype covariance is unavailable; and reference allele variance may not match the meta-analysis. The 22-variant calculation also combines HWE marginal variances with an empirical score denominator that includes reference sample cross-chromosome correlations. In the 15-variant calculation, `SE × reference genotype SD` ranges from 0.0129 to 0.0151; in the 22-variant calculation its range is 0.0123 to 0.0156, showing that a common-precision assumption is imperfect. The inferred FFS weight orientation remains unconfirmed. Different approximations and weights prevent ranking predictive performance, and a non-significant result cannot prove no association. The separate outcome, postselection and unverified sample overlap remain major limitations.

## Allele-frequency and variance sensitivity

After aligning the source effect-allele frequency to the counted allele, the largest absolute difference from the 503-person European reference was 0.036 among the 15-marker set and 0.046 among the 22-marker set; none exceeded 0.10. This is a limited coding check, not proof of strand correctness, ancestry matching or absence of cohort heterogeneity. Source frequencies are rounded, represent the contributing meta-analysis cohorts and need not describe any individual cohort.

For the 15-marker calculation only, we substituted genotype standard deviations derived from the reported effect-allele frequencies under Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium while retaining reference within-chromosome LD. FI-, inferred FFS- and insomnia-weight approximate Z values changed from −0.931, +0.426 and −0.773 to −0.922, +0.434 and −0.761. This is a descriptive robustness check under an additional assumption, not a confidence interval, formal test of differences, participant score validation or guarantee of accurate target LD. We did not apply the same substitution to the 22-marker score because its stored reference summary does not supply the full covariance needed to construct a consistent target-frequency denominator.
