# Conditional full-score summary sensitivity in All of Us

We obtained public **EUR genetic-similarity group**, whole-genome All by All summary results for all 33 distinct project variants in insomnia (`NS_333.2`) and generalized muscle weakness (`SS_807.21`). These aggregate estimates allow an approximate test of how the *fixed score weights* align with marginal associations under strong assumptions. They do **not** provide individual genotype scores, an observed score–outcome effect, prediction performance, calibration, a joint endpoint, or a validated score. The EUR group is a genetic-similarity classification, not an individual's self-identified ancestry. The reference panel is not the target cohort.

| Research sum | Insomnia conditional Z | Weakness conditional Z |
|---|---:|---:|
| 15-marker FI weights | 6.79 | 0.91 |
| 15-marker inferred FFS weights | 5.00 | 0.41 |
| 15-marker insomnia weights | 6.71 | 0.50 |
| 22-marker equal-weight count | 3.80 | 0.22 |
| 22-marker FI weights | 4.11 | 0.41 |
| 22-marker inferred FFS weights | 3.56 | 0.39 |
| 22-marker insomnia weights | 4.20 | 0.31 |

Under the assumptions below, all seven insomnia statistics are positive and have nominal two-sided p < 0.001; all weakness statistics are small, with nominal p > 0.36. These are **exploratory** and not corrected for all project searches or weight/outcome comparisons. Weakness estimates do not demonstrate no effect or prove a difference between outcomes. Different tests, selection, sample sizes and phenotype definitions mean Z values cannot rank predictive performance or establish shared causal risk. Generalized weakness is an EHR symptom, not FI or Fried frailty. Insomnia is a coded diagnosis. Source participant and close-relative overlap with all discovery and candidate-selection cohorts has not been verified; All of Us was part of a subsequently inspected clinical-insomnia meta-analysis, so those results and these are not independent of each other. Scores and follow-ups were chosen post hoc.

For each phenotype and score, the approximation is `Z = aᵀz / sqrt(aᵀRa)`, where `aᵢ = weightᵢ × sqrt(2fᵢ(1−fᵢ))`, `zᵢ` is signed from the source beta and two-sided p, `fᵢ` is source allele frequency, and `R` is 1000 Genomes Phase 3 EUR reference dosage correlation. Correlations across chromosomes were set to zero. The published p and beta/SE-derived |Z| differed by at most 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ in these 66 EUR rows; this check does not prove the tests are normally distributed. The coefficient-of-variation of `SE × HWE dosage SD` is 0.2–0.6% across each tested candidate set, consistent with but not proving the common effective-sample approximation. Different missingness, LD, case ascertainment or analysis models can still alter the covariance.

The 15- and 22-marker EUR LD matrices use 503 reference individuals with complete listed biallelic calls; they may include related people and population structure, and the remote VCF full-file checksums were not independently verified. The 22-count and 22-weighted files count opposite alleles at eight markers: the code explicitly reorients each weighted coefficient to the count allele before applying the count-oriented LD. Switching source beta signs to project-counted alleles assumes that source beta refers to ALT and that the reference/alternate contrast is biallelic and diploid. The All by All paper reports SAIGE for single-variant testing, and [SAIGE's documentation](https://saigegit.github.io/SAIGE-doc/docs/single_step2.html) describes beta as the effect of allele 2; a subsequent [source-code trace](stage6_hunt_aou_allele_direction_review.md) also follows the allele array and beta through the deployed browser's pinned decoder, strengthening the ALT-effect assumption without independently authenticating every deployed table. Some project rsIDs have multiallelic representations, and FFS source weight direction remains inferred. These assumptions must be checked before treating any signed statistic as established.

[All 14 results](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_sensitivity.csv), [per-variant terms](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_components.csv), [method receipt](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_sensitivity_receipt.json), [EUR association rows](stage6_aou_eur_candidate_association.csv) and [LD receipt](stage6_aou_count22_EUR503_ld_receipt.json) document the calculations. Single-marker omission ranges are sensitivity analyses, not confidence intervals or evidence that deleting variants improves a score. The extraction and calculation scripts reside in `work/aou_complete/`. Checks verified all 66 query rows, 14 distinct score/outcome combinations, hashes, valid allele pairs and symmetric LD; an additional synthetic check verified orientation invariance of the calculation. None of these checks evaluates outcomes in independent individuals.

## Additional source-code check for allele orientation

The [public All by All v8 analysis pipeline at commit `8b71fb3`](https://github.com/atgu/aou_gwas/blob/8b71fb31bdb3d570334cef8ebf807c4e4d4838ba/v8/pipeline/saige_aou_v8.py#L690) invokes SAIGE with `--AlleleOrder=ref-first` and [keys the result](https://github.com/atgu/aou_gwas/blob/8b71fb31bdb3d570334cef8ebf807c4e4d4838ba/v8/pipeline/saige_aou_v8.py#L582) using `[Allele1, Allele2]`. Together with [SAIGE's documented allele-2 beta](https://saigegit.github.io/SAIGE-doc/docs/single_step2.html), this **supports** the assumption that the pipeline's beta is oriented to the alternate allele. It is not a complete provenance proof: we have not established that this exact pipeline revision produced the currently served files or independently authenticated every table and multiallelic contrast. The [later Hail-to-browser source trace](stage6_hunt_aou_allele_direction_review.md) supports the transformation for the deployed slow-query route. The conditional interpretation remains appropriate.
