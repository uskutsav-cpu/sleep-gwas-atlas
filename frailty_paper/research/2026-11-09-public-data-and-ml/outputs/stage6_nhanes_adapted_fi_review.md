# Public NHANES: sleep trouble and an adapted deficit index

**Correction, 28 October 2026:** An earlier version used interview weights in error. Because the index includes MEC depression-screener items, CDC specifies MEC examination weights. The results below supersede the previous estimates; sensitivity sample sizes also changed. See [CDC 2011–12](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/DPQ_G.htm) and [2013–14](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/DPQ_H.htm) documentation.

## Result

Among adults aged 60 or older, reporting that a doctor or other health professional had been told about trouble sleeping was associated with a higher adapted 24-item deficit index after adjustment for age and sex. The survey-weighted mean difference was **0.071** (95% CI 0.051–0.092; n=1,554) in 2011–12 and **0.090** (95% CI 0.064–0.116; n=1,652) in 2013–14. The scale runs from 0 to 1: these differences correspond to about 7.1 and 9.0 percentage points in the fraction of observed deficits, respectively. These are two survey cycles, not independent genetic replication cohorts.

| 2011–12 | 1,687 | 1,554 | 420 | 0.071 | 0.051 to 0.092 |

Removing two additional potentially sleep-related deficits, concentration and psychomotor difficulty, produced differences of 0.066 (0.045–0.088; n=1,616) and 0.087 (0.061–0.113; n=1,707), respectively. A post hoc comparison on the same participants as the primary model yielded 0.070 (0.049–0.091; n=1,554) and 0.088 (0.061–0.114; n=1,652), respectively; see [matched-sample results](stage6_nhanes_adapted_fi_matched_results.csv) and [code](../work/nhanes_adapted_fi_matched.R). In the original sensitivity model, the sample changes because the minimum observed-item requirement is recalculated; this is a sensitivity analysis, not a direct decomposition of the difference.

A further **post hoc** sensitivity removed all six retained depression-screening questions, leaving 18 disease, symptom, cognitive and physical-function items. On participants eligible for both versions, the 18-item difference was 0.070 (95% CI 0.049–0.092; n=1,540) in 2011–12 and 0.078 (0.050–0.106; n=1,643) in 2013–14. The 24-item differences in those same samples were 0.073 (0.053–0.093) and 0.088 (0.063–0.113), respectively. The two indices use different denominators and content, so their coefficients cannot quantify the depression items’ causal contribution. The 18-item measure is also unvalidated and can still contain sleep-related deficits. See [results](stage6_nhanes_adapted_fi_no_dpq_results.csv) and [code](../work/nhanes_adapted_fi_no_dpq.R).

## Interpretation and limitations

The index was adapted from a published [30-item NHANES index](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13246825/) and its supplementary table, using 24 items common to both cycles. We removed the sleep and fatigue items, and four source items unavailable or unsuitable in one or both cycles. The published study focused on middle adulthood, and this adapted index and its application to older adults have not been validated here. Its chest-pain item is not a diagnosis of angina. Other retained cognitive, depressive and functional items may still share symptoms or causes with sleep trouble. The exposure is a reported conversation with a health professional, not a clinical insomnia diagnosis, and differs from the device-classified sleep-wear analysis. Age and sex adjustment does not address all confounding; missingness and health-care access could affect findings. Cross-sectional associations do not establish direction or causality. NHANES public data here contain no genotypes; this result cannot validate shared loci, a polygenic score, or cell-type findings.

The primary and sensitivity specifications are recorded in [the analysis plan](stage6_nhanes_adapted_fi_protocol.md). [Results](stage6_nhanes_adapted_fi_results.csv), [analysis code](../work/nhanes_adapted_fi.R), and [source download receipts](stage6_nhanes_fi30_sources.json) support reproduction. The analysis uses NHANES examination weights, strata, and PSUs, and treats refused, unknown, skipped, and borderline responses as missing. At least 80% of index items were required. Estimates were computed separately rather than pooling weights. Additional covariate specifications, and validation of the adapted index are future work.
