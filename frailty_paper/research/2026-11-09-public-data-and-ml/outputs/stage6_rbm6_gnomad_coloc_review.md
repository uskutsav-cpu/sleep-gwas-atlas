# RBM6 region: larger-reference insomnia and frailty comparison

## Findings

A new exploratory SuSiE-coloc analysis of a **narrowed** RBM6-region window found one Jansen insomnia–Mak frailty-index (FI) signal pair with PP.H4=0.916 and PP.H3=0.084 under p1=p2=1e−4 and p12=1e−5. That pair's PP.H4 ranged **0.521–0.982** when p12 was varied from 1e−6 to 5e−5. Several other insomnia–FI pairs favored distinct signals in this model. Both input studies use UK Biobank participants, and Mak's FI includes an insomnia item, so this result is **not independent replication, a causal direction, or proof of gene mediation**. A local association could partly reflect shared samples and measurement content. The locus and pair were examined after prior analyses, and posterior values are conditional on signal coverage, LD, prior and SuSiE model assumptions.

The comparison produced no Jansen insomnia–FinnGen hospital-frailty (HFRS) signal-pair posterior: although the HFRS fit converged, it returned **no reported credible set** under the default purity rule. Its maximum PIP was 0.026. This is not evidence that the traits have no shared signal or that HFRS is unassociated at the locus. The Jansen–Ye Fried frailty score (FFS) comparisons involved two insomnia signals and one FFS signal; at p12=1e−5 their PP.H4 values were 0.0036 and 0.0082, favoring distinct signals in this selected model. Those estimates are likewise conditional; they do not demonstrate that the underlying biological effects differ from FI or that there is no FFS–insomnia sharing elsewhere.

## Window and coverage matter

The prior 2-Mb analysis used 2,186 common variants and 503 European 1000 Genomes reference individuals; it returned two Jansen insomnia sets and no HFRS set. Here we used a 1-Mb GRCh37 interval, chr3:49,750,000–50,750,000, selected around the RBM6 region and the HFRS peak. It excludes the original 2-Mb insomnia peak rs10865954, so the results cannot be generalized to the whole region. The earlier and current references, windows and SNP sets differ; their posterior values are not independent replications or a controlled evaluation of reference LD alone.

Of the prior 1000 Genomes insomnia fit's two reported sets, the new support retained 8/60 SNPs and **4.6%** of the first set's component probability mass, and 83/83 SNPs and 100% of the second set's component mass. These values assess only the earlier model's reported sets, not all biological signal. The earlier set's reference LD was rank-deficient, and the current one is a different reference, so set labels cannot be assumed to refer to the same causal signal. `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_window_coverage.csv` records the comparison. It highlights a substantial loss of coverage for one prior signal.

## Inputs, model and interpretation

The 1-Mb input began with a postselected five-GWAS intersection; 1,034 SNPs matched the public gnomAD NFE and FIN variant indices by uniquely lifted position and exact allele pair. Both study-to-reference allele-frequency comparisons were available and passed a 0.20 absolute-difference filter: mean Jansen/NFE difference 0.0073 and HFRS/FIN 0.0072. The maximum differences were 0.033 and 0.044, respectively. These checks do not guarantee matching LD, strand or imputation. The FIN and NFE matrices were complete and symmetric, with minimum eigenvalues −4.23e−14 and 7.65e−5, respectively; the former is consistent with numerical precision.

SuSiE-RSS used L=10, 95% coverage, fixed residual variance, NFE LD for Jansen, Mak FI and Ye FFS, and FIN LD for HFRS. Working sample sizes were 313,750 effective for Jansen, 500,737 for HFRS, 402,910 for Mak FI and 386,565 for FFS; variant-specific N can differ and the models use external LD. All fits converged. Their `estimate_s_rss` values were 0.00251, 0.00346, 0.00048 and approximately 6.5e−9, respectively. These are model diagnostics, not calibrated validation tests or universal pass/fail thresholds.

In the Jansen–Mak pair with PP.H4=0.916, the reported lead markers were rs7428430 and rs2353586. They are statistical representatives of correlated signals, not established causal variants. At rs7428430, the same T allele had a negative Jansen insomnia beta (−0.02051, p=5.9e−5) and a negative Mak FI beta (−0.14645, p=3.73e−19), on different scales. The comparable direction is descriptive and neither establishes RBM6 as the effector gene nor supports a treatment effect. The HFRS and FFS fits are different phenotypes and cannot be used to infer which phenotype performs better clinically. There is no molecular QTL in this calculation; a true variant–gene–cell link still requires suitable multi-signal molecular colocalization and independent functional evidence.

## Reproducibility

`stage6_rbm6_gnomad_pair_prepare.py` defines the interval and input; `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_fin_index.py` and `_nfe_index.py` decode public Hail indices; `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_align.py` validates exact alleles and frequencies; `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_ld.py` retrieves public signed LD blocks and verifies their numerical properties; `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_susie.R` fits the models and pairwise prior sensitivities. Raw aligned inputs, member lists, diagnostics, pairwise results, index and LD receipts, and `stage6_rbm6_gnomad_window_coverage.R` are retained. Only required remote blocks were retrieved and hashed; the full reference matrices were not checksum-verified. The custom Hail-index decoder was validated against published partition counts and sequential indices but has not been independently verified with Hail. Some reproducible intermediate binaries from older analyses were removed for disk space; final source results and scripts remain.
