# Figure legends

**Figure 1. Association of sleep disclosure with reconstructed frailty and sensitivity analyses.** Points and horizontal bars show survey-weighted regression coefficient estimates and design-t 95% confidence intervals for participants who had versus had not ever told a health professional about sleep trouble. The four blue rows use the same 3,336 participants and adjust for age, sex and cycle; the gray rows are sensitivity analyses of the sleep/fatigue-excluded score, with their own sample sizes and definitions in Supplementary Table `supplement_survey_models.csv`. The additional-covariate model includes race/ethnicity, education, family income-to-poverty ratio and ever smoking; the mood/care-excluded score removes all seven mood and five health/utilization items. Neither the FI nor the exposure is a clinical diagnosis. Horizontal intervals are design-based; analyses are exploratory.

**Figure 2. Score distribution and a linked mortality construct check.** a, Weighted fraction of the common FI sample in 0.05-wide sleep/fatigue-excluded FI bins, stratified by lifetime sleep-disclosure response (n=3,336); distributions are not age-adjusted. b, Weighted proportion recorded deceased through 2019 in the mortality-linkage-eligible, score-observed sample (n=3,389), by exploratory FI cutoff >0.21; points and bars show design-based means and 95% confidence intervals. These unadjusted groups differ in age and other characteristics and follow-up time varies; the threshold is not diagnostic. Public vital status is not perturbed, but CDC has altered certain other public mortality fields; no altered follow-up time or cause is used. This is not external validation.
