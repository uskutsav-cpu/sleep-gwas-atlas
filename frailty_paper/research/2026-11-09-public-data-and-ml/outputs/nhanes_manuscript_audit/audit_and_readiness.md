# Independent audit and manuscript readiness: sleep–frailty project

**Update, 6 November 2026:** A new [CLSA public screen](../clsa_public_followup_20261106/README.md) retrieved all 33 distinct project variants against average grip, maximum grip and four-metre walk time, with exact GRCh38 position/allele-pair/rsID matches. Five of 99 displayed p values were nominally below 0.05; none survived Holm correction across the 99-test family. This exploratory, rounded-summary physical-component screen is now in the review manuscript. It does not provide a sleep/exhaustion-excluded frailty GWAS, independent composite-frailty replication or participant-level score validation. The submission-readiness assessment and highest-impact genetic gaps below therefore remain unresolved.

## Bottom line

The supplied 2011–2014 NHANES reconstruction was located in `outputs/nhanes_manuscript_sensitivity_final.zip` in the separate 29 October project workspace. Its **observational primary estimate is reproducible**, subject to the design, phenotype and inference qualifications below. This audit does not establish a sleep/exhaustion-excluded frailty GWAS, an independent genetic correlation or validation of the project genetic scores. The overall genetics manuscript remains **not ready for a Nature Communications submission**.

## Reconstruction and survey audit

* I extracted the ZIP into a separate audit directory and reran `run_pipeline.sh` using the original project's Python environment and R `survey` 4.5. The run passed the package's internal verification. I then compared its regenerated model table to the original package: all 40 rows, estimates, standard errors, confidence limits, p-values and degrees of freedom agreed exactly at stored precision. These are computational checks using the same data and closely related construction rules, not independent replication or independent validation of each scientific choice.
* I separately checked the archived checksum manifests against the actual ZIP members, before rerunning any scripts: 68 listed CDC source files and two mortality files matched their supplied SHA-256 hashes. These are internal integrity checks, not independently supplied CDC cryptographic signatures. The package's own verifier runs after its reconstruction rewrites the checksum manifest, so its hash check alone would not detect source changes made before that rewrite.
* My independent recomputation from the 49 saved coded items reproduces all four FI versions, component counts, observed-denominator rules and missingness patterns: 49 items require ≥40 observed, 48 require ≥39, and 47 require ≥38. All observed items fall within 0–1. The archived independent rowwise audit reports zero mismatches for 49 reconstructed items, four scores and 196 cycle/source-field checks. That independently implemented code still embodies the investigators' shared operational assumptions; no claim that all clinical mappings are validated follows.
* Checking original CDC XPTs against the analysis frame confirmed age, sex, exposure, weights, strata and PSU values for all 3,632 age-eligible interviewees. Of these, 3,472 have positive MEC weights and 3,336 have sleep disclosure and meet all four FI completeness rules; 969 disclosed sleep trouble. Among the 3,336, the sleep/fatigue-excluded score has 38–47 observed items (median 47); 1,779 have all 47. The two sleep-disclosure groups among positive-weight participants have 2,410/2,473 and 982/997 scorable 47-item scores. The second group excludes two with missing exposure; one of those had a scorable FI.
* `WTMEC2YR/2` matches [CDC guidance for combining two post-2001 NHANES cycles](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/tutorials/weighting.aspx). The full positive-weight design has 19,151 observations, 29 masked strata, 61 PSUs and 32 design degrees of freedom; cycle-specific strata IDs are already disjoint. Domain subsetting occurs after constructing the design. MEC weighting is a defensible choice for an index incorporating examination/laboratory measures, but item-specific nonresponse and 80%-observed scoring still require the reported sensitivity analyses.
* The age-, sex- and cycle-adjusted linear coefficient is 0.069815 FI units (95% CI 0.052779–0.086851, p=1.544922×10⁻⁹; n=3,336), with t inference on 32 design df as stated. The `survey::svyglm` default residual-df convention gives the **same coefficient and SE**, 28 df, CI 0.052683–0.086947 and p=4.42×10⁻⁹. The stated design-df choice is explicit, but its choice should be prespecified and a sensitivity reported; do not conflate it with the default. The more extensively adjusted analysis is a **different complete-covariate sample**, n=3,035, estimate 0.067726 (95% CI 0.051153–0.084299), with race/ethnicity, education, income-to-poverty ratio and smoking added. Covariate choice is not a causal adjustment set.
* The sleep exposure is SLQ050: ever telling a health professional about trouble sleeping, not clinical insomnia or current symptoms. DPQ030 combines trouble sleeping and sleeping too much; DPQ040 is tiredness/little energy. Both are absent from the 47-item outcome, which addresses **direct item overlap for this observed association**, not indirect overlap through symptoms, illnesses, drugs, disability or care-seeking. The common-sample comparison and changing denominators should not be interpreted as a causal mediation/decomposition.
* The adapted score is not the Atkins discovery FI, and this analysis does not compare its genetic effects with the Fried phenotype. The five-component NHANES approximation uses a mobility question rather than timed walking, a different fatigue question, and proxy physical activity; it is not the original Fried instrument. Other decisions are consequential: 2011–2012 visits category 4–9 straddles the chosen cutoff and is treated as missing; corresponding 2013–2014 visit wording differs; urinary leakage and BMI definitions and inferred grip mapping are adaptations; PFQ054-based walking limitations are proxies for skipped answers. The seven mood and five healthcare items can retain indirect overlap. Sensitivity analyses, outcome missingness, observational design, nonresponse and retrospective selection remain important.
* These cohorts and FI analyses overlap the published NHANES reference setting; same-cohort mortality analysis is a construct check, not independent external validation. Age topcoding, voluntary healthcare disclosure, residual confounding, recall, reverse causation, multiple comparisons and variable follow-up limit generalization.

## Genetic score provenance: original inputs rather than reference match alone

I independently scanned the exact project upstream FI (`frailty.txt.gz`, 7,663,022 rows), Jansen UKB insomnia (`Insomnia_sumstats_Jansenetal.txt.gz`, 10,862,567 rows), and article-linked Ye FFS (`Frailty_GWAS_maf0.01_info0.9_hwe1e-6_FUMA.tsv`, 8,883,488 rows) inputs. For **all 37 score rows × three traits**, exactly one row matched rsID, stated chromosome and position, and the signed weight matched after allele-order alignment. FI betas match the file's `effect_allele`; insomnia matches log(OR) with A1 per its saved original CNCR README; the FFS matches its A1 interpretation. [Per-row results](supplement/raw_score_source_audit.csv) and [file hashes and counts](supplement/raw_score_source_receipt.json) are retained. This supplements rather than relies on the package's Ensembl check.

For FI the checked upstream input is byte-identical after decompression to the [GWAS Catalog unharmonised GRCh37 release](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90020001-GCST90021000/GCST90020053/): its computed MD5 `b8956f360155cea10d8f42b31826762e` equals the Catalog's metadata value, and the [author metadata](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90020001-GCST90021000/GCST90020053/GCST90020053_buildGRCh37.tsv-meta.yaml) documents the build and effect-allele direction. Its upstream cohort meta-analysis and the TwinGene strand/sign chain have not been independently reconstructed here. The saved [Jansen README](../../work/nhanes_independent_audit/jansen_readme.txt) identifies A1 as effect allele and BP as GRCh37. The Ye article-linked file's A1 convention has strong supporting evidence from the previously audited 37 COJO lead rows and the BOLT-LMM output convention ([FFS audit](../ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md)), but the exact BOLT-to-distributed-file transformation has not been established for every row. A [later cross-trait study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11069310/) describes Ye's FP summary statistics as GRCh37, which supports the build but is not a checksum-identified specification of this exact distributed file. Confirm the exact Ye release and original candidate-level representations with its depositor before final sign-off.

The score tables' counted alleles are not uniformly the source effect alleles; weights are sign-flipped where listed alleles are reversed. Those arithmetic flips assume the same biallelic contrast. Nine of 15 and 14 of 22 rows have multiallelic entries in the reference inventory; merely observing a pair does not prove source/target contrast equivalence. The 22-marker equal-count score is a distinct model with a different counted-allele convention at eight variants from the 22-marker weighted model. Freeze the actual file and model, and do not silently substitute one for another. No NHANES genotypes or candidate scores were tested.

## Highest-impact work before submission

1. **Sleep/exhaustion-excluded frailty GWAS.** First pursue the existing [Mak et al. modified FI GWAS](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.13868), which excluded eight mental-wellbeing items including insomnia and fatigue. Its full modified-phenotype summary statistics are not identified in the authors' [19-file release](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24591573.v1); see the [source audit and request draft](../mak2023_sleep_excluded_fi_lead.md). Obtain the data and verify sample and model comparability before considering a matched-support genetic-correlation comparison. This broad exclusion cannot isolate the two items. For that specific question, in a sufficiently powered genotyped cohort with appropriate permission, reconstruct a ≥30-deficit FI with explicitly mapped items, fit full and sleep-excluded and sleep-plus-exhaustion-excluded GWAS on the same subjects and covariates, report sample flow/QC, SNP heritability and power. Use matched samples, ancestry-appropriate LD and a paired design for genetic-correlation differences; preserve the original outcome as a comparator. The NHANES observational result supplies neither GWAS. NCDS reports no genomic data charge but is application-based, has age-50 and sample-size limitations, and is not yet approved or demonstrated sufficiently powered. A larger eligible cohort and funding or collaborations may be necessary.
2. **Independent genetic replication and honest score tests.** Lock candidate selection, weights, alleles, missing-variant rules, outcome and overlap exclusions before accessing target associations; use distinct cohorts for tuning and final test. Seek independently ascertained insomnia and FI/FFS GWAS and participant-level genotype–phenotype data. Evaluate cohort-specific prediction and calibration, uncertainty, ancestry, relatedness, coverage, and incremental performance against age/sex/ancestry and appropriate published scores; compare FI and FFS on the same participants where possible. The selected candidates, FinnGen/MVP postselection and UKB overlap limit retrospective replication claims.
3. **Stronger variant-to-gene evidence.** Revisit prioritized loci with complete region coverage, ancestry-matched LD, multiple-signal fine-mapping, sample-overlap assessment and prespecified colocalization priors/sensitivities, including independent eQTL/sQTL where appropriate. FOXP2-region sleep–hospital-frailty coloc is LD-reference-sensitive; nearby FOXP2 is not proven causal. MEIS1-AS3 cerebellum/HFRS comparison yielded no qualifying frailty credible set and cannot establish colocalization. Preserve negative and inconclusive analyses.
4. **Molecular and cellular validation.** Use independently nominated, disease- and cell-context-relevant cell populations and confirm variant-to-gene links with orthogonal perturbation assays, allelic editing or equivalent causal experiments, quantitative expression/splicing and replicate controls. Single-cell enrichment and AlphaGenome predictions are hypothesis-generating, not evidence of patient-level score performance; a nearby or expressed gene alone is insufficient.
5. **Manuscript and methods.** Complete literature screening, clear phenotype and ancestry tables, transparent source and allele provenance, full multiplicity/follow-up accounting, and independent statistical and clinical review. Treat observational, GWAS, local genetic and molecular results as distinct evidence streams. For ML use held-out loci/chromosomes and independent labels, prevent selection leakage and benchmark against simple alternatives; do not present an embedding as validated without a tested external task.

## Readiness classification

| Category | Evidence and status |
|---|---|
| Completed and reproducible | NHANES observational computation, specified survey models, source-file integrity checks, numerical score-source matches, and selected existing summary-statistic analyses; each retains source and phenotype caveats. |
| Exploratory | Alternative NHANES outcomes/subgroups, postselected score projections, regional colocalization, single-cell annotations, AI model and sequence predictions. |
| Unresolved | Availability and sample comparability of Mak's broader eight-item-excluded FI GWAS; a matched GWAS excluding only sleep and fatigue; participant and family overlap across sources; exact multiallelic contrast and complete original FFS release provenance; independent cohort score performance; generalizability; FI instrument choices; locus-to-gene causality. |
| Required for the proposed stronger genetic claims | Adequately powered independent GWAS and replication, defensible phenotype overlap sensitivity, validated independent score testing if prediction is claimed, and orthogonal molecular validation for causal gene/cell claims. |

Specific analyses required by editors cannot be guaranteed; the assessment concerns scientific evidential readiness, not journal acceptance.
