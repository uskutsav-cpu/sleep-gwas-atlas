# Selected variants: FFS source and published score cross-check

The checksum-verified public [PGS005229 scoring file](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS005229/) was compared with the same Ye FFS GWAS and the two project score files. All 15 weighted-prototype markers and all 22 shared-count markers had exactly one FFS source row matching identifier, recorded GRCh37 position and the specified pair of alleles (37 list rows, 33 distinct identifiers). For all 15 weighted-prototype markers, the stored FFS coefficient equaled the source beta after applying the project's A1 effect-allele assumption and orienting it to the chosen counted allele.

Eight of the 15 list rows and nine of the 22 list rows had a unique PGS005229 row at the same identifier and coordinate; these include two repeated variants between lists. For all 17 list rows, the published PGS effect allele equals the source A1 and the listed other allele equals source A2. The other 20 list rows lacked a unique score record at the exact identifier and coordinate in this check; do not interpret that as absence of an effect or a failed genotype call.

This provides a candidate-specific cross-check of the **allele pair and counted allele**, supplementing the [37 COJO lead checks](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md). It does not by itself prove the source beta's effect-allele semantics for each record: PGS005229 was constructed from the same Ye GWAS, and the published score's SBayesR weights are not identical to marginal GWAS betas. It is not independent validation of either prototype, score prediction, direction at unmatched records, or a causal shared variant. Exact rsID/coordinate matching does not rule out multiallelic representations, alternative build mappings, or quality issues in a future cohort.

[Detailed table](stage6_ffs_selected_pgs_compare.csv) and [reproduction script](../work/ffs_selected_pgs_compare.py). The script checks MD5 `168e5c11469c0b27668a4c4d733276b1` for the PGS file and SHA-256 `912e290b2064999c7147cbe3597f87cbcb14b25d387a8b6db2f25862e7274e55` for the local GWAS source. Counted-allele agreement is not the same as effect-weight equality or external predictive performance.

## Candidate-level coefficient-sign consistency

A further check compared the signs of the marginal Ye GWAS beta and the published PGS005229 weight **on the same A1 allele**, limited to the 17 list rows with a unique source match and a unique published-score match at the recorded rsID and coordinate, and matching effect and other alleles. These rows represent **15 distinct variants** because two occur in both project lists. All 15 nonzero published weights have the same sign as the corresponding source betas; none has the opposite sign. [Per-variant output](ffs_selected_pgs_sign_review.csv), [receipt](ffs_selected_pgs_sign_review.json), and `python3 work/ffs_selected_pgs_sign_review.py` reproduce the comparison from the earlier source-match table.

This is an additional **consistency check**, not proof of the marginal beta definition. The published score was derived from the same GWAS and uses SBayesR weights, which account for LD and are not equal to marginal betas. Agreement is therefore neither an independent replication nor an expected mathematical requirement for every variant. It does not resolve the 18 other distinct candidates without a unique score match, multiallelic representation, target genotype coding, the FI weight direction, or clinical prediction. Keep the source-effect direction qualified as inferred until the original transformation or authoritative author documentation establishes it.
