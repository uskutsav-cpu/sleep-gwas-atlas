# Ontario Health Study: relevant cohort, but access charges apply

The [Ontario Health Study researcher page](https://www.ontariohealthstudy.ca/for-researchers/) describes genotype data for more than 20,000 participants, physical-measurement data for over 4,800 assessment-centre participants and an investigator application process. A [published Ontario analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10913570/) constructed an FI in 161,149 adults. Its components explicitly include both average sleep time and trouble sleeping, so any association with sleep is partly overlapping by construction unless a defensible sleep-excluded sensitivity is rebuilt. The [study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10321700/) reports a separate sleep/circadian ancillary study targeting approximately 4,000 participants. These three counts refer to different data sets and cannot establish a genotyped sleep–frailty intersection, candidate coverage, event count, independent cohort or score-validation sample.

The cohort's [official access FAQ](https://www.ontariohealthstudy.ca/for-researchers/) states that access fees are charged on a cost-recovery basis, with fees set annually and a project-specific cost estimate available through a feasibility assessment. This is therefore **not a confirmed free-access route** for the user's project, even though it is scientifically relevant. Eligibility, any fee waiver, linkage, phenotype availability and overlap with discovery cohorts have not been verified. The [public CanPath portal](https://portal.canpath.ca/study/genotype) describes over 55,000 genotyped and imputed participants across multiple cohorts, not a confirmed free alternative or an OHS-specific joint sample. No request or application was sent, and no scores were evaluated.

The FI paper reports that records missing six or more of its 30 deficit items were assigned a missing FI. This is an original-index rule, not an automatically transferable rule after removing sleep items. Rebuilding a sleep-excluded index would require recomputing its observed denominator, setting a missingness policy in advance, and documenting how the change affects inclusion and comparability. The full cohort does not overcome the documented access-fee and linkage uncertainties.
