# Canadian Open Parkinson Network: disease-specific, access-controlled lead

The Canadian Open Parkinson Network (C-OPN) is a potential **disease-specific** sleep/genetics/frailty research lead, not an established general-population validation cohort. Its [research inventory](https://copn-rpco.ca/research/) lists sleep history, fatigue and functional assessments; its [cohort publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11492019/) describes genetic data. A [2025 data-challenge announcement](https://braincanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/COPN-data-challenge-RFA-May-2025.pdf) describes genotyping for roughly 1,300 participants and says data are free for academic C-OPN members. That announcement is not a guarantee of current no-cost access, eligibility, exact variant coverage, or linkage for this project. The network's old research page describes a request and ethics documentation; current application and data-use terms must be verified. No request was submitted.

A [Parkinson Canada announcement](https://www.parkinson.ca/c-opn-data-challenge-awardees/) lists a project to develop a frailty index for C-OPN. A funded or selected proposal is not evidence that a completed FI, its items, or an insomnia–FI–genotype intersection is available. The network enrolls people with Parkinson's disease or atypical parkinsonism as well as healthy participants; the size and genetic/phenotype coverage in each group are unknown. Sleep history is not automatically an insomnia diagnosis, Parkinson-related movement and medications can affect sleep and frailty measures, and a disease-enriched analysis would not independently validate performance in an unselected aging population.

Before prioritizing, verify scope of permissible research and whether a Parkinson-focused question is required; current academic-member fees and all costs; individual-level genome-wide genotype content and imputation; specific sleep and frailty instruments; sample and event counts by diagnosis; and permission to link records. Identify overlap with discovery studies and adjust carefully for disease ascertainment and medication. There is no basis to treat the approximate genotyped total as the linked validation sample, or an unrelated Parkinson-specific score as our project score. No participant data were analyzed.

## Sleep-item and access-policy follow-up

The publicly linked [2020 epidemiological questionnaire](https://copn-rpco.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/EpidemiologicalQuestionnaire_COPN.pdf) has separate yes/no questions on difficulty falling asleep and difficulty staying asleep, plus sleepiness and acting out dreams. It also asks about an urge to move the legs, potentially useful as a prespecified restless-legs sensitivity alongside the MEIS1 candidates. These are symptom questions, not established insomnia or restless-legs diagnoses; their frequency, chronicity, impairment and exact timing are not specified in the displayed questions. The form is linked from a page labeled “OLD,” so its use, coding and completeness in any current release and in controls must be checked.

The network's [data-sharing policy](https://copn-rpco.ca/privacy-policy-2/) describes international academic membership as well as Canadian membership, and says member requests are free but may be subject to cost recovery when extra resources are needed. It requires a project consistent with network objectives and a data-use agreement and ethics oversight. The old questionnaire-access page is not itself proof that sleep and genotype files can be joined under those terms; current project-specific approval and total costs remain unresolved.

The 2025 data-challenge material describes its approximately 1,300 genotyped participants specifically in terms of Parkinson's gene variants; it does not establish that our 33 variants are included. The related [Quebec Parkinson Network publication](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JPD-191775) describes genome-wide array genotyping and targeted sequencing in its own sample, but this does not by itself establish that those files, records or all project alleles are released through C-OPN for our use. Do not equate a genotyped individual count with usable genotype coverage for these scores.
