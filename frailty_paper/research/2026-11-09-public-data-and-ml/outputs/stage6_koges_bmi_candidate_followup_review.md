# Korean BMI follow-up of insomnia–frailty candidate variants

Checked 29 October 2026. A live [KoGES PheWeb](https://koges.leelabsg.org/about) describes the same 72,298-person Korean study as [Nam et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2022.100189). Its 2022 release is separate from the later, currently inaccessible Open KoGES PheWeb discussed in the [earlier inventory](stage6_koges_open_gwas_inventory_review.md). The 2022 browser states that positions are GRCh37 and beta is for the ALT allele, and that the SAIGE analyses adjust for relatedness, sex, age, ten genetic principal components, cohort and examination year. The published Table S1 lists 72,282 participants for BMI and reports BMI in kg/m²; The [published methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9903843/) state that quantitative traits underwent rank-based inverse normal transformation before SAIGE analysis. The displayed BMI betas therefore concern the transformed phenotype and are not changes in kg/m². The exact order of transformation and covariate adjustment should be checked against the analysis code before attempting further interpretation.

We queried the union of 33 variants from the 15- and 22-variant prototypes for the browser's `KoGES_BMI` result, explicitly testing both orientations of each listed allele pair at the project's GRCh37 coordinates. We obtained exact coordinate and pair matches for 24 variants. Nine had no matching BMI record in the queried representations; this is not evidence of no effect, and different allele representations, frequency or quality filters could contribute. Association p values and effects are browser-displayed rounded values, not the underlying full precision. Source rows are saved in [the result table](stage6_koges_bmi_candidate_followup.csv), with a [receipt](stage6_koges_bmi_candidate_followup_receipt.json) and [raw query responses](../work/koges/bmi_candidate_raw.json). Reproduce with `/usr/bin/python3 work/koges/query_bmi_candidates.py`; the browser is a live resource and results can change.

Seven of the 24 matched variants have nominal BMI p < 0.05. Using Benjamini–Hochberg across the full 33-candidate family, with each missing variant conservatively assigned p=1, four have q < 0.05 (computed from rounded displayed p values):

| Candidate | KoGES ALT | ALT beta | BMI p | BH q over 33 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rs35881094 | G | -0.023 | 0.000012 | 0.000396 |
| rs17550821 | A | +0.074 | 0.00024 | 0.00264 |
| rs6452792 | A | +0.021 | 0.00024 | 0.00264 |
| rs28528997 | G | -0.022 | 0.0046 | 0.03795 |

These are *individual-variant BMI associations*, chosen after the insomnia/frailty candidates had already been selected. BMI was chosen as an available metabolic phenotype for exploratory follow-up, not as a prespecified independent validation endpoint; this family correction does not account for previous project-wide trait exploration. rs35881094 and rs17550821 are approximately 56 kb apart on chromosome 2 and could tag the same association; they cannot be counted as independent signals without a Korean-ancestry LD and conditional analysis. The exact pairs are particularly important because some rsIDs are multiallelic. The sign is relative to the browser ALT allele, which is not necessarily the allele counted in either score; the effect direction cannot be compared without harmonization. In addition, the source-study phenotype overlap with all score-discovery datasets has not been fully established.

BMI is neither insomnia nor FI nor FFS, and these marginal associations do not establish a causal gene, a shared mechanism, a gene-by-sleep interaction, or predictive performance of a score. The source has aggregate results only; no individual genotypes or phenotypes were accessed. FOXP2-region rs12536335 had a matched BMI p of 0.60, which is not proof of no effect and should not be contrasted with significance in another trait as proof that effects differ. A true score validation still requires linked participant-level genotypes and appropriate outcomes in an independent cohort.

A later [score-allele and LD follow-up](stage6_koges_bmi_allele_ld_followup.md) harmonizes the four displayed BMI betas to the prototype counted alleles. It also finds low pairwise correlation (r² ≈ 0.023) for the two nearby chromosome 2 variants in a 504-person East Asian reference panel. That external panel is not Korean-specific and does not substitute for a KoGES conditional analysis.
