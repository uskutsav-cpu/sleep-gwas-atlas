# MAD1L1 candidate and a published HRS–ELSA FI signal

An expanded [screen of all 32 selected published HRS rows](stage6_hrs_selected_lead_proxy_screen_review.md) found six nearby rows around this candidate, including this proxy. They are correlated and do not constitute six independent confirmations.

The 22-marker exploratory count contains rs28528997. In the 32 selected HRS FI rows published by [Mekli et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0207824), rs62444881 lies 21,804 bases away (GRCh37). The two have r² = 0.922 in 503 European-ancestry 1000 Genomes phase 3 reference individuals. This is a reference-panel observation, not HRS study LD. The published association is for a different SNP; it is not a direct test of the count or of rs28528997.

The HRS FI analysis reported beta −0.01092 for its stated A1 allele (p = 8.0 × 10⁻⁶, N = 8,174); the corresponding ELSA beta was −0.004092 (p = 0.1449, N = 5,239). Both traits were square-root-transformed FIs. The HRS locus was selected from the study's reported results, its p-value is not conventionally genome-wide significant, and the ELSA result provides no statistically clear replication. Comparing significance levels does not establish that effects differ between the cohorts. The magnitude is on the transformed FI scale, not an individual risk or clinical effect.

In the reference VCF, the candidate is A/G and the HRS lead is C/T. Candidate A and lead T dosage correlate r = −0.960. The source table reports A1 = A for the lead but does not provide its other allele; T is complementary to A, yet we have not verified the source strand and complete allele pair. Therefore the association's direction cannot be reliably assigned to the candidate's counted A allele. Other reference sources list an additional allele at the candidate site, so allele representation must be checked in each dataset. MAD1L1 is a positional label, not proof that this gene mediates an effect.

The HRS and ELSA names identify separate cohorts, but their complete participant and relatedness overlap with the project's discovery sources has not been established. This candidate was assessed after examining selected public associations. The comparison does not independently validate the 22-marker count, the 15-marker weighted score, a shared insomnia–frailty mechanism, or individual outcome prediction. A stronger test needs publicly accessible independent individual-level genotypes and outcomes, or full independent summary statistics plus suitable matched LD for a clearly specified association analysis; even a summary-based association would not establish individual score calibration or discrimination.

The selected source table and source audit are `stage6_mekli2018_published_fi_leads.csv` and `stage6_mekli2018_source_audit.json`. Neither the entire remote reference VCF nor its index was independently checksum-verified in this check.
