# Korean frailty GWAS: potential external follow-up

[Kwak et al. (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-026-02154-6) studied frailty in 14,664 Korean participants from KoGES and KNHANES using three phenotype definitions: a FRAIL scale, a Cardiovascular Health Study (CHS) physical-frailty index, and a frailty index (FI). This is a potentially valuable different-ancestry and different-cohort source for the project, but it is **not yet an evaluable validation of the 15- or 22-marker scores**. The specific FI and CHS definitions are not identical to the Atkins and Ye phenotypes, so any future analysis would be cross-phenotype transportability rather than exact replication. Cohort non-overlap with all insomnia and frailty sources must be verified rather than assumed.

The publisher's [Supplementary Data workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-026-02154-6/MediaObjects/11357_2026_2154_MOESM1_ESM.xlsx), sheets SData2–SData4, reports three sets of comparisons for **14 distinct preselected GWAS Catalog rsIDs**. All three sheets list the same 14 IDs. None is an exact rsID match for the original 15 prototypes, expanded 22 candidates, or the 124 published Song candidates. These tables are selected subsets, not the full genome-wide results: an absent candidate is **not an association failure, a zero effect, or evidence that it was unmeasured**. A subsequent focused proxy check found that one nearby candidate is only moderately linked in an East Asian reference panel; see [NCAM1 proxy review](stage6_korean_ncam1_proxy_review.md). No equivalence is inferred. `kwak2026_supplementary_candidate_inventory.csv` transcribes the three sheets' relevant source entries and overlap flags.

The [supplementary phenotype tables](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-026-02154-6/MediaObjects/11357_2026_2154_MOESM2_ESM.docx) report N=14,165 for the FRAIL scale, N=9,504 for the CHS index, and N=1,421 for the FI in their characteristics tables. Those are outcome-specific sample counts, not three independent samples to add together, and do not by themselves establish the exact sample size at every variant. The FI item list has no explicit insomnia question, but it includes recent fatigue and an energy question. It is therefore **not sleep-and-fatigue-free**. The CHS exhaustion item differs between source cohorts: the supplement uses an energy question in KoGES and a self-reported stress question in KNHANES. The FRAIL fatigue component similarly differs. These differences matter when comparing it with the Fried-phenotype GWAS and when interpreting meta-analysis effects. Broad questionnaire items may also capture sleep-related burden indirectly.

The article's abstract lists eight suggestive GWAS SNPs at p<10⁻⁶, not necessarily genome-wide significance at 5×10⁻⁸. Their names and gene mappings do not establish insomnia associations or a validated score. The article's [data availability statement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-026-02154-6) says individual-level data require cohort approval and study-generated data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request; it does not establish that full signed per-variant results are publicly downloadable. This review found no individual-level data and did not calculate an external replication, score calibration or predictive performance.

## Concrete data request, prepared but not sent

Request from the corresponding investigator full genome-wide signed summary statistics separately for the FI, CHS, and FRAIL phenotypes, including cohort-specific and meta-analysis estimates, genome build, effect and other alleles, beta and SE, allele frequency, p-value, per-variant N, imputation/QC and age/sex/covariate model. Ask whether the 15 and 22 candidate allele pairs are present, for cohort overlap/relatedness information, and for score/phenotype construction code. Ask whether the FI can be recomputed in the same participants excluding fatigue and energy items, together with an insomnia measure and appropriate permissions. A separate request for independent genotype-and-outcome data is needed to evaluate individual score performance. A study response and access approvals are required; no message has been sent.

## Individual-level sleep and genomic-data lead within the KoGES family

A separate [2011 KARE/KoGES study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3071826/) jointly examined questionnaire-reported insomnia and genome-wide genotypes. Its methods report 10,038 initially genotyped individuals, then 8,842 people and 81,055 SNPs after that particular study's QC; the reported insomnia and control counts refer to that analysis, not the 2026 frailty sample. Its insomnia questions addressed onset, middle and terminal symptoms, but the measurement and threshold must be verified before comparison with our discovery phenotype. Separately, [Kwak et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02154-6) derived frailty phenotypes using KoGES and KNHANES. KoGES comprises multiple cohorts and waves: the existence of each study does **not** establish that they used the same participants or that their sleep, frailty and genotypes can be linked. The earlier 81,055-marker post-QC set is not evidence that our variants are present or absent in any current release.

A [2026 KoGES cohort profile](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/55/5/dyag192/8812758) describes epidemiological and genomic resources distributed after proposal review and IRB approval through the National Biobank of Korea; it describes an international collaboration/data-access route but does not establish eligibility or cost for this project. Individual data are not an anonymous download. The 2026 frailty paper itself says individual KoGES and KNHANES data require application and approval. No request, contact, data download or agreement was made.

A useful future feasibility check would identify the exact KoGES subcohort and waves used in both studies, establish allowed linkage, the joint usable insomnia/frailty/genotype sample and events, any fees and investigator eligibility, candidate marker quality/allele contrast, ancestry/relatedness and overlap with our selection sources. The project [FI-item audit above](korean_frailty_gwas_validation_feasibility.md) notes fatigue and energy content, so a sleep-and-fatigue-excluded sensitivity would need item-level data and a documented new definition. Even with permission, this would be external phenotype and population transportability, not evidence that a European-derived score predicts frailty in this cohort. No project-score evaluation was performed.

## 2011 insomnia article: limited public SNP screen (29 October 2026)

The accessible [Ban et al. article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0018455) names three insomnia-associated SNP examples with p-values in its main text: rs11208305 (ROR1, p=5.6×10⁻⁶), rs718712 (PLCB1, p=8.5×10⁻⁶), and rs7552384 (ROR1 promoter, p=7.4×10⁻⁴). Comparing these exact rsIDs with the local 15- and 22-marker files finds **no matches**; see [the three-row inventory](kare_2011_article_named_snp_screen.csv). This deliberately small text-based screen is neither the complete set of SNPs in the paper nor the 3,354 variants in its supplementary table. The named associations are exploratory and do not independently validate any score or establish causal genes. Their nonmatches do not imply that any of our variants were unmeasured or unassociated; no LD proxies were tested and no alleles or directions were harmonized.

The article describes a Table S1 restricted to p<0.005. Attempts to download that PDF directly returned access errors; attempts to retrieve the Europe PMC supplementary archive timed out before a complete archive was received. Even if obtained, a screen of that table would be selected by p-value, and lack of a row could not establish a null effect. The paper also gives a historical site for full association results, but a working public source has not been verified. A complete, signed and harmonized insomnia summary file could support per-variant external follow-up, whereas individual-level genotype plus sleep and frailty outcomes would still be needed for our score's predictive evaluation.
