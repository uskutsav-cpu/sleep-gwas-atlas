# All of Us AFR-group difficulty-walking sensitivity

29 October 2026. The public All by All EUR follow-up previously found a nominal association of rs12536335 with both insomnia and difficulty walking, but no 33-candidate BH-adjusted joint result. We queried all 33 project candidates for difficulty walking (`NS_325.2`) in the AFR genetic-similarity group and joined them to the existing AFR insomnia results by exact GRCh38 position, REF and ALT. All 33 association rows were returned. Source metadata lists 1,344 walking cases and 58,004 controls in AFR, compared with 4,002 and 170,048 in EUR; these are group-level, not variant-specific counts.

No candidate had unadjusted p < 0.05 for both AFR insomnia and walking; the smallest maximum p was 0.154, with no q < 0.05 across 33 postselected candidates. For rs12536335, AFR insomnia p = 0.364 and walking p = 0.291. These p-values do not demonstrate absence of an effect. The max-p test is an intersection–union test against at least one null; BH adjustment depends on the joint dependence structure and does not cover the project's wider searches.

Using 661 1000 Genomes AFR-labeled reference participants and the established conditional weighted-Z approximation, the seven score-weight constructions had Z values from −0.255 to 0.537 and nominal two-sided p values from 0.592 to 0.990. These results are **not score regressions in individuals**, and the wide uncertainty does not establish that scores cannot predict walking or frailty. Different sample size, phenotype ascertainment, allele frequencies, LD and noise can affect results. There was no formal test of differences between EUR and AFR, so do not infer differences from significance thresholds across the groups. The AFR genetic-similarity classification is not interchangeable with race and is not homogeneous.

Difficulty walking is an EHR code, not measured gait speed or an FI or Fried frailty score. Candidate selection and analyses were post hoc, and target versus discovery participant or family overlap remains unverified. The source ALT-effect coding, biallelic dosage contrasts, genotype-frequency HWE variance, common effective sample size, representative reference LD and FFS-weight direction remain assumptions; some loci have multiallelic representations. `SE × HWE genotype SD` had CV 1.75% for the 15 and 1.55% for the 22 variants; this does not prove calibration. None of this tests score discrimination or calibration.
