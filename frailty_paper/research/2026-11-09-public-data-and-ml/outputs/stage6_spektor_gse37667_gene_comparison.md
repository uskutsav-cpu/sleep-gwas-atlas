# Exploratory insomnia-expression panel in a paired wakefulness study

We used the same 244 adjusted-significant gene symbols from [Spektor et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27062771) as in the [older-adult PBMC follow-up](stage6_spektor_gse208668_gene_comparison.md), and looked for them in public [GSE37667](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE37667). This is a post hoc comparison of different phenomena, **not** a replication of chronic insomnia or a genetic-score validation.

The GSE dataset measured whole-blood expression at baseline, after prolonged wakefulness and after recovery in nine male volunteers. For each source gene, we used an annotation row mapping to exactly that symbol and selected the probe with highest mean across all 27 samples without using group differences. Of 244 gene names, **226** had an eligible probe. We tested the nine within-volunteer wakefulness-minus-baseline differences using paired t tests and applied BH across those 226 tests. **None passed BH q <0.05**. This small study cannot rule out an effect; the genes and probes have varying detection, the selected probe has not been independently validated, and the list was selected in a separate observational analysis.

The estimated direction was the same as the Spektor whole-blood middle-insomnia estimate for 32 of the 226 matched symbols. This descriptive sign count is not a formal test of genetic or expression heterogeneity: the studies differ in chronic symptoms versus acute wakefulness, participants, processing and array versus RNA-seq measurement. The wakefulness study lacks a randomized parallel time control, so intervention effects cannot be separated from time and other study effects. The nine people are not 27 independent samples. Donor independence across the two studies has not been formally checked.

This follow-up contrasts with the large number of nominally consistent genes in the [GSE208668 PBMC comparison](stage6_spektor_gse208668_gene_comparison.md), but the latter's insomnia status is perfectly confounded with recruitment source. Neither comparison identifies a causal pathway, rules out an effect, or validates either research score.
