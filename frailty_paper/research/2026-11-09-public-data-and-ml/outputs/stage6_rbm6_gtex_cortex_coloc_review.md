# RBM6 cortex expression: an incomplete-support colocalization check

## Interpretation

A GTEx v8 bulk-cortex RBM6 eQTL comparison gives candidate pairwise overlap with an insomnia signal and an FI signal, but **the analysis misses the highest-probability variant in the source's published RBM6 expression credible set**. This materially limits the colocalization result and means it should not be used as independent validation of RBM6, a causal mechanism, or the proposed risk scores. The risk-score question still requires participant-level outcomes and genotypes from a suitable independent cohort, or a clearly defined alternative validation design.

The eQTL Catalogue's published cortex RBM6 SuSiE set contains 49 unique variants (52 rows include four aliases for one indel). Only 38 were present in our 947-variant coloc input; their published PIPs sum to 0.375 out of 0.953 across the reported set. The top variant, rs10049477 (GRCh38 chr3:50085532 T>C), has published PIP 0.503 but is absent from our input. It is present in the cached Mak FI and Ye FFS summaries but absent by position and rsID from the cached Jansen insomnia, FinnGen insomnia and FinnGen HFRS regional files; the five-study intersection therefore excludes it. The caches may themselves have upstream restrictions, so this does not establish absence from every original source. These are sums of reported PIPs in one published set, not a calibrated estimate of total causal-variant coverage across all possible signals. The published fit and our fit use different input variants and modeling/LD choices, so their credible sets need not be identical. Still, the missing high-PIP candidate is a concrete reason to treat our posterior as fragile.

## Results and phenotype comparison

At p1=p2=0.0001 and p12=0.00001, the fitted cortex eQTL signal and one Jansen insomnia signal yielded PP.H4=0.922 and PP.H3=0.077; the cortex signal and one Mak FI signal yielded PP.H4=0.938 and PP.H3=0.062. When p12 varied from 0.000001 to 0.00005, PP.H4 ranged 0.543–0.983 and 0.601–0.987, respectively. These are **separate pairwise results**, conditional on the chosen variants, reference LD, priors and model; they are not a joint three-trait probability. Other insomnia and FI signal pairs favored distinct variants in this fit.

The cortex eQTL versus the reported Ye Fried frailty score signal yielded PP.H4=0.0038 and PP.H3=0.996 at the same illustrative prior. The FinnGen hospital frailty fit returned no credible set, so no eQTL–HFRS signal-pair posterior was calculated. Neither observation proves that these phenotypes have no shared biology with insomnia. The UK Biobank-based insomnia and FI studies have overlapping participants, and the FI includes an insomnia item; their agreement is not independent phenotype replication. No result identifies a causal effect direction or establishes RBM6 as the effector gene.

## Coverage and tissue interpretation

The 947 matched variants comprise 91.6% of the earlier 1,034-variant five-GWAS/reference intersection. They include all 89 variants in the earlier Jansen insomnia credible set led by rs7428430 and all 96 variants in the earlier Mak FI set led by rs2353586, retaining 100% of the component mass *within those two earlier fitted sets*. This improves on the earlier SingleBrain excitatory-neuron intersection, which retained about 25% and 30% of those respective sets. It does not address variants omitted from the original intersection, the excluded earlier wider-window insomnia signal, or the missing published cortex eQTL variant described above.

Among 501 exact allele-aligned variants shared with the SingleBrain excitatory-neuron analysis, signed eQTL Z scores have a descriptive correlation of 0.943. The variants are correlated by LD, so this is not a test with 501 independent observations. The resources were generated separately but donor overlap has not been verified, and both comparisons reuse the same disease GWAS. Bulk cortex combines cell types; agreement with a neuron dataset does not establish that excitatory neurons mediate the association or exclude other cell types.

## Methods and files

The public [eQTL Catalogue GTEx v8 cortex dataset QTD000171](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/eqtl/Data_access/) reports 205 samples; RBM6 variant allele numbers in the matched data range 402–410. The source ALT allele is the effect allele. Exact position and allele-pair matching, duplicate validation and a maximum 0.20 allele-frequency difference filter against gnomAD NFE retained 947 variants; the observed maximum difference was 0.0618. We used signed gnomAD NFE LD for cortex, insomnia, FI and FFS, and FIN LD for HFRS. SuSiE-RSS used L=10, fixed residual variance and 95% credible sets; all fitted models converged. The cortex fit reported one 59-variant set with maximum PIP 0.0418; these are model-dependent, not causal assignments. Working sample sizes and diagnostics are in `stage6_rbm6_gtex_cortex_susie_diagnostics.csv`.

The region and input are restricted; the custom gnomAD Hail-index decoder was checked against partition counts and sequential indices but not independently verified using Hail. The indexed expression extract and published credible-set file have locally recorded hashes but were not checked against publisher checksums; the full remote expression file was not downloaded or verified. The published credible sets were obtained from [the eQTL Catalogue source file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/eQTL/susie/QTS000015/QTD000171/QTD000171.credible_sets.tsv.gz).

Reproduction: `stage6_rbm6_gtex_cortex_extract.py` and `_prepare.py` retrieve and harmonize the regional data; `_coloc.R` fits the models; `_coverage.R` checks earlier disease-set retention; `_external_coverage.py` audits the downloaded published cortex set and cross-resource signed Z correlation. Their associated CSV/TSV files and receipts retain the intermediate evidence. The external coverage script expects the downloaded public credible-set file in `work/` and the existing aligned inputs.

A direct position-and-allele-pair feasibility screen using the cached Mak FI and Ye FFS regional inputs recovers rs10049477 and 44 of the 49 unique published expression-set variants for each trait. Their published PIPs sum to 0.917 out of 0.953 in that set. The screens contain 1,016 FI and 1,022 FFS exact matches before reference-LD and frequency QC; `stage6_rbm6_gtex_direct_pair_feasibility.py` and its receipt preserve the counts. These are not colocalization results and do not establish that the variant can be included in an appropriate reference matrix. The cached files themselves may have upstream restrictions.

The next useful analysis is to match each direct pair to an appropriate LD reference, align alleles and check frequencies, then repeat fine-mapping and prior sensitivity if coverage is adequate. Compare results across sources and windows, then pursue independent patient-outcome validation if a genuinely accessible cohort is identified. Do not replace unavailable outcomes with molecular association evidence.
