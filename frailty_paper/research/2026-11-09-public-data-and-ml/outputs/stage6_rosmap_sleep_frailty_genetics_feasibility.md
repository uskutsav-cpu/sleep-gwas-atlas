# ROS/MAP: possible linked sleep–frailty–genetics validation cohort

**Public codebook follow-up:** The [MAP baseline interview](https://www.radc.rush.edu/docs/dictionary/show.htm?formno=mp2int) names `q1slp` (trouble falling asleep), `q2slp` (night waking), `q1md` (effort), `q10md` (could not get going) and `q2md` (restless sleep). The latter must not enter a purportedly sleep-excluded FI through an aggregate depression score. These baseline form fields are not necessarily the separate sleep-substudy fields used in the availability flag; their genotype overlap and follow-up aliases remain unverified. Details added to the [request draft](rosmap_data_request_draft.md).

**Standard outcome lead:** RADC documents longitudinal [`frailty_z`](https://www.radc.rush.edu/docs/var/detail.htm?category=Frailty&variable=frailty_z), a four-domain standardized grip, timed-walk, BMI and fatigue composite across ROS, MAP and other cohorts. Its fatigue domain uses `q1md` and `q10md`; it is not the published 41-deficit FI or the original five-component Fried phenotype. Requesting it and its source components could enable a separately designed, fatigue-excluded three-domain sensitivity, contingent on authorized data, original scaling and complete-case checks. This would be a new phenotype, not proof of a sleep-excluded genetic correlation or an available individual-level score test.

Checked 28 October 2026. The Religious Orders Study and Rush Memory and Aging Project (ROS/MAP) are a promising aging-cohort lead with phenotypic and genome-wide genetic data, but **not a confirmed freely downloadable, linked validation dataset**. A [published MAP study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11062500/) constructed a deficit-accumulation frailty index; its particular analysis included 456 selected deceased participants with autopsy data, which must not be used as the number eligible for our project. A [separate ROS/MAP genetic analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11262744/) reports 2,067 genotyped participants across two array batches. These numbers refer to different analyses and cannot be combined to estimate a joint analytic sample.

RADC's [data availability tool](https://www.radc.rush.edu/reports/dataAvailabilityCriteria.htm) lists actigraphy, a sleep questionnaire, two GWAS genotyping platforms and WGS as available-data categories. Its [cohort profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380522/) describes activity, sleep and circadian data derived from wearable measurements. The checked sources do not show how many participants have all required genotype, frailty and insomnia measurements, or whether sleep and FI were measured at the desired time. Actigraphy is not an insomnia diagnosis. Insomnia, sleep and fatigue items in a proposed FI need a specific item-level audit before modeling.

**Access and cost.** RADC describes a [resource request](https://www.radc.rush.edu/requests/data.htm) requiring an account, committee review, and an institutional data-use agreement. Its [transfer page](https://www.radc.rush.edu/requests/additionalForms.htm) describes institutional and project-specific agreements and publication review terms. [Genomic data are also described](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11262744/) as available through Synapse, with access restrictions that must be checked for each asset. The request page checked does not establish the fee for this specific linked-data request; a third-party listing saying no fee or dollar value is not proof that access or delivery is free. This is controlled/requested research access, not an unrestricted public download. No application, data access or linkage was attempted.

Before any score evaluation, verify exact 15/22-marker build, alleles and imputation coverage; eligibility, direct genotype–outcome linkage and permissible use; cohort and relative overlap with each discovery and selection source; independent timepoints, outcome definitions and events; ancestry, relatedness, QC and genotyping batch; and actual cost. ROS/MAP volunteers have specialized recruitment and enrichment that affect generalizability. An approved data custodian must determine whether the data can be linked and used for the planned research. If these checks pass, one could compare fixed scores against a baseline model in a held-out sample, including prespecified sleep-excluded FI sensitivity. Published studies of other scores are not validation of the project prototypes.

## Public aggregate overlap check

On 28 October 2026, RADC's [Data Availability Report](https://www.radc.rush.edu/reports/dataAvailabilityCriteria.htm) returned the following **all-selected-criteria** rows for MAP, where genotyping means at least one of GWAS-Affymetrix, GWAS-Illumina or whole-genome sequencing. The categories were explicitly combined with OR within genotyping and AND within the two-sleep-substudy query.

| Sleep data category | MAP participants also marked as having at least one listed genetic dataset |
|---|---:|
| Sleep questionnaire | 401 |
| Actigraphy | 922 |
| Both questionnaire and actigraphy | 398 |

The three counts overlap and must not be added. They describe availability flags, not a confirmed count with nonmissing insomnia outcomes, usable FI, study-time alignment, adequate variant dosages, consent for linkage, or ancestry/relatedness QC. The approximately 400 questionnaire-plus-genetics records may also be small for score validation and their event count is unknown. The online tool is dynamic; counts can change. The numbers do not establish that any data can be accessed for free or that the project scores are validated.

Reproduce the aggregate query with `python3 work/radc_availability_query.py`; results were checked twice and saved to [the count table](stage6_radc_public_overlap_counts.csv). The script submits the site's public criteria form, checks that the first result row has all three selected criteria flagged, and saves only aggregate counts. The three downloaded result snapshots are `work/radc_sleep_genotyping_result.html`, `work/radc_actigraphy_genotyping_result.html` and `work/radc_both_sleep_genotyping_result.html`. This was a read-only public report, not a data-access application.

A [published 41-deficit MAP FI item audit](stage6_map_fi_sleep_item_definition_review.md) finds no explicitly named sleep item in that particular index, but two fatigue items; it is a promising phenotype definition requiring item-level coding and timing checks. MAP also has a published 32-item sleep-health questionnaire, without a confirmed clinical-insomnia case definition in the availability intersection.

## Genotyping platform and imputation follow-up

The [RADC omics overview](https://www.radc.rush.edu/docs/omics/overview.htm) describes Affymetrix and Illumina Omniquad Express genotyping, imputation of approximately seven million variants against 1000 Genomes, and a separate WGS release. These are cohort-wide descriptions, not proof that every selected variant or individual passed QC or can be linked to the sleep/FI data. Reference membership and approximate variant count cannot replace checking the actual allele-specific genotype/imputation record and quality metrics. Source-specific build, strand, multiallelic representation, batch, missingness and variant identifiers must be verified before running either prototype.

The public RADC report separately flags **203** MAP participants with a sleep questionnaire and Affymetrix genotype data, **159** with a questionnaire and Illumina data, and **103** with a questionnaire and WGS. These are intersecting categories, not independent samples; they must not be added. The previously reported count of **401** is the direct OR query for any of those three categories, not their sum. Neither these flags nor the reported cohort-wide imputation count establishes which score markers are usable or confirms that corresponding FI data are available. The updated `work/radc_availability_query.py` reproduces the three per-platform queries as well as the earlier OR and sleep-measure combinations.
