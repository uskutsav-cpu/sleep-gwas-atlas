# Published TPMI insomnia score: metadata conflict

[PGS Catalog PGS018978](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS018978/) labels its score `TPMI_327.41_MegaPRS`, predicts “organic or persistent insomnia,” and lists 23,384 variants on GRCh38. It reports an evaluation of 17,271 TPMI participants, including 1,195 cases, with AUROC 0.63887 and covariates sex, age, array and ten PCs. This is a different score from either project prototype; even a correctly labeled evaluation would not validate them. The listed AUROC should not be interpreted as the score's standalone or incremental AUC without checking the original analysis definition.

There is a consequential inconsistency: the catalog's source-GWAS line lists 2,127 cases and 289,037 controls, and its training phenotype codes are `327.5, 333.94, 780.58, G25.81, G47.6`. The [TPMI phenotype table](https://pheweb.ibms.sinica.edu.tw/phenotypes) assigns precisely those codes and that case/control count to phecode 327.7, sleep-related movement disorders. Its 327.41 organic/persistent insomnia entry instead lists 16,010 cases and 287,785 controls in the GWAS browser and different case codes. Meanwhile the PGS Catalog's *evaluation* codes match the insomnia definition. Related catalog pages [PGS018976](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS018976/) and [PGS018979](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS018979/) display the same source-GWAS case count.

The authors' [supplementary tables](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09350-y/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9350_MOESM3_ESM.xlsx) clarify the counts. Table S1 lists 16,010 insomnia cases in the GWAS set and 4,607 in training; the catalog's 2,127 GWAS and 546 training cases match the table's 327.7 movement-disorder row. The catalog evaluation count (1,195 cases, 16,076 controls) agrees with S1's insomnia row. This establishes the inconsistency in the catalog's displayed development metadata, but not whether the actual scoring file has correct weights; the scoring-file download timed out and was not inspected.

Table S13 reports MegaPRS insomnia **score-only AUC 0.5276** (SE 0.0088), while **0.6389** (SE 0.0089) is for a model including the score, sex, age, array and PCs. The latter matches the catalog's AUROC and should not be presented as performance of the score by itself or its incremental gain. These are authors' held-out TPMI results for a separate published model, not tests of either project prototype. The [local extraction](stage6_tpmi_supplement_insomnia_audit.json) records the source hash and corresponding S1 and model-table rows; run `python3 outputs/stage6_tpmi_supplement_insomnia_audit.py` to reproduce it from the downloaded workbook.

[Chen et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12675292/) describe dividing 100,000 unrelated TPMI participants into training and validation subsets for their published PRS models, separate from the larger GWAS set. This is useful background on an established study design, but does not provide public participant-level genotype/outcome data or test our project scores. This catalog discrepancy also does not verify the effect allele in TPMI PheWeb's GWAS results, because model weights and marginal GWAS effects are distinct quantities.

TPMI requests acknowledgement and citation of [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09350-y) and [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09680-x).
