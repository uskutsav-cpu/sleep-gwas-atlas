# Cardiovascular Health Study: possible genetics–sleep–frailty evaluation

Checked 29 October 2026. This is a **controlled-access lead**, not an available independent validation result. Its project-specific cost, permitted linkages and sample size are unverified.

The Cardiovascular Health Study (CHS) recruited adults aged at least 65 and is the source cohort for the widely used Fried physical frailty phenotype. Its [dbGaP candidate-genetics study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000377.v6.p1) directs requests for individual phenotype and molecular data to parent study **phs000287**. Its [TOPMed sequencing listing](https://ncpi-data.org/studies/phs001368) reports 4,877 subjects and identifies phs000287 for comprehensive phenotypes. That count is not a sleep–frailty–genotype intersection; data-use restrictions vary by consent.

The [Sleep Heart Health Study (SHHS) dbGaP record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003637.v2.p1) identifies three CHS sites as parent cohorts and describes sleep-habits questionnaires and polysomnography. SHHS is primarily a sleep-disordered-breathing study; its overall count is not the number of CHS participants with all variables. The [CHS public data dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/chs/data_dictionary/CHS_2025a.pdf?link_time=2026-02-09_15%3A21%3A37.334581) lists restless sleep, grip strength, gait speed and exhaustion-related questionnaire items. These labels do not establish that the items were measured in the same visit or suffice to reproduce all Fried components, nor do they establish a diagnosed insomnia phenotype or usable participant linkage.

CHS's own sleep questions may provide a broader route than restricting analysis to the SHHS subset. A [published CHS study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15832098/) reports trouble falling asleep, frequent awakenings and excessive daytime sleepiness at baseline and follow-up for 4,467 surveyed participants. This is not the number with complete genetic data and frailty outcomes; ascertain the exact questionnaire coding, visit dates, permitted release, and overlap before choosing an outcome. Another [SHHS analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5995202/) used frequent sleep complaints and medication use as a combined “insomnia or poor sleep” measure; the medication component complicates interpretation, and that definition should not be called a clinical diagnosis. These studies establish phenotype descriptions, not an independent test of our scores.

Linkage is an important operational constraint: [CHS guidance](https://chs-nhlbi.org/node/5872) advises obtaining phenotype and genetic data from the same source because participant IDs differ among dbGaP, TOPMed, BioLINCC and the original study. An [NSRR discussion](https://sleepdata.org/forum/shhs-genetic-analyses-dbgap) describes a CHS–NSRR link dataset `pht005388` and a CHS SHHS polysomnography table `pht003699`, but these are historical identifiers; confirm current versions, access permissions and approved uses. Do not attempt cross-repository linkage without authorization.

**Independence warning:** the project already examined [CHARGE gait results](stage6_charge_gait_full_candidate_review.md), and the [gait meta-analysis](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58468961/2J9vAuiscdt8bWsNb.pdf) lists CHS in its discovery cohorts. CHS overlap in a prior post-selection component-phenotype screen does not itself show score construction used CHS or that every future participant overlaps, but it prevents an unqualified untouched-test claim. Audit all sources, follow-up decisions, participants and relatives. Sleep duration CHARGE GWAS also included CHS in some studies; evaluate whether any such results influenced the actual score decisions.

Before prioritizing CHS, verify the current no-fee terms for the requested dbGaP/SHHS data, institutional eligibility, IRB and computing costs, current consent/DUC restrictions, which release has the complete frailty items, same-person sleep and genetic coverage, marker alleles/imputation, ancestry and relatedness, and outcome events. Unlike the explicit [Framingham repository fee statement](https://www.framinghamheartstudy.org/fhs-for-researchers/data-available-overview/), no CHS-specific fee statement was verified here. Framingham is therefore the better documented no-data-fee lead at present. Neither cohort validates the project's scores without actual approved participant-level analysis.

## Further evidence for genetic linkage in CHS

An [August 2026 sleep-score and cognition preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.14.26360402v1) reports calculating sleep-trait polygenic scores using TOPMed whole-genome sequencing in CHS and analyzing them alongside cognitive measurements for 2,660 CHS participants at the selected baseline. Its data-availability statement points to dbGaP accession [phs001368](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs001368). This is direct precedent for a genetic-score/phenotype analysis in CHS and stronger evidence of a usable genetic–cognitive intersection than the total sequenced sample alone. The publication is a preprint, and its sleep scores were externally developed genetic predictors: this is **not** evidence that these 2,660 people had measured insomnia, physical frailty or the project's score variants, nor is it a validation of our scores. The preprint's participants and eligibility criteria need not match a future approved release.

The same preprint reports 3,888 MESA participants with sleep polygenic scores and baseline cognitive measures, and refers to TOPMed WGS accession phs001416. Again, this does not establish overlap with MESA's measured insomnia or later FRAIL phenotype. The paper describes access by application, not open individual downloads; no project-specific fee or authorization was established.
