# HRS: a potential precomputed insomnia polygenic index

**Checked 29 October 2026.** A new route could test a published insomnia index against frailty outcomes without needing to calculate the index from raw genotypes. It would be a separate sleep–frailty analysis, **not validation of our 15- or 22-variant scores**. Actual availability in the HRS distribution, access cost, and the usable linked sample are unverified.

The [updated Polygenic Index Repository preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12633171/) describes a version 2 index for insomnia. Its [supplementary workbook](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-7828579/v1/fbf19035b70b8fb58b216e7e.xlsx), sheet `11. Input GWAS meta-analyses`, lists HRS among cohorts assigned `INSOMNIA1`: 23andMe plus Jansen excluding 23andMe, with UK Biobank represented in the source. The workbook is cached as `work/ssgac_pgi_v2_supplement.xlsx`; this is a cohort assignment in the authors' supplement, not proof that the actual current HRS download contains the variable. The paper's 12,670 HRS participants in a general evaluation sample do not establish the number with complete insomnia index and relevant frailty variables.

The [HRS PGI Repository product page](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/ssgac-polygenic-index-pgi-repository) currently labels its contribution version 1.0 (March 2021) and says login or an account is needed. It does not establish that version 2 is distributed there. A separate [HRS PGS product](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/polygenic-score-data-pgs) requires access to sensitive health data and lists many score phenotypes; its [version 5 index](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/polygenic-score-data-pgs/included-pgs) does not list an insomnia score. Do not confuse the two products or infer that either offers this version-2 index. Neither page establishes that all required access and computing costs are zero.

The published [HRS sleep codebook use](https://www.aging-us.com/article/206102/text) identifies insomnia and related sleep questions in the 2006 public survey. The [FFS GWAS paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/) used HRS as a replication cohort for its UK Biobank-derived frailty score, so HRS participants and published findings must be audited for overlap with every step of this project's variant selection and follow-up. The paper's reported association belongs to its own genome-wide frailty score, not our prototypes. The insomnia index's discovery involves UKB, which is also used in our project; the broad index is not independent evidence for the selected cross-trait loci.

If HRS makes the index available under affordable and permitted terms, a prespecified analysis could test the broad insomnia PGI against separately defined FI and FFS, adjusting for age, sex, ancestry principal components and survey/design factors as appropriate; compare sleep-included and sleep-excluded FI where constructible. Confirm participant linkage, ancestry scope, wave dates, missingness, relatedness, score provenance and overlap before analysis. Report association and uncertainty, not causal or individual predictive claims. Calculate either prototype only with independently obtained, properly harmonized individual variant dosages.
