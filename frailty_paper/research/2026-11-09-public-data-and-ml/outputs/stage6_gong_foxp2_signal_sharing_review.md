# FOXP2-region musculoskeletal, insomnia and hospital-frailty signals

## Interpretation

On a restricted set of 790 variants, an exploratory SuSiE-coloc fit gives high modeled shared-signal posteriors for the degenerative-musculoskeletal factor against both MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital frailty. **These values are not reliable evidence of a common causal variant across all three traits.** The support excludes much of the original insomnia credible set, the musculoskeletal factor combines overlapping or potentially overlapping UK Biobank and FinnGen sources, and its summary statistics are from a Genomic SEM latent factor rather than a standard single-cohort GWAS. External reference LD, locus selection and the model's assumptions add uncertainty. The analysis cannot validate an individual risk score or identify an effector gene.

The original 1,257-SNP disease comparison had an 18-SNP MVP credible set and a 17-SNP HFRS credible set. Only 6/18 and 14/17 of those variants, respectively, appear in this 790-SNP analysis. Using the *original fitted SuSiE component probabilities*, the retained variants cover 0.462 of 0.966 probability mass in the original MVP credible set (47.8%) and 0.743 of 0.955 in the original HFRS credible set (77.8%). This is conditional on the original model and external references; it is not a direct measure of how much biology was captured. It demonstrates that the new signal-sharing posterior is vulnerable to unobserved candidate variants. Missing SNPs were not treated as null associations. The previously reported 1,257-SNP disease–disease result remains the more complete comparison for those two phenotypes.

## What was calculated

The entire public [Gong et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72164-7) MSKdg factor file was streamed, yielding 790 exact rsID, GRCh37 coordinate and allele-pair matches to the previously fixed FOXP2 reference SNPs. All passed an absolute factor-versus-gnomAD-NFE allele-frequency difference of 0.20; mean difference was 0.018 and maximum 0.061. Both disease leads rs12536335 and rs35624276 are present. The source file's reported Z matched beta/SE to numerical precision. The factor source lists N=1,131,013 as its **effective sample size**, not the number of unique individuals. It is a working proxy for SuSiE-RSS, and the appropriateness of the standard RSS likelihood for these Genomic SEM statistics has not been established.

We fit 95% SuSiE credible sets with L=10 and fixed residual variance, using gnomAD NFE signed LD for MSKdg and MVP, and FIN signed LD for HFRS. Each fit converged and returned one set on this subset. The factor fit's Z/LD inconsistency estimate was 0.01045, versus 0.00080 for MVP and 0.00112 for HFRS; these diagnostics have no universal pass/fail threshold and do not validate the model. At p1=p2=1e−4 and p12=1e−5, the pairwise PP.H4 values were 0.979 for MSKdg/MVP, 0.996 for MSKdg/HFRS, and 0.993 for MVP/HFRS on this reduced support. For p12=1e−6 to 5e−5, the first two values ranged 0.823–0.996 and 0.957–0.999, respectively. These are **separate pairwise model outputs**, not a joint posterior that three traits share a single variant. With the factor's working N changed to 100,000 or 500,000, the model outputs changed negligibly; this sensitivity does not resolve the likelihood, LD, support or overlap concerns.

The MSKdg model's highest-PIP candidate is rs12536335; both disease models' highest-PIP candidate is rs35624276 in this restricted fit. PIPs depend on which SNPs are included and must not be treated as direct experimental proof. The factor also includes musculoskeletal conditions and symptoms that may overlap clinically with HFRS coding and insomnia-related pain. Separating these effects requires different phenotypes, carefully documented sample overlap, fuller variant coverage and preferably in-sample or better matched LD. There is no eQTL or single-cell data in these fits.

## Source and reproduction

The full `MSKdg.txt` ZIP member was scanned, and Python's ZIP reader verified its uncompressed CRC. The full remote ZIP's published MD5 was not checked. `stage6_gong_foxp2_factor_extract.py` and its receipt record the 4,367,175-row source scan; `stage6_gong_foxp2_factor_prepare.py` records exact matches, sign/allele-frequency checks and the aligned input. `stage6_gong_foxp2_factor_susie_coloc.R` produces the diagnostics, credible-set members and all pair/prior results; `stage6_gong_foxp2_credible_coverage.R` and its CSV compare support with the original disease-only fit. Some older, reconstructible intermediate LD and genotype binaries were removed to recover disk space; their final results, source scripts and narrow gnomAD matrices remain. The current analysis used no target individual genotypes or outcomes.
