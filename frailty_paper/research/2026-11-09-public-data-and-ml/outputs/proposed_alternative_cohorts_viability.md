# Viability of the proposed sleep–frailty validation datasets

Checked 29 October 2026. None of the five options has yet provided an accessible, adequately characterized participant-level dataset for testing the project's fixed 15- or 22-marker scores. This is a feasibility assessment, not a failed validation or evidence that the scores lack association.

| Resource | What is available now | Decision for this project |
|---|---|---|
| [Harvard Personal Genome Project](https://pgp.med.harvard.edu/data) | Public participant-contributed genomes, surveys and other health information. The older snapshot previously examined had nine people with an insomnia-labeled condition, a demographic record and a listed URL for a genome-related data type. The live portal currently fails TLS hostname verification from this environment. | Promising open-data model, but that historical inventory does not establish measured insomnia controls, FI/FFS, usable score alleles or current consent/availability. Not presently suitable for a defensible score–phenotype test. [Detailed audit](stage6_pgp_validation_feasibility.md). |
| [openSNP](https://opensnp.wordpress.com/2025/03/31/sunsetting-opensnp-a-personal-retrospective/) | Its maintainers announced closure and deletion of hosted data in April 2025. | The original service is not a current validation route. Separately hosted or model-derived scores must not be treated as observed insomnia diagnoses. [Audit](stage6_genodex_opensnp_validation_feasibility.md). |
| [ROS/MAP](https://www.radc.rush.edu/reports/dataAvailabilityCriteria.htm) | Public availability report; participant data require request, review and agreements. Rerunning the public report returned 401 MAP participants flagged for a sleep questionnaire and at least one listed genetic platform; 922 for actigraphy and genetics; 398 for both sleep modalities and genetics. | Best targeted controlled-access lead among these options. Counts overlap and are not FI, ancestry-QC, genotype-quality or independent-case counts. Exact participant- and visit-level intersections, costs and marker coverage are not established. [Full audit](stage6_rosmap_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md); [request preparation](rosmap_data_request_draft.md). |
| [ADNI](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/data-samples/adni-data/) | Longitudinal clinical and genetic records available to approved researchers under its agreement. Published ADNI FI instruments include sleep and fatigue items; a published 11-item alternative has no explicitly named sleep item but is a different, comorbidity-dominant construct. | Possible approved-access sensitivity cohort; linked sleep–FI–genotype sample, allele coverage, fees and independence remain unverified. [Detailed audit](stage6_adni_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md). |
| [ELSA](https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/genetic-data-access) | Sleep/frailty surveys and safeguarded precomputed polygenic scores exist; linked individual SNP data require a separate application and associated fees. | Scientifically relevant, but existing precomputed scores are not the project scores and cannot reconstruct them; cost and permitted linkage depend on the data product. [Detailed audit](stage6_elsa_precomputed_scores_access_review.md). |

For comparison, [Framingham dbGaP](framingham_mentor_access_proposal.md) is a separate no-data-service-fee, authorized-access option with older-adult sleep and physical-frailty measurements; institutional and computing costs, linkage and usable coverage are unverified. Prioritize the cohort according to eligibility, projected usable sample, construct compatibility, source independence and total cost, not availability flags alone.

No participant genetic scores or outcome associations were calculated in this review. An analysis requires eligible linked data, phenotype and genotype QC, a frozen protocol, allele harmonization and overlap assessment; the previously completed NHANES observational analysis is separate.
