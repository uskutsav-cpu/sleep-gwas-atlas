# HCHS/SOL sleep-genetics resource: selected-locus check

The Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL) is a multi-centre US cohort with sleep measurements and genotyping. A [published sleep-health GWAS](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07514-0) reports 11,144 HCHS/SOL participants with genetic data in its external sleep-score analysis. The study includes self-reported insomnia symptoms, sleep duration, sleepiness and snoring; it used UK Biobank-derived composite definitions, and imputed chronotype in many participants. This is useful evidence for sleep phenotyping and cross-population transfer, not a directly measured frailty outcome or validation of our scores. Individual and relative overlap with every project dataset is unverified.

The study's [supplementary workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs42003-025-07514-0/MediaObjects/42003_2025_7514_MOESM3_ESM.xlsx), SD15, reports only 12 selected HCHS/SOL marginal associations, not the entire 400-locus analysis. An exact rsID comparison with the 33 distinct markers in our 15- and 22-marker prototypes found no matching IDs. That means no candidate association was obtained from this selected table. It does **not** mean the candidates were untested, absent, or null; a coordinate/allele representation or a result outside the selected subset could be missed. The separate SD5/SD9/SD13 tables include rs113851554 but describe UK Biobank analyses, not an independent HCHS/SOL estimate for that marker.

The [NSRR description](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/hchs/pages) says HCHS/SOL sleep datasets and ancillary sleep measurements are available through its resource; its [dbGaP entry](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003457.v1.p2) directs researchers to authorized access for participant-level phenotype and molecular data through parent study phs000810. Registration/approval, cost, valid linkage, specific genotype and FI/FFS components, sample overlap, and the number of participants with all relevant data remain unverified. A separate published HCHS/SOL study reports self-reported mobility limitations in a subgroup, but those are not FI or Fried frailty ([mobility study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13478335/)). No participant data were accessed.

Next, inspect whether a full HCHS/SOL genome-wide sleep summary file exists and whether permitted linked data can construct an appropriate sleep-excluded FI or physical phenotype; selected summary rows alone cannot support a full 15- or 22-marker score test. If authorized participant data become available, account for the cohort's complex sampling, kinship and ancestry, verify exact alleles and imputation quality, and report transportability rather than assuming the European-derived score has equal performance.

## Additional frailty-index lead (29 October 2026)

The public [AgingMicrobiome manuscript code repository](https://github.com/liekekuiper/AgingMicrobiome/tree/982d1fa8569b84fb6f2bc65565dacbe4814e5374) describes age and frailty-index analyses across six cohorts including HCHS/SOL. Its [genus meta-analysis script](https://github.com/liekekuiper/AgingMicrobiome/blob/982d1fa8569b84fb6f2bc65565dacbe4814e5374/metaanalyses/MetaAnalysis_Genus.R) loads SOL result files and runs both age and FI analyses. This is a promising route to a cohort-specific FI specification, but the repository overview and script do **not** establish from the available result files that HCHS/SOL contributed usable FI observations, identify the FI items, or demonstrate sleep–FI–genotype linkage in the same participants. The SOL results are referenced as local files, not provided as participant-level data in that script. Do not report this as a validated HCHS/SOL FI or score result without the cohort-specific methods and sample flow.

The [BioLINCC study page](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/studies/hchssol/) describes a requestable phenotype package including baseline, visit 2 and Sueño data, while the [dbGaP sleep entry](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003457.v1.p2) directs individual molecular and phenotype access requests to parent study phs000810. These are controlled/request routes, not an open linked genotype–phenotype download. Fees, eligibility, linkage across repositories, possible overlaps, FI construction and the number of older people with all three data types remain to be verified. The useful next check is the manuscript's cohort-specific FI methods or released data dictionary, especially whether a sleep-excluded FI can be constructed without removing a disproportionate number of deficits.

## Additional sleep-duration summary-data lead

A separate [multi-ancestry sleep-duration GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10656946/) states that HCHS/SOL and J-MICC cohort-specific summary statistics are available **upon request to each cohort**. This is a potential summary-association follow-up, not an established no-fee open download; the conditions, availability, variant coverage and cohort overlap remain unverified. Sleep duration is not insomnia or frailty, and summary statistics cannot measure individual score calibration or discrimination. The paper's publicly available UK Biobank summary data are a separate product and have discovery-overlap limitations for this project. No request was sent and no cohort data were obtained.

## Clarification: no-fee phenotype access components

The [NSRR data-security policy](https://sleepdata.org/about/data-security) states that it provides its data at no cost to users completing an online request, and [BioLINCC's FAQ](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/faq/) states that BioLINCC datasets have no access fee. These clarify the sleep- and BioLINCC-phenotype portions of the HCHS/SOL route described above; their access remains subject to registration, agreements and approval. They do not establish the cost of genetic access, analysis infrastructure, or a permitted cross-repository linkage. The NIH [HCHS/SOL sleep study record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003457.v1.p2) continues to direct individual-level molecular and sleep access to its parent study. Verify exact FI items, permissible linkage, genotyped sleep–FI overlap and participant/relative independence before treating this as a validation dataset. This fee clarification is not a new variant result or score test.

## Public BioLINCC sleep-variable inventory

The [BioLINCC main-study dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/hchssol/Data%20Dictionary%20-%20Main.pdf), dated May 5, 2022 in its extracted text, lists `slea_lad1.sas7bdat` (PDF page 174) with a random BioLINCC participant key `PID`, a `VISIT` field, `SLEA4` trouble falling asleep, `SLEA5` waking several times, `SLEA6` waking earlier than planned, `SLEA7` difficulty returning to sleep, and `SLEA11` typical sleep in the past four weeks. The [published composite sleep-health GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07514-0) describes the SLE4/5/7 symptom questions in HCHS/SOL, providing context for the labels. The dictionary does not alone establish exact response codes, dates, complete participants, or the `PID` crosswalk to genotypes; do not infer baseline timing from the filename. In a targeted text search of this dictionary, no literal labels `frailty index`, `grip strength`, or `gait speed` were found. This is not a proof that related items or measurements are absent, because abbreviations and other released files may use different labels. The specific HCHS/SOL FI item list and sleep-excluded version remain unverified.

Dictionary source SHA-256 `24917bcdcfcaedb2bc78c3c1e5d8a80bbab8321acad6a6451db612b836d490ad`; PDF and extracted text are retained on the external work disk. The same main dictionary lists `sfea_lad1.sas7bdat` (page 173) with `PID` and `VISIT`, and self-reported health-related limitations in moderate activities (`SFEA2A`) and stairs (`SFEA2B`); it also lists energy (`SFEA6B`). ## Baseline questionnaire verification (29 October 2026)

The public [baseline forms](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/hchssol/Forms%20-%20Baseline.pdf) contain Sleep Form SLE, version A (PDF page 160), and SF-12v2 Health Survey SFE, version A (PDF page 155). The sleep form asks how often symptoms occurred in the past four weeks: 1 = not in that period, 2 = less than once a week, 3 = one or two times a week, 4 = three or four times a week, and 5 = five or more times a week. Questions 4–7 ask about sleep initiation, repeated awakenings, early waking and difficulty returning to sleep; question 11 asks about usual sleep quality. The activity questions ask whether health limits moderate activities or climbing several flights of stairs: 1 = limited a lot, 2 = limited a little, 3 = not limited at all. These are questionnaire response categories, not a prespecified insomnia diagnosis or validated frailty classification. Reverse the activity direction if constructing a higher-is-worse outcome, and prespecify missing-data handling and any threshold before analysis.

The form codes and item numbers agree with the main dictionary's `SLEA4`–`SLEA7`, `SLEA11`, `SFEA2A` and `SFEA2B` labels. The forms are included in the baseline document, supporting their intended use at baseline; this does not prove the actual participant records were collected on the same date or that a particular individual has both forms. A text search of the separate [visit 2 forms](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/hchssol/Forms_-_Visit_2.pdf) did not find these exact question phrases; that search cannot establish whether equivalent measures exist under other wording, forms, or releases. Thus the documented pairing is a **potential cross-sectional baseline analysis**, not a demonstrated longitudinal sleep-to-function comparison. Check `VISIT`, actual dates, linkage permissions and completeness in the approved data before analysis. A physical-function association would be a secondary phenotype, not validation against FI or Fried frailty.


## Visit 2 mobility: a documented prospective outcome candidate (29 October 2026)

The [visit 2 form](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/hchssol/Forms_-_Visit_2.pdf), PDF page 40, contains participant disability screening form PDE, version 1, September 10, 2014. Questions 4, 5 and 6 ask, respectively, about serious difficulty walking or climbing stairs, difficulty walking half a mile, and difficulty climbing 10 steps; the form codes no as 0 and yes as 1. The [main dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/hchssol/Data%20Dictionary%20-%20Main.pdf), page 214, describes `pde_v2_lad1.sas7bdat` with BioLINCC `PID`, `VISIT`, and corresponding `PDE4`, `PDE5`, and `PDE6`. The form itself labels half a mile as approximately one kilometre; preserve the questionnaire wording rather than converting it into a measured walking distance. Verify that the released data retain the form coding and check missing-value codes.

A [2026 HCHS/SOL paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41522-026-01019-2) defines self-reported mobility impairment as answering yes to any of these three questions and no impairment as answering no to all three. Its analytic sample comprised 1,772 people at least 50 years old with microbiome and mobility data, selected for a different scientific question; this number is **not** our sleep–genotype–mobility sample size. Its exclusions and findings need not be copied to a genetic analysis. This is a prior operational definition of a mobility outcome, not a validated Fried score or frailty index. For missing responses, define an explicit policy rather than coding incomplete negative records as unaffected.

Potential design: use baseline SLE insomnia symptoms as an exposure and visit 2 PDE mobility impairment as a later outcome; evaluate each frozen genetic score against visit 2 mobility, and, if data support it, assess whether measured baseline sleep adds predictive information. Verify form dates, matching `PID`, participant consent, permitted genotype crosswalk, exact genotype–outcome overlap, cohort independence, ancestry and kinship, sampling design and covariates. A later mobility outcome by itself does not establish *incident* mobility impairment: an equivalent baseline measurement or other defensible baseline definition is required to exclude prevalent impairment. Distinguish association from prediction, evaluate the prototype allele directions before scoring, and reserve FI/FFS as separate outcomes if constructible. No individual records or genetic-score associations were analyzed.


## Repeated mobility follow-up and baseline sleep timing

The [NSRR HCHS/SOL dataset introduction](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/hchs/pages/dataset-introduction.md) explicitly identifies `slea_lad1.sas7bdat` as a **baseline visit** sleep-history file, and describes its NSRR files as processed from BioLINCC data with `PID` as the primary subject identifier. This improves on inferring timing from file names. It does not by itself establish that every released sleep record links to every BioLINCC visit 2 record or to genotypes; verify identifier transformations, consent and permitted linkage. A [published HCHS/SOL methods description](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/44/4/zsaa218/5937003) dates baseline to 2008–2011 and visit 2 to 2014–2017.

The study's [visit 3 public investigator codebook](https://sites.cscc.unc.edu/hchs/sites/default/files/public_docs/HCHS%20Visit%203%20CODEBOOK%20INV2_20240920.pdf), PDF page 100, lists the same named mobility items `PDE4`, `PDE5`, `PDE6`, with response values 0 = no and 1 = yes. Marginal yes counts are 1,996, 1,527 and 1,685 respectively; their overlap is unknown, so they cannot be summed to count individuals with any impairment. The [visit 3 release overview](https://sites.cscc.unc.edu/hchs/sites/default/files/public_docs/HCHS%20Visit%203%20OVERVIEW%20INV2%20-%2020240923.pdf) describes data collected January 2020–January 2024 and 9,864 visit 3 participants in that release, not the number with complete mobility, sleep and genetic data. Collection changed during the COVID-19 pandemic; assess mode and period effects before comparing visits.

If approved linked visit 2 and 3 records are available, using the same three-item definition at both visits could support a more direct **incident mobility-impairment** analysis among people without reported impairment at visit 2. Baseline sleep precedes both assessments; determine dates and account for survival, loss to follow-up, timing, missingness and changes in ascertainment. Check the actual wording and versions at visit 3 rather than relying on labels alone. Visit 3 is an investigator-use release; whether it is obtainable without a data fee, whether it is included in the current BioLINCC package, and whether linking it to sleep and genotypes is permitted are not established. These aggregate summaries are not independent validation of any genetic score.


### Visit 3 access and participation check

The [current BioLINCC page](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/studies/hchssol/) specifically describes its available package as baseline and visit 2 plus five ancillary studies; it does not list visit 3 in that description or in its listed forms. This is evidence for using the baseline-to-visit-2 design as the documented BioLINCC route. It is not conclusive proof that no newer visit 3 file can be obtained through any route: the page's detailed available-data description is dated May 2022, despite a later page-update date. The visit 3 codebook describes an investigator-use release; do not claim it is a free public participant-level download or included in the BioLINCC release.

The [visit 3 investigator release overview](https://sites.cscc.unc.edu/hchs/sites/default/files/public_docs/HCHS%20Visit%203%20OVERVIEW%20INV2%20-%2020240923.pdf), pages 3–6, reports 9,090 in-person examinees plus 774 phone-only participants, and 9,089 unique IDs with the PDE/PDS disability screener. It lists separate visit 3 sampling weights for exam participants and all participants. These counts are not the matched baseline sleep–visit 2–visit 3–genotype sample; the questionnaire count should not be assumed to equal it. Selecting participants with the screener could disproportionately exclude phone-only participants, so check actual mode and questionnaire availability and use appropriate design and follow-up weights only after verifying their purpose and suitability for the analysis.

The [visit 3 codebook](https://sites.cscc.unc.edu/hchs/sites/default/files/public_docs/HCHS%20Visit%203%20CODEBOOK%20INV2_20240920.pdf) identifies the disability form as `PDE`, version 1, matching the code and version number shown in the public visit 2 form. Its item labels and 0/1 codes match the earlier documentation, but version numbering and abbreviated labels alone do not establish identical question wording. The visit 3 full instrument wording is still unverified.
