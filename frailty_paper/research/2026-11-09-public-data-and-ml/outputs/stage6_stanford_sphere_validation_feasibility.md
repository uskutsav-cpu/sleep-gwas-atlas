# Stanford ADRC SPHERE: synthetic multimodal data and score feasibility

Checked 28 October 2026. The [Stanford ADRC SPHERE portal](https://adrc-sphere.stanford.edu/) describes a privacy-preserving synthetic version of an aging and cognitive-disease cohort. Its published overview describes 644 synthetic records across modalities, including a subset with selected Alzheimer-associated genomic variants and blood-cell-type pseudobulk expression. These are synthetic values, not measurements on independent individuals. The authors' [preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596149/) describes the synthesis and states that the original clinical data follow a separate access process.

The release does **not** contain genome-wide variant calls, and the blood expression release is pseudobulk rather than single-cell-resolution counts. Its [public genomics data dictionary](https://github.com/stanford-developers/adrc-sphere-portal/blob/main/data/dd/DD_wgs_genodata.csv) lists 167 rsID columns. An exact identifier intersection against the 15-marker and 22-marker project lists yielded **0/15** and **0/22**, respectively; this does not rule out LD proxies and is not a phenotype test. All 167 dictionary descriptions specify a carrier coding that collapses heterozygotes and alternate homozygotes, rather than a 0/1/2 alternate allele count. Even if an rsID were present, that coding would not by itself implement either additive project score.

The portal describes registration and acceptance of a data-use agreement, including research-use restrictions; the study [preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596149/) also describes registered, click-through access without committee review. We inspected only the openly available dictionary and documentation, did not accept the agreement, download the cohort, establish its detailed phenotype availability or conduct a prediction analysis. The resource may support methods prototyping or hypothesis generation within its permitted terms, but it cannot serve as independent patient-level validation of our current scores. Synthetic preservation claims require model- and question-specific verification; a synthetic cohort generated from existing participants is not an independent cohort.
