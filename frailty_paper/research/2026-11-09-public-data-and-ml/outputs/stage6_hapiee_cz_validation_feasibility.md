# HAPIEE-CZ: no-data-preparation-fee cohort lead

The Czech arm of HAPIEE is a promising **access-by-request** lead for the insomnia–frailty genetic-score project, not a freely downloadable or already validated dataset. A 2026 [cohort-profile preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13082134/) reports sleep assessments, physical functioning, a prior frailty-index analysis, and baseline-sample genotyping on the Illumina GSA v3 array. The report says the genetic data passed QC and were still being processed when it was written. This source is a preprint; present release status and exact access rights must be confirmed.

The same preprint states that Czech data can be requested with a research proposal, subject to review and a material/data transfer agreement, and that the nonprofit cohort charges **no costs for data preparation and release**. This is unusually useful evidence for the user's no-fee constraint, but it does not explicitly guarantee availability or cost of the specific genomic files, joint linkage, storage, computing or institutional review for our project. The [2025 longitudinal-dataset directory](https://atlaslongitudinaldatasets.ac.uk/datasets/hapiee) reports no publicly available access information; it predates the 2026 preprint and covers the broader HAPIEE study, so do not treat it as an equivalent Czech-specific fee statement. No request has been sent.

## Phenotypes and sample

The preprint lists sleep at baseline and the 2023–24 Czech follow-up, with intervening sleep assessments in specified postal waves. The later examination included measured walk speed, grip strength and anthropometry. It reports 2,350 questionnaire respondents, of whom 1,519 also attended physical examination, and 1,388 blood samples; these figures are **not** counts with usable genotypes, insomnia and frailty outcomes together. The questionnaire/exam participants were younger and less functionally limited than non-attenders, raising follow-up selection concerns. The exact sleep questions, insomnia phenotype, timing and outcome event counts need verification.

A [published multicountry HAPIEE frailty analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11671974/) used a 21-item comprehensive-geriatric-assessment-derived deficit index, rather than the project's original FI or Fried score. It included a depression assessment; the exact depression item construction and any indirect sleep-item overlap require an item-level audit before sleep–FI comparisons. The multicountry published sample is not the Czech genotyped subsample. Existing grip and walking measures alone do not establish a complete five-domain Fried phenotype, nor establish that a later FI is ready for use.

**Measurement-overlap follow-up:** The frailty paper specifies that its depression deficit in Czech participants was a binary indicator based on the 20-item CES-D total, with a threshold of 16. A [separate Czech HAPIEE paper](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/30/5/948/5825338) explicitly states that this CES-D includes a sleep-related item. Consequently the published FI has a documented **indirect sleep pathway** through the depression indicator: a sleep-related response can contribute to the CES-D total and, for some people near the threshold, can affect the binary depression deficit. This is not equivalent to the sleep item being a separate FI deficit, and the amount of overlap or influence on associations cannot be inferred without individual responses and exact scoring. The 2026 Czech cohort profile also lists a shorter CES-D at later waves; do not assume its scoring or the published baseline FI transfers unchanged to later years. If data become available, predefine a sensitivity excluding the entire depression deficit and recompute the FI denominator; label the resulting index as modified and report both versions. A revised CES-D threshold after removing a sleep item would need external justification and should not be treated as the published threshold.

Published HAPIEE research in the Russian Novosibirsk cohort used a Jenkins sleep questionnaire, but this does not establish the same instrument or items were used in the Czech genotyped cohort. The Czech-specific 2026 inventory says “sleep” without specifying insomnia items in the accessible table; exact item wording and waves remain to be verified before designing the test.

## Time ordering for a possible prospective analysis

The [Czech cohort inventory, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13082134/) lists sleep at baseline in 2002–05, postal surveys in 2012 and 2013–14, and the 2023–24 examination. Its generic `frailty` row is marked for 2017–18 and 2020–21, but not 2023–24; the table does not define that row as the published 21-item FI, a Fried phenotype, or an incident-frailty outcome. Thus 2012 or 2013–14 sleep could precede those later frailty assessments **if** the measures, timing and records are actually available and linkable. The 2023–24 sleep measure occurs after the two listed frailty surveys and must not be treated as their prospective baseline. Although grip, gait and anthropometry are listed for 2023–24, reconstructing a later full FI or physical-frailty phenotype requires all necessary items, definitions and permissions. Dates and missingness must be checked per person; deaths and loss to follow-up require prespecified handling, and examination participation was health-selected.

The [Maelstrom study metadata](https://mica.maelstrom-research.org/study/hapiee) lists 6,933 Czech participants with biological samples. That count is not a number with usable genotype calls or a linked complete sleep–frailty sample, and the source lists 8,857 baseline participants while the 2026 profile reports 8,856; retain the sources' different denominators rather than reconciling them by assumption. The public catalogue did not identify exact sleep item wording in the study description. We have not established free participant-level access to the necessary genetic and survey files or evaluated a score.

## Genotyping-platform coverage screen

The Czech profile says baseline DNA was genotyped on Illumina Global Screening Array v3. We scanned all 654,027 assay rows of Illumina's public standard [GSA-24v3-0 A1 GRCh37 manifest](https://support.illumina.com/content/dam/illumina-support/documents/downloads/productfiles/global-screening-array-24/v3-0/GSA-24v3-0-A1-manifest-file-csv.zip). None of the 33 distinct variants across the project's 15- and 22-marker lists had a manifest assay at the project's assumed GRCh37 chromosome and position; none had a matching rsID and coordinate. The assay-row count matched the manifest header, and the separate control section was excluded. [Full position/name results](stage6_hapiee_gsa_manifest_check.csv), [source hash and receipt](stage6_hapiee_gsa_manifest_receipt.json), and [script](stage6_hapiee_gsa_manifest_check.py).

This finding concerns a **standard manufacturer manifest**, not HAPIEE participant calls. The exact cohort manifest revision, custom content, build, probe mapping and sample-level QC have not been verified; project positions are assumed GRCh37 and rsIDs can have multiple allele representations. In particular, variants absent from an array can be imputed, and no information on HAPIEE's reference panel, imputation, candidate dosages or INFO scores has been obtained. The result is therefore a reason to check the actual data before planning validation, not proof that any project marker is absent from the final dataset or that the scores cannot be evaluated. No participant data were downloaded.

### Nearby reference-panel tagging

To gauge whether array-based inference is plausible, we used 503 public 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European reference individuals and searched ±100 kb around each candidate for a non-intensity-only SNV at a position in the standard GSA v3 A1 manifest. All 33 candidates had unique exact position/rsID/biallelic matches and complete variable reference genotypes; each had at least one candidate nearby tag. Within this same reference, the best dosage-correlation r² was at least 0.8 for 7 of 15 and 6 of 22 score variants, and below 0.3 for 2 of 15 and 6 of 22. The score sets overlap, so counts must not be added. For example, the best reference tag for the 15-marker rs113851554 had r² 0.153 in this restricted search. [Per-candidate results](stage6_hapiee_gsa_reference_tags.csv), [receipt](stage6_hapiee_gsa_reference_tags_receipt.json), and [reproduction script](stage6_hapiee_gsa_reference_tags.py).

These numbers describe **selected single-marker correlations in the same reference panel used to select the tag**, not imputation accuracy. Imputation can use many markers and a different reference; EUR reference LD need not equal LD in HAPIEE, and tag probes were checked by position only, without verifying the exact array allele, build or strand. Actual HAPIEE genotype QC, imputation and candidate INFO scores are unknown. Small, selected reference samples can overstate best-tag correlations; a low r² within this window does not prove a variant cannot be imputed. Replacing missing variants with tags would create a different score and require separately verified phase, weighting and independent validation. No HAPIEE participant data were analyzed.

## Decision before use

Request-specific confirmation is needed for: genotype-file release and imputation quality at each score allele, no-cost availability for our institution, allowed linkage and consent, count and timing of people with each sleep and frailty outcome, sleep/fatigue overlap in FI items, valid physical-frailty construction, ancestry and relatedness controls, and participant or family overlap with candidate-selection sources. If an appropriate genotyped subset and event count are available, freeze the score and test separate prespecified outcomes with suitable calibration and uncertainty; do not infer clinical performance from an association alone. No individual data or genetic scores were downloaded or calculated.
