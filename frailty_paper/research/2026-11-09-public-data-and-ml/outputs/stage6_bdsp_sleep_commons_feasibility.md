# Sleep Health Data Commons and Human Sleep Project: access and genetic-score feasibility

Checked 29 October 2026. The [Sleep Health Data Commons v1.0.0](https://www.bdsp.io/content/gv7d0mwcpb95xopthe93/1.0.0/), published 28 October 2026, describes polysomnography, EEG, brain MRI and linked electronic health records across multiple health systems. Its approximately 157,000 patients with polysomnography are provisional: the description itself says final counts and several quality-control details remain to be filled in. It lists no genotypes among its described data types, and does not establish an FI or Fried frailty measure, score-marker coverage or usable genetic linkage. Its files require credentialing, human-subjects training and a data-use agreement; it is not an unrestricted public download. The platform calls itself freely available, but any project-specific access, computing, training and storage costs have not been verified.

The related [Human Sleep Project v3.0](https://www.bdsp.io/content/hsp/3.0/) reports 115,129 full PSG sessions plus 4,105 home sleep apnea tests in 90,166 patients, with diagnosis linkage and, when available, 1,024-dimensional sleep-EEG representations and brain-health scores. These figures count the full corpus, not participants with relevant genetic or frailty data. Its public description does not list genetic data in v3.0; an [older v2 description](https://www.bdsp.io/content/hsp/2.0/) mentioned genetics as a future addition, which is not proof of availability in the current release. The v3 files require credentialing and a data-use agreement; direct S3 examples require approved access. Some derivatives are described as planned, so current artifact availability must be checked before analysis.

**Decision:** neither resource currently supplies the documented linked genotype–outcome data needed to validate the 15- or 22-marker scores. HSP is a possible later source for a separate sleep-signal/health ML question, conditional on access and suitable outcomes; its representations are physiological EEG embeddings, not genetic embeddings. Any later model evaluation should group repeated sessions by patient, assess transfer across sites and acquisition hardware, and check whether model training data overlap a proposed evaluation set. The v3 documentation itself flags hardware differences and a CAISR training-set indicator. A diagnosis code or predicted brain-health score must not be treated as validated FI or Fried frailty.

No application, download, participant analysis or external contact was made. The presence or absence of specific genotype files and linkage cannot be established from the public descriptions alone.
