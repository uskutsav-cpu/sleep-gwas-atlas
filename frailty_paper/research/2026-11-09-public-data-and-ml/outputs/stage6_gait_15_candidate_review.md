# Public gait-speed GWAS: 15-marker prototype follow-up

The full [CHARGE older-adult gait-speed GWAS](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/grasp/downloads/FullResults/2017/2017_Ben_Avrahim/Gait-Aging-All.txt) was streamed and compared against all 15 prototype rsIDs and unordered allele pairs. Eight matched uniquely; seven rsIDs were absent. One matched marker, rs1032524, had nominal p=0.04313; the remaining seven did not have p<0.05. This is a postselected single-marker observation, not a prespecified replication, and would not meet a simple 0.05/8 multiple-testing threshold. It should not be interpreted as evidence that rs1032524 causally affects gait, or that the other seven have no effect. Allele frequency and strand/position checks were not performed in this scan. Source beta in the table is in the source allele orientation, not necessarily the prototype counted-allele orientation.

The result is a marker-level exploration of gait speed, not a joint insomnia–frailty endpoint, FI, FFS, or a directly measured weighted-score association. The comparison cannot determine whether the FI- or FFS-weighted scores predict better: only 8/15 markers were present, the GWAS has no phenotype-linked genotypes for scoring, and component effects and uncertainty are correlated by LD. Prior cohort overlap and prior candidate selection further limit independence. The separate 22-marker lookup matched 11/22 pairs but flagged rs4886140's major reference-frequency discrepancy, so it too cannot establish score performance. Both scans share the exact same normalized source-file digest.

Reproducibility: `python work/gait_charge_2017/prototype15_lookup.py`, [candidate rows](stage6_gait_15_candidate_lookup.csv), [receipt](stage6_gait_15_candidate_lookup.json). Source file contains 2,474,503 rows; normalized SHA-256 `b31bbbedd2c4297cf96649de37d53bf1176d73cb6e6e267088971b6490885702`. IDs and allele strings were matched case-insensitively; no coordinates are supplied in the source. The source [readme and restrictions](stage6_charge_gait_full_candidate_review.md) apply; do not redistribute the full source data or significant subsets without permission. No subject-level data or clinical predictions were generated.

## Follow-up on rs1032524

The source explicitly codes T as allele 1 for rs1032524, the same as the 15-marker prototype's counted allele. Its gait effect is +0.0033 m/s per T (SE 0.0016, p=0.04313), so the nominal point estimate is in the direction of faster gait. The source T frequency is 0.4566 versus 0.4841 in the existing 503-person European 1000 Genomes reference, a difference of 0.0275. This reduces an obvious frequency-mismatch concern but does not establish source strand, coordinates, cohort-specific coding or identical genetic effects. The reference is not the gait-study population.

The prototype lists negative FI, FFS and insomnia weights for T; under their existing coding assumptions the gait point estimate is directionally compatible with lower frailty and faster gait, not proof that sleep and frailty share a causal variant. The original FI includes sleep/tiredness and was used in candidate selection; the FFS source convention is supported by same-study lead-table checks, not marker-specific author confirmation. Independent insomnia follow-ups for this marker were imprecise (MVP p=0.1613; FinnGen p=0.4084), and FFS, FI and gait differ in phenotype and units. The gait p-value remains nominal, postselected and uncorrected; seven matched markers without nominal p<0.05 and seven absent markers cannot be treated as negative replication.
