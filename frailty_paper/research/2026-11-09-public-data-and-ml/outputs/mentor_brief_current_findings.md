# Insomnia and frailty genetics: current discussion brief

**Research question.** Do insomnia and frailty share genetic influences, do those influences differ for the frailty index (FI) and Fried frailty score (FFS), and can they eventually improve prediction or identify biological mechanisms? The present work is exploratory: neither project genetic-score prototype has been independently validated, and no findings should be used to predict individual health.

**What FI and FFS measure.** FI summarizes accumulated health deficits across many domains; FFS counts physical-frailty components. These definitions need not identify the same people. The FI GWAS used here includes insomnia and tiredness items, while the FFS includes exhaustion. That overlap can contribute to an insomnia–frailty relationship; it does not establish how much it explains. Definitions differ across cohorts. The PISA evaluation FI also has multiple sleep-related deficits, whereas the ELSA evaluation FI includes restless sleep. [Definitions and item audit](fi_ffs_component_crosswalk.md); [published-score audit](fi_ffs_pgs_published_validation_audit.md); [PISA](stage6_pisa_sleep_frailty_validation_feasibility.md).

**Shared genetic signal.** Our auxiliary LDSC estimate of FI–FFS genetic correlation is 0.757 (SE 0.021; 95% CI 0.715–0.799). In previously analyzed insomnia GWAS, genetic correlation was higher with FI than FFS, including a UK Biobank insomnia paired FFS-minus-FI difference of −0.156 (95% CI −0.213 to −0.100). These use overlapping source cohorts and are not causal effects, individual risk, or percentages of shared variants; the FI's sleep content complicates interpretation. [LDSC discussion](mentor_discussion_fi_ffs_risk.md); [reproducible FI–FFS estimates](direct_fi_ffs_genetic_correlation.csv).

**Candidate genes and cell types.** FOXP2-region insomnia–hospital-frailty signals have conditional colocalization support in some analyses, but the fitted signals differ substantially between the 503-person European reference and its 99-person Finnish subset in a broad-window analysis. A later narrower analysis using larger ancestry-oriented references supports sharing under its assumptions; the smaller references agree on a common narrow subset, while lower shared-variant priors weaken support. This remains a regional hypothesis, not proof of FOXP2 as the causal gene. RBM6-region brain-expression analyses differ across insomnia studies, and a leading expression variant is missing in one matched comparison; the mechanism remains unresolved. Single-cell and QTL evidence can prioritize testable cell contexts but proximity, expression and a predicted variant effect do not establish mediation. [FOXP2](stage6_foxp2_independent_cohort_coloc_review.md); [RBM6](stage6_rbm6_sleep_matched_review.md); [full molecular synthesis](mentor_insomnia_frailty_synthesis_2026-10-28.md).

A [2026 multivariate musculoskeletal study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72164-7) also identified our rs12536335 as a leading association for a degenerative-musculoskeletal factor. Our existing restricted-region follow-up found high modeled pairwise sharing with insomnia and hospital frailty, but retained less than half of the original insomnia credible-set probability mass and uses a latent-factor GWAS with potentially overlapping source cohorts. It does **not** establish a common causal variant across the three traits, validate the score, or identify a causal gene or cell type. [Musculoskeletal follow-up](stage6_gong_foxp2_signal_sharing_review.md).

An open aging-brain atlas also reports that the rs7734045 C allele has SMAD5 expression associations in excitatory neurons and oligodendrocytes with opposite estimated signs. This is a postselected molecular hypothesis, not a formal cell-type difference; ROSMAP analyses overlap. A [single-signal sensitivity](stage6_niagads_smad5_exc_coloc_review.md) favored distinct FI and excitatory-neuron expression signals, while the physical-frailty and insomnia comparisons were unresolved. The model has substantial coverage, LD and multiple-signal limitations and does not prove a mechanism. [Atlas screen](stage6_niagads_xqtl_candidate_review.md).

The 15-marker set includes rs113851554 near MEIS1, which has published associations with periodic limb movements and restless legs. Its original FI and FFS associations in our lookup were not statistically clear; a strong sleep association alone does not show shared frailty risk. An exploratory All of Us summary-data contrast between the FI- and FFS-weighted 15-marker sums falls from Z = 2.82 to 1.39 when this marker is removed; the change persists in a reference-only bootstrap. This is postselected and depends on external LD and allele assumptions, not evidence that either score predicts better. Where data permit, examine restless-legs and movement phenotypes as prespecified sensitivity analyses without treating adjustment as a causal test. RLS results for another candidate, rs78206187, have discordant effect directions across public sources, and multiple correlated nearby rows show similar discrepancies; the cause is unresolved. Do not use that direction to infer a mechanism or select score weights. [RLS source audit](stage6_rls_three_study_region_review.md). Prior work reports that the candidate-containing MEIS1 regulatory element is accessible in tested GABAergic neural models and that CRISPR interference of the element lowers MEIS1 expression in neural stem cells; this is not an allele-specific test or proof of a frailty mechanism. [MEIS1 sleep evidence](stage6_meis1_independent_sleep_phenotype_review.md); [neural regulatory study](stage6_meis1_neural_regulatory_prior_study.md); [RLS GWAS follow-up](stage6_rls2024_candidate_followup_review.md); [paired sensitivity](stage6_aou_paired_contrast_locus_sensitivity.md); [reference bootstrap](stage6_aou_paired_reference_bootstrap_review.md).

**Current scores.** The 15-marker prototype has three alternative weight sets. A separate 22-marker set has both an equal-weight count and three exploratory weighted sums; the two 22-marker files use opposite counted alleles at eight markers and must not be mixed. Their selection and source overlap prevent interpreting previous association checks as independent validation; A cross-check of 37 published FFS lead rows from the same study supports A1 as the effect allele for the source beta, though candidate-specific multiallelic contrasts and source coding still need verification. [Allele audit](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md). They have not been tested for calibration, discrimination or added value beyond clinical variables. [Score status](stage6_score_validation_evidence_summary.md).

Earlier insomnia studies provide useful comparators: Lane and colleagues reported that their separate 57-variant insomnia score associated with sleep measures in HUNT and Partners Biobank. That published score is not one of our prototypes and its association does not show improved frailty prediction. [Prior-score context](candidate_selection_and_sleep_score_context.md).

**Latest public genetic follow-up.** All of Us aggregate results cover all 33 distinct candidate variants. In a postselected 33-variant analysis, two passed within-family BH for coded insomnia and none did for generalized muscle weakness. A separate conditional score-level sensitivity using EUR-similarity results produced positive insomnia statistics across the existing research sums; corresponding AFR-similarity results were uncertain. These are aggregate calculations with external LD, inferred allele conventions and unresolved discovery/relative overlap. Different uncertainty and sample composition mean they do not establish a group difference, score performance, shared causality or lack of effect in either group. A subsequent difficulty-walking follow-up did not survive correction across its selected variants or seven score summaries. Weakness and difficulty walking are EHR codes, not FI or FFS. A separate intersection–union check found no candidate with nominal evidence for both coded insomnia and weakness within either tested group; this does not rule out shared effects. [AoU variant follow-up](stage6_aou_full_candidate_association_review.md); [EUR score sensitivity](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_sensitivity_review.md); [AFR sensitivity](stage6_aou_afr_weighted_z_sensitivity_review.md); [walking follow-up](stage6_aou_eur_walking_followup.md).

**Practical next steps.** (1) Obtain an independent cohort with permitted linked genotypes and observed sleep and frailty; confirm exact alleles, sample and relative overlap, phenotype timing, event numbers, and ancestry before analysis. Compare original FI, sleep-excluded FI and sleep-plus-fatigue-excluded FI alongside a defined physical phenotype. (2) Freeze score definitions before the held-out evaluation, compare with age/sex and appropriate clinical baselines, and report paired discrimination and calibration with uncertainty. (3) Extend the existing [variant–gene–cell–phenotype evidence graph](stage6_evidence_graph_readme.md) with provenance, missingness and uncertainty, and compare simple statistical baselines with any embedding model using held-out genomic regions. Its gene links are positional, molecular-set membership is not disease colocalization, and its allele conventions must not be substituted for either score file. AlphaGenome predictions can prioritize experiments but are not patient outcomes; observe applicable terms and do not train a model on covered outputs without confirming rights. [Validation inventory](independent_outcome_validation_cohort_feasibility.md); [AI plan and caveats](mentor_insomnia_frailty_synthesis_2026-10-28.md).

**Data status.** No freely public cohort meeting the full linked-genotype and outcome requirements has been established. NHATS is a potential no-fee controlled-access route, subject to approvals and coverage checks; PISA and others require project-specific requests with fees or eligibility unverified. Public phenotype and GWAS summary data support background and mechanistic follow-up, not individual score validation. [Cohort review](independent_outcome_validation_cohort_feasibility.md).

**Additional validation leads.** Framingham describes no data-access service fee through dbGaP or BioLINCC and has same-exam sleep questions and published Fried and 37-item FI measures, but linked genotypes, eligibility, complete FI items and cohort independence still require verification. CHS has published sleep-symptom measures, frailty and genetic datasets with access and project-specific costs unresolved. Both cohorts contributed to a gait GWAS examined in this project, so any use as a held-out test needs a participant and decision-history audit. [Framingham](framingham_no_fee_validation_route.md); [CHS](cardiovascular_health_study_validation_feasibility.md).

SOF is a potential complementary older-women cohort with controlled-access genotypes, published sleep measures and a distinct three-item physical-frailty measure. The required participant linkage and variant coverage are unverified; SOF also contributed to the gait GWAS already inspected. [SOF feasibility](sof_women_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

**Most useful newer data leads.** The 1958 British birth cohort has a published age-50 FI, a distinct sleep-maintenance question and genome-wide data offered without charge after application. Its FI itself contains two sleep items and fatigue; the overlap between genetic and phenotype samples, target marker coverage and independence from discovery cohorts are unverified. CLS says the separate EGA genetic release is not linked to survey data, but offers a distinct application process for genetic data linked to a bespoke survey dataset. Approval and availability for our exact variables are unverified. [NCDS feasibility](stage6_ncds_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md). GEPITEL is an actual openly downloadable genotype archive from an older-adult cohort with published Fried frailty measurements, but the public genotype file lacks phenotype values and its tested array has no candidate marker matches by the checked IDs or coordinates. It cannot currently score our prototypes. [GEPITEL audit](stage6_gepitel_public_validation_feasibility.md).

**Canadian comparison and its limits.** In the Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging, public documentation describes baseline sleep questions, genotyping, and published FI and Fried-type measures in the Comprehensive cohort. The 93-item FI contains a restless-sleep item and shares exhaustion, grip and walking inputs with the physical phenotype, so their agreement is partly a comparison of overlapping measurements. The published supplement's walking-time cutoff is inconsistent and needs source-code or author clarification. A trainee fee waiver is conditional on eligibility; whether it covers the exact genotype files, and the usable linked sample, remain unverified. Its linked participant data are controlled access. The public CLSA PheWeb site still displayed maintenance on 29 October, with restoration stated for November 1; neither phenotype availability nor score associations can be checked there yet. [CLSA feasibility](clsa_trainee_waiver_validation_feasibility.md); [item audit](clsa_fi_sleep_fatigue_overlap_audit.md); [public summary-data lead](stage6_canadian_public_gwas_feasibility.md).

InCHIANTI has a published 42-item FI without an explicitly named sleep item, but with exhaustion and physical-function items, and has prior genotyping. The cost and availability of linked insomnia and genetic records are unknown, and it contributed to a gait GWAS previously examined by this project. It is a secondary controlled-access lead, not a completed validation. [InCHIANTI](inchianti_score_validation_feasibility.md).

**Candidate direction context.** Under the source-supported ALT-effect assumption, rs12536335 in the FOXP2 region has negative G-allele association signs for coded insomnia, difficulty walking and malaise/fatigue in All of Us EUR results. The insomnia–walking joint screen did not pass correction across 33 candidates, these outcomes can overlap, and direction or proximity does not establish causality or score performance. [Four-outcome context](stage6_foxp2_aou_eur_direction_context.md).

**Fatigue sensitivity.** A postselected public All of Us screen returned associations for all 33 candidates in coded insomnia and broad malaise/fatigue. Five have nominal p < 0.05 for both, but none passes BH adjustment within the 33-candidate max-p screen. Fatigue is not FI or FFS and can overlap directly with insomnia, so this is not evidence of shared causal frailty risk. [Details](stage6_aou_insomnia_fatigue_eur_conjunction_review.md).

**Additional candidate follow-up.** Published HUNT and Partners insomnia tables cover six of the 33 unique candidates; rs113851554 near MEIS1 has a nominal insomnia association in both. Among the five of these in the 15-marker prototype, three have unadjusted HUNT insomnia p < 0.05 but none has unadjusted TwinGene FI p < 0.05. TwinGene contributed to candidate selection, nonsignificant findings do not show no effect, and these postselected comparisons are not score validation or evidence of a shared causal variant. [HUNT and Partners](stage6_lane2019_candidate_replication_review.md); [TwinGene comparison](stage6_hunt_twingene_fi_candidate_context.md).

**External summary-data follow-up.** A Japanese insomnia GWAS matched 18 of the 22 counted variants and 13 of the 15 weighted-prototype variants, but had only 389 cases. An approximate 14-marker count association was positive in an external Japanese reference-LD sensitivity; the subset and analysis were postselected, its allele orientation and sample independence remain unverified, and rare case dosages limit calibration. It is neither a direct score regression nor a shared sleep–frailty outcome. [Japanese candidate review](stage6_bbj_insomnia_candidate_review.md); [matched-subset method](stage6_bbj_insomnia_matched14_z_review.md). A [reference-genotype check](stage6_bbj_subset_reference_correlation_review.md) shows that this partial count correlates with the complete count in small reference groups, but says nothing about whether either predicts an outcome.

**New cohort lead:** ROS/MAP has published frailty-index and genome-wide genotype analyses, and lists sleep measures. It requires access review and an institutional agreement; the public availability tool reports 401 MAP participants flagged for both a sleep questionnaire and at least one genotype/WGS dataset, but usable frailty outcomes, exact marker coverage, discovery overlap and cost remain unverified. [ROS/MAP feasibility](stage6_rosmap_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

A published 41-deficit MAP FI has no explicitly named sleep item but does include two fatigue items; it offers a candidate phenotype for a no-explicit-sleep-item and fatigue-excluded comparison if access and item-level data are available. [MAP item audit](stage6_map_fi_sleep_item_definition_review.md).

LASA has a published three-question sleep-symptom measure and a 32-item FI with no explicitly named sleep item but two fatigue-related items; it may be useful for a future paired phenotype analysis if linked-data access, costs and genetic coverage are resolved. [LASA definitions](stage6_lasa_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

A [2026 multi-omics frailty preprint](stage6_multiprs_frailty_external_study_review.md) provides a comparison for analyzing FI, FFS and frailty domains alongside molecular data. Its exposures are chronic pain and rheumatoid arthritis, not insomnia, and it does not validate our score or establish a sleep mechanism. Its generated data are reported as available upon request rather than as an open participant-level download.

**Exploratory sequence-model update.** A simple neuronal reporter model showed large changes in variant rankings when sequence orientation was reversed. An alternative strand-invariant feature representation was implemented and passes an exact orientation-invariance check; its AUROC was 0.482 in one selected reporter holdout and 0.569 in a second. The redesign followed inspection of both test sets, so these are exploratory, not independent validation or evidence that either model predicts patient outcomes. [Methods and limitations](stage6_matoba_external_baseline_transfer_review.md).

A subsequent cross-assay check of the fixed strand-invariant baseline on 6,573 selected Salomon neuronal reporter pairs gave AUROC **0.501** and average precision **0.0234**, close to the 0.0225 positive-call prevalence. A gene-group bootstrap range for AUROC was 0.461–0.540 under imperfect independence assumptions. The assay and significance calls differ from the training source, and this is a post hoc molecular benchmark, not a patient-risk test or an AlphaGenome evaluation. No project candidate exactly matched the assayed contrasts. [Cross-assay report](stage6_salomon_neuronal_reporter_feasibility.md).

**Public phenotypic comparison.** In NHANES adults aged 60 or older, reported sleep trouble was positively associated with both an adapted deficit index and a modified physical-domain count in the same participants. A post hoc, paired comparison after expressing both outcomes as fractions of their items had intervals crossing zero in both survey cycles; it does not establish that one measure is more strongly related to sleep. The physical count is not the discovery FFS, the index is not a validated FI, and these data do not evaluate genetic scores. [Definitions and results](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison_review.md).

**Precomputed sleep PGS route.** ELSA distributes precomputed insomnia and other sleep scores to registered UK Data Service users under a licence. UK Data Service states its collections are free to download for non-commercial use, subject to access conditions; the separate linked-genotype service has charges. Linking the existing scores to ELSA phenotypes could test a sleep PGS against frailty after access and sample checks, but would not validate this project's selected marker scores. Its 2019 insomnia score has discovery-source overlap with some of our analyses. [Feasibility and analysis plan](elsa_precomputed_sleep_pgs_route.md).

**Additional cohort lead.** The Childhood Cancer Survivor Study has published modified physical frailty and later sleep-quality measurements in 9,044 survivors, and separately describes genome-wide genetic data. The number with all required data and score-variant coverage is unknown; the population is much younger and cancer-selected, and data access and permitted outputs need review. [CCSS feasibility](ccss_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

**Open portal exploration.** In SJLIFE, an existing insomnia PGS and right-hand grip were jointly available in 3,203 complete cases for a post hoc model adjusting for sex, baseline age and two ancestry PCs. The unscaled-score coefficient was −2.135 kg per unit (95% CI −6.343 to 2.073; p=0.3199). A post hoc adult-baseline-age sensitivity had 2,827 complete cases and coefficient −2.667 kg (95% CI −6.700 to 1.367; p=0.195). Two PCs and uncertain exact visit timing limit interpretation; this is a single physical measure in childhood-cancer survivors, not validation of the project's scores. [Model details](sjlife_insomnia_pgs_grip_exploration.md).


**Recent free-data findings and decision.** A public SJLIFE portal variant lookup found two project markers, but separate variant-list and locus regression routes failed with server-side genotype-file errors; no marker association or custom weighted score was obtained. A published PGS001932 insomnia score had raw AUC 0.542 against FinnGen's insomnia endpoint, and its baseline short/long sleep-duration associations in the Singapore Chinese Health Study were uncertain. These are different outcomes and cohorts and do not test our selected scores. [SJLIFE marker screen](sjlife_project_variant_portal_screen.md); [FinnGen published score](pgs_browser_webgwas_free_resource_review.md); [Singapore published score](schs_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md). The Singapore cohort has sleep, a four-component modified frailty phenotype, and genetic data in different published subsets, but their overlap and marker coverage are unverified; participant data are not public and a third-party directory reports fees. It is not a demonstrated free route.

WebGWAS supplied an approximate UKB G47 sleep-disorder AND R53 fatigue GWAS, with phenotype-fit R² 50.3% and 15 of 33 unique project markers located. This is neither insomnia with FI/FFS nor independent replication. There is a plausible code-based explanation for differing anonymized-phenotype and GWAS sample-size fields, but deployment-specific confirmation is missing. [Results and limitations](webgwas_sleep_fatigue_candidate_screen.md). The strongest actionable no-fee cohort lead remains NCDS through the separate linked-genotype/phenotype application, subject to eligibility and actual coverage; do not confuse this with its unlinked EGA release.

**Mexican cohort lead.** MHAS has documented sleep-onset questions, frailty measures and genetic collections, but the exact linked sample and genetic-data fees are unverified. Its [data-use page](https://www.mhasweb.org/DataProducts/Linkages.aspx) explicitly prohibits LLM/AI processing of MHAS-distributed microdata, including public files. No MHAS microdata were accessed; a human-led analysis would require the applicable data-use arrangements. [Feasibility review](mhas_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

**Additional ARIC lead.** A 2026 sleep-score paper used 1,550 ARIC participants with sleep and genetic-score data at Visit 4; separate ARIC work established a five-component frailty phenotype and a cumulative FI at Visit 5. The true cross-visit, genotype and outcome intersection and marker coverage are unknown. Individual-level genetics require controlled access; free registration does not prove the complete linked analysis is fee-free. ARIC contributed to the CHARGE gait analysis already examined here, so study-history and participant overlap must be assessed before any held-out use. The published sleep score and its insomnia association are not our score or a frailty test. [Feasibility review](aric_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

**NCDS later physical-function opportunity.** Public documentation identifies age-62 measured grip and timed-walk variables, with 6,177 and 5,998 respective fieldwork responses. Their overlap with QC-passing genotypes and the sleep measures is unverified. These measures could support separate physical-function score tests after approved linkage; they are not FI, FFS, or validation of a joint score. [Age-62 feasibility](ncds_age62_grip_gait_genetic_validation_lead.md).

**NCDS prior frailty outcome evidence.** In an NCDS publication, the observed 34-item age-50 FI's highest quintile was associated with mortality at ages 50–58 (sex-adjusted HR 4.07, 95% CI 2.64–6.25). This is not a genetic-score validation. The paper reports robustness to separate item/domain removal, but the exact sleep-domain sensitivity was not available in the article text and must not be inferred. [Prior evidence and caveats](ncds_34_fi_prior_mortality_evidence.md).

**Monocyte ML lead.** A small [2026 preprint](stage6_monocyte_frailty_ml_public_feasibility.md) models primary monocyte behavior and physical frailty, providing a possible experimental context. It does not establish linked insomnia, project genotypes or score validation, and cell-level splitting must not be confused with independent donor-level evaluation.

**Public muscle data.** [GSE167186](stage6_gse167186_skeletal_muscle_frailty_review.md) provides skeletal-muscle RNA counts and physical-function metadata in a male cohort. An exploratory age-adjusted count model found a FOXP2 expression difference passing BH across four selected genes, whereas a simpler model did not; the same count method found no clear FOXP2 difference in a separate public frailty cohort (participant overlap unverified). Bulk expression cannot identify a cell type or causal genetic mechanism, and insomnia and linked project genotypes were not established.

**FOXP2 muscle bridge.** A [variant–chromatin–expression evidence crosswalk](foxp2_muscle_genetic_evidence_bridge.md) distinguishes the type-I fiber molecular credible-set membership of rs12536335 from the inconsistent bulk FOXP2 expression comparisons; no variant-to-FOXP2 muscle mechanism has been established.

**Sleep, strength and broader disease context.** A 2026 phenome-wide Mendelian-randomization paper reports associations of insomnia instruments with several health outcomes, including lower handgrip strength. Its exposure and two listed grip GWAS derive from UK Biobank, raising participant-overlap questions; detailed figure-to-dataset mapping has not been confirmed. The instrument is not our score and handgrip is only one physical component. [Source and limitations](stage6_hernandez_sleep_phenome_mr_review.md).

**Circadian context in an older Mexican cohort.** A 2026 COSFAMM paper measured insomnia, Fried frailty, chronotype and PBMC clock-gene expression. Its small cross-sectional expression subset and single morning sample support a hypothesis, not a causal variant or score validation. Its printed ACTB/REVERBA primer pairs and expression-subset counts need clarification. [Study review](stage6_cosfamm_clock_sleep_frailty_review.md).

### Additional example for comparing frailty measures

In Look AHEAD, published analyses reported lower deficit-accumulation FI scores in the lifestyle-intervention group, but did not detect a difference in a later modified physical-frailty assessment. The latter used four Fried components and omitted weight loss; differences in time, participants, definitions and precision prevent treating this as a paired test of FI versus the five-component FFS. The FI includes a sleep disturbance item, making a sleep-excluded sensitivity relevant. See the [source and access audit](stage6_look_ahead_fi_ffs_sleep_feasibility.md). Look AHEAD genetic data are reported as unavailable publicly, so this is background rather than independent score validation.

**Exploratory Korean BMI context.** A public [KoGES follow-up](stage6_koges_bmi_candidate_followup_review.md) found exact position and allele-pair BMI results for 24 of 33 project variants; four passed a 33-candidate BH adjustment based on browser-rounded p values. These are marginal associations with rank-normalized BMI, not insomnia or frailty, and prior candidate and phenotype selection restricts their interpretation. Two chromosome 2 candidates are weakly correlated in a 504-person East Asian reference, but Korean cohort LD and conditional effects have not been tested. No causal gene or individual score performance follows from these results. [Allele and LD follow-up](stage6_koges_bmi_allele_ld_followup.md).
