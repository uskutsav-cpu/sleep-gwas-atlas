# Candidate-gene expression in human muscle stem cells

I matched stable gene IDs in the current variant-to-gene table to the gene annotation in the Kedlian human muscle stem-cell subset. Of 182 candidate Ensembl IDs in that table, **134 mapped uniquely to the file**, 48 were not present, and none had duplicate mappings. The [expression table](stage4_human_musc_candidate_gene_expression.csv) contains 536 rows: the 134 measured genes across four cell annotations. The [coverage file](stage4_human_musc_gene_expression_coverage.json) lists missing IDs. Absence from this particular matrix is not evidence that a gene is absent from muscle or from other tissues; some candidates with no stable ID in the initial table are outside this count.

For each gene and subtype, I calculated the mean of each eligible donor's average normalized expression, weighting donors equally, and the median of donor-specific fractions of cells with a positive matrix value. A donor was eligible for a subtype if it contributed at least 10 cells. The broad MuSC group had 12 eligible donors, ICAM1+ and TNFRSF12A+ each had 11, and MYOG+ had three. The matrix is treated as already normalized; its precise original processing has not been independently verified beyond the sampled checks in the [input receipt](stage4_human_musc_input_receipt.json).

For example, SMAD5 expression was detected in all 12 eligible donors in the broad MuSC group, with a median donor-specific detected-cell fraction of 0.207. This is descriptive RNA detection, not evidence of an SMAD5 genetic effect in those cells. Expression can be sparse and affected by capture efficiency, and a zero is not a verified biological absence. No formal differential-expression, donor-aware enrichment, variant-effect or frailty comparison is made.

The [network](stage4_variant_gene_phenotype_network_scaffold.csv) includes 498 gene-to-cell edges where at least one eligible donor had a positive detection. Edges with zero median detection remain possible when some, but fewer than half, of donors show expression; their counts are explicit. These expression edges are not significance tests. The separate cell-to-insomnia scDRS edges test a genome-wide gene program and **do not make each expressed candidate gene associated with insomnia**. Nor does connecting a bulk or cell-specific eQTL edge to an expression edge establish mediation or a causal disease pathway.

The updated extraction reads the atlas in bounded row blocks and includes Ensembl IDs mapped from the ABC proxy target list; its SMAD5 donor count and median agree with the prior extraction. ABC target assignments remain model predictions.
