# Fried frailty score: external validation and reporting checks

[Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/) developed an FFS polygenic score from their UK Biobank GWAS and evaluated it in the Health and Retirement Study (HRS), an older, separate US cohort. Their paper reports 9,720 participants, mean age 73 (SD 7), and 890,487 SNPs in the applied score. The score-only AUC was 0.57 (95% CI 0.56–0.59); the paper reports AUC 0.72 (0.70–0.73) for a model combining the score, age, sex and 10 ancestry principal components. This is evidence that the **full FFS-derived score**, in this evaluation, carries information about frailty. It is not validation of the project's 15 markers or an insomnia–frailty score. AUC 0.72 cannot be attributed to the score alone, and comparing the two reported AUCs does not isolate the incremental contribution over age, sex and PCs; that requires the corresponding covariate-only model and a paired comparison. Calibration, absolute risk and prospective clinical usefulness are not established by these numbers.

## Checks of the reported association

The paper reports OR 1.27 per SD (95% CI 1.22–1.31), p = 1.3×10⁻¹¹. If these refer to the **same** estimate and a conventional two-sided Wald test and log-symmetric 95% Wald interval, the interval implies SE ≈ [ln(1.31) − ln(1.22)] / (2×1.96) = 0.01816. Using ln(1.27) gives z ≈ 13.16 and p ≈ 1.4×10⁻³⁹, not 1.3×10⁻¹¹. The geometric midpoint of the reported limits is 1.264, versus the printed point estimate 1.27. Ordinary rounding to two decimals cannot plausibly explain the large p-value gap. **This is a conditional consistency check, not a corrected result:** non-Wald intervals, a different test or outcome, or a reporting discrepancy could explain it. The original analysis output is needed to resolve which quantity or method applies.

## Checks of the lead-variant sign test

The same paper reports that 18 of 22 eligible variants or proxies had the same direction as in UK Biobank, and gives a binomial sign-test p = 0.00042. Under an ordinary independent fair-coin binomial null, Pr(X ≥ 18 | n=22, p=0.5) = 0.00217, and the exact symmetric two-sided p is 0.00434. The reported 0.00042 is close to the *one-sided* tail for 19 or more of 22 (0.000428), but this numerical resemblance does not establish a cause or which count/test the authors intended. Independence, proxy selection and any unreported test definition also matter. The article supports a broadly concordant direction pattern, but the reported exact p-value and count should be checked against the underlying replication table rather than silently substituted.

## What the validation can and cannot say about the 15 candidates

Of 37 lead FFS variants, the paper says 22 were eligible or well proxied in HRS and three passed its 5% FDR criterion. The companion [supplement workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-023-00771-z/MediaObjects/11357_2023_771_MOESM2_ESM.xlsx) reviewed here lists discovery loci and other post-GWAS analyses but does not provide a machine-readable HRS variant-level replication table. The paper’s figure does not supply an independently verified lookup for all 15 project candidates. Consequently, no candidate has been labeled HRS-replicated from this screen. The full FFS score is distinct from both the 15-row prototype and the Foote et al. FFS score, despite using the same discovery GWAS.

An author query should request the HRS variant-level results, proxy identities and LD, genotype harmonization, precise FFS outcome and model, and the original score association estimate, interval, test and covariate-only AUC. HRS genotype data are controlled-access; no individual-level HRS data were obtained or evaluated here.

A subsequent [exact-lead and LD screen](stage6_ye_ffs_lead_proxy_screen_review.md) found no direct match to either project marker list; the 22-marker candidate rs28528997 is correlated in a European reference panel with a Ye discovery lead, but that lead’s HRS result remains unverified.
