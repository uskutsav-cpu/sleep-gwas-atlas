# CD4-naïve chromatin peak versus frailty and insomnia GWAS

We compared the TenK10K CD4-naïve chromatin QTL for peak `chr5:136132117-136134284` with three existing project GWAS: Mak's FI and physical frailty (FP), and FinnGen R13 insomnia. This is a **post hoc sensitivity analysis**, not an independent score validation. Mak FI and FP use UK Biobank; FinnGen insomnia is an EHR endpoint, and cohort and relative overlap with all project sources remains unverified.

Using a single-causal-variant approximate Bayes factor model, illustrative priors p1 = p2 = 10⁻⁴, p12 = 10⁻⁵, and QTL effect prior SD = 0.15, the results are:

| Disease phenotype | Matched variants | QTL only (H1) | Distinct QTL and GWAS signals (H3) | Shared signal (H4) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mak FI | 3,359 | 0.0027 | 0.9964 | 0.0010 |
| Mak FP | 3,359 | 0.9726 | 0.0158 | 0.0116 |
| FinnGen insomnia | 3,371 | 0.9004 | 0.0544 | 0.0453 |

Under these assumptions, FI favors distinct signals. For FP and insomnia, the data favor a chromatin QTL without sufficiently clear GWAS evidence in this tested set; they **do not establish either shared or distinct disease signals**. Across the tested QTL effect priors 0.075–0.30 and p12 10⁻⁶–5×10⁻⁵, H4 ranged 0.000096–0.00487 for FI, 0.00117–0.0556 for FP, and 0.00471–0.1924 for insomnia. These are model posteriors, not p-values, proof of no sharing or probabilities that a specific gene causes disease. Priors are illustrative and the QTL effect scale may not make these values universally appropriate.

The starting peak had 5,023 source rows. We excluded 1,530 indel or palindromic rows; 3,493 nonpalindromic biallelic SNPs with source ALT frequency 1–99% had a unique positive-strand coordinate liftover. Both the candidate rs7734045 and the peak's published lead (`5:136187682:T:G`) survived the disease-matching filter. The SMAD5 molecular-colocalization table's top SNP `5:136131620:C:G` is palindromic and was **excluded**: the tested set therefore misses a potentially informative molecular variant. The peak signal may contain multiple causal variants, and a single-signal model cannot resolve them. Unknown per-variant QTL imputation quality, limited regional cache coverage, differences in ancestry and phenotype model, missing variants, LD and sample overlap all restrict the conclusions. The association is for chromatin accessibility, not SMAD5 expression directly.

An exact allele-pair match and positive-strand coordinate liftover were required, with source and GWAS ALT frequency 1–99%. One Mak-matched SNP (`5:135777788:G:A`) had a 0.517 frequency difference and was excluded in the primary sensitivity using an illustrative 0.2 absolute-difference cutoff. Omitting that SNP did not materially change the baseline posterior; all other Mak pairs and all FinnGen pairs passed this check. This screen does not resolve multiallelic representation or all strand/reference errors. The source parquet was read by ranges rather than fully downloaded; its complete LFS digest was not independently verified.

The [full matched-variant sensitivity](stage6_tenk10k_peak_af20_disease_coloc_sensitivity.csv), [coverage receipt](stage6_tenk10k_peak_af20_disease_coloc_receipt.json), and aligned input files preserve results. `work/stage5_env/bin/python work/tenk10k_peak_disease_coloc_af20.py` reproduces the calculations using the saved peak and regional files. An independent implementation of the coloc 5.2.3 ABF formulas reproduced the three baseline calculations to <10⁻¹⁰: `Rscript work/stage6_tenk10k_coloc_validate.R` and the [formula-check results](stage6_tenk10k_peak_coloc_formula_check.csv). This checks arithmetic, not source harmonization or scientific assumptions. The unfiltered initial calculation is retained separately as a sensitivity.

The [molecular variant follow-up](stage6_tenk10k_smad5_variant_caqtl_review.md) and [SMAD5 eQTL–chromatin evidence](stage6_tenk10k_smad5_molecular_review.md) explain the molecular source and its provisional correction status. Independent biological validation, multi-signal analysis with matched target LD and a true external FI/FFS score evaluation remain outstanding.

## Palindromic-variant sensitivity

A subsequent post hoc sensitivity retained A/T and C/G SNPs only when the TenK10K ALT frequency was outside 0.4–0.6 and differed by no more than 0.1 from the disease source's aligned ALT frequency. The existing 0.2 frequency-difference cutoff remained in place for all variants; indels still remained excluded. This criterion reduces orientation ambiguity but **cannot prove strand or allele correctness**, particularly across different populations or multi-allelic representations. The candidate and molecular coloc top SNP `5:136131620:C:G` were present in all three matched datasets; its ALT frequencies were 0.291 in TenK10K, 0.308 in Mak and 0.301 in FinnGen.

The sensitivity retained 3,877 variants for each Mak phenotype and 3,879 for FinnGen insomnia. At the same illustrative priors, FI H3 = 0.99637 and H4 = 0.00097; FP H1 = 0.97060, H3 = 0.01785 and H4 = 0.01155; insomnia H1 = 0.89040, H3 = 0.06547 and H4 = 0.04413. The qualitative conclusions are unchanged: FI favors distinct signals under this single-signal model, while FP and insomnia are not resolved. QTL prior and shared-prior sensitivity ranges for H4 were 0.000096–0.00484, 0.00116–0.0553 and 0.00459–0.1883, respectively. This postselected, frequency-based inclusion does not repair unmeasured LD, omitted indels, multiple causal signals, missing variant quality or uncertain sample overlap.
