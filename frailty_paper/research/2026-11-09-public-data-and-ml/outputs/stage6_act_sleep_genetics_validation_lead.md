# Adult Changes in Thought: sleep and genetics access lead

Checked 29 October 2026. The University of Washington's [aging data resource inventory](https://halo.dlmp.uw.edu/big-aging-database-resources/) points to the [Adult Changes in Thought (ACT) repository](https://actagingresearch.org/resources/act-data-repository), an older-adult longitudinal cohort. ACT describes a take-home questionnaire with sleep questions beginning in April 2016 and a subset with device-based sleep measurement. The study also documents a structured insomnia symptom instrument, as described below. The [ACT CES-D form summary](https://www.actagingresearch.org/index.php/resources/act-data-repository/biennial-study-visit-data/form_ces-d) independently documents a restless-sleep item in its 11-item version collected since 1994, with visit-level response options. This is a single depressive-symptom sleep item, not a clinical insomnia diagnosis or a comprehensive insomnia scale.

ACT's [data sharing plan](https://www.actagingresearch.org/download_file/14d8501c-12fe-4033-84c9-c25b6927f269/9) describes genome-wide SNP data and whole-genome/exome sequencing among its resources, while the [dbGaP eMERGE release phs000234](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000234.v2.p2) describes 2,613 ACT participants with viable specimens for genotyping. That historical figure is not the number with usable current genotypes, our 33 variants, post-2016 sleep outcomes, or frailty data. The same release includes other cohorts; 3,756 consented subjects is not the ACT-only number. Genomic files require authorized access.

A [published ACT frailty analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738027/) constructed a modified five-component Fried phenotype in 2,619 participants, requiring complete components and excluding selected conditions. It used grip strength, gait speed below 0.6 m/s, fewer than three exercise episodes per week, weight loss between visits, and either of two CES-D exhaustion items; these definitions differ from the project discovery phenotype. Its table says weight loss of at least 7.5%, whereas the narrative says more than 7.5%; consult source code before reproducing. The 2,619 phenotype-analysis count and the 2,613 genotyping-specimen count are from different selections and cannot be assumed to refer to the same people. The 11-item CES-D includes both restless sleep and the two exhaustion questions; related symptom content and the depression context matter when interpreting a sleep–frailty association. We have not established an FI or whether all five frailty components and the sleep item are available at the same visit for authorized genotyped participants. A published GWAS in an ACT autopsy subset shows that some ACT participants have genome-wide imputed data ([Mukherjee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.043573)), but it does not establish access or coverage for our selected variants. Record linkage across authorized genomic and ACT sleep/frailty sources, consent, overlapping samples and study participants' survival to later sleep assessments must be checked.

The ACT ancillary-study [policy](https://actagingresearch.org/application/files/4016/6250/8791/ACT_ancillary_study_policy_FINAL_9-6-2022.pdf) says a one-time data-processing fee **may** apply to a type of ancillary study; it does not establish the charge for this project's exact data request. We have not verified an unrestricted individual-level download or no-fee access. ACT is therefore a controlled-access feasibility lead, not validated free data or a completed score test.

A useful next check is to inspect ACT and dbGaP data dictionaries for genotype-linked forms and relevant visits, then verify the permitted intersection, the exact 33 variant contrasts and imputation quality, pricing, ancestry and any discovery overlap before seeking access.

## Public data dictionary: a clearer route to sleep and frailty measures

The [public 2025-freeze data dictionary](https://www.actagingresearch.org/download_file/f1058bf6-f034-46ba-94bf-96af632448a4/9) describes a seven-item **Insomnia Severity Index** in `Form66 - Sleep`. The individual items and `ISI_Score` are potentially available only starting in **2022**; `ISI_Score` is recorded only when all seven items have valid responses. The ISI is an insomnia symptom-severity measure, not necessarily a clinical diagnosis. The same form lists an eight-item PROMIS sleep-disturbance T-score potentially available from 2016, conditional on six valid items. One ISI item asks about daytime interference including fatigue, so clinical symptom overlap with physical-frailty exhaustion must be considered.

The dictionary lists `SUBJECT` and `VISIT` as form linkage keys and describes visit-level measured `Grip_Strength`; `Form73_Form23 - CESD` contains the effort and restless-sleep items. Its person-level `Genetics - PRS` tab lists precomputed vascular and Alzheimer's scores, but these are not our 15- or 22-marker prototypes and cannot reconstruct them. The dictionary says PRS availability depends on consent and historical GWAS inclusion. A genetic source that actually supplies our variant dosages and permits linkage remains necessary. An ACT Alzheimer score is annotated as overfit because ACT participants contributed to that particular discovery GWAS; this is a reminder to audit each score's discovery overlap, not evidence our prototypes share ACT participants.

The late start of ISI makes the proportion of the earlier genotyping cohorts with same-visit sleep and frailty data especially uncertain. A documented dictionary variable or study-wide sample count does not prove a nonempty, adequately sized permitted intersection. A future request should ask separately for `SUBJECT`-linkable genotype data, the relevant sleep and frailty forms and visits, participant and marker QC, complete-case counts, costs, and institutional and consent eligibility. No participant-level information has been downloaded.

## Controlled access and possible lower-cost genetic route

The NIAGADS [ADSP umbrella release ng00067.v18](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00067-v18/) lists **1,397 ACT participants and 1,553 samples** in its cohort table. This is a cohort inventory across the resource, not a verified count of ACT participants in any one joint-call file, and it cannot be added to the 2,613 historical dbGaP participants without deduplication. The resource includes WGS and WES data, but the modality and coverage of the ACT participants relevant to this project remain to be established. The 2025 release also lists harmonized phenotype domains, but the listing does not establish that ACT ISI or the five frailty components are included in those files.

The [NIAGADS FAQ](https://adsp.niagads.org/frequently-asked-questions/) says NIAGADS covers download costs for most ADSP data, including joint genotype-called project-level VCFs and associated phenotypes and metadata; other files such as CRAMs, individual gVCFs and structural-variant VCFs may use requester-pays billing. This is a possible route to no download charge for a specific eligible file, not a guarantee that the entire project is free. An approved NIAGADS data request and permitted research use are required.

The [ACT sharing plan](https://www.actagingresearch.org/download_file/14d8501c-12fe-4033-84c9-c25b6927f269/9) states that external researchers seeking ACT repository data are expected to collaborate with an ACT investigator and submit an ACT proposal for committee review; it describes a collaborative research agreement before data release. Joining NIAGADS genetic records to ACT repository sleep and frailty data would additionally require verification of consent, keys, permissions and the correct processes at both sources. Neither an ADSP inventory count nor a general download-cost policy grants that linkage or proves the total project is no-fee.

A [variable-level feasibility crosswalk](stage6_act_candidate_variable_crosswalk.md) maps the public dictionary to the five historical frailty components and insomnia measures, including missingness and timing concerns. It has not been applied to participant data.

A [manufacturer array design screen](stage6_act_array_manifest_review.md) found exact rsID and position matches for 4/15 and 5/22 project candidates on one public Human660W-Quad manifest; this does not determine imputed or sequenced variant coverage or ACT post-QC genotypes.

## ACT aggregate cohort-query tool

The [ACT Data Query Tool](https://act.kpwhri.org/dqt/) is intended to generate preliminary feasibility counts, including information on genetic data availability, according to ACT's sharing plan. On 29 October 2026 its public home page requested a name, email address, affiliation and reason for visit before enabling submission; attempting to select the query tab did not open it. No personal details were entered or submitted, and no cohort-intersection count was obtained. The site states that its counts are preliminary and should not be used in publications, grants or presentations without ACT permission. It is an aggregate feasibility tool, not a source of participant-level data or genetic-score validation. A user with appropriate details may choose to use it; its actual coverage of ISI, five frailty components, genetic markers and their intersection remains unverified.

## Published sleep and physical-function overlap

A [2025 ACT physical-function study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12110824/) analyzed 905 selected activity-monitoring participants with longitudinal physical-performance and activities-of-daily-living measures. Its PROMIS sleep-disturbance outcome table reports 64 missing values within this analytic cohort, implying 841 with a nonmissing PROMIS score under that table's accounting. This is evidence that those forms have been used together in a research sample, not a count of participants with genotypes, ISI, all five Fried criteria, or usable data for our analysis. Study selection and timing must be checked before extrapolating to the broader ACT cohort.

The authors report no observed association between the examined physical-function trajectories and sleep quality, and caution that the selected sample had relatively little reported sleep disturbance. They used self-reported sleep measures, including a PROMIS score; the 905-person analysis is not a genetic-score test and does not establish that insomnia and frailty are unrelated. The publication says its participant datasets require an appropriate request and data-sharing agreements. In the ACT sharing plan, future public-use datasets are described as non-linkable to each other; the plan does not establish that a relevant downloadable linked dataset has been released.
