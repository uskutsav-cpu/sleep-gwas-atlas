# MEIS1-AS3 cerebellum eQTL and hospital frailty: exploratory comparison

The available hospital-frailty data do not produce a qualifying SuSiE credible set in this restricted region. Consequently, this run produces **no colocalization posterior**, and it cannot establish either a shared causal variant or absence of sharing. This is a locus-level sensitivity analysis using public summary statistics, not validation of the project's individual-level scores. Hospital frailty risk score (HFRS), based on hospital diagnostic codes, is distinct from a deficit-accumulation frailty index and Fried physical frailty.

## Data and approach

We compared nominal GTEx v8 cerebellum MEIS1-AS3 expression associations from [eQTL Catalogue QTD000166](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/eQTL/sumstats/QTS000015/QTD000166/) with the available FinnGen HFRS GWAS, GCST90707291, across chr2:66,350,000–66,750,000 on GRCh38. This is an exploratory, postselected window, substantially narrower than the approximately 2 Mb cis-region used by the Catalogue. The expression trait is the antisense transcript MEIS1-AS3; these data do not demonstrate an effect on MEIS1 protein expression.

The input had 995 non-palindromic variants common to both association datasets. Matching the external gnomAD non-Finnish European (NFE) and Finnish (FIN) LD indices left 958 shared variants; all passed the absolute allele-frequency difference threshold of 0.20 against their corresponding source datasets. The largest observed differences were 0.0913 for eQTL versus NFE and 0.0385 for HFRS versus FIN. Alleles were aligned to the eQTL ALT allele. These checks can detect some errors but do not prove correct strand, population matching, or LD calibration. The two LD matrices were separately aligned to the same variant ordering. The FIN matrix had numerical rank 794 of 958 at the recorded tolerance, while NFE had rank 958.

We fitted SuSiE RSS with up to 10 effects and 95% credible-set coverage, using NFE LD and n=209 for expression and FIN LD and nominal total n=500,737 for HFRS. Both models converged. The expression fit returned one credible set and maximum PIP 0.592; the HFRS fit returned zero credible sets and maximum PIP 0.122. These PIPs are conditional on the included variants, region, external LD, sample-size assumptions and model. The [original study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y) analyzed HFRS as a continuous outcome using a linear mixed model; the previously included warning about case–control effective sample size was inapplicable and has been removed. The total N is still a study-level input and may not capture variant-specific information or fully represent the mixed-model summary-statistic likelihood. The eQTL and HFRS z–LD diagnostic estimates were 0.0282 and 0.00227, respectively; neither establishes that the external LD is appropriate. Colocalization was only to be run if both fits produced credible sets, so no posterior probabilities were calculated.

The published eQTL credible set contains three variants. Its rs12621789, with published PIP 0.421, is A/T palindromic and is excluded from this run, although the other two published members are represented. Excluding a major candidate can alter fine-mapping and makes this an especially incomplete test of sharing. The prior [insomnia comparison](stage6_meis1_cerebellum_susie_sensitivity.md) explored a limited palindromic-variant sensitivity; its results do not resolve this HFRS comparison. External LD, population differences, the low-rank FIN matrix, incomplete cis coverage, uncertain source overlap, allele ambiguity and unverified cross-implementation validation of the LD decoder further limit inference.

## Conditional sensitivity including the published palindromic candidate

We repeated the HFRS comparison with the same 958 variants plus **rs12621789** and **rs6546230**, under the explicit working assumption that the reported alleles from all sources have been oriented consistently on the forward strand. Both are palindromic; the assumption is not verified by matching allele pairs or similar frequencies. **Correction:** the previously asserted GRCh37/GRCh38 reference-base change for rs12621789 was wrong: UCSC reference sequence, 1000 Genomes and the gnomAD index support T as the reference at the mapped positions in both builds. This does not independently establish the orientation of each study's effects. The resulting 960-variant set contains all three members of the Catalogue's published molecular credible set. Both added variants passed the same 0.2 absolute frequency-difference filters; the largest difference for either was 0.0119. No original variants were removed.

Both fits converged. The expression model retained one credible set, but its allocation shifted: rs6738144 PIP changed from 0.592 to 0.368 and rs12621789 received PIP 0.359. Thus the original model's top-variant ranking is sensitive to including this variant. The HFRS fit again returned **zero credible sets** (maximum PIP 0.1225); no colocalization posterior was calculated. This does not establish absence of a shared effect. The NFE and FIN matrices had numerical ranks 960 and 795, and z–LD diagnostic estimates were 0.0281 and 0.00226, respectively. The same caveats about narrow cis coverage, mixed-model summary assumptions, external reference mismatch and custom decoder apply. The sensitivity does not establish strand correctness, biological causality or individual-level score performance.

## Independent-panel LD spot check

We compared the signed genotype correlations for all 15 pairs among the five fitted eQTL credible-set members and the postselected HFRS regional lead rs111736174 against public 1000 Genomes Phase 3 hard calls, using 503 EUR and 99 FIN individuals. All six variants had unique biallelic coordinate/allele matches after forward GRCh38-to-GRCh37 liftover. All observed pairwise correlation signs agreed across the previously decoded gnomAD NFE/FIN matrices and 1000 Genomes EUR/FIN panels. For example, rs6738144–rs62143990 correlations were 0.984 in gnomAD NFE and 0.987 in 1000G EUR, and 0.995 in gnomAD FIN and 1.000 in 1000G FIN. The HFRS lead and eQTL lead had weak positive dosage correlation (0.124 gnomAD NFE, 0.161 1000G EUR, 0.076 gnomAD FIN, 0.124 1000G FIN). These selected-pair results support a basic orientation/scale sanity check, **not** independent validation of the custom decoder: the populations and calls differ, FIN99 is small, and no same-source comparison was performed. The excluded palindromic variant was not assessed. This check does not change the absence of an HFRS credible set or produce a colocalization result. See [pairwise results](stage6_meis1_hfrs_ld_crosscheck.csv), [receipt](stage6_meis1_hfrs_ld_crosscheck_receipt.json), and [script](stage6_meis1_hfrs_ld_crosscheck.py).

## Reproducibility and next step

The [fit script](stage6_meis1_hfrs_susie.R), [diagnostics](stage6_meis1_hfrs_susie_diagnostics.csv), [credible-set members](stage6_meis1_hfrs_susie_members.csv), [alignment receipt](stage6_meis1_hfrs_align_receipt.json), and NFE and FIN LD receipts in this directory record this run. The script expects intermediate data and R packages under `work/`; it is not a self-contained download pipeline. Before stronger locus conclusions, resolve rs12621789 strand from source build and alleles, cross-check the LD extraction with an independent implementation, use an appropriate study-matched reference and sample-size specification, and revisit the complete cis-region and diagnostic assumptions. Neither this nor additional summary-data analysis can replace an independent participant-level score validation.
