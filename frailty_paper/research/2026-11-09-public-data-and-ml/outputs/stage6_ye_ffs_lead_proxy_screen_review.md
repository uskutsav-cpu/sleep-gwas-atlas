# Ye FFS leads and exploratory insomnia–frailty markers

The [Ye et al. FFS discovery study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-023-00771-z) used UK Biobank and reported follow-up in HRS. Its [supplementary workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-023-00771-z/MediaObjects/11357_2023_771_MOESM2_ESM.xlsx), sheet ST1, lists 37 COJO discovery leads. Comparing those rows with both project score lists yielded **zero exact ID matches and zero exact chromosome/position matches** for the 15 and the 22 candidate markers. Position comparison relies on the project lists' inferred/assumed GRCh37 coordinates. This does not rule out correlated variants or associations elsewhere in the locus.

The nearest notable pair is project candidate rs28528997 and discovery lead rs62444907, 9,145 bp apart at MAD1L1. In 503 1000 Genomes phase 3 European-ancestry reference samples, alternate-allele dosage correlation is r = 0.859 (r² = 0.738); candidate A and lead T therefore have opposite dosage direction in this panel. Ye's discovery table reports rs62444907 p = 2.59 × 10⁻¹³ for its marginal UK Biobank FFS association. This is discovery evidence for a correlated variant, **not an independent replication of the project candidate**. Reference LD is not HRS LD, the conditional COJO association differs from the marginal association, and a positional gene label does not identify the causal gene.

Ye et al. state that 22 of 37 leads were eligible or suitably proxied in HRS, with 3 passing their FDR threshold. The available machine-readable supplement does not identify an HRS result for rs62444907. Its presence among the 22, the tested HRS variant and its effect are **unverified**. Neither proximity nor LD supplies an HRS result. The published [Mekli FI proxy review](stage6_mad1l1_hrs_proxy_review.md) concerns a separate study and proxy; it does not resolve the Ye FFS result or validate either project score. Sample independence, outcome harmonization and effect alleles must be established for any eventual replication.
