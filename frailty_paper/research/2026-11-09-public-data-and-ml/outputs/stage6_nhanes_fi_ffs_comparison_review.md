# Same-participant adapted FI and modified physical frailty phenotype

An exploratory post hoc comparison used public NHANES 2011–12 and 2013–14 examined adults aged 60+, with complete physical-phenotype components, a valid sleep response and at least 20 of 24 adapted deficit-index items. Separate survey-weighted linear models used the same individuals, age and sex in each cycle. Estimates compare participants who had ever told a health professional about sleep trouble with those who had not.

| Cycle | Participants | Adapted index, difference on 0–1 scale (95% CI) | Five-domain count, difference on 0–5 scale (95% CI) | Four-domain count without exhaustion (95% CI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2011–12 | 1,479 | 0.071 (0.052–0.091) | 0.230 (0.081–0.379) | 0.129 (0.026–0.233) |
| 2013–14 | 1,575 | 0.088 (0.061–0.114) | 0.340 (0.208–0.472) | 0.205 (0.086–0.324) |

Both constructed outcomes were positively associated with reported sleep trouble in these models; the four-domain sensitivity also remained positive after omitting exhaustion. This provides a closer same-cohort comparison than grip alone, but the coefficients have different units and cannot be directly compared as effect sizes. No formal test of a cross-outcome difference was performed. The same participants are not a new external replication and the survey cycles have appeared in previous sleep–frailty studies.

A further post hoc cutoff sensitivity assigned participants exactly at the sedentary threshold to the lower rather than upper group. The five-domain estimates were 0.238 (95% CI 0.109–0.366) in 2011–12 and 0.349 (0.209–0.489) in 2013–14; the same participants were included. The corresponding four-domain estimates were 0.137 (0.056–0.217) and 0.214 (0.094–0.334). These results are similar under these two tie choices, but neither rule reliably creates exactly a highest 20% group or establishes the correct published algorithm. The [sensitivity results](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_ties_excluded_results.csv), [run log](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_ties_excluded_runlog.txt), and [code](../work/nhanes_fi_ffs_ties_excluded.R) retain the exact definitions.

The physical phenotype adapts a [published NHANES implementation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9100462/) of Fried domains: self-reported lifting difficulty, sedentary time, exhaustion, difficulty walking between rooms and unintentional weight loss. It is **not identical to the original Fried phenotype or the project's discovery FFS**: walking and weakness are self-reported, not direct gait speed and grip measurements; sedentary time substitutes for activity. Its sedentary cutoffs are derived separately by cycle and age group using unweighted 80th percentiles, with ties included, because the published cutoff algorithm was not fully specified. In 2011–12 these ties flagged 30% of eligible ages 60–69 and 38% of ages 70–79, rather than exactly 20%; this choice affects results and comparability. Run log records all group cutoffs and counts. The four-item count is an additional nonvalidated sensitivity measure, not a validated frailty diagnosis.

The 24-item deficit index is a separate unvalidated adaptation, omitting direct sleep and fatigue items but including functional questions. The two measures share physical-function concepts, so they are not independent constructs. The exposure is a report of having discussed sleep trouble, not an insomnia diagnosis, and could reflect healthcare access. Age/sex adjustment and complete-case selection leave confounding and selection bias possible; this cross-sectional analysis cannot determine causality or direction. NHANES data used here do not provide linked genotype scores and the findings do not validate project loci, a polygenic score, or genetic overlap.

The [analysis plan](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison_plan.md), [results](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison_results.csv), [run log](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison_runlog.txt), and [R code](../work/nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison.R) document the procedures. CDC source definitions: [weight-history skip](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/WHQ_G.htm), [physical function](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/PFQ_G.htm), and [sedentary time](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/PAQ_G.htm). Examine threshold sensitivity and assess a validated FI and directly measured FFS in an independent genotyped cohort before stronger conclusions.

## Exploratory paired contrast on fraction-of-items scales

To compare the two fitted sleep-trouble associations with their within-person covariance accounted for, we fitted the same survey-weighted age/sex model to `adapted FI − (five-domain count / 5)` on the same complete participants. This difference outcome compares both as fractions of their respective deficits or domains; it is not a common clinical severity scale. A positive coefficient means a greater sleep-trouble association with the adapted FI fraction than with the five-domain fraction under these particular definitions.

| Cycle | Paired difference in adjusted associations (95% CI) | p |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2011–12 | 0.025 (−0.005 to 0.056) | 0.094 |
| 2013–14 | 0.020 (−0.008 to 0.047) | 0.148 |

The contrast is post hoc and the intervals include zero. These data do not establish a difference between the associations, nor that FI or Fried frailty is more predictive or biologically more closely related to insomnia. A five-domain fraction and a deficit-index fraction can respond differently simply because their component prevalence, thresholds, item selection, and measurement precision differ; the two scores also share functional content. The outcome is an adapted physical phenotype rather than a validated Fried score. Sampling, participant selection, sedentary thresholds and other caveats above still apply. Neither analysis includes genetic scores.

The [paired contrast output](stage6_nhanes_paired_proportion_contrast.csv), [run log](stage6_nhanes_paired_proportion_contrast_runlog.txt), and [code](../work/nhanes_paired_proportion_contrast.R) use the original two-year NHANES survey designs and the same complete-case domains. The estimated differences equal the corresponding fitted coefficients of the index and the rescaled count apart from rounding. This direct model incorporates their joint design covariance; its uncertainty is not obtained by comparing separate confidence intervals.

A subsequent [post hoc sensitivity](stage6_nhanes_function_item_sensitivity_review.md) removes the seven functional items from the adapted index and repeats the same-participant analysis. The resulting 17-item index is not a validated FI, and the contrast remains sensitive to differences in item definitions and scales.
