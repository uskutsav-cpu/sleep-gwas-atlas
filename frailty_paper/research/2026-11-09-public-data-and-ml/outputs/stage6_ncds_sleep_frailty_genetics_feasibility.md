# 1958 British birth cohort: no-fee but application-based validation lead

The [1958 National Child Development Study (NCDS)](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/NCDS.html) is a longitudinal birth cohort. CLS reports 6,396 people with a TOPMed-imputed, QC-filtered genetic set on GRCh38 and 6,312 with a separate HRC-imputed set on GRCh37. The two counts describe overlapping releases, not independent samples. CLS says [genome-wide genetic data are available without charge following an application reviewed by its data access committee](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/access.html); they are not an unrestricted public download. Actual eligibility, permitted environment, incidental costs, data-use terms and current file access for this project remain unverified.

**Access-route distinction:** The current [CLS genetics access page](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/data-access-training/genetic-data-and-biological-samples/) explicitly describes the separate EGA-hosted NCDS genetic data as **not linked to survey data**. Consequently, that EGA route by itself cannot establish score performance against the age-50 questionnaire outcomes. The [CLS direct-access guidance](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/data-access-training/data-access/accessing-data-directly-from-cls/) explicitly describes a separate arrangement for **genetic data linked to survey data**, involving a bespoke survey dataset identified by a specific ID. This establishes that CLS offers a linkage pathway in principle; it does not establish approval, inclusion of our exact variables, target-marker quality or the number of eligible people. CLS suggests allowing at least three months for a request, and its [framework](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/CLS_Data_Access_Framework.pdf) describes genetic-data access through UCL Data Safe Haven. No request has been sent.

An [age-50 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8327203/) constructed a 34-item FI in 8,711 participants. Its published table explicitly lists fatigue (“feeling tired”) and two sleep items (sleep-onset duration and waking rested). Thus the original FI is not independent of sleep-related content. [CLOSER's age-50 questionnaire](https://discovery.closer.ac.uk/item/uk.cls.ncds/b30ad1bb-74e7-47c9-924b-80abc20638d4) separately documents a question about waking during sleep and difficulty returning to sleep; it is a symptom measure, not a clinical insomnia diagnosis. The awake-and-return-to-sleep question and the FI item about waking rested are distinct but correlated concepts and appear in the same questionnaire; exact item overlap and timing must be carefully handled.

This looks like a more substantial no-fee **conditional-access** lead for a future analysis than a small volunteer dataset. However the 8,711 phenotype and 6,396 genotype counts are not their verified intersection, and a score test requires actual alleles, dosage quality, per-person completeness and non-overlap with all discovery and candidate-selection participants or relatives. The [CLS genotype documentation](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/NCDS.html) says its combined sets remove variants with more than two alleles and minor-allele frequency below 1%; this could affect candidate coverage, but whether any specific project marker survives is unknown. Build and allele harmonization must use the chosen release. This cohort's age-50 outcome also differs from frailty in older populations and from Fried's phenotype.

A future prespecified analysis would compare the published FI with an adapted FI excluding both explicitly sleep-related items and, separately, excluding fatigue as well, while retaining the distinct sleep question as a symptom outcome. Revised indices would be new adaptations, not automatically validated substitutes. Freeze score definitions and verify source/target allele coding, linkage, independence and sufficient sample before any association or prediction test. No application, participant data download or score validation was performed. The paper's public Europe PMC XML (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8327203/fullTextXML`) was used to check the published item names.

**Concrete survey variables:** The [UK Data Service age-50 guide](https://sp.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6137/mrdoc/pdf/ncds_2008_user_guide_to_the_dataset.pdf) names `n8scq12` (time to fall asleep), `n8scq13` (sleep hours), `n8scq14` (awakening and difficulty returning to sleep), and `n8scq15` (feeling rested on waking). It also labels `n8scq10a`, `n8scq10e`, `n8scq10g` and `n8scq10i` as feeling full of life, having a lot of energy, feeling worn out and feeling tired. The [published FI table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8327203/) lists **all four** under fatigue, not only tiredness; it lists two sleep items. The names match the published descriptions and need coding confirmation against the actual release. These are candidate request fields, not proof that the CLS bespoke linked release contains them or that every FI component has been mapped. Verify all 34 component mappings before reconstruction. `n8scq14` is a distinct symptom question, not a diagnosis. A sensitivity excluding the explicit sleep items and **all four** fatigue-domain items removes six of the 34 published deficits, with a denominator of 28 before accounting for individual missingness; specify missing-item rules and validate this altered index separately.

## Specific phenotype and independence checks

The [original age-50 questionnaire](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCDS-2008-Self-Completion-Questionnaire.pdf) identifies Q12 as time to fall asleep, Q14 as waking and having trouble falling back to sleep, and Q15 as getting enough sleep to feel rested on waking, all over the previous four weeks. The [FI paper's table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8327203/) includes Q12's sleep-onset construct and Q15's waking-rested construct, but not the distinct Q14 sleep-maintenance item. Q14 is nevertheless contemporaneous with and related to the FI items; removing the explicit sleep items and then fatigue is a sensitivity analysis, not a guarantee of independent constructs or absence of shared measurement bias.

The updated SSGAC PGI Repository paper lists insomnia and 6,267 NCDS participants in its cohort table; the separately described CLS PGI product does not list insomnia. Actual access and the specific NCDS insomnia score need confirmation, and a precomputed external score would not validate our prototype. [Product and access comparison](stage6_ncds_pgi_repository_lead.md).

The original study included people with at least 30 of 34 deficits observed. For each 32- or 31-item adaptation, prespecify how to handle missing deficits and the minimum completion threshold; do not silently reuse or describe a modified rule as the published one. The original table codes onset as a deficit only when it takes **over one hour**, waking unrested as a deficit when sufficient rest is reported **none of the time**, and fatigue as a deficit when feeling tired **all or most of the time**. These are the published FI's rules, not recommended thresholds for a separate insomnia diagnosis. The Q14 response has six frequency categories, and any binary cutoff must be justified and fixed before analysis.

As a preliminary check of the CLS 1% frequency filter, existing 1000 Genomes European reference estimates give a smallest scored-pair frequency of **5.07%** among the 15-marker list (rs78206187) and **3.18%** among the 22-marker list (rs9845387); none falls at or below 1% in these 503 reference samples. These are external reference frequencies, not NCDS frequencies, a check of exact variants in the NCDS files, or a guarantee of imputation quality or inclusion. The project's 15-marker list includes multiallelic identity concerns: a frequency for a selected biallelic contrast cannot verify the target release's handling of all alleles. The comparison therefore makes a blanket claim that the 1% filter necessarily removes these markers unsupported, while leaving actual coverage unknown. Reproduce with `python3 work/ncds_marker_frequency_reference_check.py`; the [receipt](stage6_ncds_reference_maf_check.json) gives input hashes.

The project's saved GRCh37 reference annotations list more than two alleles for **9 of 15** weighted-prototype rsIDs and **14 of 22** count-prototype rsIDs. These are reference annotation inventories, not counts of variants NCDS excludes: third alleles might be absent in NCDS or represented differently or as separate records. Nonetheless, they make the release's `--max-allele 2` filtering and exact allele contrasts consequential. The [CLS QC description](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/NCDS.html) says both imputed workflows filter on imputation quality (R² INFO <0.8 removed), MAF and >2-allele records, and provide combined PLINK-format data. The TOPMed final release is GRCh38 and retains ancestry-outlier flags; the HRC final release is GRCh37 and its documented QC excludes samples classed non-European. These are overlapping releases, not independent replications. The public description does not establish whether the delivered PLINK product preserves the imputed dosages needed by the scorer or only hard calls; inspect actual files and data dictionary before use. The [reference inventory](stage6_ncds_multiallelic_reference_inventory.json) is generated by `python3 work/ncds_multiallelic_inventory.py` and preserves exact scored pairs.

Published UK Biobank/NCDS work describes their recruitment as independent ([study reporting summary](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Aa1992ab0-5d76-43a4-9652-10b725b83ef1/files/rcr56n242n)). Independent recruitment alone does not demonstrate that no individual or relative occurs in both samples. In another genetic study including 1958 Birth Cohort controls and UK Biobank, researchers checked sample overlap by identity-by-descent and removed duplicate samples ([study abstract](https://acrjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/art.42154)). This supports checking overlap rather than assuming either overlap or independence for this specific project. Also check any 1958 cohort contribution to the original discovery GWAS, not just UK Biobank membership.

A further [age-50 item-mapping update](ncds_age50_frailty_candidate_questionnaire_map.md) identifies documented candidate dataset fields for three of the published FI's cognitive deficits, bringing the candidate field map to 22 of 34. It also identifies the health and mental-health question blocks for several remaining deficits. Exact construction, cognitive quartile cutoffs, the correct-target letter score and the survey–genotype intersection are still unverified; this is not an executable FI or a score validation.

A separate [published 30-item NCDS age-50 FI](ncds_alternative_30_item_fi_review.md) excluded exhaustion, but its supplement shows it includes the same nighttime awakening and difficulty returning to sleep (`n8scq14`) previously considered as an outcome, as well as chronic fatigue syndrome. It is not a sleep-free FI. Its supplement also appears to use pain-severity labels for the sleep-frequency item; scoring must be clarified before implementation.

The [sleep-item comparison](ncds_sleep_phenotype_overlap_matrix.md) distinguishes the age-50 sleep measures actually included in each FI and identifies a later sweep described by a 2026 intrinsic-capacity preprint. That composite also contains sleep items; the preprint sample sizes are not linked genotype-validation counts.

The later [age-62 grip and timed-walk review](ncds_age62_grip_gait_genetic_validation_lead.md) identifies objective physical outcome fields and published fieldwork response counts. These may enable a more targeted physical-function score test after linkage and coverage checks, but they are not a complete FFS or verified genotype–outcome intersection.

A new [build-aware marker request](ncds_score_variant_request.md) collects all three project score definitions, with provisional GRCh37 and GRCh38 locations. All 59 list rows have unique reciprocal coordinate conversions, but this does not establish target-genotype coverage or source-allele identity. Public documentation did not expose a retained-variant list in the bounded search.

The public version-11 [genetic-sharing dictionary audit](ncds_genetic_sharing_dictionary_audit.md) confirms all 22 candidate age-50 FI field names appear, while warning that the main age-62 fields were pending a future dictionary update. The six queried grip/walk fields were not found in that version; this is not evidence that they cannot be requested.

The [age-50 FI map](ncds_age50_frailty_candidate_questionnaire_map.md) now has 28 candidate fields after a dictionary audit identified the correct-target cancellation count and five additional health fields. Exact thresholds and six remaining deficit constructions are unresolved; no FI has been calculated.
