# BioBank Japan insomnia: complete candidate association follow-up

**Result:** Of the existing score markers, 13/15 for the weighted prototype and 18/22 for the equal-weight count have exact GRCh37 position and allele-pair matches in the public autosomal insomnia GWAS. This is a SNP-level association follow-up, not an individual-level score test or validation of a sleep–frailty outcome. The study has 389 cases and 177,864 controls; the target is a browser-labeled insomnia diagnosis, not FI or FFS. Exact absence of overlap with discovery/selection samples and relatives has not been established, so do not claim confirmed independent replication.

The project uses the Japanese-only source linked by [PheWeb.jp](https://pheweb.jp/pheno/Insomnia) for [Sakaue, Kanai and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00931-x); the browser describes positions as GRCh37. A checked-range stream of the source archive's autosomal gzip member scanned **13,435,997 rows**. The pipeline exited zero and gzip's CRC and length checks passed; the whole ZIP was not retained or checked against a publisher's digest. The [receipt](stage6_bbj_insomnia_extract_receipt.json), [matched source rows](stage6_bbj_insomnia_candidate_matches.csv), [allele-oriented candidate table](stage6_bbj_insomnia_oriented_candidates.csv) and preparation script preserve the review. One additional row is a knee-study paper control, not a score marker. The earlier interrupted fragment is superseded and was not used as complete data.

The matched sets contain 27 distinct variants because four variants occur in both score lists. Exact coordinate and pair matching is not proof of correct study strand or causal-allele identity. All matched pairs are non-palindromic; for rs817509 the source SNPID is position-and-allele text rather than the candidate rsID, while its position and allele pair match. The remaining source SNPIDs agree with their matched candidate rsIDs. Source imputation information for the 27 distinct matches ranges from 0.931 to approximately 1.0. The code orients beta to each score's counted allele **assuming** the [standard SAIGE convention](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/weizhouUMICH/SAIGE/wiki/SAIGE-Hands-On-Practice-%2809-2021-updated%29), in which beta is the Allele2 effect; direct study-specific encoding and multiallelic representation remain to be confirmed. Unadjusted case/control frequency differences have the same sign as the source beta for 26 of 27 distinct variants; rs17550821 differs slightly. Adjustment and sampling mean this is a consistency check, not proof of coding error.

The 22-marker reference comparison uses counted-allele frequencies from the existing 504-person 1000 Genomes EAS panel. The largest observed difference is for rs12536335 (BBJ A frequency 0.293; pooled EAS 0.417). This pooled panel contains multiple populations; the separate 104-person JPT reference has A frequency 0.3125, closer to BBJ. For rs12671330, the BBJ C frequency is 0.055, pooled EAS 0.124 and JPT 0.0673. Small reference samples, imputation and sampling affect these comparisons; frequency agreement does not prove allele identity or validate a score. The JPT values come from the existing [knee-study allele audit](stage6_japanese_knee_allele_frequency_audit.csv), and the remote reference VCF was not full-file checksum verified.

Two 22-marker SNPs have nominal source p < 0.05: rs12671330 (counted C beta 0.327, SE 0.152, source p 0.0318) and rs12524473 (counted A beta −39.76, SE 14.26, source p 0.00529). These are postselected exploratory results, with multiple markers and prior analyses, and neither is evidence of replicated shared risk. For rs12524473, source G dosage count is approximately 15 in the entire study and case G frequency corresponds roughly to one imputed dosage copy; its extreme beta is unstable and the count allele A is nearly fixed. The reported source normal-approximation p (4.67×10⁻¹⁰) differs markedly from its saddlepoint p (0.00529). See the [low-count warning](stage6_bbj_insomnia_partial_rare_variant_qc.md), originally raised from the partial stream and now confirmed for this row in the complete stream. Do not use that variant's beta as a credible risk effect or treat its p-value as evidence that the full score is associated.

The simple case-control effective sample size is approximately 1,553, a rough balanced-design information comparison, not the actual N or an exact power calculation. Most candidate effects are uncertain. A subsequent [matched 14-marker weighted-Z sensitivity](stage6_bbj_insomnia_matched14_z_review.md) compared marginal input conventions and reference LD. It is a postselected approximation with important limitations, not target score regression or a validated score-level saddlepoint test. A non-significant result cannot exclude a modest effect. Patient-level genotype/outcome validation and an FI excluding sleep items remain outstanding.

## Case-specific dosage check

A follow-up check used the source's case and control allele frequencies and the published totals of 389 cases and 177,864 controls to estimate minor-allele dosage sums separately in cases. Among the 18 exact-matched variants from the 22-marker count, **three have estimated case minor-allele dosage sums below 5**: rs12524473 about 1.0, rs817509 about 1.2 and rs2815764 about 3.0. These are approximate sums of imputed dosage, **not numbers of people or definitive hard-called alleles**. They assume every case contributed at each variant and depend on the source frequency, imputation and allele conventions. The total sample's large minor-allele count can therefore conceal very sparse case information. These markers are particularly vulnerable to unstable estimates; low information alone does not prove a false association or dictate a universal exclusion threshold.

The postselected 14-marker weighted-Z subset excludes those three markers; its smallest estimated case minor-allele dosage sum is approximately 18 (rs2604551), and two of its 14 variants are below 50. Its case information and score-level calibration still require caution. This audit does not establish a valid aggregate p-value or a patient-level score effect. Reproduce with `python3 work/bbj_case_dosage_audit.py`; [aggregate receipt](stage6_bbj_case_dosage_audit.json) and [variant-level summary](stage6_bbj_case_dosage_components.csv) contain source-derived aggregate data only.
