# Estonian sleep-disorder follow-up for a project candidate

The public supplement to [Kunorozva et al. (2025), a preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.21.25340447), contains selected sleep-disorder GWAS lead results and a separate Estonian Biobank follow-up. Searching exact rsIDs in the selected lead table (ST3) and Estonian table (ST5) found one of the 33 distinct variants across our 15- and 22-marker lists: **rs113851554**, an intronic MEIS1-region variant in the 15-marker score. The other 32 identifiers were not present in these selected tables; that is not evidence that they were tested and null. We did not search full GWAS results or nearby proxies.

The authors describe five discovery cohorts and Estonian Biobank as a separate follow-up cohort. Their diagnosed-insomnia meta-analysis includes UK Biobank, FinnGen, MVP and All of Us, which overlap cohorts used in our project; its lead results are not an independent test of our candidate selection. The Estonian table is a more informative external single-variant follow-up, although participant and family overlap with *every* project discovery and selection source has not been verified. Its diagnostic codes and ascertainment differ from self-reported insomnia symptoms. It is not a test of our complete score or of frailty.

| Estonian phenotype | Reported BETA (SE) | Reported p | Variant-level N |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Insomnia | 0.0551 (0.0291) | 0.0583 | 205,165 |
| Restless legs syndrome | 0.5799 (0.0677) | 1.05 × 10⁻¹⁷ | 205,165 |
| Sleep apnea | −0.0224 (0.0320) | 0.484 | 205,422 |

The insomnia estimate is statistically uncertain at nominal 0.05 and its p value should not be presented as a confirmed replication or as proof of no effect. The restless-legs association is much stronger in this selected comparison; RLS is a distinct outcome and cannot stand in for insomnia or frailty. The authors note that MEIS1-region effects in their sleep-disorder clustering were primarily associated with RLS, a useful alternative explanation for sleep associations that warrants careful phenotype-specific follow-up. The study is a preprint, and the tables were selected after discovery; repeated cross-study lookups and multiple phenotype tests further limit interpretation.

ST5 lists allele 1 `T`, allele 0 `G`, frequency 0.05654 and imputation INFO 0.933009 for the insomnia row. The project's counted allele is also T with G as the listed other allele; however, **the ST5 beta-to-allele convention has not been independently verified**, and the variant has multiallelic reference representations. Therefore the signed beta is reproduced as the source labels it, without asserting harmonized direction or assuming the same biallelic contrast. ST5 lists chr2:66523432, while the project file uses an inferred GRCh37 position chr2:66750564; the matching rsID and letters do not themselves verify reference build or a liftover. The authors' Table S1 lists 12,962 insomnia cases and 192,943 controls in the Estonian phenotype, while this variant's ST5 N is 205,165; those are different denominators and neither is a joint sleep–frailty sample.

Re-run the exact-ID search with the bundled Python on `work/kunorozva2025/extract_project_candidates.py`. It checks 15- and 22-marker input sizes and does not calculate a score or assert locus-level colocalization. The Estonian cohort has not validated either project score.
