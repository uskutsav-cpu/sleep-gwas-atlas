# MEIS1 candidate: external sleep-phenotype evidence and limits

The project's rs113851554 is supported by published results in sleep cohorts beyond the UK Biobank, but that evidence points to **multiple sleep-related phenotypes**, especially periodic limb movements and restless legs syndrome (RLS). It does not establish a shared causal effect on insomnia and frailty, nor validate the project's genetic score. A competing explanation to investigate is that sleep-related movement symptoms contribute to part of the insomnia association; the available studies do not establish how much they contribute.

| Evidence | Published result | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| [Foldager et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8807892/) | In 2,770 people from the Wisconsin Sleep Cohort, Osteoporotic Fractures in Men (MrOS), and Stanford Sleep Cohort, rs113851554 was associated with the polysomnographic periodic limb movement index: reported beta 8.68 events/hour, p=1.99e−9. Their table lists A1=T and A2=G; confirm the model's effect-allele encoding before attaching the beta sign to T. | This is an objective movement-during-sleep phenotype, not a frailty measure or confirmation of the project's insomnia score. The reported threshold was 0.05/(7×240)=2.97e−5. Phenotype-specific N may differ from the overall N. |
| [Periodic limb movement GWAS](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/46/4/zsac121/6603639) | A 6,843-person analysis used MrOS and Wisconsin for discovery and HypnoLaus and MESA for replication; the combined rs113851554 estimate was OR 1.63 (95% CI 1.49–1.76), p=3.51e−12. | The published phenotype and model differ from Foldager's continuous index. The Wisconsin and MrOS overlap means the two publications cannot be counted as wholly independent confirmations. |
| [Partners Biobank insomnia GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-63792-0) | The previously identified rs113851554 T/G contrast was reported with OR 1.14 and p=0.0096 for physician-coded insomnia. | Nominal cross-cohort sleep association, not a frailty or score test. Partners also appears in Lane et al.'s insomnia replication; do not count the same resource twice. Exact participant/relatedness overlap with all project sources remains unverified. |
| [HUNT3 symptom study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12881-019-0916-6) | For T versus G, the onset-plus-maintenance symptom subgroup was reported at OR 1.5 (95% CI 1.1–2.0), p=0.007 before false-discovery-rate correction. | This is a selected subgroup and unadjusted p-value, not confirmation of all insomnia phenotypes. HUNT also features in Lane et al.'s replication; do not count those analyses as independent samples. |
| [French Canadian clinical comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215811/) | The rs113851554 minor-allele frequency was 5.3% in chronic insomnia without RLS versus 5.6% in controls, with no detected difference. The study included 646 insomnia patients with and without RLS. | This result challenges a simple RLS-independent interpretation in that sample, but limited power does not rule out a smaller insomnia effect. |

Foldager's **240-variant normalized score** is not the project's 15- or 22-marker score. It was associated with arousal index and wake after sleep onset under the paper's correction, but not with its insomnia outcome; these results cannot be attributed to our scores or compared as though they used the same weights. The studies differ in insomnia definition, ascertainment, genetic models and outcomes. Some sleep cohorts were used in earlier movement studies, and exact non-overlap with every discovery sample has not been independently established.

In this project, the counted T allele had a nominal Partners insomnia association above, and the earlier FinnGen hospital-frailty lookup gave beta −0.00053 (SE 0.02475, p=0.983) for the same T/G contrast. The latter estimate is imprecise and does not prove a zero frailty effect. Multiallelic representation at this rsID and selection of these findings require caution. A meaningful next test would use an independent, sufficiently powered dataset that measures both insomnia and RLS/periodic movement, with frailty measured separately, and compare prespecified models with and without movement symptoms. Analyses must avoid interpreting adjustment as proof of mediation and must not pool overlapping cohorts as independent replications.

The curated source table is `stage6_meis1_external_sleep_evidence.csv`. Numerical values above are transcribed from public reports, not newly fitted participant-level models; no complete per-variant source files or individual outcome data were obtained. The project's separate UK Biobank device-measured REM/NREM lookup is in `stage6_device_sleep_followup_review.md`.

## The separate movement-study replication sample

The [Edelson et al. study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/46/4/zsac121/6603639) separately reports rs113851554 T versus G for the dichotomous PLMS+ outcome (periodic limb movement index at least 15/hour). Its Table 2 lists OR 1.51 in the Wisconsin/MrOS discovery sample and OR 1.74 in the HypnoLaus/MESA replication sample; the latter pooled replication result has p=9.94e−9 in the table. This gives more specific evidence that the movement association extends to two additional cohorts. It is a published replication of a **movement phenotype**, not independent validation of a frailty outcome or the project's selected score; replication sample ancestry, relatedness, exact overlap with other studies, and individual cohort estimates should be checked before any further pooling.

The article has internal p-value discrepancies: its text gives discovery p=2.0137e−6 and replication p=9.49e−9, whereas Table 2 gives 4.56e−5 and 9.94e−9 respectively. These are transcribed as different source statements; do not silently choose one as a corrected result. Both descriptions agree on the direction of the reported ORs. The table reports heterogeneity p=0.04 for the combined rs113851554 result, which merits attention, although heterogeneity tests can have limited power. The results use a dichotomous threshold and should not be directly equated to Foldager's per-hour beta.

A fresh [GWAS Catalog v2 query](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/rest/api/v2/studies?pubmed_id=35670608&size=100) returned two curated entries for this publication, GCST90270120 and GCST90270121, both marked `full_summary_stats_available: false` and `full_summary_stats: NA` as queried. This contrasts with the paper's statement that summary statistics would be released. It does not prove that the full statistics are unavailable outside the Catalog, nor that authors did not submit them elsewhere. It does mean we did not obtain a genome-wide file suitable for a full 15-marker association analysis from this query.

## Partners summary-statistics availability and score-marker check

Checked 29 October 2026. The [Partners insomnia GWAS paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-63792-0) says its underlying datasets are not public due to IRB restrictions and that full summary statistics are available from the authors on request; this does not establish a freely downloadable full file. We inspected its public [supplementary workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-020-63792-0/MediaObjects/41598_2020_63792_MOESM2_ESM.xlsx) for exact rsIDs from the current 15- and 22-marker score files. The workbook's selected association tables 1–6 contain two candidate IDs, rs113851554 and rs6938026, both in Supplementary Table 6. That table is **meta-analysis 2**, which combines Partners, STARRS and UK Biobank, and provides p-values without signed effect estimates. It therefore cannot be treated as a Partners-only independent direction test, complete variant inventory, composite-score validation, or evidence that other score markers have null associations. The previously recorded Partners-only rs113851554 result remains a selected, single-variant association, subject to its own allele-representation caveat.\n
The inspected workbook is retained locally at `work/partners_insomnia_supp.xlsx`; no author request was sent. No participant-level data were accessed.

An [exact-variant GTEx v10 check](stage6_meis1_gtex_v10_exact_variant_review.md) corroborated the G/T allele and GRCh37/38 positions for rs113851554 but returned no single-tissue eQTL or sQTL rows for that exact contrast. Absence from these endpoint responses is not evidence that the variant lacks molecular function or that nearby variants cannot affect MEIS1.

## Additional source consistency warning

The same [published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091093/) gives 6,843 total participants in its abstract, but the Figure 2 caption refers to 7,016 PLMI cases. Those statements cannot both represent the same set of case participants. Until the source authors or study-level sample counts resolve this discrepancy, do not use the figure caption as a cohort count or the abstract total as the exact denominator for each SNP. The table's replication estimate remains a HypnoLaus-plus-MESA pooled result; it does not supply a MESA-only effect. The supplement could not be retrieved during this check, so no broader score-variant or individual-cohort comparison was performed.

A previously saved [GWAS Catalog API record](stage6_gwas_catalog_v2_pmid_35670608.json) provides an additional denominator cross-check: GCST90270120 lists 2,747 cases and 4,096 controls, summing to the abstract's 6,843, for all four cohorts combined. GCST90270121 separately lists 6,100 individuals for the continuous index (4,786 + 573 + 250 + 491). These are catalog metadata rather than verified variant-specific or MESA-only counts; they do not independently resolve the contradictory figure caption. At the time of the saved record neither catalog entry reported full summary statistics as available. The paper's stated intention to release genome-wide summaries should not be mistaken for proof that those files can currently be downloaded. The article supplement retrieval again failed in this check, and the Catalog evidence does not supply participant-level score validation.

A fresh GWAS Catalog API query for PMID 35670608 again returned `full_summary_stats_available=false` and `full_summary_stats=NA` for both GCST90270120 and GCST90270121 on 29 October 2026 (local time). This verifies only their Catalog listing, not absence from other repositories. The public abstract also divides the 2,747 cases into 1,745 discovery and 1,002 pooled HypnoLaus/MESA replication cases; it does not report a MESA-only number. The publisher and archive list a PDF and small ZIP supplement, but their accessible search records did not expose a downloadable file in this check. No new variant comparisons were made.
