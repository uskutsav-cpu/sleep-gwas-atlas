# New neuronal reporter dataset: candidate and ML feasibility

A [2026 preprint by Salomon and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13405209/) reports a lentiMPRA in NGN2-induced human excitatory neurons. It describes 38,684 unique assayed variants, with 38,968 variant/orientation records after QC. This is potentially useful as a **new molecular assay benchmark** for the project's sequence-model work. It does not provide linked insomnia/frailty outcomes or validate an individual genetic score.

The authors' [analysis repository](https://github.com/kircherlab/80K-Analysis) contains a publicly downloadable, approximately 12 MB compressed variant annotation table with allele sequences and reporter readouts. The inspected copy has 38,968 rows and 38,684 distinct `chr_pos_ref_alt` values; 1,018 rows are flagged significant by the source. Among chromosome 1 and 2 rows, 148 of 6,575 are flagged significant. These are **row counts**, not necessarily independent variants or independently sampled loci; some variants have more than one orientation. A nonsignificant reporter call is not proof of no functional effect, and cell-culture reporter activity is not a disease outcome.

We compared all 33 distinct project candidate contrasts with the table by GRCh38 coordinate and unordered allele pair. **None matched exactly.** This is a search of the downloaded processed table only: it does not prove that variants lack effects, and it does not assess proxies, other representations, or untested constructs. The [screen receipt](stage6_salomon_neuronal_candidate_screen.json) includes input hashes and counts; reproduce with `python3 work/salomon_neuronal_candidate_screen.py` using the local copy or the [source table](https://github.com/kircherlab/80K-Analysis/blob/a686f1d552f97ebcc0224057b5a835002f7a3dc8/variant_based_analysis/data/80k_tested_variant_annotation_table_with_readout.tsv.gz). The source table's local SHA-256 is recorded there; a publisher checksum was not independently verified. No sequence-level output is redistributed in the receipt.

A supplementary **physical-distance** scan found nine distinct tested contrasts within 10 kb of three project candidates: rs1942262 and rs11152363 in the TCF4 region, and rs17312861 in the FOXP2 region. None was flagged significant by the source assay. The nearby variants are not the candidates themselves; the distance scan does not establish LD, phase or shared regulatory effects. Nonsignificant calls are not evidence of no effect; proximity does not establish a gene or disease mechanism. Reproduce with `python3 work/salomon_candidate_nearby_screen.py`; the [receipt](stage6_salomon_candidate_nearby_screen.json) gives coordinates, distance and source calls.

In a follow-up using the 503 European-labeled 1000 Genomes participants, seven of those nine pairs had unique biallelic records with variable calls. The strongest observed dosage correlation squared was **0.381** for rs11152363 and nearby rs9320010; the other six were at most 0.033. One nearby contrast was monomorphic and one lacked a unique biallelic match, so their LD could not be assessed. One of the seven matched variants had only one alternate-allele copy in the panel, making its estimate especially unstable. These results do not rule out LD in the source donors, identify a causal variant, or make a nonsignificant reporter result a negative test of the project candidate. The reference is small and potentially related, and its remote VCF was not fully checksum verified. Reproduce with `python3 work/salomon_nearby_reference_ld.py`; [receipt](stage6_salomon_nearby_reference_ld.json).

We also checked the opposite direction: source-flagged significant reporter variants within 1 Mb of five project candidates in the TCF4 and FOXP2 regions. The source table contained **11 distinct significant contrasts**, but only three had unique biallelic, variable matches in the EUR reference. For those three, the largest candidate–reporter dosage r² was **0.080**; one had only one reference alternate-allele copy. The other eight were not assessable with this reference-matching rule. Thus this limited screen did not identify a strong reference proxy; it cannot rule out one among unassessed variants, in other ancestries or in the assay donors. The selected significant variants are molecular reporter observations, not evidence of an insomnia–frailty association. Reproduce with `python3 work/salomon_significant_region_ld.py`; [receipt](stage6_salomon_significant_region_ld.json).

The source's gnomAD annotations classify the eight contrasts without a unique biallelic reference match as **six rare variants and two singletons**, with reported global allele counts of 1–11. Their rarity is consistent with limited coverage in a small reference panel, although allele representation and other matching issues could also contribute. These annotations have not been independently checked in gnomAD, and do not establish allele frequency in the assay donors or LD with the project variants. [Frequency-audit receipt](stage6_salomon_missing_reference_frequency_audit.json), generated by `python3 work/salomon_missing_reference_frequency_audit.py`.

The study states that rare variants were partly selected using Enformer predictions. This selection affects generalization and creates a potential comparison bias for methods related to that model. Account for duplicated orientations and genomic-region dependence, and check overlap with prior Retallick training constructs and other model-training data. The paper's repository has an MIT **software** license; the independent reuse terms of the underlying data and any third-party prediction columns need confirmation. The paper cites an IGVF dataset, but its portal returned HTTP 403 in this environment; the repository table was accessible. Do not infer general unavailability from that response.

The existing strand-invariant baseline was developed after seeing earlier test sets, but this newly located assay was not used in that redesign. Its target, assay design, prevalence and sequence length differ, so any future test would assess cross-assay transfer rather than clinical prediction. The [prior model review](stage6_matoba_external_baseline_transfer_review.md) records those limitations.

## Fixed baseline cross-assay test

We reconstructed the previous strand-invariant 1–3-mer logistic baseline, training only on its original 801 Retallick chromosome 3–22 pairs, then evaluated it without fitting to Salomon data. The test used chromosome 1 and 2 source records with exactly one record per genomic contrast, a reported readout, and two 270-base sequences differing at one base. One duplicated contrast (two rows) was excluded; 6,573 pairs remained, including 148 source-significant calls. The baseline achieved **AUROC 0.501** and **average precision 0.0234**; the significant-call prevalence was **0.0225**, which is the constant-ranking average-precision baseline. This particular model shows little useful ranking on this selected assay set.

This is a post hoc cross-study check, not a formal prespecified validation or proof that the model cannot work elsewhere. The model redesign followed inspection of earlier test results; this source had not been used in that redesign. The two studies use different sequence lengths, neuronal systems, selected variant sets and significance conventions. Reported significant/nonsignificant calls depend on assay power and are not true functional/nonfunctional labels. Observations can share regulatory elements and are not independent; exact or homologous sequence overlap with training, donor overlap and underlying source use rights have not been fully checked. The low positive prevalence limits precision; a gene-cluster sensitivity is reported below. Neither results nor model predictions validate the genetic scores or clinical risk. No AlphaGenome output was used.

Reproduce with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 work/salomon_neuronal_invariant_transfer.py`; the [aggregate receipt](stage6_salomon_invariant_transfer.json) includes counts and input hashes. The program reconstructs the model from the original training workbook; it writes only aggregate benchmark statistics, not sequence-level predictions. Compare to the [prior model review](stage6_matoba_external_baseline_transfer_review.md).

The benchmark was rerun with an additional source-internal check: all **6,573** evaluated pairs have their single changed base at the stated position, with reference/alternate letters consistent with the source's stated genomic contrast and plus/minus sequence orientation. AUROC and average precision were unchanged. This checks internal representation, not the sequences against an independent genome assembly, study-label validity, independence or model performance.

As a sensitivity to correlated observations, we resampled the **78 source-reported associated-gene groups** with replacement for 2,000 draws, keeping the trained model fixed. The 2.5th–97.5th percentile AUROC range was **0.461–0.540** around 0.501; all draws contained both call classes. This assumes the gene groups are exchangeable, independent clusters, which is not established; different genes can be nearby or share biology, and gene labels need not define independent genomic loci. The interval excludes training, assay and selection uncertainty and does not prove equivalence to chance. Reproduce with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 work/salomon_neuronal_gene_bootstrap.py`; [receipt](stage6_salomon_gene_cluster_bootstrap.json).

An additional post hoc breakdown by the source's frequency labels yielded AUROCs of **0.519** for common (29 significant calls), **0.546** for very common (32), **0.501** for rare (37), and **0.465** for singleton (50). Each subgroup is selected, contains few significant calls and has no calculated uncertainty interval; these values do not establish differences in performance between frequency groups. The source partially selected rare variants using a sequence model, and neither source annotations nor the target labels have been independently validated. The breakdown is a diagnostic for future assay design, not a clinical result. Reproduce with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 work/salomon_frequency_strata.py`; [receipt](stage6_salomon_frequency_strata.json).
