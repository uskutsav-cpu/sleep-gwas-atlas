# rs8067974: a published brain-expression lead for frailty domains

A search of the project’s 15 candidate rsIDs against the top-SNP column of [Foote et al. Supplementary Tables 31–37](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41588-025-02269-0/MediaObjects/41588_2025_2269_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx) found two exact matches, both **rs8067974–HEXIM1**. These tables report significant SMR-Multi results for a general frailty factor and six frailty-related factors, not an exhaustive list of all QTLs. The match uses rsID, GRCh37 chromosome 17 position 43,232,566, and the same G/A allele strings as the project candidate. The locus is multiallelic in reference data, so study-specific exact contrast QC remains important.

| Factor 5: poorer cognition | BrainMeta cortex expression of HEXIM1 | 0.00200 | 0.446 | No heterogeneity detected by this test at the paper’s cutoff |

The paper uses FDR q < 0.05 across its QTL analyses and rejects associations with HEIDI p < 0.01; a nonsignificant HEIDI result is not proof that the same causal variant drives both signals. The reported G-allele QTL beta is −0.206 in both rows, while the frailty-domain GWAS betas are +0.0142 and +0.0159, respectively. These are associations on different outcome scales, not an estimated combined risk. The paper’s [supplementary methods](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41588-025-02269-0/MediaObjects/41588_2025_2269_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) describe BrainMeta v2 as cortex eQTL data from 2,443 European-ancestry individuals across seven cohorts. This is bulk brain tissue, not a single-cell or cell-type result.

In the project, the candidate’s nearest annotated gene body is the antisense gene AC002117.1. VEP also reports a downstream HEXIM1 transcript annotation; the published QTL finding supplies an additional molecular link to HEXIM1 that simple nearest-gene labeling missed. It does **not** establish HEXIM1 as the causal insomnia–FI gene or prove that expression mediates frailty. The published factor GWAS is related to, but not identical to, the original FI or FFS GWAS; the datasets may share UK Biobank participants and should not be presented as independent replication.

The project’s FI meta-analysis p-value for this allele is 7.77×10⁻⁷ and FFS p-value is 0.027. Insomnia association is strong in the original UKB source (p=1.52×10⁻¹¹), but not nominally significant in the reviewed FinnGen or MVP inputs (p=0.223 and 0.261). These are post-selected, unadjusted results with different definitions, sample sizes and possible overlap; no conclusion of absence follows from the two nonsignificant tests. The uncertainty argues against treating this candidate as a validated shared-risk marker or assigning it a clinical score weight.

`foote_candidate_exact_smr_matches.csv` preserves both complete source rows. Search scope: the exact top-SNP rsID column in all seven published significant-result tables, not LD proxies or full QTL datasets. No exact match in these filtered tables is not evidence that the other 14 variants have no QTL effects. The publisher workbook SHA-256 is `d170d2596ed43cb168d4463a722894969198aa6d8e493f73cdf7cd6d5637b5b4`. The next informative test would use dense, harmonized insomnia and frailty GWAS plus cortex and cell-type QTL data to test whether signals colocalize, accounting for multiple causal variants and sample overlap; it has not been performed here.
