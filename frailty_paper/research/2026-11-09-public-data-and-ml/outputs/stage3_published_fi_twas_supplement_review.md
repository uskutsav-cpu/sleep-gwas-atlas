# Published FI TWAS supplement and candidate-gene matches

[Lin et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2024.1404456) provide 36 reported significant cross-tissue UTMOST genes and 107 significant whole-blood FUSION genes in their supplementary document. I extracted both tables into [the transcribed results](stage3_published_fi_twas_supplement_genes.csv), with a [source and hash receipt](stage3_published_fi_twas_supplement_receipt.json). These are the authors' selected significant results, not a complete list of all tested genes.

Nine source gene symbols match names in the current candidate inventory, all in the whole-blood FUSION list: RBM6, UBA7, MST1R, GMPPB, DAG1, AMT, CUL9, TTC12 and MST1. Seven are linked in our inventory to rs34290943, while CUL9 and TTC12 are associated with rs6938026 and rs2155292 respectively through the inventory's existing gene assignments. Those marker labels indicate why the gene was considered; they are **not** evidence that the published TWAS signal is caused by that marker. Some candidate assignments are predictions through LD proxies rather than established molecular effects. A gene-symbol match is not an independent stable-ID reconciliation, and neighboring genes can share correlated genetic predictors. Study overlap and shared expression-reference data also prevent treating the source as independent replication.

The published FI TWAS results add a separate gene-model evidence layer to the candidate table and network. They do not show a shared insomnia–FI variant, a specific causal tissue, or mediation. Missing symbols in this selected supplement are not evidence of a negative association. The previously examined [FI–insomnia published TWAS overlap](stage3_published_fi_insomnia_twas_review.md) remains limited to the two articles' jointly selected main-text tables; we have not obtained the full insomnia gene-level supplement or run a new TWAS here.
