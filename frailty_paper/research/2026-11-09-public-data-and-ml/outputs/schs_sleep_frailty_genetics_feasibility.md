# Singapore Chinese Health Study: sleep, physical frailty and genetics

Screened 29 October 2026. **Promising cohort scientifically, but not an established free open validation dataset.** No SCHS participant records were accessed and neither project score was evaluated.

## What is available in published studies

[Chua et al. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-39228-6) analyzed 10,792 SCHS participants with self-reported sleep duration at three time points and a late-life physical frailty assessment. Mean age at baseline was 52 and at the late assessment 72. Compared with 7 h/day at baseline, fully adjusted odds ratios for late-life frailty were 1.43 (95% CI 1.14–1.79) for ≤5 h/day and 1.62 (1.26–2.08) for ≥9 h/day. This is observational evidence for sleep-duration associations, not evidence that insomnia causes frailty or that a genetic score works. It does not measure insomnia directly. Early frailty was not measured, and selection and residual confounding are possible.

Its physical phenotype was a **four-component modification**, not the original five-component Fried score: slowness (timed up-and-go), grip weakness, weight loss and exhaustion; low activity was omitted, and ≥2 of 4 defined frailty. This distinction matters for cross-study comparisons. Frailty data were complete in the published analysis subset but linkage to genotype has not been established.

[Chang et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08675-8) reported 23,622 participants after genomic QC and evaluated 2,173 existing polygenic scores against baseline traits, using GRCh38 and imputed variants with a local reference panel. The cohort's [own description](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/research-programmes/introduction-to-the-singapore-chinese-health-study-schs/) describes approximately 26,000 genotyped members. These numbers and the 10,792 sleep-frailty paper sample represent different selections; **their intersection, number of frailty cases, genotype coverage and insomnia phenotype availability remain unverified**. The public PGS analyses do not evaluate our custom 15- or 22-marker score. East Asian evaluation could assess transportability, not assume performance generalizes from European discovery.

## Access and fees

The 2026 sleep/frailty paper explicitly says its participant datasets are **not publicly available** and may be requested from the corresponding author. The 2025 genetics paper places genetic data and generated scores in RAPTOR, and says supporting study data can be requested from the authors. The [cohort's application page](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/research-programmes/introduction-to-the-singapore-chinese-health-study-schs/contact-us/) lists a research data application and guidelines. An external [longitudinal dataset directory](https://atlaslongitudinaldatasets.ac.uk/datasets/schs) lists fees for all data as of its February 2026 assessment; it warns details may be outdated and should be independently verified. Thus **no-fee access is not established and a fee is plausible**; this is not an unrestricted or verified free route.

A search located [dbGaP phs001273](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs001273.v2.p2), which mentions SCHS but is a multi-cohort lung-cancer case-control collection requiring authorized access. Its description does not demonstrate availability or linkage of the follow-up frailty phenotype, or of the full 23,622-person genotyped sample. Do not equate this accession with the cohort-wide dataset.

For a future no-fee feasibility inquiry, ascertain whether academic access fees can be waived; whether custom score calculation is permitted; exact sleep/frailty/genotype intersection and case counts; genotype build, score-marker coverage and imputation QC; and discovery cohort overlap. This work requires permission and potentially formal data-use agreements; no outreach or application was submitted. Under the current free-public-data constraint, prioritize established no-fee options while retaining SCHS as a contingent cross-ancestry validation opportunity.

## Public cross-context check of an existing insomnia score

The open [supplementary workbook](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs42003-025-08675-8/MediaObjects/42003_2025_8675_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx) includes PGS001932, the *published* insomnia score used in the separate SJLIFE grip exploration. Its inventory lists 35,935 of 37,712 score variants present in SCHS (4.71% missing); that is not our custom marker coverage. The workbook also gives existing-score associations with self-reported baseline sleep-duration categories: for short sleep (<6 h) vs 6–8 h, beta 0.0344, SE 0.0225, nominal p=0.126; for long sleep (>8 h) vs 6–8 h, beta −0.0401, SE 0.0271, nominal p=0.139. Each row reports adjusted p=1 across the paper's multiple-testing correction. The authors report 2,253 short-sleep cases / 19,902 controls and 1,467 long-sleep cases / 19,902 controls in their trait inventory. These are not diagnoses of insomnia, frailty outcomes or our score's validation. A lack of statistically clear association does not establish no effect. No inference of cross-population performance equivalence is warranted.
