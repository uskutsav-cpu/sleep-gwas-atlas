# CHARLS 2026 sleep-duration and incident-frailty study: outcome overlap

Chen et al., [The association between sleep duration and risk of frailty: a prospective cohort study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00466-7), reports a longitudinal CHARLS analysis with 18,282 participants and 2,125 incident frailty events. The paper defines exposure as self-reported nighttime duration and outcome as a 44-item frailty index (FI ≥ 0.25). The fully adjusted discrete-time model reports odds ratios of 2.05 (95% CI 1.85–2.27) for <7 hours and 1.45 (1.16–1.83) for >9 hours, compared with 7–9 hours. This observational association is not evidence that modifying sleep duration will prevent frailty and is not an insomnia GWAS or validation of our genetic scores.

**Direct outcome-composition finding:** the authors' supplementary table 9 explicitly lists “Poor sleep quality” as item 41 and “Feeling that doing things is very effortful” as item 42. The outcome therefore includes a sleep-related deficit and an exhaustion-related deficit. Sleep duration and sleep quality are related but distinct measures, and the outcome was evaluated longitudinally; this is not literal duplication of the exposure or proof that the reported association is entirely circular. Nevertheless, this study cannot on its own establish an association with sleep-excluded frailty. The table states that at least 30 deficit items were needed, making missing-item handling important for any reanalysis.

The supplement describes four sensitivities: excluding events at the first follow-up, alternative age-timescale Cox modelling, additional BMI adjustment, and LOCF/NOCB imputation. It does not identify a sensitivity that removes the sleep-quality or effort items. In the Cox analysis, the adjusted long-sleep estimate was 1.12 (0.88–1.44), showing that its precision and interpretation depend on modelling choices. These are reported study results, not a reanalysis of individual participants.

If the available CHARLS survey waves can be accessed, a stronger follow-up is a prespecified same-person comparison of the original FI with an FI recomputed without items 41 and 42, explicitly updating its denominator and eligibility rule. Compare the continuous FI and threshold-based outcomes with uncertainty, document any change in event classification and missingness, and consider a separately constructed physical phenotype. Excluding two items alone does not solve confounding, reverse causation, selection, or genetic validation. Appropriate access to linked genotypes remains a separate requirement; see [CHARLS genetic access audit](stage6_charls_genetic_access_2026.md).
