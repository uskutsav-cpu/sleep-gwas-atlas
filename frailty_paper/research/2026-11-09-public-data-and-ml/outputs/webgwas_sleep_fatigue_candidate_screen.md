# Exploratory WebGWAS sleep-disorder and fatigue screen

29 October 2026. [WebGWAS](https://webgwas.org/simple) was accessible in the browser despite a prior HTTP 403 from the web fetcher. Its simple interface searched **frailty**, **grip**, and **insomnia** without a match, while a hypertension positive control returned results. The broad `G47` sleep disorders (13,630 indicated) and `R53` malaise and fatigue (5,562 indicated) fields were available. No-match results pertain to the literal field-label searches in this interface, not proof that relevant concepts are absent elsewhere.

The job defined **G47 AND R53**, not insomnia AND Fried frailty or FI. It returned results in 6.7 seconds with a **phenotype approximation R² of 50.3%**. According to the [method paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13170032/), that number measures how well the nonlinear phenotype is approximated, not score prediction or heritability. Approximation error is substantial. Diagnostic sleep disorders include more than insomnia, and fatigue is nonspecific and a component or correlate of frailty, not a frailty phenotype. No biological or predictive conclusion about insomnia and frailty can be inferred from this construction.

The downloaded metadata says UKB White British, cohort size **34,235**, WebGWAS v0.8.0, whereas every inspected variant row reports `sample_size` **342,350**. This tenfold disagreement is unresolved, and neither number should be presented as the verified number of cases, controls, or analytical participants. The site reported marginal field counts above, but no joint-case count was shown. The job uses UK Biobank, overlapping original discovery and candidate selection; it is not independent replication.

The local exploratory screen compared exact rsIDs from the two prototypes with the unfiltered result file: 15 of 33 unique candidate IDs matched, comprising 7/15 in the 15-marker list and 10/22 in the 22-marker list. All matched coordinates agree with the corresponding prototype's stated GRCh37 positions; the output build and effect-allele convention remain to be confirmed from the method/data documentation. No matched row had nominal p < 0.05 by the reported `neg_log_p_value`. This observation is **not** evidence that the missing markers are null, nor a test of either score: partial coverage, phenotype misclassification, modest fit, selection, overlap, unknown power and allele uncertainty limit it. No directional claims or combined score statistic were made.

The exact rows and fields are preserved in [the candidate extract](webgwas_sleep_fatigue_candidate_screen.csv). ## Follow-up: why do the two sample counts differ?

The public [WebGWAS backend source](https://github.com/tatonetti-lab/webgwas-backend) checked at commit `62856b572110fb0b008572a85c9fbfca773073f6` declares version 0.8.0, matching the download's version label. In that source, [`create_metadata_file`](https://github.com/tatonetti-lab/webgwas-backend/blob/62856b572110fb0b008572a85c9fbfca773073f6/src/worker.rs#L170) labels `cohort_info.features.nrows()` as the cohort size. The [registration code](https://github.com/tatonetti-lab/webgwas-backend/blob/62856b572110fb0b008572a85c9fbfca773073f6/src/bin/webgwas-register.rs#L635) stores MDAV cluster centroids as the anonymized phenotype table, and the [results code](https://github.com/tatonetti-lab/webgwas-backend/blob/62856b572110fb0b008572a85c9fbfca773073f6/src/igwas.rs#L256) derives each GWAS row's sample size from its degrees of freedom plus covariate count and two.

**Interpretation:** if the deployed site's matching version uses this same code and registration path, 34,235 is the number of anonymized phenotype rows/centroids used in phenotype approximation, whereas 342,350 is the inferred GWAS sample-size field; the 10:1 relationship is consistent with the paper's k=10 microaggregation. This is a plausible code-based explanation, not verified deployment or proof that all centroids contain exactly ten people. It does not establish the joint-case count, complete-case analytic sample, sample overlap or validity of either count. Retain both numbers with their distinct provenance until confirmed by the authors or deployment documentation.

The [method paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13170032/) says the advanced builder can support quantitative fields. On 29 October, direct searches in the advanced builder for `grip` and `height` returned no options; `sleep` returned only G47 and F51, and `walking` returned a seemingly unrelated result, suggesting fuzzy matching. This limited check does not prove that the full advanced catalog lacks these measurements. A positive quantitative-field control was not found, so exact available fields remain unverified. Consequently no sleep-excluded FI or Fried phenotype was built.

The source code aligns reversed input allele pairs by negating beta, but this does not identify the effect-allele conventions of the registered input GWAS or verify the precise deployed build. Candidate signs remain uninterpreted.
