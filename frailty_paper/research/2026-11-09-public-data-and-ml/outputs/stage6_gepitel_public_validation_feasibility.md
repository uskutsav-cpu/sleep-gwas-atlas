# GEPITEL: openly downloadable older-adult genotypes and frailty-related GWAS

[Ginevičienė et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13394718/) studied Lithuanian community-dwelling adults aged 65 and older, assessed Fried frailty, and reported 181 successfully genotyped participants in the paper. The authors released a [Figshare dataset](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28845761) under CC BY 4.0, including PLINK genotype files and a lean mass index (LMI) association workbook. This is a real open individual-genotype resource, but the downloaded files do **not** establish a usable individual genetic-score validation cohort. **Coordinate warning:** the paper says positions are GRCh38, while four checked LMI workbook positions match GRCh37 rather than GRCh38; do not assume the workbook follows the paper’s stated build.

We checked the released files, not just the paper's access statement:

* `GEPITEL.zip` contains a PLINK BED/BIM/FAM dataset with 192 FAM records and 654,027 BIM variants; all FAM phenotype fields are `-9`. The archive contains no separate frailty phenotype file. The paper reports 181 successfully genotyped participants, whereas the release and log say 192; the difference is not resolved. The paper describes a selected sample with substantial eligibility restrictions, and any shared participants/relatives with project inputs remain unverified.
* None of the 33 candidate IDs appears in the released direct array BIM. No candidate coordinate matched when comparing against either the project's GRCh37 or GRCh38 crosswalk. The source BIM's build is not independently confirmed; a zero direct-array match does not exclude proxies, differently represented variants or separate imputed genotypes. It does establish that this particular archive cannot directly score our listed alleles as released.
* The public `GWAS for LMI in elderly.xlsx` has 584,739 association rows, all with reported p < 0.05 in a full-file audit; it is therefore a selected set rather than an all-p-value GWAS summary file. One project candidate, rs34290943 from the 22-marker list, appears at the project's GRCh37 coordinate with reported p=0.0431484. This is one postselected nominal LMI association, not a frailty score test, replication of shared sleep risk, or evidence of a causal gene. Allele orientation, sample composition, imputation and build need confirmation before interpreting its direction. The remaining candidates are absent by rsID and checked coordinate from this file; this is not evidence of no effect.
* The [separate Figshare release](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28874378), despite its summary-data title, contains a methylation matrix and a 60-row sample metadata sheet with age, sex, condition and lean mass. A documented linkage to the genotype archive is not established; see the detailed check below.

Files were checked against Figshare metadata: genotype ZIP MD5 `5a01fa78c023ffb96c6eaaab4c6aad32` and LMI workbook MD5 `c58c6d9b4427fc9b16c223fe4bc1faae`. `work/gepitel/audit_public_files.py` reproduces the ID and coordinate inventory; results are in `outputs/stage6_gepitel_public_data_audit.json`. The FAM was counted without retaining or displaying participant IDs. A defensible score evaluation would require a documented linkage to permitted individual FI/FFS and sleep measurements, independent sample verification, and imputed or accurately proxied candidate genotypes with adequate quality and power. None is established in this open release; the small selected sample would also make prediction performance highly uncertain.

## Nearby-marker feasibility

We compared the actual released GEPITEL BIM to the existing generic-array, 1000 Genomes European reference tag screen (`stage6_gsa_reference_tag_screen.csv`). Among its preselected best tags with reference r²≥0.8, exact tag ID, chromosome and GRCh37 position appear in the GEPITEL BIM for **5 of the 15-marker candidates and 6 of the 22-marker candidates**; one candidate belongs to both lists. This is narrower than a fresh search across all possible array markers: it only checks the previously selected single best tag per candidate. The actual cohort allele contrast, strand, missingness, quality, target LD, phase and direction have not been verified, and a 1000 Genomes correlation is not imputation accuracy or a license to substitute the tag in the score. It does not solve the absent linked frailty and sleep outcomes or establish adequate power. Reproduce with `work/gepitel/check_precomputed_tags.py`; results: `outputs/stage6_gepitel_existing_tag_screen.json`.

A Figshare API title search for `GEPITEL`, the project title, and `Gineviciene frailty` returned the already checked genotype/lean-mass article and the separate EWAS article; no additional linked outcome file was identified in those searches. This limited search cannot establish that such a file is unavailable elsewhere.

For the LMI row at rs34290943, the workbook lists alleles C/T, `beta_1` −1.72945 and `se_1` 0.912109. A two-sided normal Wald p computed from those values is approximately 0.05795, different from the workbook's `frequentist_add_pvalue` 0.0431484. These fields may reflect different test statistics or estimation procedures; the cause has not been resolved and should not be presumed to be a data error. We have not verified which allele `beta_1` refers to. Thus this row should not be used for a signed or pooled association without source-specific documentation. The discrepancy also means its nominal p should not be promoted as independent support for the candidate.

## Assembly and test-field audit

The paper states that genomic positions are GRCh38, yet four workbook rows checked against Ensembl primary chromosome mappings—rs34290943, rs8034223, rs182813191 and rs9638291—match GRCh37 and differ from their GRCh38 positions. For example, the workbook lists rs34290943 at chr3:49,161,660, matching [Ensembl GRCh37](https://grch37.rest.ensembl.org/variation/human/rs34290943?content-type=application/json); [Ensembl GRCh38](https://rest.ensembl.org/variation/human/rs34290943?content-type=application/json) places it at chr3:49,124,227. This is strong evidence of a build inconsistency affecting at least these rows, not proof every workbook row or the separate genotype archive uses GRCh37. Source documentation or genome-wide verification is required before joining or interpreting regions. Script `work/gepitel/check_lmi_assembly.py` and `outputs/stage6_gepitel_lmi_build_check.json` record the checks.

The article reports SNPTEST v2.5.4. [SNPTEST documentation](https://mathgen.stats.ox.ac.uk/genetics_software/snptest/snptest_v2.5.1.html) generally codes allele B as the modeled additive allele and documents score tests alongside parameter estimates, making different p-value and Wald calculations plausible. However, the public workbook has shortened column labels and no command, header provenance or specific test method, so its exact allele convention and cause of the difference are not established. Do not infer them from generic documentation alone.

## Methylation sample metadata: correction and linkage check

We downloaded and checksum-verified the second archive (MD5 `d18b38b6e535d2b74c73e83cd5b22be3`) and inspected workbook structure and aggregate metadata. Contrary to our earlier description based on its title, the workbook contains an individual methylation matrix and a 60-row metadata sheet with `IDAT`, sex, age, condition and lean mass. Its condition labels comprise 59 `Sarcopenia_Frailty` and one `Control`; no sleep variable is named. The IDAT strings have zero exact matches to genotype FAM IDs. An unlabeled first column happens to match genotype identifiers numerically, but is exactly the sequence 1 through 60 and could simply be a row index. There is no documented crosswalk to show it identifies the same individuals, and no records were linked. Inferring identity from this coincidence would be unjustified. Even if linkage were verified, the one-control case mix would severely limit a conventional frailty case-control evaluation, and direct score variants remain absent on the array.

The aggregate audit is `outputs/stage6_gepitel_ewas_metadata_audit.json`; script `work/gepitel/audit_ewas_metadata.py` requires redownloading the linked 604 MB archive and extracting its workbook. The large temporary download and extracted copy were removed after inspection to conserve disk. No methylation participant data or IDs are included in the aggregate audit. The information creates a lead for a documented crosswalk, not an established validation cohort.

## Expanded direct-array reference-tag screen

On 29 October 2026 we broadened the earlier limited check to search **all eligible markers** in the released GEPITEL BIM within 100 kb of each candidate, rather than only checking a preselected tag from a generic array. We intersected BIM chromosome, position and unordered A/C/G/T allele pair with the existing GRCh37 503-person European 1000 Genomes reference, then calculated pairwise-complete reference dosage r² with at least 400 paired calls. All 33 distinct candidates had a unique reference anchor and at least one evaluable nearby array/reference marker. The highest reference r² was at least 0.8 for **6/15** of the 15-marker list and **6/22** of the 22-marker list; one candidate appears in both lists, so there are 11 distinct candidates across the two counts. The ≥0.8 threshold is descriptive, not a guarantee of usable imputation or a validated score. [All candidates and best tags](stage6_gepitel_full_reference_tags.csv); [receipt](stage6_gepitel_full_reference_tags.json); reproduction: `python3 work/gepitel/full_reference_tag_screen.py` using the existing released BIM and the MAGMA 1000 Genomes reference.

This screen assumes the BIM's positions and allele labels are compatible with the GRCh37 reference; its build and strand were not independently verified. It excludes markers with zero, non-SNP or nonmatching allele labels, does not resolve duplicate platform records or phase, and searches only ±100 kb. The results are reference-panel correlations selected and evaluated in the same 503 people, not GEPITEL target-cohort QC, imputation performance, or appropriately harmonized proxy dosages. Tag-based score substitution would require target-specific verification and would define a different score. Most importantly, no documented participant linkage to FI/FFS or insomnia outcomes was established; this expanded search does not enable validation.

## Released genotype calls at the selected tags

We then inspected the PLINK BED calls for the **11 distinct candidate–tag pairs** meeting the prior reference r² threshold. Each selected tag had one matching released BIM record by the tag's name, chromosome and position. Across the 192 released FAM records, each had 188–192 nonmissing calls, and the smaller of the two observed allele frequencies among called records ranged from 0.121 to 0.495. [Aggregate call summary](stage6_gepitel_reference_tag_call_summary.csv), [input-hash receipt](stage6_gepitel_reference_tag_call_receipt.json); reproduce with `python3 work/gepitel/verify_selected_tag_calls.py`. No participant IDs or phenotype values were extracted. The source paper's reported 181 successfully genotyped people differs from the archive's 192 FAM records, so these counts must not be assumed to describe the study's analyzed sample.

A nonmissing, variable tag call is not evidence that it passed the study's QC, is oriented correctly for a score, accurately tags the candidate in this cohort, or can be used to reconstruct a score. The original score variants remain absent from the checked array, reference LD was not evaluated in GEPITEL, and outcomes and linkage remain unavailable. These checks do not validate either score.

## Lean-mass workbook selection audit

A complete scan of the checksum-verified LMI workbook found **584,739 valid p-values, all below 0.05** (maximum 0.0499999). The workbook is therefore consistent with a p-value-filtered result set; the source's full set of tested variants and the filtering procedure are not established. A search for the 11 selected reference-tag rsIDs found no exact identifier matches. This is an rsID-only lookup and does not exclude coordinate aliases. Given the apparent selection, absence cannot determine whether a tag was tested, failed QC or had a p-value of at least 0.05, and it is not a zero association. The result cannot establish whether a tag or score predicts lean mass. Existing warnings about source build, allele orientation and the difference between beta/SE and reported p remain in force.

[Scan receipt](stage6_gepitel_tag_lmi_receipt.json); [lookup table](stage6_gepitel_tag_lmi_summary.csv); reproduce with `python3 work/gepitel/lookup_reference_tag_lmi.py`. The observed filtered distribution does not by itself prove that every omitted variant was tested.
