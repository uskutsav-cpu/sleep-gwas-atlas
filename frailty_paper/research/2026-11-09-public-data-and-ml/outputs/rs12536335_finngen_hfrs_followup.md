# rs12536335: FOXP2 candidate across a hospital frailty measure

A focused exact-rsID search of the published [2025 HFRS GWAS supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs43587-025-00925-y/MediaObjects/43587_2025_925_MOESM1_ESM.xlsx) found **rs12536335** in both its FinnGen hospital frailty risk score (HFRS) and HFRS-without-dementia genome-wide significant results. This is one of the project's 15 exploratory insomnia–frailty candidates, and the published HFRS GWAS is a useful additional phenotype, not the same FI or FFS.

| HFRS outcome | FinnGen effect for G versus A | SE | p | UK Biobank effect aligned to G versus A | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full HFRS | −0.073 | 0.013 | 7.85×10⁻⁹ | −0.100 | 1.24×10⁻⁹ |
| HFRS without dementia | −0.055 | 0.009 | 4.66×10⁻⁹ | −0.099 | 1.03×10⁻⁹ |

The UK Biobank source reports the A allele as effect allele and positive betas, so its displayed values above reverse the sign to express the G effect; the corresponding source rows are preserved without changes in `rs12536335_hfrs_published_rows.csv`. These are regression effects on each study's continuous HFRS scale, not odds ratios or absolute risk. The paper describes FinnGen n=500,737 and UK Biobank n=407,463, but does not make the table values independent of other analyses using the same cohorts. The source describes the variant as intronic in FOXP2 and not an independent lead SNP. The HFRS uses weighted hospital diagnosis codes and differs from both the deficit FI and Fried physical score.

The project’s existing Ensembl mapping identifies the candidate at GRCh37 chr7:114043159 and uniquely maps it to GRCh38 chr7:114403104, matching the supplement coordinate; reference and alternate strings also match A/G. Coordinate agreement alone does not independently establish all original study harmonization details. In the project’s reviewed GWAS, the G allele is associated with lower FI meta-analysis, lower FFS and lower insomnia estimates in each of UKB, FinnGen and MVP inputs. **However**, the UKB FI and Swedish TwinGene FI component estimates had opposite signs under an inferred, not author-confirmed, TwinGene direction adapter. Thus the cross-phenotype directional pattern should not erase the FI cohort inconsistency. Effects cannot be added across different phenotype scales.

The HFRS and insomnia analyses using FinnGen may share participants, as may the UK Biobank analyses. The separate HFRS phenotypes also reuse participants and variants. None of these are independent replications of an insomnia–frailty *shared causal effect*: genetic correlation, locus colocalization, and independent outcome validation remain unproven. Even a significant result for both traits can reflect linked distinct variants, pleiotropy or sample overlap. HFRS’s diagnosis coding and timing are also different from FI and FFS. The published code list excludes the common F51 and G47 sleep-disorder categories, but includes R40 (which covers drowsiness as well as stupor and coma) and Z73 (which includes vital exhaustion among other life-management problems). The dementia-free comparison removes dementia only; it is not a sleep- or exhaustion-free score. See `hfrs_sleep_overlap_audit.md`.

Search coverage: exact rsID against the supplement's two FinnGen genome-wide significant variant lists only. rs12536335 was the only exact candidate match in either table. The lists are prefiltered by p < 5×10⁻⁸, so nonmatching variants are not demonstrated to lack an HFRS association. The workbook SHA-256 is `123c340cf063e2ef618b8ef543dedea6b7c8ef69008488f01027eedbffc04625`. The original [study methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y) describe the HFRS definition, cohort composition, models and study access. FinnGen results should be acknowledged if used in a publication.
