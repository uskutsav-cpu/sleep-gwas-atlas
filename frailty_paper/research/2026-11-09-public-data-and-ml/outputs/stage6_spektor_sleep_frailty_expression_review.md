# Public 2026 sleep/frailty blood-expression supplement

[Spektor et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27062771) studied 1,002 clinical patients aged 60–90 using sleep questionnaires and geriatric assessment. They report higher observed frailty prevalence in the sleep-maintenance-symptom group (61%) than its comparison group (39%); this cross-sectional comparison does not establish temporal direction or causality, and the study states insomnia was not clinically verified. Exact frailty operationalization and overlap with sleep/fatigue items require verification before comparisons with FI or Fried outcomes.

The paper reports a whole-blood RNA-seq subset of 236 participants with BMI <30, including 26 with middle insomnia. It reports 244 genes with adjusted p <0.05 for middle insomnia versus its comparator. The methods describe a randomly selected transcriptomic sample, while the reported analysis additionally applies a BMI restriction; the latter is not representative of all older adults or all 1,002 patients. Authors report inclusion of sequencing batch as a covariate. A single small group and disease/medication differences can affect findings; blood gene expression is not cell-type-resolved, and an expression association does not identify causal genetic regulation.

We downloaded the [public supplementary archive](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijms/ijms-27-02771/article_deploy/ijms-27-02771-s001.zip), examined Table S5, and found 1,022 distinct gene-name rows with nominal p <0.05, of which 244 have adjusted p <0.05. None of the four project genes **FOXP2, RBM6, SMAD5 or MEIS1** appears in this threshold-selected table. This means only that their results are not in this table: they could have failed the nominal selection, failed expression filters or been listed under another name. It is not proof that the genes are unexpressed or have no effect, nor a valid formal replication test. This table represents a sleep comparison, not a frailty-stratified expression analysis. We have not claimed participant independence from other sources without confirmation.

The paper states that underlying data are available on request subject to institutional and ethics restrictions. Its sequencing methods concern RNA, not a confirmed genome-wide participant genotype release; no data were requested. The public tables do not validate either genetic score, and expression alone cannot establish risk or causality.
