# PolyGenie multiple-testing display audit

The GCAT PolyGenie interface offers a setting labelled “Bonferroni (domain-wise).” For the Fried-frailty score, top-versus-bottom decile and ICD-code tab, the live graph returned a single threshold of p = 0.00238, annotated as 21 codes. The corresponding results table has 691 outcomes in 21 distinct domains. The published [app code](https://github.com/gcatbiobank/polygenie-pipeline/blob/main/app/app.py) computes the setting by dividing 0.05 by `filtered_data['domain'].nunique()`, which yields 21 here. The live graph is consistent with that code, although its exact deployed version has not been verified.

This is not the same as Bonferroni correction across outcomes **within each domain**. For the 691 rows returned for this score and tab, the nervous-system domain contains 20 outcomes and the symptoms domain 66. If those returned rows define the respective prespecified families, 0.05/20 = 0.0025 for G47 sleep disorders and 0.05/66 = 0.000758 for R53 malaise/fatigue. Their reported p-values, 0.0360 and 0.00151 respectively, do not pass those within-domain thresholds. The 691-outcome tab-wide Bonferroni threshold is 0.0000724; the live interface's global option displays that same value. These example corrections cover a single score and tab only; they do not account for selecting among scores, tabs, definitions, and other project analyses.

The graph's global FDR setting displays p = 0.00170, and R53's nominal p = 0.00151 is below it. That result should not be presented as a prespecified replication of our project: the domain, score and phenotype were explored post hoc, the outcome is fatigue rather than FI/FFS, the broad PRS differs from our candidates, and the family definition is narrower than all analyses conducted. No hypothesis about project-score prediction is validated here. We do not infer that the underlying regression results themselves are erroneous; the issue is the interpretation of the displayed multiple-testing setting.
