# FOXP2-region insomnia–hospital-frailty colocalization: reference sensitivity

## Main result

Using public summary statistics from the European-ancestry Million Veteran Program (MVP) insomnia GWAS and FinnGen R12 hospital frailty risk score (HFRS), a postselected FOXP2-region SuSiE-coloc analysis produced **highly reference-dependent results**. It is a hypothesis-generating analysis, not a validated shared causal locus.

With the 503-person 1000 Genomes European reference, on a 3,019-variant set also available in the Finnish reference, one insomnia signal and one HFRS signal both had lead rs35624276. Under p1=p2=1e-4 and shared prior p12=1e-5, the model gave PP.H4≈0.983 for that signal pair. A second HFRS signal paired with the insomnia signal favored distinct signals (PP.H3≈0.995, PP.H4≈0.00031). Changing p12 from 1e-6 to 5e-5 gave PP.H4≈0.854–0.997 for the first pair, conditional on this same reference and model.

With the much smaller Finnish subset of the same reference (99 people), using the **same 3,019 variants**, the HFRS credible sets changed; neither had rs35624276 as its lead. At p12=1e-5, the two reported insomnia–HFRS signal pairs had PP.H4≈0.000093 and 0.000725. These are different fitted signal pairs, so the numbers are not direct estimates for an identical fixed causal signal. They nevertheless demonstrate that the apparent shared signal is unstable under these reference choices. The Finnish subset is part of the European reference, not independent, and has only 99 donors. It is too small to establish that the European result is false or that separate signals are correct.

The original exploratory candidate rs12536335 and the EUR-fit signal lead rs35624276 have reference dosage r²≈0.827 in the 503-person EUR panel and ≈0.840 in the 99-person FIN subset. This supports their being correlated markers in these samples, not that either is causal or that FOXP2 is the effector gene. The locus was prioritized after earlier results; HFRS differs from FI/FFS and may capture sleep-adjacent codes. Different trait scales, diagnostic ascertainment, postselection, cohort ancestry and any undisclosed overlap all matter. The source files are from different named cohorts, but absence of all participant or family overlap has not been independently verified. These results do not validate the 15- or 22-marker score.

## Larger Finnish pairwise LD check

A subsequent public SISu 4.2 r² lookup supports strong linkage between rs12536335 and the EUR-fit lead, and suggests the small FIN99 panel can overestimate r² for members of the selected credible set. Because the service does not supply the full signed LD matrix used by SuSiE, it does not resolve the conflicting fits. Details: `stage6_foxp2_sisu_ld_reference_review.md`.

## Larger European signed LD sensitivity

A public 362,063-sample UKB European matrix supports a shared signal on a reduced 553-variant HapMap3+ subset, and the three examined references agree on that subset. This subset omits both original lead variants, the UKB matrix required a PSD correction, and the earlier full-set disagreement remains unresolved. See `stage6_foxp2_ukb_ld_sensitivity_review.md`.

## Larger ancestry-oriented LD, narrow window

A subsequent one-megabase analysis uses public gnomAD NFE LD for MVP and FIN LD for FinnGen HFRS. On 1,257 common SNPs, one signal pair has PP.H4=0.979 at the specified priors, and diagnostics improve relative to the earlier broad small-reference fits. This is conditional, postselected regional evidence and excludes the distal signal. Full results and caveats: `stage6_foxp2_gnomad_matched_coloc_review.md`.

## Input and QC

Full local copies of the [MVP insomnia GWAS, GCST90475826](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90475001-GCST90476000/GCST90475826/) and [FinnGen HFRS GWAS, GCST90707291](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90707001-GCST90708000/GCST90707291/) were checksum-verified before extracting GRCh38 chr7:113403104–115403104. This is a two-trait phenotype analysis; no molecular QTL or three-trait colocalization was included. Matching required the same position, rsID and non-palindromic SNP allele pair; both effect estimates were oriented to the MVP effect allele. Two rows with an ambiguous repeated rsID were excluded. The MVP file's field named `alt` equals its reported effect allele throughout the retained region and is **not treated as genomic alternate/reference**; exact unordered alleles were separately matched to the GRCh37 1000 Genomes genotype record after unique positive-strand liftover. This correction is important for valid LD signs.

MVP's available regional file has no standard-error values. Its Z scores were constructed from the two-sided p values and direction of log(odds ratio), rather than inventing SEs. Rows with odds ratio exactly one, invalid p, or effect-allele frequencies outside 1%–99% in either GWAS were excluded. The original source provides 78,566 cases and 329,572 controls; the working effective sample size is 4×cases×controls/(cases+controls)=253,769, with total N=408,138 tested as a sensitivity assumption. Neither is necessarily the exact per-variant effective N for the meta-analysis. The published source rounds effect estimates and p-values, and either N approximation may affect fine-mapping. FinnGen HFRS uses beta/SE directly and working N=500,737.

Of 3,203 initially aligned, frequency-eligible variants, 3,100 matched exact 1000 Genomes European-reference alleles and passed reference MAF/missingness filters. One additional SNP, rs1083033, had a >0.2 absolute effect-allele-frequency difference between FinnGen and the EUR reference and was conservatively excluded pending source verification. This discrepancy alone does not prove a strand error. The EUR and FIN comparison then uses the 3,019 variants with FIN reference MAF≥1%. Source full-file checksums, per-stage counts, allele checks and reference provenance are recorded in `stage6_foxp2_independent_gwas_extract_receipt.json`, `stage6_foxp2_mvp_hfrs_harmonization_receipt.json`, `stage6_foxp2_reference_receipt.json` and `stage6_foxp2_allele_frequency_qc.csv`; the full remote 1000 Genomes VCF was not checksum-verified.

For the 3,019 common variants, reference matrices have rank at most 502 in EUR and 98 in FIN. The SuSiE Z/LD inconsistency estimates were approximately 0.024–0.033 in EUR and 0.194–0.230 in FIN; larger values indicate more inconsistency, with no universal threshold. On the 3,099-variant EUR set, no reference SNP pair with |r|>0.98 had an aligned Z-score difference above 2, but this check and convergence do not prove consistency. The FIN result is particularly constrained by limited reference sample size, LD differences, variant coverage and possible sample mismatch.

## Reproduction and next step

The official coloc 5.2.3 and susieR 0.14.2 analyses used L=10, 95% credible-set coverage and residual-variance estimation disabled; both fitted models converged. Result tables are `stage6_foxp2_eurcommon_susie_coloc_prior_sensitivity.csv`, `stage6_foxp2_fin_susie_coloc_prior_sensitivity.csv`, and corresponding `*_susie_diagnostic_status.csv` and `*_susie_candidate_sets.csv`. The 3,099-variant EUR sensitivity is `stage6_foxp2_susie_coloc_prior_sensitivity.csv`. The available inputs, scripts, checks and logs are `stage6_foxp2_independent_gwas_extract.py`, `stage6_foxp2_mvp_hfrs_harmonize.py`, `stage6_foxp2_reference_genotypes.py`, `stage6_foxp2_ld_qc_inputs.py`, `stage6_foxp2_fin_reference_indices.py`, and the three `stage6_foxp2*_susie_feasibility.R` / `*_susie_coloc_attempt.R` scripts. `stage6_foxp2_fin_common_ids.txt` is the exact common-support list.

The next useful test requires better study- or ancestry-matched LD and fuller fine-mapping inputs, including an appropriately matched MVP reference and FinnGen-specific LD; compare on identical variants and account for multiple candidate loci. Molecular colocalization would be a separate analysis. The strong EUR-only posterior must not be presented as proof of a common causal variant while the reference sensitivity remains unresolved.
