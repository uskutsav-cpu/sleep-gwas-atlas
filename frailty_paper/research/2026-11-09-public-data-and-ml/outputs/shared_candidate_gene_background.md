# Biological background for selected shared-risk gene labels

Among the exploratory markers, **rs2838788 is annotated in ADARB1 transcripts** and has nominal same-direction associations in the eight reviewed GWAS results. An independent experimental literature makes ADARB1 an interesting circadian hypothesis: its protein is an RNA-editing enzyme, and a [mouse study](https://www.nature.com/articles/ng.3731) reported rhythmic Adarb1 expression and changes to molecular and behavioral circadian rhythms in Adarb1-knockout animals. Another [mouse study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6172258/) examined altered responses to light and signaling in the central clock.

**This is a lead, not an established bridge from rs2838788 to human insomnia or frailty.** The mouse studies tested gene disruption, not this human variant. The project's variant is post-selected, the GWAS datasets overlap, the FI includes sleep-related items, and neither a shared causal variant nor an effect on ADARB1 expression, editing, or circadian function has been established. Insomnia symptoms and circadian rhythms are related research topics but are not interchangeable phenotypes. Do not translate knockout findings into an effect direction or clinical risk for an allele carrier.

The [allele-specific VEP review](shared5_variant_annotation_review.md) reports the exact contrast and transcript annotations. The [eight-source review](shared22_eight_source_evidence_review.md) describes selection, LD and allele limitations. In particular, the counted allele of rs2838788 is T, whereas the annotated alternate is C; neither coding orientation alone implies a molecular direction of effect.

A [candidate-centered regional scan](adarb1_candidate_regional_scan.md) found smaller marginal p-values than rs2838788 in each of the four inspected Mak and clinical-insomnia files, but none reached the conventional genome-wide threshold in its scanned window. Different marker coverage and the absence of conditional analysis prevent interpreting these as distinct or shared causal signals.

## Follow-up that can discriminate the hypothesis

Verify the human variant's build and allele identity; use conditional analysis and fine-mapping in adequately powered, ancestry-matched data for each phenotype; then examine whether molecular-QTL and trait associations colocalize while allowing multiple regional signals. If ADARB1 remains supported, evaluate expression, splicing or RNA-editing QTLs in relevant available human tissues and consider whether sampling time and tissue/cell composition are measured. These are proposed analyses, not findings from this project. Cell-type experiments and AlphaGenome predictions would be later hypotheses to test against molecular and genetic evidence.
