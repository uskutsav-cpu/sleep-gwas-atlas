# Aging-brain molecular associations for the candidate variants

The [ADSP FunGen xQTL Atlas](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/NG00184/) is an open-access GRCh38 collection of molecular association summary statistics from aging-brain cohorts. Its [public variant-download interface](https://xqtl.niagads.org/download) returns only hierarchical-multiple-testing (HMT)-significant associations, across bulk and single-nucleus contexts. Individual-level source data are controlled access. This is molecular context for the project, not a sleep/frailty participant dataset or genetic-score validation.

We queried the 33 distinct rsIDs in the project's two candidate lists on 29 October 2026 UTC. **26 rsID queries returned at least one selected molecular association; seven returned the interface's no-record message.** The 26 responses contained 79 single-nucleus eQTL rows across 15 candidate rsIDs, alongside other molecular-association types. These are numbers of returned rows, not independent loci, donors, genes, tests or replications. As a positive control, rs6733839 returned 17 rows. The portal is significance-filtered: a missing row does not establish that a variant was tested, has no effect or has failed to replicate. An rsID hit alone also does not confirm that the reported allele contrast matches every project's source association.

One concrete link is **rs7734045–SMAD5**. The atlas returns the exact GRCh38 `chr5:136202653:A:C` contrast already checked in the project's [TenK10K molecular analysis](stage6_tenk10k_smad5_variant_caqtl_review.md). In the atlas's `ROSMAP_CUIMC1_2_MIT` single-nucleus mega-analysis, the C allele is associated with SMAD5 expression with beta +0.1717 (SE 0.0223; p 4.97×10⁻¹⁴) in the `exc` context and −0.2616 (SE 0.0344; p 1.06×10⁻¹³) in `oli`. Its [documentation](https://xqtl.niagads.org/about) defines the alternative allele as the effect allele; the [browser documentation](https://xqtl.niagads.org/genomeBrowser) describes this dataset as a ROSMAP single-nucleus mega-analysis. The C allele is the 22-marker count allele. Reported expression effects are on the source's normalized molecular scale, not disease risk or a predicted individual outcome. Cell labels refer to excitatory neurons and oligodendrocytes, subject to the source's annotation.

The opposite point-estimate signs motivate a cell-context hypothesis, but **are not a formal test of a difference between cell types**. These records are selected, and the mega-analysis and constituent ROSMAP datasets overlap; their entries must not be counted as independent validation. Molecular associations at one SNP can reflect linkage disequilibrium rather than a causal SNP. The [earlier disease–chromatin sensitivity](stage6_tenk10k_expanded_fi_susie_review.md) favored distinct frailty and chromatin signals under its tested models, with substantial reference-LD limitations; it does not test this brain eQTL. A new appropriately harmonized regional disease–eQTL colocalization with full unfiltered statistics, matched LD and signal diagnostics would be needed to investigate whether the brain molecular and disease signals are shared. Neither this link nor the earlier chromatin link establishes SMAD5 as the causal gene or a shared insomnia–frailty mechanism.

Reproduce the bounded public rsID screen with `python3 work/niagads_xqtl_candidate_screen.py`; [the receipt](stage6_niagads_xqtl_candidate_screen.json) stores raw returned rows, request statuses and input hashes. It uses the portal's documented interface, not full unfiltered files, and checks neither target donor overlap nor every multiallelic representation. The source requests acknowledgment and citation of NIAGADS accession NG00184 and its contributing studies; see the [dataset page](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/NG00184/). This exploratory screen is not prespecified evidence and should not be used to train a model with the same associations as labels and features.

## Published single-context fine-mapping check

We downloaded the atlas's 87.6 MB single-nucleus 95% credible-set archive and verified its MD5 against the public manifest. In the mega-analysis SMAD5 SNP files, the exact rs7734045 A/C contrast occurs in the excitatory-neuron credible set with reported PIP **0.0061**, and in the inhibitory-neuron set with PIP **0.0073**. The examined SNP files contain 122 and 119 SMAD5 rows in those respective sets. The same contrast was absent from the 111 SMAD5 SNP rows in the oligodendrocyte 95% set, despite its significant marginal association there. These row counts cover SNP files only; they do not count any indels. The source's PIP is conditional on its single-context fine-mapping model and inputs; it is not the probability that the variant causes insomnia or frailty. Absence from a 95% set does not prove no molecular effect. The diffuse molecular signals reinforce that a marginal QTL association alone cannot pinpoint a causal variant.

The full unfiltered single-nucleus archive is approximately **251 GB** in the public manifest, exceeding the approximately 1.7 GiB free at the time of this check. The smaller, filtered credible-set archive cannot substitute for complete regional summary statistics in coloc. Reproduce this inspection using `python3 work/niagads_smad5_cs_audit.py`; the [receipt](stage6_niagads_smad5_cs_audit.json) records the specific archive members and checksum. File sizes and access paths are from [NIAGADS NG00184](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/NG00184/); these are molecular data, not participant-level score validation.

## Unfiltered excitatory-neuron regional access

We subsequently obtained unfiltered **SMAD5 excitatory-neuron** data for its source-reported cis window, GRCh38 chr5:135,132,845–137,132,845, without downloading the full archive. The publisher's member manifest and HTTP byte ranges located the compressed chromosome-5 member and its tabix index; the exact member header and index MD5 were verified. Approximately 6.77 MB of compressed member blocks were retrieved. `pysam` queried the indexed region and produced **7,755 SMAD5 variant rows**; the rs7734045 C-allele beta and SE match the portal record above. Its row reports 737 samples and a non-reference frequency of 0.2985, both specific to that row/source, not the number of sleep/frailty study participants.

This enabled a [regional single-signal disease–eQTL sensitivity](stage6_niagads_smad5_exc_coloc_review.md). The source also reports a wider TAD-enhanced window beyond this cis interval, so potential missed signals must be assessed before interpreting any colocalization. Target-compatible LD, sample/ancestry checks and multi-signal diagnostics remain necessary. The original 716 MB compressed member and 251 GB tar were not downloaded or checksum-verified in full. Reproduce with `python3 work/niagads_smad5_remote_region.py`; its [receipt](stage6_niagads_smad5_exc_region_receipt.json) records byte ranges, index checksum, input member and output hash, and the [regional data](stage6_niagads_smad5_exc_region.tsv.gz) contain only SMAD5 rows.

After excluding palindromic SNPs and using the existing coordinate-liftover and allele matching approach, **4,617** variants matched each Mak FI and physical-frailty GWAS cache and **4,319** matched the FinnGen insomnia cache; the candidate was present in all three. Four matched variants in each Mak dataset and 104 in FinnGen insomnia had absolute QTL/GWAS allele-frequency differences over 0.1. Frequency differences may reflect ancestry, sampling, imputation or harmonization problems; they are not by themselves proof of a coding error. The largest difference in the Mak comparisons was 0.517, requiring source-level follow-up or exclusion in sensitivity analysis. This is a coverage check, not evidence of colocalization or phenotype prediction. The [receipt](stage6_niagads_smad5_harmonization.json) and `work/niagads_smad5_disease_harmonize.py` record the process. Source caches' full-window coverage and remaining contrast, model and sample-overlap assumptions have not been established.
