# Conditional comparison of FI- and FFS-weighted candidate sets

This is an exploratory statistical sensitivity analysis of previously collected All of Us public summary statistics, **not a comparison of individual-level score prediction**. The question is narrow: under the prior approximation for the EUR genetic-similarity group, do the FI-weighted and FFS-weighted versions of the *same candidate set* align differently with the same outcome's marginal variant associations? FI and FFS here name the source weights; the outcome is coded insomnia or generalized muscle weakness, not an observed FI or Fried score. See the [original summary sensitivity and assumptions](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_sensitivity_review.md).

Each score's weight vector is standardized by its variance estimated using 503-person European reference LD. We then account for correlation between the two standardized score projections when calculating the difference in projected Z statistics. This is more appropriate for this specific sensitivity than comparing the two Z values as if they were independent. It assumes the reference approximates the target genotype and Z-score covariance, the same effective sample size across variants, HWE dosage variance, correctly harmonized alleles, source ALT-effect orientation and appropriate normal approximations. Cross-chromosome reference correlations were set to zero. FFS weight direction remains inferred, several variants are multiallelic, and target/source cohort overlap is unresolved. Small reference size, clinical case ascertainment and score selection can violate the approximations.

| Candidate set and outcome | FI-weighted projected Z | FFS-weighted projected Z | Reference-implied correlation | Contrast Z | Nominal two-sided p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15, coded insomnia | 6.79 | 5.00 | 0.799 | 2.82 | 0.0048 |
| 15, coded weakness | 0.91 | 0.41 | 0.798 | 0.79 | 0.43 |
| 22, coded insomnia | 4.11 | 3.56 | 0.775 | 0.81 | 0.42 |
| 22, coded weakness | 0.41 | 0.39 | 0.775 | 0.03 | 0.97 |

The 15-marker insomnia contrast is nominally nonzero under those assumptions, but the sets, outcomes, weights and this contrast were examined after seeing related data. The displayed p values do not account for the full project-wide search; they are not an independent confirmation. The other contrasts are imprecise, not evidence of equal effects. These tests do not compare FI with FFS as phenotypes, establish differences across ancestry groups, infer shared causality or show that either score improves discrimination or calibration. The reference-implied correlation is not a correlation measured in the All of Us cohort. Even a valid association difference would not alone demonstrate that one score predicts better.

Reproduce with `/opt/miniconda3/bin/python3 work/aou_complete/eur_fi_ffs_paired_contrast.py`. Inputs are the [component table](stage6_aou_eur_weighted_z_components.csv) and the original 15- and 22-marker reference LD matrices. The script verifies aligned variant order, identical input Z values for each weight set, matrix symmetry/positive semidefiniteness, and the algebra for the contrast variance; [results](stage6_aou_eur_fi_ffs_paired_contrast.csv) and [hash receipt](stage6_aou_eur_fi_ffs_paired_contrast_receipt.json). These checks verify the calculation, not the biological assumptions. A defensible comparison still requires fixed definitions and paired assessment in independent participants with measured FI and FFS, sleep outcomes and genotype coverage.

A subsequent [variant-omission sensitivity](stage6_aou_paired_contrast_locus_sensitivity.md) reduced the 15-marker insomnia contrast from Z = 2.82 to 1.39 after rs113851554 was omitted. This highlights dependence on the chosen markers under the reference assumptions; the modified sum is not a validated alternative score.
