# Lausanne cohort 65+: sleep/frailty, but no demonstrated genetic-score route

Checked 29 October 2026. The Lausanne 65+ cohort (Lc65+) follows community-dwelling adults recruited at ages 65–70. Its [original cohort description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2532683/) documents sleep-perturbation questions and repeated assessments of physical frailty; its [2022 cohort profile](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyab245) describes Fried-derived categories. A later [2026 Lc65+ paper](https://academic.oup.com/innovateage/article/10/8/igag055/8690946) also describes sleep-disturbance measures in follow-up data. These are potentially useful for sleep–frailty epidemiology, although their exact measures, overlap, temporality and accessible files need independent verification.

The 2022 cohort profile explicitly states that the study lacked biomarkers and genetic data. We did not find evidence of subsequent genome-wide genotyping; this is not proof none has been added since 2022. Without linked genotypes covering the project variants, it cannot evaluate the 15- or 22-variant scores. A [2025 Lc65+ paper](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/15/12/e097424) describes data as available on reasonable request for that paper; this does not establish an unrestricted public download, project eligibility or no cost. A directory page displaying a generic “Genotyping” heading has no content under that heading, and does not override the explicit cohort-profile statement.

Conclusion: retain as a possible observational sleep/frailty source, but do not spend score-validation effort on this cohort unless a current linked genotype release is independently demonstrated. No data or requests were made.
