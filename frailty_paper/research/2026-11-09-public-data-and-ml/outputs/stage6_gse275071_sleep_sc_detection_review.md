# Sleep-restriction single-cell data: exploratory candidate detection

The public [GSE275071 dataset](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE275071) contains human PBMC single-cell RNA sequencing from **eight distinct participants**, four sampled during habitual sleep (HS) and four during sleep restriction (SR). Although the parent study used a crossover design, the sequenced samples are from distinct people across conditions; they must not be modeled as four paired donors. The [associated paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998484/) describes this small human dataset in a broader sleep and inflammation study. The sleep restriction intervention is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis, and this dataset has no project-score genotypes or frailty phenotype. It cannot independently validate the scores or link a risk allele to a cell type.

We downloaded the GEO TAR and read all **32 HDF5 matrices**, corresponding to four capture wells per donor. To prevent pseudoreplication, wells were combined within donor, and the reported percentage is the unweighted mean of four donor-specific detection fractions. For this *descriptive coverage screen*, cells needed ≥500 UMIs, ≥200 detected features and <20% mitochondrial UMIs. We used stringent simple marker gates: CD14 and LST1 each detected, CD3D and CD3E absent for a monocyte-marker-positive subset; CD3D and CD3E each detected, CD14 and LST1 absent for a T-marker-positive subset. These gates are provisional, not validated cell annotations; doublets, other lineages, dropout, gene ambiguity and quality thresholds can change composition. They are not the authors' full clustering analysis. Detection means at least one transcript count, not differential expression or protein activity.

| Gene | Monocyte-marker subset, HS | Monocyte-marker subset, SR | T-marker subset, HS | T-marker subset, SR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FOXP2 | 0.067% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| SMAD5 | 22.3% | 19.0% | 7.4% | 7.7% |
| RBM6 | 52.9% | 49.9% | 26.8% | 25.4% |
| MEIS1 | 0.34% | 0.17% | 0% | 0.033% |

Each value is the mean of four donor-specific fractions. The total gated cells across the four HS/SR donors were 1,677/1,455 for the monocyte-marker gate and 2,648/2,822 for the T-marker gate; cells are not independent biological replicates. The mean differences between conditions are not statistical tests and must not be interpreted as a sleep-restriction effect. FOXP2 and MEIS1 were rarely detected under these gates, consistent with limited sensitivity for follow-up in this small dataset; nondetection does not prove absence of expression. Detectability varies with sequencing depth, cell type and filtering. This helps contextualize the [bulk insomnia PBMC analysis](stage6_gse208668_insomnia_candidate_review.md), whose FOXP2 measurement lacks available raw probes; the two datasets differ substantially and are not a formal replication or biological contradiction.

For a stronger cell-specific follow-up, use larger, well-annotated human datasets with more independent donors, balanced conditions and appropriate donor-level pseudobulk methods; prespecify QC, batches and cell types. Connecting variants to expression requires an allele-aware eQTL/colocalization analysis in relevant cells, not expression detection alone.

## Marker-gate sensitivity

A follow-up retained the same QC but also examined **all QC-passing cells**, a relaxed CD14-detected set, and a relaxed CD3D-detected set, without excluding other markers. These overlapping, nonexclusive groups are not validated cell types. Among all QC-passing cells (9,315 HS and 8,692 SR), the mean donor detection fractions for FOXP2 were 0.020% and 0%, respectively; for MEIS1 they were 0.218% and 0.152%. These values remain low without the strict two-marker gates, so the conclusion about *limited measured coverage in this dataset* is not solely an artifact of those gates. They do not establish absence of either gene or explain the prior bulk-microarray signal. Low expression, dropout, ambient RNA, differing cell proportions, assay preparation and the small sample remain possible explanations.

The relaxed CD14- and CD3D-positive groups produced the same broad qualitative pattern; their differing detection fractions cannot be compared as formal cell-type enrichment because they have different capture rates and mixed membership. No condition effect or statistical significance was estimated. Reproduce with `work/stage4_env/bin/python work/sleep_sc/gate_sensitivity.py`; the [complete aggregate results](stage6_gse275071_gate_sensitivity.csv) and [receipt](stage6_gse275071_gate_sensitivity.json) retain the donor ranges and data hash. The additional screen was conducted after seeing the original results and is a sensitivity analysis, not independent replication.
