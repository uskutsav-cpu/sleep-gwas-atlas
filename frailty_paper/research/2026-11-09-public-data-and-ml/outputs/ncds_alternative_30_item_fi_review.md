# NCDS 30-item FI: direct sleep and fatigue overlap found

Updated 29 October 2026. **The alternative 30-item age-50 frailty index is not sleep-free.** Its [published supplement](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jcm/jcm-12-05568/article_deploy/jcm-12-05568-s001.zip) lists as item 9 the frequency of waking during the night and having trouble falling back asleep. This is the same sleep-maintenance question (`n8scq14`) we had identified as a potential separate symptom outcome. The index also lists chronic fatigue syndrome (ME) as item 23. Although the [paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10487987/) says it excluded exhaustion and depression symptoms, that statement does not mean all sleep and fatigue concepts were excluded.

This changes the proposed analysis: an association between the original 30-item FI and the sleep-maintenance question partly reflects **the same response being included in both measures**. Removing item 9 yields at most 29 components; removing both items 9 and 23 yields at most 28. These are modified research indices, not the published index, and both fall below the commonly used 30-deficit convention. Excluding chronic fatigue syndrome addresses a diagnosis label, not necessarily every fatigue-related influence. Such exclusions cannot establish construct independence, remove all measurement overlap or demonstrate a causal relationship.

## Scoring issue needing resolution

The supplement labels the sleep-maintenance question with response categories “very severe” through “none”, identical to its bodily-pain row immediately above. The original [NCDS self-completion questionnaire](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCDS-2008-Self-Completion-Questionnaire.pdf) instead gives six frequency categories, from all of the time to none of the time. Frequency categories are not the listed pain-severity labels; check the data dictionary as well. This apparent inconsistency prevents a faithful implementation of the published scoring from the supplement alone; obtain the authors' scoring code or an authoritative clarification, and verify response mapping and missing-value handling. The supplement also gives quintile scoring for four cognitive measures without specifying fixed cutoffs or tie handling. The correct letter-cancellation count needs its own field-level verification.

## Clinical and methodological limits

This 30-item FI differs materially from the 34-item published FI: it includes a sleep-maintenance question and a chronic-fatigue-syndrome diagnosis, whereas the 34-item index contains two other explicit sleep deficits and four fatigue symptoms. The authors acknowledge that their 30-item index's validity is unknown and that they had not validated it against a Fried phenotype or downstream hospitalization or mortality outcomes. It was developed in a midlife cohort, not as a validated older-adult FFS substitute. The original paper reports 9,789 survey participants and 2,905 classified frail in its table; these are not counts with usable genotypes and project outcomes. [Author manuscript](https://uel-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/453630).

No individual data or project-score validation was performed. Genetic coverage, linkage, consent, independence and sample sizes still need confirmation. CLS describes genomic access without a data fee following an application and a separate linked-survey pathway; free survey availability alone does not authorize genotype access. [CLS genetics](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/access.html); [linked-data route](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/data-access-training/data-access/accessing-data-directly-from-cls/).
