# FOXP2 region: FI results in UK Biobank and TwinGene

## Result

In the published Atkins FI cohort-level file, the project's FOXP2 marker rs12536335 has opposite estimated effects on the counted G allele: UK Biobank beta **−0.01889** (SE 0.00345; p=4.6e−8) and TwinGene beta **+0.03922** (SE 0.01454; p=0.006997). This uses the project's inferred correction to the TwinGene beta direction; that correction is supported by source-file reconstruction checks but **has not been confirmed by the study authors and conflicts with the archive README**. Without confirmation, even the direction of the apparent discrepancy remains provisional. The cohort effects are from separately normalized, differently constructed FIs, and the comparison is postselected.

To check whether this is isolated to one marker, I matched 1,187 variants in the same 1-Mb gnomAD reference window by rsID, position and exact allele pair against the public cohort file. Twelve of those markers—including rs12536335—have reference r²≥0.8 with it in gnomAD non-Finnish Europeans. After applying the same inferred TwinGene sign adapter, all 12 show opposite UKB and TwinGene marginal Z directions. Their median Z values, oriented to the same anchor phase, are +5.16 and −2.62 respectively. The table is `stage6_foxp2_fi_cohort_high_ld.csv`. These markers are highly correlated and are **not 12 independent replications**. The gnomAD LD is external, not either cohort's in-sample LD. Reversing the disputed TwinGene adapter reverses the reported TwinGene direction for all 12, illustrating why confirming source coding is a prerequisite to biological interpretation.

Both cohort-wide SuSiE-RSS models converged using the 1,187 matched variants and gnomAD NFE LD. UKB FI returned one credible set; TwinGene FI returned none (maximum PIP 0.037), so there is **no SuSiE-coloc signal-pair posterior** for the two FI cohorts. The number or PIP of sets depends on sample size, coverage, reference LD and modeling choices. A missing credible set is not proof that the locus has no effect or that the cohorts have different causal variants. The small Swedish sample (n=10,616) and prior failure of the project's TwinGene LDSC heritability precision gate remain important limits.

## Implications

The observed pattern is a reason to **avoid calling rs12536335 consistently associated in the same direction across FI cohorts**. It does not show that the variant acts in opposite directions biologically, determine whether insomnia is responsible for FI differences, or refute the separately analyzed MVP-insomnia/FinnGen-HFRS colocalization. The latter involves different traits and cohorts; HFRS is not FI. UK Biobank and TwinGene contributed to the FI meta-analysis used in candidate selection, so this is not independent validation of the score. The two cohort FI instruments and age distributions differ, source allele semantics are unresolved, and estimates are selected after looking at results. No formal significance claim is made from the correlated high-LD rows.

## Method and provenance

The full 16,557,800-row public Atkins cohort archive was scanned. The 1,187 valid unique source matches retain raw TwinGene beta and the inferred adapter-aligned beta in `stage6_foxp2_fi_cohort_region_aligned.csv`; no source data were altered. `stage6_foxp2_fi_cohort_region_extract.py` and its receipt document exact matching. Source file MD5 was not reverified in this scan. `stage6_foxp2_fi_cohort_high_ld.py` computes signed reference LD and the phase-oriented descriptive table. `stage6_foxp2_fi_cohort_susie.R` uses 164,610 and 10,616 as working cohort sizes, L=10 and fixed residual variance; diagnostics and model fits are retained. No participant-level data were accessed.

The prior direction audit and the rationale for the provisional adapter are in `twingene_direction_genomewide_audit.md`; the source-specific candidate and confidence-interval checks are in `candidate_twingene_sensitivity.md` and `fi_candidate_cohort_difference_sensitivity.md`. Confirm the original source direction and allele representation against the study team or original analysis output before drawing stronger conclusions.
