# Japanese knee-strength GWAS: candidate-variant screen

The [Ito et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06108-6) GWAS comprises 3,452 older Japanese participants from the Shimane CoHRE and Bunkyo Health cohorts. Its [public GWAS Catalog file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90319001-GCST90320000/GCST90319502/) was downloaded in full and its MD5 matched the publisher metadata; all 9,146,474 rows were scanned. This is a potentially separate-population association follow-up, but complete participant and close-relative non-overlap with every discovery and candidate-selection dataset is not established. It is not an individual-level validation dataset.

The screen found exactly one GRCh37 position-and-allele-pair match for **12 of 15** weighted-prototype markers and **14 of 22** count-prototype markers. No rows at the expected positions were found for three and eight markers respectively; their names and all extracted values are in the [candidate table](stage6_japanese_knee_candidate_matches.csv). No matching row does not prove the variant is absent in the cohort or lacks an effect: frequency and imputation filters, representation or other factors may account for it. Exact alleles do not by themselves resolve every multiallelic or strand issue.

Among the observed markers, rs10865954 (15-marker set) and rs11636381 (22-marker set) had nominal p-values of 0.0208 and 0.00765 respectively. These tests were postselected and are unadjusted for multiple tests. They are not replicated shared sleep–frailty loci, and non-significant markers are not proven null. In particular, knee strength is a continuous physical measure; it is not FI, the full Fried frailty phenotype, or a joint insomnia–frailty outcome. The small sample and ancestry differences can affect power and transfer. The table records the source effect allele and conditional orientation to each prototype's counted allele; do not read positive beta as increased frailty: the GWAS phenotype increases with transformed knee strength.

The source-specific lead control agrees with the paper: rs10749438 A versus G has beta −0.1481, SE 0.0270 and p 4.237×10⁻⁸, consistent with its published ALT-A result. This supports the source effect-allele interpretation in this example but does not eliminate variant-specific strand or multiallelic uncertainty. A subsequent [partial-score weighted-Z screen](stage6_japanese_knee_weighted_z_review.md) had statistically uncertain estimates. Neither a full-score association nor individual prediction, discrimination or calibration has been calculated. A comparison of score weights cannot be inferred from counts of nominal SNP associations.
