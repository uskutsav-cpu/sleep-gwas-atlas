# Candidate selection, independent follow-up, and an insomnia-score comparator

## Where the 15 candidates came from

[Song et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2024.1358996/full) used Atkins frailty-index (FI) GWAS summary statistics and the UK Biobank `ukb-b-3957` sleeplessness/insomnia GWAS. The latter is based on UK Biobank field 1200; the Atkins UK Biobank FI also includes an insomnia item. The discovery traits therefore share both source participants and phenotype content. This matters for interpreting cross-trait findings; it does not, on its own, quantify how much of any association the overlap explains.

The published Table S4 listed 124 cross-trait candidates. For the project's exploratory prototype, we ranked those rows by their published CPASSOC p-value, took the top 30, then retained 15 available after the project's FI/insomnia and FFS source checks. An audit of all 30 rows shows that **every one of the 15 excluded rows is in the approximate chromosome 6 MHC exclusion interval**, and every one of the 15 retained rows is outside that flagged interval. None of the 15 excluded IDs occurs in the filtered FI or insomnia inputs. This is a description of this particular selection, not proof that each absent row has only one possible exclusion reason, nor a claim that all MHC candidates lack biological relevance. The [complete 30-row audit](candidate_top30_selection_audit.csv) records the reported rank, position, MHC flag and source lookup counts. The Song table's multi-trait p-values do not provide independent per-trait effect estimates or valid score weights.

Selection was not a random or LD-independent sample of the genome. The first-ranked retained variant, rs113851554, has very strong insomnia evidence but FI p=0.199 and FFS p=0.19 in the project’s source lookups. Conversely, rs12536335 and rs1909650, ranked 22 and 12 in this subset of Song's list, have the strongest joint MVP-insomnia/HFRS screen values among the 15. Selection and phenotype overlap prevent treating the original discovery results as independent confirmation. FI and FFS reuse UK Biobank; exact participant overlap between all study cohorts remains unverified.

## Interpreting the follow-up datasets

The later joint screen uses the European-ancestry VA Million Veteran Program (MVP) insomnia GWAS `GCST90475826`, and Finnish hospital frailty GWAS `GCST90707291`. These sources differ from Song's named UK Biobank sleep input and Atkins FI input at the cohort level, and their clinical/registry definitions differ from the original self-report inputs. That is useful context for the follow-up, but the complete participant/relatedness overlap and all GWAS QC assumptions have not been audited. It is not sufficient to call the post-selected result independently replicated. In particular HFRS is a distinct hospital-record outcome, not the same FI or FFS, and broad HFRS diagnostic codes may reflect drowsiness or exhaustion. Full allele and build caveats, and the limitations of the post hoc maximum-p screen, are in `mvp_insomnia_hfrs_joint_candidate_screen.md`.

## New published sleep-score comparator

Earlier, [Lane et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6415688/) developed a 57-variant insomnia-symptom genetic score from UK Biobank and tested it in HUNT and Partners Biobank. They reported HUNT OR **1.015** (95% CI 1.010–1.020) and Partners OR **1.017** (95% CI 1.007–1.027) per score allele under their published scale. HUNT used self-reported symptoms; Partners used physician-diagnosed insomnia. These are published associations for a **different** 57-variant score, not the present 15- or 22-marker prototypes, absolute disease risks or estimates of predictive improvement. The scale is per score allele as reported, not per SD, and must not be compared numerically with the standardized Wyss estimates below. A meaningful future comparison would calculate both a prespecified broad insomnia score and the project scores in the same independent participants, after checking allele coding and sample/relative overlap.

The [published supplementary workbook](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6415688.1/NIHMS1519714-supplement-2.xlsx) was subsequently obtained from the NIH open-access repository. Its Table S7 contains HUNT individual-variant results and Table S8 contains Partners results. Six of the 33 unique project candidates occur in both tables; their results and exact-match coverage are in the [follow-up review](stage6_lane2019_candidate_replication_review.md). These selected-locus results still require allele and cohort-overlap checks and cannot establish prediction by either complete project score.

[Wyss et al. (2026)](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/doi/10.1093/sleep/zsag200/8739310) evaluated genome-wide sleep scores against several sleep definitions across HCHS/SOL, ARIC, MESA and BHS. The MVP European-ancestry insomnia score ([PGS019929](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS019929/)) uses PRS-CSx and 1,085,967 variants, based on the same MVP insomnia GWAS used in this project's follow-up. For a combined WHIIRS-defined insomnia outcome across the cohorts, the paper reports an odds ratio of **1.19 per SD** of that score (95% CI 1.10–1.27), with heterogeneity p=0.49. Its UK Biobank-derived insomnia score ([PGS019928](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS019928/)) had a reported OR of **1.17 per SD** (95% CI 1.11–1.22) for the corresponding combined outcome. These are association estimates with sleep measures, not absolute probabilities, head-to-head predictive superiority, or estimates for frailty. They are from the same study, and the scores and endpoints have different origins and definitions. The MVP score’s original GWAS includes clinically recorded insomnia whereas the UKB score uses a self-report sleep question; the authors also report differences across sleep definitions.

This provides a useful **pre-existing genome-wide sleep benchmark** for a future independent evaluation alongside the FI and FFS scores and the project's sparse candidate sums. It does not validate our 15-marker score, nor can the published sleep associations be assumed to transfer to frailty. Any evaluation must verify licensing, score-file alleles and genome build, ancestry and imputation quality, define the outcome and follow-up, establish discovery–evaluation non-overlap, and compare calibration and incremental performance in held-out individuals. The MVP-derived score cannot be independently tested in the same MVP GWAS participants merely by reusing the summary statistics.
