# PISA: linked genetics, sleep and frailty access and phenotype feasibility

Checked 28 October 2026. The Australian Prospective Imaging Study of Ageing (PISA) is a potentially relevant cohort but **not an established free, openly downloadable validation dataset**. [Foote et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12626885/) report a PISA analysis of 3,265 people with an FI linked to genotypes, and the supplementary methods describe selection of one twin from each twin pair. Their published PRS findings use other scores, not the project's 15- or 22-marker prototypes. The [PISA cohort description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7750170/) reports a sleep-monitoring subset in its onsite arm: at the 2020 snapshot, 102 had completed monitoring. This is not the total number with genotypes, FI and usable sleep data; contemporary sample overlap and completeness are unknown.

**29 October 2026 recheck:** The study's [current QIMR page](https://www.qimrb.edu.au/studies/prospective-imaging-study-of-ageing-%28pisa%29) describes ongoing extensions and an onsite smart-sensing device for sleep and activity. It does not provide a current count of participants with the required joint data, establish that objective sleep monitoring measures insomnia, or state a research data-access price. The historical 102 count should therefore not be treated as the present total; the new page does not remove the access or linkage uncertainty.

**Key phenotype issue:** Foote et al.'s [supplementary workbook](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41588-025-02269-0/MediaObjects/41588_2025_2269_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx), Table 54, lists sleep problems, need for longer sleep, poor sleep, overall sleep quality, a sleep-or-mood medication item, sleep apnea and tiredness among the deficits used in the PISA FI. The medication and apnea items have wider meanings than insomnia, and the table alone does not establish how each item was coded, completeness or temporal overlap with every possible exposure. Nonetheless, this FI is not sleep-excluded. Using it as a sleep-associated outcome can partly capture shared or related content. A useful future evaluation would compare the original FI with an explicitly reconstructed sleep-excluded FI, then a sleep-and-fatigue-excluded sensitivity, with updated denominators, eligibility and event counts, and distinguish recorded sleep from a clinical insomnia diagnosis.

**Access:** The cohort description states that de-identified data are shared with research groups on project-specific request under a data-sharing agreement, possibly requiring local ethics approval. Foote et al. likewise state individual-level PISA data are available on reasonable request. Neither source establishes no-cost access, approval eligibility for this project, or an unrestricted public download. No application was submitted. Variant availability, permitted linkage, genotype QC, covariates, overlap with the project's discovery/selection cohorts and relatives, and actual sample size for jointly observed endpoints remain unverified. Genetic enrichment for Alzheimer's disease in the onsite cohort and recruitment of twins and relatives can affect selection and generalizability. This is a possible controlled-access follow-up, not a completed validation or a workaround to access restrictions.
