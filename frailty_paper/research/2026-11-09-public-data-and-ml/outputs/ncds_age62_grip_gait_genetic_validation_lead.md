# NCDS age-62 measured strength and walking: validation lead

Reviewed 29 October 2026. **Potential no-data-fee route to objective physical outcomes, subject to data approval and linked-sample confirmation.** The NCDS age-62 survey includes measured grip and timed walking in addition to sleep questions and older age-50 frailty data. These are individual physical-function outcomes, not the full Fried frailty phenotype and not direct validation of a combined insomnia–frailty endpoint. They do not themselves include a sleep questionnaire response.

## What the public documentation establishes

- The [CLS age-62 sweep page](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/cls-studies/1958-national-child-development-study/ncds-age-62-sweep/) describes fieldwork at ages 61–65, 2020–2024, with grip strength, timed walking, balance and other measures. The [UK Data Service notice](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/2025/09/02/new-national-child-development-study-age-62-data-now-available/) says most age-62 survey data are free under an end-user licence; selected sensitive files require secure access. The page identifies main study 9412 and sensitive study 9413. Determine the exact access tier and release of each requested variable before treating this as an immediately downloadable linked dataset.
- A [UKRI catalogue entry](https://gtr.ukri.org/project/9515A490-C0C2-4493-BFB4-7BDA83117BBE?fetchSize=50&pn=0&selectedSortOrder=ASC&selectedSortableField=parentPublicationTitle) identifies study 9412’s second edition (October 2025) as including an updated biomeasures dataset, including updated grip variables. This strengthens evidence that grip data were deposited in the main survey collection. It does not establish exact field availability in today’s release, walking-field access, or permission to use them in a genetic-linked extract.
- The [age-62 guide](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ncds_sweep10_age62_user_guide.pdf) identifies grip fields `n10mmgsl1`, `n10mmgsr1`, `n10mmgsl2`, `n10mmgsr2`, hand averages and `n10mmgsres`; grip is measured in kg with two trials per hand. It identifies timed-walk outcomes `n10mmtrya` and `n10mmtryb` and seconds `n10mmwlka` and `n10mmwlkb`, for two 8-foot/244-cm walks. It lists these measures in the health-visit data, but the guide being mirrored in secure-deposit documentation does not prove each variable's precise public release or permitted linkage to genotypes.
- The [technical report](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NCDS_sweep10_-age-62_-technical-report.pdf) describes 6,309 productive health visits, 6,177 with at least one grip measurement and 5,998 with at least one timed walk in eligible subsets. These are fieldwork response counts, **not** verified usable target-data counts, not evidence that the same people contributed both measures and age-50 sleep, and not the intersection with QC-passing NCDS genotypes. Eligibility and inability-to-perform require explicit handling; study report counts do not imply complete, high-quality repeated trials. Mop-up-only respondents were not offered a health visit.
- [CLS genomics](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/access.html) describes genetic data available without charge following application. The [direct CLS access page](https://cls.ucl.ac.uk/data-access-training/data-access/accessing-data-directly-from-cls/) describes a separate genetic-plus-survey linkage pathway. Approval, institutional setup, actual costs beyond the data fee, specific age-62 field linkage, genotype coverage, ancestry, selection and sample/relative independence from each source remain unconfirmed.

## Practical study design if access is granted

Freeze the score files and resolve allele/build, imputation and quality issues before outcome analysis. Prespecify separate continuous outcomes: measured grip strength in kilograms (define a maximum or other rule across the four trials) and walking speed in metres per second (using 2.44 metres and valid timed trials, with an explicitly defined trial-selection rule). Inspect outcome codes, units and documentation rather than converting invalid or zero times; report inability/refusal separately. Account for age, sex, ancestry principal components and relevant technical variables; report selection, missingness, score coverage and uncertainty. Compare each score against the same baseline model on the same participants. These outcomes provide a targeted physical-function test and do not establish FI, FFS, calibration of binary frailty risk, or validation of a joint sleep–frailty score.

Age-50 insomnia symptoms can be examined against age-62 physical function as a separate exploratory longitudinal analysis where timing and linkage permit; neither a genetic-score association with function nor this observational association is proof of causation. An FFS-style composite would additionally require defensible contemporaneous definitions of weakness, slowness, weight loss, physical activity and exhaustion, appropriate thresholds and completeness; these are not yet documented or validated. Its exhaustion component would still require an explicit sleep/fatigue overlap audit. Do not assert that a complete FFS has been located.

## Required gates

1. Confirm the current data record, release and access conditions for each of the named fields and longitudinal survey identifier, and whether their inclusion in an approved CLS genotype-linked extract is permitted.
2. Obtain actual counts with QC-passing genotype, exact score variants and alleles, age-50 sleep, age-62 valid outcomes and covariates; verify participant and family overlap with discovery, selection and earlier exploratory datasets.
3. Check test eligibility, protocol, trial/failed-measure coding, study weighting and sensitivity to age, sex, disability and selection at the health visit. Check whether any change in delivery over 2020–2024 affects measurement comparability.
4. Prespecify hypotheses, minimum variant coverage and handling of missing alleles, with no claim of a full-score result if the score is truncated. Analyze only within the permitted environment and terms.

No participant-level records have been accessed and no project score has been tested in NCDS.

The [five-component crosswalk](ncds_age62_fried_component_crosswalk.md) identifies candidate activity and energy questions as well as the objective measures. It does not establish a standard five-component FFS: recent unintentional weight loss and exact exhaustion/physical-activity definitions remain unverified, and current weight-change intention cannot supply them retrospectively.
