# ASPREE/ALSOP as a potential older-adult validation cohort

**Assessment:** ASPREE/ALSOP is a promising newly identified *controlled-access* lead, not an immediately public linked genotype–phenotype dataset and not a completed validation. Cost, the actual availability of the relevant genotype files through the same access route, participant-level linkage and score-marker coverage remain unknown.

ASPREE enrolled 19,114 initially relatively healthy older adults in Australia and the US, mostly aged ≥70 (≥65 for US minority participants), and collected longitudinal health information; it was a randomized aspirin trial, so treatment arm and selection criteria matter. The Australian ALSOP ancillary questionnaire included ten modified Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index questions, including sleep duration and frequency of problems ([ALSOP profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6693806/)). These are sleep measures, not proof of a clinical insomnia diagnosis.

A published [ASPREE/ALSOP analysis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-024-17794-6) reports sleep problems and an adapted Fried classification for the same cohort, using weight, grip, exhaustion, walking speed and activity. A separate ASPREE analysis has used both a 66-item deficit index and modified Fried phenotype ([abstract](https://academic.oup.com/innovateage/article-pdf/6/Supplement_1/330/48309396/igac059.1302.pdf)); exact item definitions, wave consistency, overlap with sleep questions, eligibility and the sample with all three data types must be verified. The same-cohort sleep/frailty report alone does not establish genotype availability for those particular participants.

[Monash's ASPREE Genomics program](https://www.monash.edu/medicine/sphpm/genomics-program/aspree-genomics) describes large-scale sequencing and genotyping; an [associated project](https://research.monash.edu/en/projects/genetic-analysis-of-the-aspree-cohort) describes funding to genotype 15,000 samples, not confirmation that 15,000 linked participants or all project SNPs are accessible. The Medical Genome Reference Bank contains an ASPREE subset but is a selected healthy-elderly reference, not an independently phenotype-linked score-validation dataset. Actual imputation quality, genomic build, allele encoding, ancestry, relatives and sample overlap with project discovery and selection studies must be checked.

[ASPREE access](https://aspree.org/usa/for-researchers/) is by reviewed application, with NIH's AgingResearchBiobank mentioned for US researchers. The [NIH request instructions](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/how-to-make-a-request/) require an eligible senior PI, institutional arrangements and reviewed access. The repository's [cost page](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/costs/) says basic data queries are free but charges for biospecimens and complex support; it does **not** establish zero cost for the specific linked genomic–ALSOP–frailty data. Its [FAQ](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/faq/) describes large-scale genomic availability prospectively, without confirming this dataset. The [ASPREE access policy](https://ams.aspree.org/public/request-data/access-aspree-data/) says sub-study requests require contacting the relevant lead before an EOI and normally use a safe haven; that is a requirement for this controlled route, not a completed request. No contact, application or individual-level data access occurred.

If a qualified researcher can use an eligible, genuinely free route, first verify whether the same individuals have genotypes, ALSOP sleep responses and all FI/FFS components at prespecified waves, together with actual score variants, imputation INFO and appropriate permissions. Estimate the number of complete observations and outcomes before committing to validation; compare sleep-excluded and original FI, a physical phenotype, and the frozen scores on the same held-out participants. This cohort's healthy-entry selection, treatment randomization, age, ancestry and attrition limit generalizability.
