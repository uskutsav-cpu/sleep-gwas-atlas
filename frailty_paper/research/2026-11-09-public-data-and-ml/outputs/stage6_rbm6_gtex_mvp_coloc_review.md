# RBM6 cortex expression and veteran insomnia: expanded-coverage test

## Finding

In a 1,000-variant analysis that includes rs10049477, the GTEx cortex RBM6 expression signal and the Million Veteran Program (MVP) clinical-insomnia signal **favor distinct variants under the illustrated prior**, with PP.H3=0.847 and PP.H4=0.129 at p1=p2=0.0001, p12=0.00001. PP.H4 varies from 0.015 to 0.425 as p12 varies from 0.000001 to 0.00005. This is not a definitive negative result: it depends on the prior, reference LD, variants, working sample size, phenotype and model. It is also a postselected pairwise analysis, not a joint probability for sleep, frailty and expression.

On the same input, the cortex eQTL–Mak frailty-index (FI) pair retained a component with PP.H4=0.948 and PP.H3=0.052 at the illustrated prior; two other FI component pairs favored distinct signals. For MVP insomnia versus that FI component, PP.H4=0.300 and PP.H3=0.679 at the illustrated prior; PP.H4 changes from 0.041 to 0.682 across the shared-prior range. These separate pairwise comparisons do not establish a shared three-trait causal variant, gene mediation, a causal direction or patient-level risk-score performance. MVP insomnia and Mak FI are distinct named studies; complete sample and relatedness non-overlap has not been established. Mak FI uses UK Biobank participants and includes an insomnia item.

The 1,000 variants include 44 of 49 unique variants in the published cortex RBM6 expression credible set, representing a sum of 0.917 of 0.953 of its published PIPs. They include rs10049477, the leading published eQTL variant (PIP 0.503); five set variants are missing, including an indel excluded by our non-palindromic SNP filter. The MVP fit gives this variant PIP 0.00089, whereas the expression fit gives it PIP 0.210. PIPs are conditional on the respective fits and are not proof of distinct causal variants. The earlier Jansen insomnia comparison could not include rs10049477 and yielded a higher PP.H4; cohorts, insomnia definitions, variant support and sample properties differ, so the two values do not isolate the cause of disagreement or amount to a formal heterogeneity test.

For context only, the harmonized MVP record for rs10049477 reports C versus T odds ratio 0.9785, p=0.0001683; the QTL source reports an increase in RBM6 expression for C. Marginal effects do not prove the same variant drives either signal, and different scales cannot be interpreted as a causal effect of expression on insomnia.

## Methods and limitations

The public [GWAS Catalog MVP insomnia harmonized file GCST90475826](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST90475826) was queried by its GRCh38 indexed region. The index hash matched the previously checked published MD5, but the complete remote harmonized file was not checksum-verified. All 1,000 retained allele pairs, p values, effect frequencies and oriented odds ratios exactly agree with the locally available original source regional extract, after reciprocal transformation for flipped effect alleles. The 549-MB original file's SHA-256 matches the previously recorded expected hash. `stage6_rbm6_mvp_source_consistency.py` records that cross-check. The original study reports odds ratios and p values but no usable standard errors, so signed Z scores were reconstructed from p and the sign of the rounded odds ratio; rows with an odds ratio exactly one were excluded. This approximation and the original source's ancestry and phenotype properties matter.

We matched this source by exact non-palindromic SNP position and allele pair to the previous GTEx–FI–FFS reference set and retained 1,000 of 1,006 variants after requiring available valid MVP statistics and an absolute frequency difference ≤0.20 against gnomAD NFE. Maximum observed MVP/reference frequency difference was 0.0521. FFS frequency was unavailable, so it could not be checked. Full source and AF receipts are retained; the reference is external, and the custom Hail-index decoder has not been independently verified with Hail.

SuSiE-RSS used L=10, 95% credible sets, fixed residual variance, signed NFE LD and working N=205 for cortex, effective N≈253,769 for MVP, 402,910 for FI and 386,565 for FFS. All fits converged; cortex, MVP, FI and FFS returned one, one, three and one credible sets. Repeating with total MVP N=408,138 instead gave cortex–MVP PP.H4=0.1287 and MVP–selected-FI PP.H4=0.3000 at p12=0.00001, virtually unchanged in this sensitivity run. This does not correct other possible sample-size, study-LD or model misspecification. The same expression and frailty data recur across comparisons, so they are not independent replication. Bulk cortex does not establish a relevant cell type. None of these results test the 15- or 22-marker scores in patients.

Reproduction scripts: `stage6_rbm6_mvp_harmonised_extract.py`, `_mvp_original_extract.py`, `_mvp_source_consistency.py`, `_gtex_mvp_prepare.py`, `_gtex_mvp_coloc.R`, and `_gtex_mvp_totaln_coloc.R`; aligned data, diagnostics, set members, priors and source receipts are saved with matching prefixes. The original-file extraction requires the referenced external drive, while the harmonized public index and region can be retrieved again. The reference LD used is the saved subset from the prior expanded frailty analysis.
