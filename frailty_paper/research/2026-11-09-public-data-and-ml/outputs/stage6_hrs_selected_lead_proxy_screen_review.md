# HRS published FI leads near the project candidates

The public [Mekli et al. HRS/ELSA supplementary table](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0207824) contains 32 selected HRS frailty-index associations. I checked every pair of these published variants and the 15- and 22-marker prototypes within 1 Mb on GRCh37. All six nearby pairs involve the same 22-marker candidate, rs28528997 in the MAD1L1 region. No 15-marker candidate is within 1 Mb of these 32 selected rows. This is a screen of a selected table, not of all HRS variants, so lack of a nearby row does not imply absence or a null association.

Five of the six HRS variants had matching biallelic records in the 503-person European 1000 Genomes reference. Their reference r² with rs28528997 ranged from 0.66 to 0.92; the sixth, rs6947019, was multiallelic in this reference and was not tested. The strongest proxy, rs62444881, has r² = 0.922; the other four range from 0.660 to 0.715. The six HRS associations are nearby and correlated, not independent confirmations. HRS p-values were selected and range from 2.95×10⁻⁶ to 8.18×10⁻⁶. The ELSA p-values for these six were 0.145–0.711, so none supplies a nominally significant replication in that cohort; this does not prove a zero effect or an effect difference between cohorts.

HRS and ELSA used transformed frailty indices, not the genetic count or a joint insomnia–frailty outcome. The published HRS table gives A1 but not a complete allele pair or verified strand. Accordingly, signed HRS effects cannot be confidently mapped to the score's counted allele. The 1000 Genomes values are external reference LD, not HRS LD; the reference VCF was accessed by indexed region and was not full-file checksum verified. Participant and relative overlap with all discovery sources remains unverified. These observations do not validate either score, establish a causal gene, or evaluate individual prediction. The more detailed prior [single-proxy review](stage6_mad1l1_hrs_proxy_review.md) explains the strongest pair.
