# MIDUS genetic and sleep–frailty data: feasibility review

Checked 28 October 2026. MIDUS is a second candidate cohort for person-level validation, especially for a physical frailty outcome and sleep measures. Its precomputed genetic scores are publicly available through the MIDUS portal, but its individual imputed SNP data are controlled access. No MIDUS participant data were downloaded or analyzed for this review.

## Data relevant to this project

* A published [MIDUS frailty study](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1499.pdf) used a modified Fried phenotype including exhaustion, grip strength, weight change, walking and physical activity. This supports feasibility, but the phenotype is modified and should not be presented as identical to the discovery FFS.
* In a [MIDUS 2 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206915/), participants completed the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index; a subset at one site used wrist actigraphy and a sleep diary for seven days. Published [sleep and physical-function analyses](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2352.pdf) included grip and chair stands. Subsample sizes differ, and overlap with genotyped people, eligible older adults and complete frailty measures must be checked before promising adequate power.
* The [MIDUS genetic repository](https://midus.wisc.edu/midus-genetic-data-repository/) states that PRS are available through its portal, while imputed SNP data require an approved application and data-sharing agreement. These imputed data could in principle support computation of the project’s 15- and 22-marker scores, conditional on marker coverage, permission and sample overlap. We have not verified fees, per-variant coverage or approval for this exact use; do not describe the restricted SNP resource as open or definitively free.
* The [2021 score documentation](https://midus-study.github.io/public-documentation/Genetics/DNA/MRM2/M2MR_P6_PolygenicRiskScores_Documentation_20211129.pdf) describes a 2,118-row file for MIDUS 2 and Refresher biomarker cases, with usable PRS calculated for 1,296 participants after that release’s ancestry and QC filters. It lists 47 PRSice scores and an Alzheimer's PRS-CS score; its trait list does not list insomnia, sleep or frailty. This does **not** prove that no newer release contains them. The [2022 selected list](https://midus.wisc.edu/genetics/pdfs/PRS%20and%20Phenotypes%20in%20MIDUS%2020220503.pdf) is explicitly a subset and likewise does not establish the absence of newer scores. No precomputed score can substitute for the project's custom score without exact equivalence.

## Access and analysis constraints

The [August 2026 genomic access policy](https://midus.wisc.edu/genetics/pdfs/MIDUS_Genomics_Guidelines%20for%20Requesting%20Genomic%20Data%20Access_20260818.pdf) requires the applicant to be a permanent employee equivalent to a tenure-track professor or senior scientist; trainees can be part of a study team. Approved access requires an institutional signature, secure data handling, and adherence to local IRB and institutional requirements. The policy describes a review target of two weeks, not a guarantee. Confirm present fees, requested samples, variant access and permitted linkage with the custodian; do not submit a request under another person's identity.

The [MIDUS data-access conditions](https://midus.wisc.edu/data-access/) prohibit uploading MIDUS data to public large language models or AI platforms. Any authorized analysis must comply with the current data agreement and use an approved environment. Metadata, published descriptions and citations above are distinct from participant-level data.

If access is granted, freeze score weights and clarify the inferred FFS weight orientation first. Determine genotype/phenotype matching by sample and collection date; independently check discovery-participant/relative overlap, allele harmonization and variant quality, twins/relatedness, genotype PCs, missingness, sampling/selection and calibration. Compare FI and FFS only if an appropriate independent FI can be constructed and its item overlap with sleep and exhaustion documented. Define outcomes before examining associations. Given modest sample size, limit claims about discrimination, interactions or ancestry-specific performance and report uncertainty.

## Next practical steps

Compare this cohort with [NHATS](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md): NHATS has a larger genotyped sample and free restricted-data access stated by its custodian; MIDUS offers richer actigraphy in a smaller subset and a published modified physical frailty measure, with access costs unverified. A qualified team member can check the MIDUS portal metadata for a more recent insomnia PRS and request the current genomic application, sample overlap counts, genetic QC, marker availability and costs. Neither lead establishes independent validation yet.
