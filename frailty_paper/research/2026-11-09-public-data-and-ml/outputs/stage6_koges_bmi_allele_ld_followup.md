# KoGES BMI follow-up: score alleles and reference linkage disequilibrium

The [KoGES BMI candidate screen](stage6_koges_bmi_candidate_followup_review.md) identified four exploratory individual-variant associations after correcting the 33 candidate p values, as displayed with limited precision by the source. This follow-up checks allele orientation and the two nearby chromosome 2 variants. It does not test either score in individuals.

## Score-allele orientation

The [harmonization table](stage6_koges_bmi_score_allele_harmonization.csv) flips each browser ALT beta where needed to describe the allele actually counted in the prototype. The 15-variant prototype counts rs35881094 G and rs17550821 A: their displayed BMI betas are −0.023 and +0.074, respectively. The 22-variant equal-count prototype counts rs28528997 A and rs6452792 A: the displayed BMI betas for those alleles are +0.022 and +0.021, respectively. The source rounds betas. The [original methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9903843/) state that quantitative traits were rank-based inverse normal transformed before analysis, so these are effects on a transformed scale, not kg/m² or predicted score effects. Harmonization assumes that the prototype's listed pair correctly captures the discovery-study contrast, which requires special care for multiallelic sites.

For the two 15-score variants, the listed insomnia weights have the opposite signs to the corresponding BMI betas. This is a descriptive comparison of marginal associations across different populations and phenotypes, not evidence that insomnia reduces BMI, or that an insomnia–frailty score predicts BMI. The score variants were selected using prior association data, and the FFS direction and cross-study overlap have unresolved qualifications. Reproduce the table with `python3 work/koges/bmi_harmonize.py`.

## The chromosome 2 pair

Both rs35881094 T/G and rs17550821 G/A are exact biallelic records in the [1000 Genomes Phase 3 chromosome 2 reference](https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg19/1000Genomes/phase3/ALL.chr2.phase3_shapeit2_mvncall_integrated_v5a.20130502.genotypes.vcf.gz). Using phased haplotypes from 504 East Asian reference participants, the Pearson haplotype correlation for G at rs35881094 and A at rs17550821 is r = −0.151, r² = 0.023. An exploratory 2,000-draw individual bootstrap gives an r² 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.011–0.036; it describes reference-sample variability only. The G and A allele frequencies in this pooled panel are 0.562 and 0.021; the browser reports rounded KoGES BMI frequencies of 0.62 and 0.017. The reference comprises multiple East Asian populations, **not a Korean-specific LD panel**; the lower-frequency A allele is represented only 21 times among 1,008 reference haplotypes.

This low reference correlation makes a single shared tagged signal less compelled than physical distance alone would suggest, but it does not establish independent causal signals or demonstrate conditional association in KoGES. LD differs across populations, and the two results may have other correlations or confounding. A Korean-ancestry LD estimate and conditional or joint analysis in the actual BMI study remain necessary. The machine-readable [reference result](stage6_koges_bmi_chr2_eas_reference_ld.json) records subgroup values, sample counts, allele frequencies and a bootstrap; subgroup analyses are especially unstable. Reproduce with `python3 work/koges/bmi_chr2_reference_ld.py` using the public reference and checked local population panel.

No individual-level validation cohort was identified in this step. [ELSA](https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/genetics), [HRS genetic data](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/polygenic-score-data-pgs), and [CLSA genetic data](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/12/3/e059021) remain access-controlled rather than anonymous public downloads. The request, availability of all prototype alleles, permissions, cost and discovery overlap remain to be assessed for any future cohort.
