# Women's Health Initiative: no-fee enclave and controlled genetic-validation lead

Checked 29 October 2026. **A promising women-focused validation route, not an open participant-level dataset or a completed genetic-score test.**

The [WHI Observational Study insomnia analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12795745/) describes the five-item WHI Insomnia Rating Scale at baseline and year 3. Separately, a [WHI frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3354009/) used baseline and year-3 observations in initially non-frail women age 65 and older; its year-3 frailty definition used self-reported RAND-36 physical function to represent weakness or slowness, plus exhaustion, physical activity and weight loss. This is a WHI-adapted Fried-style measure rather than the original measured grip/gait FFS or a deficit-accumulation FI. The frailty study reported 27,652 women in its own analysis, of whom 4,125 were frail at year 3; those counts are **not** the genotype–sleep–frailty overlap. The insomnia paper's participants likewise cannot be assumed to be the same people as this frailty study.

[NIH](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/science/womens-health-initiative-whi) says phenotypic and genetic data from some WHI participants are available through dbGaP. [WHI's genetics page](https://www.whi.org/md/gwas) lists multiple GWAS ancillary studies with different samples and platforms and points to dbGaP for access. The exact score SNPs, dosage quality, approved sleep/frailty data, consent and individual overlap remain unknown; biological sex and ancestry determine the scope and transferability of any evaluation.

## Fee and access boundaries

WHI's [virtual data enclave](https://www.whi.org/md/virtual-data-enclave) states there has been **no VDE access fee since February 2021**. It requires an approved manuscript or ancillary-study proposal, local IRB approval and signed agreements. The enclave page lists WHI and Medicare data but does not establish that the specific participant-level genotype files for our scores are present, or that external genotype data can be linked without additional permission; it says additional linkage requires prior discussion. It lists R, SAS and Stata, with no additional software installations supported. These details do not prove that the complete project, genetic transfers or compute are free.

WHI's [data access policy](https://www.whi.org/md/access-to-whi-data) describes approved-proposal and data-use steps. Its [direct genetic-data policy](https://www.whi.org/doc/WHI-genetic-data-transfer-policy.pdf) describes study approval, full or expedited IRB approval, and a signed agreement; phenotype data are handled separately. The [dbGaP information sheet](https://www.whi.org/doc/WHI-dbGaP-PI-Info-Sheet.pdf) provides older WHI-specific data-use restrictions; verify the current consent group and certification for the exact release. No request was made, and an eligible investigator and institutional process remain necessary.

## Research use if approved

Freeze the original score definitions, verify exact alleles, multiallelic contrasts, imputation/quality, participant and relative non-overlap with discovery and all earlier follow-ups, then estimate associations with WHIIRS and separately with the adapted physical-frailty phenotype, using baseline and year-3 dates and prespecified missingness and selection rules. A prospective design starting with participants non-frail at baseline could assess later frailty, subject to matching and event counts; separate phenotypes and time windows avoid implying a single jointly validated score. Check whether frailty exhaustion and sleep symptoms overlap conceptually and evaluate sensitivity without treating a modified phenotype as validated. Compare incremental performance and calibration against appropriate baselines only if sample and event counts support it.

This cohort complements male-only MrOS, but it does not establish performance in men or all populations. WHI already lists a project titled [Multidimensional Sleep Health and Frailty in Postmenopausal Women](https://whi.org/study/W64); scope and overlap should be checked against existing WHI proposals and published work before an application. No individual-level data were accessed or analyzed.


## Genetic-file inventory and competing proposal

The [WHI genetic data inventory](https://www.whi.org/md/gwas) states that raw GWAS data from more than 42,000 WHI participants are available through dbGaP across multiple studies and that a WHI investigator data file indicates which participants have dbGaP genetics. This is a resource-level count, not the number eligible for our project, genotyped on one platform, or linkable to both relevant phenotypes. The page lists different ascertainment groups and assays; it does not establish the 33 variants, imputation quality or access within the no-fee enclave. The public [WHI SHARe dbGaP record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000386.v6.p3) describes genotyping 12,008 African American and Hispanic participants in the WHI clinical trial or observational study who gave supplemental wide-sharing consent. This is a useful ancestry-diverse candidate release, but its count is not a phenotype-complete sample and its applicable version, consent, approval, imputation and crosswalk must be checked. Use ancestry-aware QC and report performance and uncertainty by relevant population where adequately powered; do not assume score portability.

WHI's [proposal listing](https://whi.org/study/W64) identifies **“Multidimensional Sleep Health and Frailty in Postmenopausal Women,” Full et al., approved March 2026, manuscript ID 5559**, with keywords including actigraphy and sarcopenia. The public title and keywords show relevant thematic overlap but do not establish whether that project analyzes genetic scores, its exact hypotheses, or whether our proposed genetics question would be approved. WHI says overlap with existing work can affect approval. This makes a careful proposal-scope check necessary before an application; no contact was made.


## Genetic data linked to a physical-function measure in prior work

A 2026 [WHI preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.30.26356931v1) reports a multi-substudy analysis in which 2,051 women had baseline handgrip and genetic information. It is preliminary and used fracture-related polygenic scores and outcomes, not our scores or an FI/FFS. It does establish published precedent for a same-person genotype–physical-function analysis across several WHI substudies, while showing that the completed analytic subset can be far smaller than the overall genotyped inventory. The 2,051 participants are not an estimate of the overlap with WHIIRS, WHI frailty, the project's variants or eligible consent. Its heterogeneous case-control and age-restricted sources also warrant attention to selection and sampling before using calibration or transportability estimates.

No freely downloadable WHI-specific full insomnia or frailty GWAS summary file was verified in this search. The existence of public analyses and of the no-fee VDE does not imply open access to individual genotypes.


## Public BioLINCC variable crosswalk

The public [WHI CTOS dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/whi_ctos/data_dictionary/WHI_CTOS_2022a.pdf), dated February 2022 in the PDF, lists `f37_ctos_bio.sas7bdat` (pages 100–105) with `ID`, `F37DAYS`, `F37VTYP`, `F37VY`, `F37VCLO` and `F37EXPC`; it includes sleep items `TRBSLEEP`, `WAKENGHT`, `UPEARLY`, `BACKSLP`, `QUALSLP`, and derived labels `SLPDSTRB`, `PHYSFUN` and `ENERFAT`. A separate `f38_ctos_fu_bio.sas7bdat` (pages 106–109) lists analogous `ID`, `F38DAYS`, `F38VTYP`, `F38VY`, `F38VCLO`, `F38EXPC` and the corresponding constructs. The naming and label `SLPDSTRB` do not by themselves prove it uses the exact WHIIRS derivation or threshold from a given paper. `PHYSFUN` and `ENERFAT` are components or constructs, not a complete frailty phenotype; activity and weight-loss fields, cutoff derivation and missingness remain to be identified.

The public [WHI form inventory](https://www.whi.org/datasets/psychosocial) describes Form 37 as baseline/main, while published [WHI-OS insomnia methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12795745/) describe baseline and year-3 measurement. The follow-up dictionary's visit fields are promising, but exact form–visit correspondence, visit-type codes, time ordering, row uniqueness and current release must be verified using the approved files and documentation. A common `ID` within this BioLINCC dictionary is not evidence that it equals any dbGaP or WHI enclave subject ID, or that cross-repository joins are authorized. No sample overlap or score validation follows from a dictionary. BioLINCC says data packages are no-access-fee subject to request and approval; no participant files were downloaded.

### Additional year-3 frailty-component fields

The same 2022 CTOS dictionary lists `f143_av3_os_bio.sas7bdat` with `ID`, `F143DAYS` (days since enrollment), `LOST5LBP_3` (lose at least five pounds on purpose), `LOST5LBA_3` (lose at least five pounds not on purpose), and activity questions, plus `TEXPWK` (total recreational activity energy expenditure in MET-hours/week), on pages 31 and 36. It separately lists `f80_ctos_bio.sas7bdat` with `ID`, `F80DAYS`, `F80VTYP`, `F80VY`, visit flags and `WEIGHT` in kilograms on page 148. These supply plausible source fields to investigate alongside `PHYSFUN` and `ENERFAT` from Forms 37/38.

The [published WHI phenotype](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.16747) used measured weight change since enrollment and information on intentional weight loss at year 3, whereas the exact dictionary field descriptions alone do not establish how the study coded each response or linked visits. Nor does an energy-expenditure variable by itself prove the published total activity calculation or population-specific cutoff. Verify form versions, valid value and missing codes, closest-visit flags, availability in the requested dataset and the precise published algorithm before reconstructing; a change in threshold or denominator defines a different outcome. The 2022 dictionary establishes metadata only and does not prove matching records, authorized genotype linkage or a usable score-validation sample.


## Same-repository phenotype route and score definition

The public variable inventory for [dbGaP WHI parent release phs000200.v12.p3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetListOfAllObjects.cgi?object_type=variable&study_id=phs000200.v12.p3), inspected 29 October 2026, lists `SLPDSTRB`, `PHYSFUN` and `ENERFAT` in both `f37_rel1` and `f38_rel1` (pht001005.v6.p3 and pht001006.v6.p3), and lists year-3 `LOST5LBP_3` and `TEXPWK` in `f143_rel1` (pht000990.v6.p3). Its `f80_rel1` (pht001019.v6.p3) has `SUBJID`, visit fields and `WEIGHTX` in kilograms; note that the BioLINCC dictionary calls the weight field `WEIGHT`, not `WEIGHTX`. These labels substantially improve the prospect of requesting relevant phenotype and genomic data through the dbGaP parent/substudy structure, but do not establish individual-level access, exact record matches, appropriate consent or that all genotypes are in the parent release. The [earlier release's Form 80 description](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/dataset.cgi?pht=1019&study_id=phs000200.v9.p3) explicitly says it has multiple rows per person and a compound subject/visit key; confirm the current release's key and interpret visit codes before joining.

The older public [WHI baseline form 37 documentation](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/whios/hidden/doc/whi/datadoc/f37_os_pub.pdf) defines `SLPDSTRB` as a 0–20 sum of five sleep items and says the construct is missing if any of the five is missing. The corresponding [follow-up form 38 documentation](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/whict/hidden/doc/whi/datadoc/f38_ct_pub.pdf) also describes a five-item 0–20 sum with a reversed final item and complete-item requirement. These agree at the construct level with the [published WHIIRS description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12795745/), but the 2007 Form 37 document itself appears to use inconsistent question numbers within its paragraph and is an older OS-specific release. Verify the actual raw items and codes in the chosen current files; do not rely solely on item numbers or assume the 2007 missingness rule applies unchanged.


## Public variable-report denominators: do not treat rows as people

The public WHI dbGaP [variable-report directory](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000200/phs000200.v12.p3/pheno_variable_summaries/) includes per-field aggregate reports. The Form 37 report gives `SLPDSTRB` **189,940 non-null records**, but the same variable's female subject profile is 141,153; its overall `SUBJID` record count is 194,688 and female subject profile is 143,204. This makes clear that records and people must not be conflated, consistent with multiple visits. Form 38 reports 133,766 non-null `SLPDSTRB` records; Form 143 reports 71,264 non-null `TEXPWK` records; and Form 80 reports 613,312 non-null `WEIGHTX` records. These counts are across the entire report, not restricted to baseline or year 3, consent-eligible genotyped participants, independent individuals, or complete cases. They cannot establish any sleep–activity–weight overlap or score-validation sample, and no intersection bound was computed from repeated-row denominators.

There is a further provenance discrepancy: files served under the public `phs000200.v12.p3` directory declare `study_id=phs000200.v13` and `date_created=2025/07/20` inside the XML. The precise release relationship and date labeling require confirmation; do not silently call these counts v12-specific. Reports and hashes are retained on the external work disk; for example, Form 37 XML SHA-256 `8b7b07894ef457424bad87b57ad63477884c57ebe2772e866cc0a522577a20e0` and Form 143 XML SHA-256 `c136de32b2f9c272283fd88c966e2f067922332cdb508b3d4739ddc51961def1`. No participant data were accessed.


## Concrete combined genotype–phenotype request route

The [WHI harmonized and imputed GWAS substudy, phs000746.v3.p3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000746.v3.p3), says individual-level phenotype **and** molecular data for the parent and substudies can be requested through authorized access to `phs000200`. It describes six constituent genotyping studies, including SHARe, jointly imputed to a 1000 Genomes reference, with chromosome-by-study dosage files, SNP information files and subject-level principal components. Its prose describes 29,846 unique subjects while the page separately lists 29,814 consented subjects; neither number is a sleep–frailty complete-case count, and the discrepancy should not be silently reconciled. Samples overlap across substudies and duplicates are retained, so deduplicate on the authorized identity map and handle relatedness. Some substudies are case–control ascertained.

The substudy description says SNPs with imputation R² at or below 0.1 were omitted for that study, with missing columns retained where another study had the SNP. That is a release inclusion threshold, **not** evidence that our variants have adequate quality or that R² > 0.1 is suitable for a score analysis. Inspect each score marker's reported count/alternative allele, build, quality and missingness by study and ancestry; prespecify an appropriate QC policy rather than inferring coverage from rsID or a nonempty shared column. Subject-level genotypes and variant info require authorization; no dataset was obtained.

A [WHI dbGaP data-preparation guide](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetPdf.cgi?id=phd002022.5) states that phenotype files use a common `SUBJID`, and the [WHI Sample table description](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/variable.cgi?phv=80444&study_id=phs000200.v9.p3) describes subject-to-sample mapping including SHARe and other studies. This is positive documentation for a join within the authorized dbGaP framework. The guides are older/version-specific; verify actual requested file versions, the mapping for each genotype sample, permitted uses and complete-case overlap. An eligible institutional investigator and approvals remain required, and the known no-data-fee dbGaP route does not settle all project or computing costs.


### Marker-file availability and format

A direct attempt to open the guessed public FTP directory for `phs000746.v3.p3` returned HTTP 404 on 29 October 2026; no public marker inventory was obtained, and this does not prove that no other public path exists. The [public v2 distribution guide](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/document.cgi?phd=4964&study_id=phs000746.v2.p3) describes the files but is for a superseded version. It specifies that SNP identifiers are chromosome plus GRCh37 position; the SNP-info files report coded and baseline allele, imputation R² and coded-allele frequency. Its allele codes 1–4 correspond to A/C/G/T, while 5–7 are non-base categories requiring further resolution; do not blindly convert them into unverified nucleotide alleles.

The same guide says SHARe chromosome files contain a dosage matrix, whereas other studies contain two probability matrices. Their probability ordering, dimension names, missing-value representation and current v3 format must be established from actual authorized metadata before computing expected allele counts; do not assume a formula from array order. The existing scorer expects verified allele-specific dosage, so directly passing probability values or unvalidated code 7 would be invalid. This additional documentation makes a future request and data-ingestion audit more concrete, but cannot establish which of our score variants are present or high quality.


### Marker checklist for an approved analyst

The [33-marker checklist](whi_score_marker_checklist.csv) lists each distinct project rsID, project-assumed GRCh37 coordinate and a provisional `chromosome:position` string corresponding to the older WHI SNP-info naming convention. It retains separate counted and other alleles for the 15-marker weighted score, the 22-marker count and the 22-marker weighted scores; eight 22-marker rows use opposite orientations between count and weighted versions. Blank WHI-specific fields mean **not checked**, not absent or failed. This checklist is not a score file, does not include weights, and should not be used for individual predictions.

Generated with `python3 work/build_whi_marker_request.py`; row-count checks confirmed 15, 22 and 22 represented markers and eight count-versus-weighted reversals. The positional strings are candidate lookup keys, not verified genome builds or evidence of exact multiallelic equivalence; never assume that finding a coordinate establishes the correct biallelic contrast. Authorized analysis must consult the current file's full allele representation, study-specific R² and missingness, and the actual score files for weights. No WHI marker info or participant data was accessed.


A further checklist audit found no duplicate project chromosome–position keys among the 33 rsIDs. Four markers appear in both the 15- and 22-marker lists: `rs7734045`, `rs12536335`, `rs1909650` and `rs4886140`. The 15-marker counted allele is opposite the 22-marker **count** allele for rs12536335 and rs4886140; it is opposite the 22-marker **weighted** allele for rs1909650 and rs4886140. This is a comparison of the existing project definitions, not confirmation of the WHI contrast. The generator now fails on duplicate project coordinate keys, while continuing to retain separate allele columns for each score. Matching source and output row counts do not validate any genotype, score weight or prediction.
