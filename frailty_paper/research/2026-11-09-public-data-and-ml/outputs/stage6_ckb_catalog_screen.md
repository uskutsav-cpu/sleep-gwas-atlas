# China Kadoorie Biobank PheWeb: public phenotype inventory check

On 28 October 2026, the [CKB PheWeb catalog](https://pheweb.ckbiobank.org/phenotypes) displayed 3,149 entries, and its [downloadable phenotype-code table](https://pheweb.ckbiobank.org/download/phenotypes.tsv) contained 3,149 distinct codes, 219 with a case count. A case-insensitive search of the `phenocode` field found no code containing `frail`, `grip`, `sleep`, `insomn`, `walk` or `weak`. A separate exact check for `g47`, `f51`, `r54`, `r53`, `m62`, `m62.81`, `r26` and `r26.2` returned none. As positive controls, the codes `e11` and `i10` each appeared once. The [receipt](stage6_ckb_catalog_screen.json) records the full patterns, data hash and counts; `python3 outputs/stage6_ckb_catalog_screen.py` reruns the local inventory check.

**This does not show that CKB lacks sleep or frailty measurements, that no related study exists, or that no alternate code could represent a useful phenotype.** The inventory contains abbreviated codes and does not provide a complete natural-language phenotype dictionary in this download; the selected exact-code list is not exhaustive. It is a screen of one browser's currently displayed analyses, not a catalog of all CKB data or a search of individual participant phenotypes. We did not find an immediately identifiable FI, FFS or insomnia GWAS to use for a full summary-statistic comparison from this screen.

The [CKB browser's About page](https://pheweb.ckbiobank.org/about) states that the browser displays GRCh38 positions whereas summary-statistic downloads from some named studies use GRCh37. It also states that summary files hosted on the browser are encrypted and decryption keys require an application; it does not imply that all CKB-related summary files everywhere are encrypted. Some named CKB blood-pressure GWAS summary statistics are independently available through the [GWAS Catalog](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50297-x), but blood pressure is not the requested frailty outcome. No decryption-key application or external message was sent, and the user requested public free data.

No association, score-prediction test or individual-level linkage was carried out. The original [independent validation gap](independent_outcome_validation_cohort_feasibility.md) remains.

A useful counterexample to overinterpreting the catalog screen is [Zhu et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-022-02557-0): a CKB observational study explicitly analyzed sleep and frailty transitions in 23,847 participants, removing insomnia symptoms from a 27-item FI. Thus these phenotypes have been studied in CKB even though an insomnia/FI GWAS was not identified in the browser inventory above. That observational analysis is relevant background, not a genome-wide summary-statistic release or a validation of either genetic score; its limitations and estimate are already summarized in [the project's observational review](observational_sleep_frailty_risk.md).

A separate 2026 [CKB proteomic age-model review](stage6_ckb_proteomic_age_ai_context.md) notes published AI and genetic analyses relevant to the mentor's multimodal question. It does not establish access to linked genotypes for our score or identify a full FI/insomnia GWAS release.
