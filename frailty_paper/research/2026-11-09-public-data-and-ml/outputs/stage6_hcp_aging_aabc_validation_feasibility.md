# HCP-Aging / AABC: newly identified sleep, physical-function and genetics lead

Checked 29 October 2026. This cohort is a possible research follow-up, **not an immediately public individual-level score-validation dataset**. The newer genetic release is relevant to the project, but is controlled access and requires a separate application from the phenotype release. A full frailty definition, exact score-variant coverage and the usable linked sample have not been established.

## What is available

The [NIAGADS NG00186.v1 genetic release](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00186/) describes 1,024 HCP-Aging/AABC participants genotyped with the Illumina GSA v3 array and imputed using TOPMed on GRCh38. The release describes 10,147,726 variants passing its stated 1% minor-allele-frequency and 98% call-rate thresholds; that global count does not establish coverage or imputation quality of any of our specific markers. The genetic file contains no phenotype data; the provider directs researchers to BALSA for phenotypes, APOE and precomputed scores. The latter are not established to be either of the project's specific scores.

The HCP-A [study overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6649668/) describes Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), physical activity, four-meter gait, two-minute walk and grip strength measurements. These are useful adjacent phenotypes, but PSQI alone is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis, and physical function alone is not a complete frailty index or Fried phenotype. The study protocol does not by itself establish that all relevant measurements were released for every genotyped person or that all five Fried components can be reconstructed. AABC Release 2 describes 1,396 baseline participants, including HCP-A and AABC participants, with follow-up data for smaller subsets; **1,396 is not the linked genetic sample**. Its [release page](https://www.humanconnectome.org/study/hcp-lifespan-aging/data-releases) describes the non-imaging phenotype release and its academic, nonprofit or government email and data-use conditions.

The [known issues list](https://wiki.humanconnectome.org/docs/Lifespan%20HCP%20Data%20Release%20Updates%20Known%20Issues%20and%20Planned%20fixes.html) reports that some PSQI and physical-activity summary scores were miscalculated in the older Lifespan 2.0 release, with correction planned for a subsequent release. The status in the specific AABC release has not been verified; check the release-specific documentation and component data before analysis. Cognitive-health and other eligibility screening also limit generalization to frail or clinically diagnosed insomnia populations.

## Access, cost and suitability

NIAGADS says NG00186 requires a data-access application and approval, so its individual genotypes are **not anonymously public**. Its listed consent is GRU-IRB-PUB; project-specific eligibility, linkage permissions and data-use conditions must be reviewed rather than inferred from the shorthand. The [NIAGADS FAQ](https://niagads.scrollhelp.site/support/frequently-asked-questions) says NIAGADS covers download costs for most available data, with exceptions and possible AWS requirements for larger files. It does not establish that the specific project incurs zero total costs. The BALSA release specifies separate eligibility and agreement terms. No account, application or download was made.

At most 1,024 genotyped participants are available in this release before linkage, QC, age, missingness and outcome restrictions. A small, health-selected sample could support a clearly labeled preliminary association with prespecified sleep or physical-function measures if access and marker quality are adequate, but it is unlikely to establish well-powered discrimination, calibration, subgroup performance or clinical utility. Verify participants and relatives were absent from all discovery and candidate-selection sources before describing a follow-up as independent.

For feasibility, first confirm an authorized join key and agreement covering cross-repository linkage; exact insomnia questions and timing; whether an FI or all Fried components can be constructed without overlapping sleep/fatigue content; project-variant build conversion, alleles and imputation quality; age and event counts; ancestry, kinship and other covariates; current release corrections; and any file, cloud, institutional and computing costs. Do not infer these from cohort-wide counts. The 15- and 22-variant project scores were selected using previously examined associations, so even an approved analysis needs a frozen score and prespecified outcomes.

## Public variable and completeness documentation follow-up

The public [Release 1 non-imaging data dictionary](https://www.humanconnectome.org/storage/app/media/documentation/AABC1/AABC_Release1_Data_Dictionary_XL.xlsx) identifies `psqi5a` (difficulty falling asleep within 30 minutes), `psqi5b` (waking in the night or early morning), `psqi_global`, dominant and non-dominant grip-strength variables, current `weight`, and IPAQ activity variables. These sleep items assess symptoms in the past month, not an insomnia diagnosis; the sleep questionnaire's own daytime-function items also create potential phenotype overlap with exhaustion. A single weight value does not establish unintentional weight loss. The dictionary lists Alzheimer's scores `AD_APOE_PRS` and `AD_nonAPOE_PRS`; these are different from the project's insomnia–frailty scores. The dictionary's presence/collection indicators do not prove participant-level nonmissingness or that the same fields are unchanged in Release 2.

The separate [Release 2 completeness summary](https://www.humanconnectome.org/storage/app/media/documentation/AABC2/AABC_Release2_Completeness_Summary.xlsx) reports V1 availability indicators of 1,394 for PSQI, 1,396 for IPAQ, and 1,389 for NIH Toolbox (`TLBX`), among 1,396 V1 participants described on the release page. These are instrument-level counts for the phenotype release: they do **not** establish valid values for each item, the size of their intersection, overlap with the separate 1,024-person genotype release, or a complete FI/FFS. Counts at later visits cannot be added as independent individuals. The 2.0 PSQI/IPAQ scoring warning above remains relevant until the appropriate version's corrections are checked.

The Release 2 documentation page links a data dictionary, but its linked file returned HTTP 404 during this check; we therefore used Release 1 only for named variable descriptions and do not assert unchanged Release 2 definitions. The two public documentation workbooks, not participant data, were retrieved on 29 October 2026. Their SHA-256 hashes were `36a698e64375c711a8e19b6b74eb61718221da910de9e2e3c209362bb19cfbc1` for the Release 1 dictionary and `2493dcfa1655800fd5dd25b43043ee7fa90c4c1012417eb4ec48af60b1013e21` for the Release 2 completeness file. A [published AABC analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13551573/) also used PSQI with Alzheimer's genetic scores in a subset, providing evidence of one sleep–genetic-score linkage in that analysis, not evidence that our candidate genotypes or frailty measures are linkable in the necessary sample.

## Frailty-component and genetic-file follow-up

The Release 1 dictionary lists `tlbx_walk_4_rawscore` as the better of two timed four-meter walks, and `cesd7` and `cesd20` as the effort and difficulty-getting-going items conventionally used to measure exhaustion. These offer a path to evaluate components of a **modified** physical frailty phenotype alongside grip and activity. However, the dictionary marks the CES-D variables as collected for AABC but not HCA; whether this coding and the exact variables carry forward into Release 2 must be checked. The Release 2 instrument-availability summary reports CESD `YES` for **198 V1**, 306 V2, 471 V3 and 96 V4 records. These are release-wide instrument indicators, not complete item values or counts overlapping the genotyped participants. At baseline, the listed CES-D availability limits a same-visit reconstruction to at most 198 before missingness and linkage, assuming these instruments and indicators are consistent across releases. Later-visit measurements are not automatically incident frailty and cannot be counted as independent participants. Weight-loss history and intent, thresholds, and the remaining actual individual values are still unverified. Sleep symptoms and exhaustion may be correlated even if questionnaire items differ; prespecify definitions and perform a sensitivity analysis without exhaustion where appropriate.

The public [NIAGADS file manifest](https://st1.niagads.org/portal/download-public/NG00186.v1/fm) lists a PLINK bed/bim/fam fileset: approximately 42.2 GB, 6.0 GB and 0.11 MB respectively, plus a small README. All three genotype files carry `requires_requester_pays=false` in that manifest, while the dataset page requires an approved request. This is stronger evidence about **download charging for the named files**, but not proof that approval, local storage, cloud access, analysis and institutional administration are cost-free. The manifest contains file names and checksums, not the genotype or variant records; exact score-variant availability and imputation quality remain unverified. No genotype files were accessed or downloaded.

## Array context for this release

NG00186 also describes the Illumina GSA v3 array, so the [standard GSA v3 A1 manifest screen](stage6_hapiee_gsa_manifest_check.csv) made for the HAPIEE lead applies as **manufacturer-platform context**: none of the 33 distinct project candidate positions appeared among its standard assay rows. The [screen's methods and caveats](stage6_hapiee_cz_validation_feasibility.md) are relevant here too. This is not a check of the AABC array manifest or participant data: the exact revision, custom probes and allele representations remain unknown. Crucially, NG00186 explicitly reports TOPMed imputation, so missing directly assayed markers might still be present and usable in its released files. Confirm each project's variant, alleles, missingness and imputation quality in the approved release before scoring; the single-marker reference correlations in the HAPIEE screen cannot establish AABC imputation quality either. No genotype file was accessed.
