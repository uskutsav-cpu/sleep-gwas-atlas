# GEHAS: circadian, frailty and genomic cohort lead

The [2026 Guangxi Eco-Environmental Health and Aging Study (GEHAS) profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13343040/) describes a Chinese cohort that could be relevant for sleep–aging research and for evaluating transfer beyond European-ancestry discovery samples. It is **not an established free, publicly downloadable validation dataset**: its data-availability statement says data are available upon reasonable request, with no project-specific cost, eligibility, genomic release or permission to link data verified.

GEHAS recruited 5,645 participants at baseline, primarily identifying as Yao or Han. Its subsequent circadian subcohort enrolled 596 willing participants with high adherence; the study reports sleep questionnaires and wearable-ring sleep and activity monitoring, and describes genome-wide genotyping in the subcohorts using an Asian Screening Array. The 596 is the enrolled subcohort size, **not** a confirmed count with usable individual genotypes, a project-specific insomnia measure and frailty outcomes. Self-reported ethnicity is not a substitute for genotype-derived ancestry or a prediction of individual genetic architecture. Exact sleep questionnaire, timing, allele contrasts, imputation and post-QC candidate coverage require verification.

A [separate GEHAS methylation investigation](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.4c13675) reports frailty-related measures in 235 participants with follow-up; the report does not establish their intersection with the genotyped sleep subcohort or show that the outcome matches the project's FI or Fried score. Methylation is not the germline genotype required to compute either prototype. The circadian cohort was selected for adherence and is small; even if linked records exist, this may limit precision and generalizability. The source's five-year active follow-up design and its smaller subcohort schedule do not establish the timing of a usable prospective joint dataset.

## Follow-up on sleep and frailty overlap

A [2026 GEHAS sleep paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304389426021011) identifies the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) as its sleep instrument and states that sleep quality was first assessed during the 2023–24 follow-up. It analyzes 2,350 participants, of whom 770 were classified as having poor sleep quality. These are the paper's analysis counts, not the 596-person circadian subset, its genomic intersection, an insomnia-diagnosis count, or a frailty-event count. The paper's methylation score is based on CpG measurements and is not either project germline score. Existing sleep measures cannot be assumed to precede frailty outcomes measured before or at the same follow-up.

The 2026 cohort profile explicitly describes the earlier frailty/methylation work as using circadian subcohort II; the 2025 investigation reports 235 GEHAS participants with follow-up for its methylation analysis. This is stronger evidence that **frailty and molecular measurements occurred within the same named subcohort**, but it does not verify that each of those 235 has usable PSQI, wearable, genotype and FI/FFS records, or that all records can be linked for this project. The timing, sample flow and candidate coverage remain priority checks.

## Frailty definition and timing from the original supplement

The [2025 paper's supplementary methods and Table S2](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Air_Pollution_Control_Mitigates_Frailty_Progression_Evidence_from_Two_Cohorts_of_Older_Adults_and_DNA_Methylation_Insights/29040497) describe a **27-variable GEHAS deficit index**, including activities of daily living, cognitive impairment, self-rated health, sensory limitations, medications and conditions. The listed items do not explicitly name sleep or fatigue. That is useful for reducing direct item overlap with PSQI, but it does not rule out indirect overlap through general health, medications or illness, or prove that an available release reproduces this exact index. The index is different from a Fried physical-frailty score. Table S2 gives the number of serious illnesses a possible value of 2 and uses ambiguous wording for one pulmonary/heart diagnosis; the denominator, exact coding, raw questions and longitudinal comparability require clarification before reconstruction.

The supplement says 321 people had methylation assessed in 2023 and 235 of those had complete frailty and covariate data for its analysis. Its table reports 13 frail in 2019 and 47 in 2023 among those 235; these are not incident cases, PSQI–genotype intersections, or score-validation event counts. The 2019-to-2023 frailty change precedes or coincides with the reported first sleep-quality assessment in 2023–24, so it cannot be assumed that measured sleep preceded the frailty change. A future prospective analysis would need actual dates and later frailty data. The downloaded public supplement matched the publisher-provided MD5 `554beef04e2f23bea05208bd0b149d71`; no individual records were accessed.

## Array-manifest coverage screen

As a bounded feasibility check, we scanned all 657,490 assay rows of Illumina's publicly downloadable [Asian Screening Array v1.0 E1 GRCh37 manifest](https://webdata.illumina.com/downloads/productfiles/asian-screening-array/infinium-asian-screening-array-24v1-0_E1-manifest-file-csv.zip), confirming that the count matches the manifest header and excluding its separate control section. Against the project's assumed GRCh37 positions, only **1 of 15** prototype variants and **1 of 22** shared-count variants had the same rsID and coordinate in this version: rs1032524 and rs6938026, respectively. The two are different variants. The [variant-level table](stage6_gehas_asa_manifest_check.csv), [receipt](stage6_gehas_asa_manifest_receipt.json), and [script](stage6_gehas_asa_manifest_check.py) preserve the exact scan, input hash and probe labels.

This is a **platform-position and name screen**, not a verified allele- or sample-level match. Illumina probe alleles require strand-aware interpretation; the exact GEHAS array version, customization, genome build, sample QC and imputation are not stated in the cohort profile. Markers absent from this E1 manifest may be imputed or present in another version or custom content. Conversely, a listed probe may fail QC, represent a different allele contrast, or be unavailable in the released file. Thus these counts neither establish that GEHAS has only one score variant nor enable score calculation. Before relying on this cohort, request the actual assay/imputation manifest, per-variant quality and permitted genotype–phenotype linkage.

The manufacturer's [E2 GRCh38 manifest](https://webdata.illumina.com/downloads/productfiles/asian-screening-array/infinium-asian-screening-array-24v1-0_E2-manifest-file-csv.zip) provides a second build representation of the **same v1.0 array**, not an independent genotyped sample. After uniquely mapping all 33 distinct assumed GRCh37 candidate positions to forward-strand GRCh38 coordinates, the E2 screen also found only rs1032524 and rs6938026 at matching names and positions. All 657,490 declared assay rows were scanned. This is a cross-build consistency check, not evidence that GEHAS used E1 or E2, an independent measurement of coverage, or validation of the liftover, strand and original-study alleles. [E2 results](stage6_gehas_asa_e2_manifest_check.csv), [receipt](stage6_gehas_asa_e2_manifest_receipt.json), and [script](stage6_gehas_asa_e2_manifest_check.py).

For any request, establish the specific file availability and no-fee terms, sleep measures and valid FI/FFS items, genotype–phenotype join permission, sample and event counts, QC and exact score-variant coverage, ancestry/relatedness and independence from the score-selection cohorts. Genotyping platform alone does not prove the variants were directly typed or accurately imputed. No application or individual-data analysis has been performed; the article's open-access license does not imply that individual records are public.
