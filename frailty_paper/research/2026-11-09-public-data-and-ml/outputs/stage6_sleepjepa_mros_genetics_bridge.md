# SleepJEPA: a possible sleep–genetics–frailty extension

Checked 29 October 2026. Fox et al., [SleepJEPA: Learning the latent world of sleep with at-home sleep data to estimate disease risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41929312/), medRxiv, DOI 10.64898/2026.03.20.26348834, is a preprint. It learns representations of multichannel overnight polysomnography and reports disease-prediction experiments, including independent cohorts. It does **not** establish that sleep embeddings predict Fried frailty or validate our insomnia–frailty genetic scores. Its representations are of measured sleep signals, not DNA sequence embeddings or AlphaGenome predictions.

The [paper's data-availability statement](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348834v3) names MrOS among approved sleep datasets and says outcome datasets require project approval. Its [supplement](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/medrxiv/early/2026/03/25/2026.03.20.26348834/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true) lists multiple sleep recordings used for pretraining, including Wisconsin Sleep Cohort, but not MrOS in that listed pretraining set. That does not establish MrOS is untouched for all published outcome experiments, or independent of our proposed analyses; review the precise model version and all training, tuning and test cohorts before claiming independence. MrOS has public documentation of both sleep and genetic research, but relevant linked genotypes and later frailty outcomes remain controlled and their overlap and costs are not established. [Existing MrOS access review](mros_access_analysis_brief.md).

The [public code repository](https://github.com/benmfox/SleepJEPA/) provides preprocessing and model code under AGPL-3.0. Its inference documentation requests a Hugging Face account, permission and token for model weights. The [model page](https://huggingface.co/benmfox/SleepJEPA) identifies the weights as gated and lists a CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 license. The paper describes access by request; no approval or weights have been obtained, and terms and permitted uses must be checked. Code availability does not make the weights, linked patient data, cloud compute or score validation automatically free and public.

## Concrete proposal if linked data become available

1. Prespecify baseline sleep visit, later physical-frailty outcome, all five frailty components and exclusion/attrition rules. Ascertain variant and person overlap with every score-discovery and prior outcome study; use the same participants for paired models.
2. Compare a simple age/sex and other prespecified demographic baseline with (a) the existing frozen genetic score, (b) conventional sleep summaries such as sleep efficiency and duration, and (c) the genetic score plus conventional sleep measures. Separately evaluate a frozen sleep embedding and its incremental contribution only if authorized weights and suitable polysomnography are available. Include the same covariates and paired uncertainty in each comparison.
3. Treat a score-by-sleep interaction as exploratory and prespecified, with restricted complexity and multiplicity control; require an independent cohort or a truly held-out cohort-level replication before interpreting it. Associations and predictive improvement do not establish biological interaction or causality. Avoid using the target outcomes to select score markers or representation features before a held-out evaluation.
4. For mechanistic follow-up, keep sequence-based predictions such as AlphaGenome and variant-to-gene evidence as separately labeled annotations. Join by verified variant, gene and tissue context, and test against independent functional evidence; a sleep embedding is measured physiology and does not itself identify a causal variant, gene or cell type.

This proposal requires a verified sleep–frailty–genotype intersection, appropriate consent and governance, compatible signal channels, cohort independence and sufficient events. Sleep and frailty measures can share fatigue or physical-function content; distinguish FFS from a separately defined sleep-excluded deficit FI if both are available. No model was run, no access request submitted, and no participant data or model weights downloaded.
