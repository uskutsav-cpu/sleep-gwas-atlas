# Semmelweis Study: future validation lead, not a verified free dataset

Checked 29 October 2026. The [Semmelweis Study cohort protocol](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-023-01018-7) describes a prospective occupational cohort of Semmelweis University employees, age 25 and older. It plans to assess the Hungarian Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI-HUN), including sleep quality and timing, and to measure grip strength; frailty is a research objective. Grip strength is a frailty component, not a complete Fried frailty phenotype or frailty index, and PSQI is not itself a clinical insomnia diagnosis.

The protocol describes genetic paneling among future biomarker approaches; the [study's participant information](https://semmelweis.hu/dolgozoivizsgalat/adatgyujtes/) describes characterizing common genetic variants. These descriptions do not establish whether our 33 markers are covered, whether data are already available, or how many participants have jointly usable genotype, sleep and frailty measures. The planned eligible workforce of 8,866 and assumed 70% participation are not observed linked sample counts. Younger working adults may have few frailty events.

The protocol envisages sharing data with the research community under FAIR principles but also describes a restricted-access data safe haven. No current public individual-level download, access procedure, fee, marker-quality report, project-level eligibility, or discovery-sample independence was verified. A stated intention to share is not evidence of unrestricted access or a no-fee release. Treat this as a future secondary lead, not a cohort available for present score validation. A useful later check would seek an official current data catalog and verify genotype coverage, sleep and complete FI/FFS variables in the same participants, access conditions, and cohort overlap.
