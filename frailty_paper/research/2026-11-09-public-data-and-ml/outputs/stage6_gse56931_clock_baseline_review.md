# Public blood time series: descriptive clock-gene check

[GSE56931](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE56931) is an openly available study of 14 healthy adult volunteers, with repeated blood samples at four-hour intervals during baseline, acute sleep deprivation and recovery. It does not contain an established frailty phenotype, our genotype scores, or an insomnia–frailty validation cohort. Its young-adult population and acute deprivation protocol do not reproduce the older-adult cross-sectional study discussed in [the circadian background note](stage6_circadian_clock_frailty_2026_context.md).

I downloaded the public series matrix and array platform annotations and examined the eight clock genes in that paper, mapping symbols to array probe identifiers. Restricting to the six baseline time points specified by the GEO study design—day 1 at 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00, and day 2 at 00:00 and 04:00—gave 79 measurements from 14 participants and 14 annotated probes. The sample titles contain some day 2 entries labeled “baseline” even for hours that the GEO overall design describes as sleep deprivation, so this analysis follows the stated day/hour schedule and excludes those ambiguous later hours. Actual biological circadian phase is not provided by this calculation.

For each probe, within-person expression was centered using that person's available selected samples; means at each of the six clock hours were fit with a fixed 24-hour sine/cosine curve. The [descriptive output](stage6_gse56931_clock_baseline_descriptive.csv) records coefficients as fitted amplitude and fitted peak clock hour, not a test of rhythmicity. For example, the two PER1 probe fits have amplitudes 0.0311 and 0.3453 in the supplied expression scale and peak hours 01:47 and 06:10, respectively. The three CLOCK probes also have different fitted peak times. Such probe differences underscore the need to check probe specificity and replicate in another assay before making a gene-level claim. Six observed time points and missing/uneven sampling make these fitted summaries unstable; subject centering may itself be affected by missing time points. No inferential p-values, multiple-testing claims, disease associations, single-cell results or genetic effects were calculated.

Reproduce with `python3 work/circadian_clock_public_screen.py`; it requires NumPy and the two public inputs retained on the external work disk. Source files: [series matrix](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE56nnn/GSE56931/matrix/GSE56931_series_matrix.txt.gz) SHA-256 `cd20a18fbfb55eb9663ec1a2e8a847eb5b85808979d0fd84f89c6f952c0a5330`; [GPL10379 annotations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GPL10379&targ=self&form=text&view=full) SHA-256 `8caf107789803596e5f319158f9ad328ab3c0e10ca310c5d0e0f9535de290071`. Values reflect the supplied processed matrix, not a newly normalized raw-data analysis. This exploratory check motivates careful timing and probe selection; it does not resolve the new paper's clock expression findings or validate either score.


## Project candidate-gene crosswalk

I applied the same descriptive calculation to the positional nearest-gene labels in the project's 15- and 22-marker files. Exact symbol matching found array annotations and baseline matrix rows for **17 of 32 distinct labels**, corresponding to 42 probes; the matches include MEIS1, FOXP2 and TCF4. The remaining 15 labels did not yield an exact current-string annotation and usable row in this scan; gene aliases, platform content or annotation differences could explain a nonmatch. A probe row is not proof that the transcript is detectably expressed, and a nearest-gene label does not establish that a variant regulates that gene. No statistical threshold or gene ranking was applied.

[Candidate-probe summaries](stage6_gse56931_candidates_baseline_descriptive.csv) are reproducible with `python3 work/circadian_clock_public_screen.py --candidates`. All caveats above apply. In particular, this file cannot determine whether a selected variant changes expression, whether any gene affects insomnia or frailty, or which tissue or cell type mediates a signal. It is useful only as an inventory for designing subsequent well-powered, genotype-linked eQTL or colocalization analyses.


## Important phase-label discrepancy

A 2017 [independent methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5329904/) reports 130 control/baseline samples for this series, whereas the GEO overall-design schedule used here identifies six nominal baseline time points yielding 79 samples. Counting the actual sample titles shows exactly 130 labeled `baseline`: the extra 51 are day 2 at 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00, which the series-level design instead places in the continuous-wakefulness phase. Thus the sample-level labels and GEO series description conflict, and an independent study appears to have used the broader labeling. We cannot determine the true intervention status from these descriptions alone. **Treat all the calculated profiles as provisional day/hour-selected descriptive summaries, not a verified baseline or an independent sleep-deprivation comparison.** The chosen 79 records are labeled baseline in both schemes, but the ambiguity means their relationship to the study's intended control phase still requires source clarification. Do not use these fits as validated circadian-phase estimates or as a basis for biological claims about the intervention.
