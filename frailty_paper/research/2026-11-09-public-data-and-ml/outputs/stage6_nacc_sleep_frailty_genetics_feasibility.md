# NACC and NIAGADS: no-fee phenotype data and controlled genetic access

The [National Alzheimer’s Coordinating Center (NACC)](https://www.naccdata.org/about-nacc-data) states that its longitudinal clinical data are freely available to researchers through a data request. The [NACC data request page](https://www.naccdata.org/requesting-data/data-request-process/) separately directs researchers to partners for genetic data. This is an application-based research resource, not an unrestricted download of linked genome-wide participant data.

There is evidence for the needed types of data, but not yet for their usable intersection:

* A [published NACC analysis](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2826165) constructed a deficit-accumulation FI in an aging cohort. Its published NACC item list includes **nighttime behaviors** and **lacking energy**. Therefore an association with a sleep measure could involve overlapping FI content; a version excluding sleep and, separately, fatigue would be an adaptation requiring its own definition and checks.
* A [separate NACC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8062578/) used an informant-reported NPI-Q sleep-disturbance item, combining night waking, early rising and excessive daytime naps. It is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis. Its analyzed 8,460 participants are not the verified intersection with the FI study or genotype datasets.
* NIAGADS says its [ADC1–ADC15 release](https://dss.niagads.org/15-genotyping-array-datasets-from-the-alzheimers-disease-research-centers-adrc-now-available/) contains genotypes for 29,694 ADRC participants, with phenotypes supplied by NACC. [ADC15 (NG00151)](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00151/) specifically lists QC-filtered PLINK data and TOPMed-imputed BGEN plus basic harmonized phenotypes, and requires an approved application. The total genotype count does not establish the number with the relevant outcomes, consent, quality-controlled score variants or independent participants.
* [NIAGADS access guidance](https://niagads.scrollhelp.site/support/frequently-asked-questions) requires qualified investigators, an IRB and a data-access request for controlled datasets. It says NIAGADS pays download costs for most joint genotype VCFs and phenotype files, while some raw-data downloads are requester-pays. This does not establish that a particular ADC15 BGEN download, local computing, storage or institutional review is cost-free. Dataset-specific consent may restrict the allowed research question, including dementia-related use, and must be checked; neither NACC nor NIAGADS approval alone automatically authorizes all desired data or linkage.

This cohort is enriched for Alzheimer's research and its NPI-Q item is an informant-reported mixed sleep behavior. It is better viewed as a conditional **sleep-disturbance and FI** validation lead than a general-population clinical-insomnia or Fried-score dataset. Some NACC participants may overlap ROSMAP, ADNI or other project source datasets; verify individuals and relatives against all discovery and candidate-selection cohorts. Exact FI item coding, visits, ancestry, sample size, permitted linkage, genotype build/dosage, imputation quality and all score contrasts remain unverified.

The [NACC version-3 data dictionary](https://files.alz.washington.edu/documentation/uds3-ivp-ded.pdf) identifies the nighttime-behavior NPI-Q item as `NITE`, with 0=no, 1=yes and 9=unknown. This is a strong indication that the published FI's item bearing the same label and the published sleep-study item may be the same construct, but the FI paper's exact variable mapping and coding must be verified before calling it an identical field. If they are the same, testing `NITE` against an FI that includes it introduces a direct mechanical component. A sleep-excluded FI should be recalculated using observed items and a prespecified minimum completeness rule; 9 is missing/unknown, not a negative response. Apply comparable missingness criteria and compare both FI definitions in the same participants, with fatigue removed separately. These adaptations are not automatically validated and do not eliminate indirect associations or bias.

For a concrete illustration, the paper's displayed NACC FI table has **44 named deficits**. If every deficit were complete, each had equal weight, and its nighttime-behavior deficit were exactly binary `NITE`, then `FI_original = (NITE + 43 × FI_without_NITE) / 44`. In otherwise identical linear models on the same participants, the coefficient of `NITE` for the original FI would therefore equal `1/44 + (43/44) ×` its coefficient for the sleep-excluded FI. The additive **0.0227** is an algebraic consequence of the hypothetical coding, not an observed association or an estimate of genetic risk. Actual coding, incomplete items and the paper's implementation may differ; this identity does not apply unchanged when denominators vary by participant. It also does not imply the same additive term in a model whose predictor is a genetic score rather than `NITE`.

## ADC15 array coverage check

The ADC15 dataset page names the **Illumina GSA-24v3-0_A1** array and reports 654,027 markers. The same manufacturer's 654,027-row manifest was already scanned for our candidates in the [GSA audit](stage6_nhats_gsa_v3_manifest_review.md): there were **zero exact GRCh37 coordinate matches** for both the 15-marker and 22-marker sets. That is a stronger platform match for ADC15 than for NHATS, whose reported marker count differs from the manifest. The manifest is a design file, not the actual ADC15 QC output, and our candidate positions and allelic representations still need source-specific confirmation. A control marker was recovered in the scan. Most importantly, ADC15 also lists **TOPMed-imputed BGEN data**, and the manifest result cannot show whether the candidate variants are present or high quality in those files. The other ADC datasets use different arrays and are not assessed by this check. The appropriate next step under approved access is to inspect imputed variant identity, alleles, dosage and quality for each score marker; no score or individual data were obtained here.

ADC15 alone lists **894 participants**, not the 29,694 across the 15 releases. Its published table also separates multiple general-research, health/medical/biomedical and disease-specific consent groups. The number eligible for this exact question, with both outcomes and complete dosages, could be smaller; no blanket permission across consent groups can be inferred from the dataset listing.

## Public file-manifest check

The [ADC15 public file manifest](https://st1.niagads.org/portal/download-public/NG00151.v1/fm) lists 202 files, all with `requires_requester_pays=false`, including 29 files named `.bgen.gz` and 29 named `.bgen.bgi.gz`. Listed sizes range from **2.35–13.48 GB** per compressed BGEN and **4.45–4.75 GB** per compressed index. These are manifest metadata, not verified file transfers; the surprisingly large listed index sizes and the ability to extract only specified variants need confirmation from documentation or an approved environment. Different files correspond to ancestry/consent subsets, so the 29 file counts are not 29 independent cohorts and it is not necessarily necessary or permissible to download them all.

The payer flag improves the cost evidence for this release but does not establish that AWS accounts, local storage, computing, institutional review or the entire project are cost-free. Current local free space is far below even the smallest listed BGEN. No controlled file was accessed. The [audit receipt](stage6_nacc_adc15_manifest_audit.json) records byte counts and a hash of the public manifest; run `python3 work/nacc_adc15_manifest_audit.py` using the downloaded metadata. The manifest does not establish marker coverage or the number of participants with sleep and frailty outcomes.

An approved analysis would first establish the eligible genotyped intersection and permissions, freeze one primary outcome and the score definition, and use the published FI alongside explicitly defined sleep- and fatigue-excluded sensitivities. NPI-Q should be described as a composite sleep-disturbance measure; an adapted FI is not automatically validated. Assess score association and, only if sample and events permit, added prediction, calibration and uncertainty against a prespecified clinical baseline. No account, application, participant data, genetic score, or validation result was obtained in this check.

## Public cohort-query check

On 29 October 2026, the public [NACC Multimodal Query Tool](https://www.naccdata.org/multimodal-query-tool/) was set to its **September 2026 data freeze**, `Availability of GWAS data at NIAGADS: Yes`, and `Age at first UDS Visit: Between 60 and 90` (the interface states ages 90 and above are represented as 90+). The filter summary returned **23,568 of 57,269 participants**. With the GWAS filter alone, it returned **26,664**. The filtered dashboard separately displayed 23,550 as having genetic data, an 18-person difference from its 23,568 query total that has not been explained. NACC describes this tool as a prototype subject to discrepancies with current releases.

These counts are data-availability flags, not counts with measured `NITE`, all FI items, the 33 candidate contrasts, valid dosages or project-eligible consent. They do not establish how many participants are independent of score discovery, how many are suitable for each outcome, or how many remain after QC. The MQT page says genetic filtering will be expanded; a specific complete-case sleep/FI/variant query was not established here. No participant identifiers were exported. The [NIA guidance](https://www.nia.nih.gov/research/dn/how-researchers-can-tap-data-and-samples-alzheimers-disease-research-centers) describes a path to request additional NACC phenotypes linked to NIAGADS genotype datasets using supplied NACC IDs, subject to the relevant access approvals. That supports feasibility of linkage but is not approval for this project.

A follow-up added the `UDS Versions: UDSv3` filter to the same age and GWAS filters. After the dashboard finished updating, the displayed query count was **13,428**; its separate genetic-data tile showed **13,420**, another unexplained discrepancy. The version-3 form contains the `NITE` question, but a UDSv3 availability flag is **not proof of a completed NPI-Q response**, a usable sleep or FI outcome, correct temporal linkage, or eligibility for a particular NIAGADS dataset. Some participants have other UDS versions and are excluded by this particular filter; the count is not a study-wide usable-sample estimate. No IDs or individual records were exported.

The older [NACC query system](https://www.naccdata.org/query-system/) reported itself temporarily unavailable when checked on 29 October 2026, and its displayed controls were disabled. Thus it did not supply an additional sleep/FI complete-case count. This status applies to that system at the time checked, not to NACC's data or the separately functioning MQT. A direct data request and appropriate genetic approval remain necessary to determine the actual intersection.
