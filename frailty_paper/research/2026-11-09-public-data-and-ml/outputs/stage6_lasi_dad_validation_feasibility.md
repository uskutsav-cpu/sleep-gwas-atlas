# LASI-DAD India: sleep, physical frailty and genomic validation lead

Checked 29 October 2026. LASI-DAD is a new **conditional-access** opportunity to evaluate the project's actual scores in an older South Asian population, subject to permission and adequate linked data. It is not an openly downloadable linked individual-level dataset, and no score validation has been performed.

**Phenotypes.** A published analysis of the parent [LASI wave 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10339638/) reports three self-reported sleep-initiation/maintenance questions and an adapted five-component physical frailty score. Its components include measured handgrip and 4-m walk, self-reported exhaustion and activity, and weight loss attributed to insufficient household food. This last item is a narrower proxy than the original Fried weight-loss criterion; the adaptation must not be called the project's FFS or assumed directly interchangeable. The sleep composite is symptom-based, not a confirmed clinical insomnia diagnosis, and exhaustion can overlap conceptually with sleep. The paper reports 31,902 older respondents in its phenotype analysis; **this is not a genotyped complete-case count**. A separate published 40-deficit LASI FI directly includes the same sleep questions; the [item-level review](stage6_lasi_fi_ffs_sleep_overlap_review.md) assesses a potential sleep-excluded adaptation. A linked same-person FI/physical-phenotype/genotype analysis is not established.

**Genotypes.** [NIAGADS NG00106](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00106/) describes 960 LASI-DAD participants in its initial array sample, plus 33 reference controls; its 1,008 scans include repeat samples and controls. The larger [LASI-DAD WGS sample set](https://dss.niagads.org/sample-sets/snd10033/) reports 2,768 sequenced samples, of which 2,686 passed sequencing metrics and QC including six technical replicates. An [independent methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12945573/) describes 2,762 LASI-DAD participants initially carried forward for sequencing and controlled WGS through the NIAGADS ADSP umbrella; different releases and filtering explain why counts must not be treated as identical or added. Neither number establishes the number with adequate project-marker dosages and complete sleep and frailty outcomes. Exact build, marker representation, imputation or sequencing quality, consent and access to the required files need checking.

**Linkage and access.** LASI-DAD is a subgroup of LASI. The public [LASI data-family presentation](https://lasi-dad.org/pdfs/LASI_data_family_overview.pdf) demonstrates a person-level merge of harmonized LASI-DAD and LASI survey files using a shared key. This supports a survey-linkage route in principle, but does not prove that a NIAGADS genotype identifier can be linked to those variables in an applicant's permitted environment. The [LASI-DAD access page](https://www.lasi-dad.org/data/overview) says US-based researchers may apply for a free Gateway enclave account; applicants outside the US may apply for the AD Workbench without charge. Approval and a data-use agreement apply. NIAGADS lists genetic data under qualified access, and the [NIAGADS FAQ](https://niagads.scrollhelp.site/support/frequently-asked-questions) says it covers downloads of most data including joint-called VCFs, whereas CRAM, gVCF and structural-variant VCF downloads may incur requester charges. This does not guarantee that the required LASI-DAD files, secure computing, cross-platform linkage or all related processing are free. The parent [LASI access page](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/content/LASI-data) restricts downloads to legitimate academic research; terms and permitted use must be verified separately.

**Linkage follow-up.** The [LASI-DAD scientific-data paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9852797/) explicitly identifies `PRIM_KEY` for joining the harmonized LASI and LASI-DAD survey files and describes both the 1000 Genomes and TOPMed imputed releases as VCFs for 960 LASI-DAD subjects. It also describes `R1LASIDY`, the time between the two survey interviews. Separately, [NIAGADS NG00171](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00171/) says subject-ID mapping between LASI-DAD datasets can be sought through the study's data-access process. This is evidence of a mapping pathway, not confirmation that it will map either particular genomic release to `PRIM_KEY` or that cross-platform transfer is permitted. The [AD Workbench description](https://www.alzheimersdata.org/lasi-dad) offers free compute for qualified LASI-DAD users and allows approved external datasets, but the official LASI-DAD page directs US-based applicants to the Gateway enclave; eligibility, resource limits and permission to combine NIAGADS genomic data with LASI survey data must still be confirmed for the selected route. No application or contact was made.

**Version-specific field and QC check.** The public [Harmonized LASI-DAD B.1 codebook](https://lasi-dad.org/codebooks/Harmonized%20LASI-DAD%20B.1%202017-2024.pdf) lists `r1i_sleepa` and `r2i_sleepa`: self-rated sleep quality **the previous night**, with five response categories. Wave 1 asked it only starting in phase 2; wave 2 asked all respondents. It is not the parent LASI four-question, prior-month sleep measure or a clinical insomnia diagnosis. The codebook also documents precomputed polygenic scores for Alzheimer's disease and cognitive function, plus `r1prs_pc1`–`r1prs_pc10`; those published scores are not the project's 15- or 22-marker scores. Its array-score construction reports 932 unrelated study samples after its QC, a narrower and different count than the 960 LASI-DAD subjects described for the initial imputed VCF release. These are not verified numbers with sleep, frailty and score-variant data. The documented PCA variables may be useful for the applicable array subset but cannot be assumed to cover the separate WGS release or remove all ancestry confounding. The parent LASI field names for the four sleep questions and FI components remain to be confirmed against the relevant parent codebook; no names have been inferred from this separate DAD codebook. Local codebook SHA-256: `bb60777dd6d03bf6b9822e0c2aafe315e93bcaba5afb428e4f547d48f5143f1f`.

**Public LD resource.** Li and colleagues release a [LASI-DAD LD reference](https://github.com/zhengli09/LASI_DAD-LD-Reference-Panel) estimated from WGS samples, with per-chromosome lookup tables and PRS-CS matrices. The lookup reports pairs within 1 Mb only when r² is at least 0.2; absence from the lookup cannot by itself establish zero LD or variant absence. LD summaries can help check score-marker dependence in this population but cannot calculate participant scores or establish prediction. The [full Zenodo archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17946912) is about 26.9 GB, while individual chromosome files are available separately. Record actual sample and allele definitions from the released resource before comparing with other ancestry references.

The first [chromosome 18 check](stage6_lasi_chr18_candidate_ld_review.md) found each of two score-marker G/A representations in the lookup, but no row for the pair. Under the stated reporting rule this is consistent with r² below 0.2, not a measured exact r² or phenotype validation.

The [chromosome 2 check](stage6_lasi_chr2_candidate_ld_review.md) found each of four candidate representations but no row for either selected pair. The thresholded absence is compatible with r² below 0.2 under the source's reporting assumptions; it does not determine exact LD or validate a score.

**Next feasibility check.** Confirm that the exact WGS or imputed dosage release and a permitted genotype-to-survey crosswalk can be used together with LASI wave-1 sleep, grip, walk and remaining physical-frailty components. Determine complete-case and frail-event counts, exact score-marker alleles and quality, discovery/relative overlap, and the population-structure and relatedness model before opening outcome results. LASI-DAD's ascertainment and genotyped subset can affect generalizability; European-derived weights should not be assumed transferable. Account for the survey and subsample design where appropriate. If feasible, evaluate a frozen project score on the adapted physical phenotype and sleep symptoms, reporting uncertainty and missing markers; this cannot by itself establish FI-versus-FFS performance or clinical utility. No application, contact or individual-data download was made.
