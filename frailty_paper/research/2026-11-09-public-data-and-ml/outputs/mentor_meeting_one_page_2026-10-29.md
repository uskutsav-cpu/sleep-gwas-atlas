# Insomnia–frailty genetics: mentor discussion brief

**Question and motivation.** Are genetic influences on insomnia also associated with frailty, and do they differ for physical frailty and accumulated health deficits? A shared signal could help prioritize mechanisms linking sleep and aging, but we need to distinguish shared genetics from overlap in how outcomes are measured. Our immediate aims are to compare the two frailty definitions, examine candidate variant–gene–cell evidence, and test whether any genetic score adds information in a separate cohort.

**FI versus Fried frailty score (FFS).** FI is a proportion of accumulated health deficits across domains; FFS counts five physical criteria: weight loss, exhaustion, low activity, slow walking and weak grip. They are related but answer different questions. The FI discovery definition used here includes insomnia and tiredness, while FFS includes exhaustion. That overlap can contribute to their relationship with sleep; it does not tell us how much. For a fair comparison, measure both in the same people and prespecify FI versions excluding sleep, and excluding both sleep and fatigue where feasible. [Definitions and item audit](fi_ffs_component_crosswalk.md).

**Prior genetic evidence.** Our LDSC analysis estimates genome-wide FI–FFS genetic correlation at 0.757 (SE 0.021). In the UK Biobank insomnia comparison, the paired FFS-minus-FI genetic-correlation difference was −0.156 (95% CI −0.213 to −0.100). These are correlations of genetic effects, not the proportion of shared causal variants or a causal effect of insomnia on frailty. Sample overlap, phenotype construction and FI sleep items limit interpretation. [LDSC analysis](mentor_discussion_fi_ffs_risk.md); [FI–FFS estimates](direct_fi_ffs_genetic_correlation.csv).

An additional FI and modified Fried-phenotype GWAS pair from Mak et al. gave an LDSC correlation of 0.796 (SE 0.012) on its available SNP support. This pair uses overlapping UK Biobank source data and a sleep-containing FI, so it is not independent replication or a sleep-excluded comparison. Clinical agreement is a separate question: in one published Spanish cohort, 103 of 228 people classified frail by FI also met the Fried frailty cutoff, with results dependent on the chosen measures and thresholds. [Genetic analysis](mak2023_same_cohort_fi_fp_ldsc_review.md); [clinical comparison](fi_ffs_same_cohort_comparison.md).

**Public phenotype sensitivity.** In two NHANES cycles, associations of reported sleep trouble with an adapted deficit index remained positive after seven physical-function items were removed. This post hoc 17-item index is unvalidated, the physical phenotype is modified, and these data do not test a genetic score. [Sensitivity analysis](stage6_nhanes_function_item_sensitivity_review.md).

**Candidate mechanisms.** The FOXP2 region has conditional colocalization support for insomnia and hospital-frailty signals in some analyses, with sensitivity to LD reference, prior and region; that does not establish FOXP2 as the effector gene or prove the same mechanism for FI and FFS. [FOXP2 assessment](stage6_foxp2_independent_cohort_coloc_review.md). The 15-marker set includes rs113851554 near MEIS1, with separate evidence for sleep and restless-legs phenotypes. A prior neural study found the candidate-containing regulatory element accessible in tested GABAergic models and found reduced MEIS1 expression after element suppression in neural stem cells. It did not edit the candidate allele or establish a frailty mechanism. [Neural study](stage6_meis1_neural_regulatory_prior_study.md). Another candidate, rs78206187, has conflicting reported RLS association directions across public sources; related nearby variants show a similar discrepancy. Do not select weights or infer a biological direction from it until source-level coding and cohort differences are resolved. [RLS audit](stage6_rls_three_study_region_review.md).

A 2026 [cross-species MEIS1 follow-up](stage6_meis1_2026_cross_species_experimental_data.md) reports altered sleep maintenance after zebrafish meis1b perturbation and identifies cerebellar developmental cell hypotheses. A preceding human neural-progenitor study reported chromatin contact between the MEIS1 promoter and an insomnia-proxy-containing region; its proxy has very low LD with our candidate variant in the 1000 Genomes European reference (r² = 0.00043), so the contact should not be attributed to our variant. GTEx v10 also reports proxy associations with MEIS1-AS3 expression in bulk cerebellum; that is a distinct antisense transcript, not proof of a MEIS1 protein-coding or frailty mechanism. This evidence does not establish the effect of our scored human allele; the animal workbook download returned 403 in this environment.

In a separate [MEIS1-AS3–hospital frailty sensitivity](stage6_meis1_hfrs_cerebellum_susie_sensitivity.md), including two strand-ambiguous candidates changed allocation within the expression credible set, while the frailty model returned no qualifying set. No colocalization posterior could be calculated; the result neither establishes nor rules out sharing and depends on unverified allele orientation.

**RBM6 region: competing evidence.** Prior research highlighted the 3p21.31 region, and our follow-up found an RBM6 brain-expression association near sleep and frailty signals. However, matched-variant colocalization supported sharing for one insomnia GWAS but favored distinct signals for another, and the matched data omitted the strongest published cortex eQTL variant. These are model-dependent analyses with incomplete variant coverage, so RBM6 is a hypothesis for further testing rather than an established causal gene or cell type. [Matched analysis and limitations](stage6_rbm6_sleep_matched_review.md); [single-cell follow-up](stage6_rbm6_brain_molecular_coloc_review.md).

**Genetic scores: current status.** We have a 15-marker prototype with alternative FI, FFS and insomnia weights and a separate 22-marker count and weighted prototypes. Candidate selection was post hoc, source overlap is not fully resolved, and some alleles are multiallelic; the 22-marker count and weighted files use opposite counted alleles at eight variants. None has been validated for prediction or clinical use. Summary-statistic projections in All of Us and other public studies are association sensitivities, not measured score effects, calibration or discrimination. The exploratory 15-marker FI-versus-FFS insomnia contrast decreased markedly after omitting rs113851554, emphasizing sensitivity to the marker set. [Score evidence](stage6_score_validation_evidence_summary.md); [omission analysis](stage6_aou_paired_contrast_locus_sensitivity.md).

**Free-data plan.** Public GWAS summaries support further variant and mechanism follow-up, but no freely downloadable cohort with verified linked genotypes and appropriate sleep and frailty outcomes has been established. Framingham describes no data-access service fee through dbGaP and has published exam-8 sleep measures and later Fried frailty; NHATS describes no-cost restricted access and has sleep and physical-frailty measures. Both require approvals and verification of the linked sample, phenotype definitions, imputed marker quality and sample/relative independence. Framingham's published FI thresholds conflict between methods and a table footnote. Neither route is a completed validation or guaranteed access. [Framingham](framingham_no_fee_validation_route.md); [NHATS](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md).

MESA is another controlled-access candidate: its repository describes exam-5 sleep data as mergeable with posted molecular datasets, and a separate publication constructed a modified FRAIL outcome at exam 6. That outcome differs from both FI and Fried FFS; exact released fields, linked sample size, consent and total cost remain to be verified. [MESA assessment](stage6_mesa_sleep_frailty_validation_feasibility.md).

HAPIEE-CZ offers a further request-based lead: its investigators report no charge for data preparation or release, as well as sleep, frailty-related measures and genome-wide genotyping. Whether the specific genetic files and linked sleep–frailty subset are available without cost remains unverified. [Cohort assessment](stage6_hapiee_cz_validation_feasibility.md).

NCDS is a concrete no-data-fee lead: CLS says genome-wide data are available without charge by application and describes a separate arrangement for genotype-linked surveys. The age-50 FI includes sleep items, and later grip and walking data are potential physical outcomes rather than a verified FFS. The eligible linked sample, target variant quality and any computing costs remain unknown. A [draft field inventory](ncds_request/candidate_variable_inventory.xlsx) and [application research draft](ncds_cls_application_research_draft.md) support review; neither is a completed application. [NCDS assessment](stage6_ncds_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md).

The Human Phenotype Project has published genetic-score analyses linked to sleep symptoms and a controlled imputed-genotype dataset. Its competitive academic access program does not state fees, and FI/FFS coverage and our markers remain unverified. Public sleep association files appear selected and cannot validate the complete scores. [HPP assessment](stage6_hpp_insomnia_score_validation_lead.md); [public-file check](stage6_hpp_public_sleep_subset_review.md).

LASI-DAD is an additional India-based controlled-access lead: parent LASI reports both a 40-item FI and an adapted physical phenotype, but the FI directly includes the sleep questions and the shared grip and walk measures are coded differently across the two definitions. Permission to link genotypes to the relevant survey records, costs for the exact workflow and the complete sample remain unverified. [LASI phenotype comparison](stage6_lasi_fi_ffs_sleep_overlap_review.md); [access review](stage6_lasi_dad_validation_feasibility.md).

**ELSA cost correction.** ELSA's published policy charges for GWAS or candidate-polymorphism data linked to phenotypes, so that route is not a confirmed free way to calculate our scores. A separately distributed precomputed PGS product includes insomnia scores, but its exact cost and permitted phenotype linkage remain unresolved; even if accessible, these different scores cannot reconstruct our prototypes. [Access assessment](stage6_elsa_precomputed_scores_access_review.md).

**Additional cohort options.** Understanding Society has a published 36-item FI, sleep questions and an [HRC-imputed genetic release](stage6_ukhls_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md) listing 9,920 samples. Its published FI list does not explicitly name sleep, but includes energy; the usable joint sample, variant quality, independence, linkage approval and total cost remain unverified. ACT has a published modified Fried phenotype, sleep measures and controlled genetic data; its [public variable map](stage6_act_candidate_variable_crosswalk.md) shows an insomnia severity instrument potentially available from 2022. Older ACT genetic cohorts may have limited overlap with that later assessment. Both are leads rather than free-access confirmations or completed validations. In the Cleveland Family Study, a selected visit's public catalog lists only 35 positive insomnia responses before genetic or frailty restrictions, so it is a lower-priority option. [CFS](stage6_cfs_insomnia_genetics_feasibility.md).

**Methodological context.** A recent sleep–depression/anxiety genomic SEM preprint illustrates why apparent shared pathways can depend strongly on model assumptions: alternative equally fitting models gave very different allocations to insomnia. It is not a frailty or score-validation study. This supports comparing symptom-excluded FI definitions and testing alternative models before drawing mechanistic conclusions. [Preprint review](stage6_sleep_internalizing_genomic_sem_identification.md). CDC also confirms that anonymized NHANES genetic datasets cannot be linked to public-use survey files; its restricted genetic data require a separate application. [NHANES access check](stage6_nhanes_genetic_linkage_access_check.md).

**Next analysis.** Freeze candidate definitions and resolve candidate-specific allele, build and multiallelic ambiguities before looking at target outcomes. The Ye FFS convention that A1 is the effect allele is supported by 37 published COJO lead-variant comparisons and the BOLT-LMM convention, but the released-file transformation was not independently documented and this is not proof for every candidate. [FFS source audit](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md). In a permitted independent sample, report sample flow and missing variants, compare FI- and FFS-weighted scores on the same people and same outcome, and estimate paired uncertainty and incremental prediction over a baseline. Use appropriate family/ancestry handling and distinguish contemporaneous association from prediction of later frailty. Separately, use full variant-level evidence, matched LD and independent QTL or experiments to test gene and cell hypotheses; do not equate nearby genes or expression with a shared causal mechanism.

**ML and AlphaGenome.** A source-tracked [variant–gene–cell evidence graph](stage6_evidence_graph_readme.md) is available for hypothesis generation. Before claiming an embedding model works, use a separately ascertained tested set, hold out loci or chromosomes before feature selection, prevent outcome-label leakage and compare against simple baselines. Existing cross-assay sequence-baseline tests showed little useful ranking on one external neuronal reporter set; those assays do not validate patient scores. AlphaGenome outputs can be treated as separately labeled research annotations, subject to applicable [output terms](ALPHAGENOME_OUTPUT_TERMS_NOTICE.md); they are not patient outcomes and their allowed use in model training must be checked. [ML split audit](stage6_evidence_graph_split_audit.md); [external assay](stage6_salomon_neuronal_reporter_feasibility.md).

**Clinical AI design caution.** A [recent unreviewed MIMIC-IV frailty–sleep prediction preprint](stage6_mimic_sleep_frailty_ml_design_review.md) is relevant to the mentor’s ML request, but its balanced holdout cannot directly establish population calibration, and index-stay billing codes need timing checks before any admission-time claim. Its clinical-code outcome and model do not validate our genetic scores.

**Sleep AI extension.** SleepJEPA provides a relevant published example of a sleep-signal embedding, with MrOS among its data sources; model weights are gated and this is not a genetics or frailty validation. A proposed baseline comparison and conditions for combining a frozen sleep embedding with genetic scores are in the [SleepJEPA review](stage6_sleepjepa_mros_genetics_bridge.md).
