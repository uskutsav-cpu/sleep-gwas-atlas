# Audit of sample independence and shared reference resources

**Conclusion:** The named Japanese insomnia and knee-strength analytic cohorts differ from the named UKB, TwinGene, MVP and FinnGen cohorts used in the project's discovery and selection, which makes the Japanese studies useful *potential* external follow-ups. The public source descriptions and summary files do not establish participant- or close-relative-level disjointness across every study. Neither Japanese source is individual-level validation of the project scores, and the two Japanese outcomes are not measured jointly.

| Dataset or resource | What the sources establish | What remains unverified |
|---|---|---|
| 22-marker count selection | The project selected markers by nominal MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital-frailty results; see [score definition](shared22_score_research_guide.md). | Those same data cannot independently replicate this selected count. Other discovery and prior screens can add overlap or postselection. |
| 15-marker weights and earlier candidate work | Project sources include UK Biobank and TwinGene FI, UK Biobank FFS and insomnia, and previously inspected candidate associations; see [candidate evidence review](shared22_eight_source_evidence_review.md) and [weighted-score status](stage6_score_validation_evidence_summary.md). | UKB appears in several traits; selection, score weights and target overlap must be assessed separately. |
| Japanese insomnia follow-up | The extracted member is explicitly `GWASsummary_Insomnia_Japanese_SakaueKanai2020.auto.txt.gz`; the [receipt](stage6_bbj_insomnia_extract_receipt.json) records its source, successful gzip check and table hash. Every extracted candidate row reports N=178,253, matching the [PheWeb insomnia page](https://pheweb.jp/pheno/Insomnia) (389 cases, 177,864 controls). The [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00931-x) describes BBJ as a Japan-based disease-oriented biobank. | Cohort names and sample size do not rule out a participant or relative also being represented in a discovery dataset. The report is a GWAS of insomnia, not the same people evaluated for FI or FFS. |
| Sakaue cross-population meta-analysis | The same paper and [PheWeb downloads](https://pheweb.jp/downloads) also provide a *different* insomnia meta-analysis that combines BBJ, UK Biobank and FinnGen (1,791 cases, 663,089 controls). | That meta-analysis includes UKB and FinnGen, so it would be unsuitable as a clean non-overlapping follow-up of scores selected or weighted using those resources. It was **not** the BBJ-only file used in the Japanese insomnia review. |
| Japanese knee strength | [Ito et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06108-6) identify Shimane CoHRE and Bunkyo Health Study participants; the [checksum-verified summary file](stage6_japanese_knee_candidate_receipt.json) reports N=3,452. | Different cohort names do not establish zero BBJ–knee overlap or relatives, and strength is not FI, FFS, or an insomnia/frailty joint outcome. |
| Imputation and LD references | The [BBJ paper](https://saorisakaue.github.io/papers/s41588-021-00931-x.pdf) describes 1000 Genomes Phase 3 and a population-specific Japanese whole-genome reference for imputation. The [knee paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11106293/) describes 1000 Genomes Phase 3 and a BBJ-derived 3,256-genome reference for imputation. The project separately uses 1000 Genomes JPT/EAS genotypes as an external LD approximation. | A shared or BBJ-derived **imputation reference** is a shared methodological resource; it is not by itself evidence that GWAS participants or outcome data overlap. Conversely, differing named cohorts do not prove disjoint individuals. The exact overlap between the two studies' reference panels was not established. |

The BBJ file is the Japanese-only association source used in the [matched 14-marker analysis](stage6_bbj_insomnia_matched14_z_review.md); the pooled UKB/FinnGen/BBJ result was not substituted. The scores were selected or reviewed using multiple outcomes and source results. Their BBJ comparison was also developed after exploratory work, so even complete participant disjointness would not eliminate postselection, multiple testing, phenotype mismatch, allele-coding uncertainty, small case count or reference-LD limitations.

For a future confirmatory study, obtain a source-specific cohort and recruitment list, documented construction and version of each GWAS, and an approved participant/kinship overlap assessment where possible. Freeze the score, contrast and outcome before evaluating it. Actual participant-level genotypes and phenotypes are needed for discrimination, calibration and added value over baseline. The current sources do not permit that calculation.
