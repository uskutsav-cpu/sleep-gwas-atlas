# ELSA precomputed sleep scores: a lower-barrier analysis route

## What is available

The English Longitudinal Study of Ageing distributes individual-level **precomputed** polygenic scores through [UK Data Service study 8773](https://datacatalogue.ukdataservice.ac.uk/studies/study/8773). Its [access note](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/8773/read8773.htm) says the collection is available to registered users under the End User Licence; commercial use needs additional owner approval. The [ELSA genetics page](https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/genetics) describes download access. The [UK Data Service FAQ](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/help/new-users/faqs-for-new-users/) states that its collections can be downloaded free of charge for non-commercial use; it also says some collections restrict users outside the UK. Thus this is a documented no-download-fee route for eligible non-commercial use, subject to registration and licence, not unrestricted open download. Check the current 8773 and 5050 catalogue access tabs for specific eligibility, geography and linkage conditions. No data were downloaded. This UKDS product is distinct from ELSA's separately charged linked genotype service described below.

The [score documentation](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/8773/mrdoc/pdf/8773_full_report_elsa_gwas_pgs_2022.pdf), sections 3.3.9 and Table 18, reports scores for insomnia (2019), insomnia complaints (2017), sleep duration, short and long sleep, morningness and other sleep traits. The table reports 7,183 people with each listed score, not the number with complete covariates or frailty outcomes. The 2019 insomnia score uses Jansen-derived summary statistics; the report has some inconsistent wording about source cohorts, so verify the precise score metadata and underlying GWAS before interpreting independence or reproducing it.

ELSA's longitudinal phenotypes can be requested separately through [study 5050](https://datacatalogue.ukdataservice.ac.uk/studies/study/5050); a published analysis ([Flint et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-63229-y)) constructed a 62-deficit FI from ELSA, requiring at least 30 observed deficits. It reports 5,448 participants after joining separately requested frailty scores to phenotypes, not necessarily the sample available for the 8773 sleep scores. Its FI score association is prior evidence for a different frailty PGS, not a validation of our score. ELSA genotypes needed to compute our own selected variants are a separate [genetic data access](https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/genetic-data-access) route, for which ELSA explicitly states that approved linked genetic–phenotype requests incur project-specific cost-recovery charges; it therefore does not meet the user's free-data constraint as currently described.

The authors' [supplementary table ST9](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-024-63229-y/MediaObjects/41598_2024_63229_MOESM1_ESM.docx) lists all 62 ELSA deficits, transcribed in [this CSV](elsa_flint_published_fi_items.csv). It explicitly includes `pscedc`, restless sleep during the past week. `pscedb` (everything was an effort) and `pscedh` (could not get going) are fatigue-related but are not insomnia diagnoses. A sleep-excluded version has at most 61 candidate items; excluding those two fatigue-related items too leaves at most 59, before individual missingness. Both are modified indices and must use a prespecified minimum observed count. The supplement provides item descriptions, not a fully executable coding algorithm or release-specific wave mapping.

## What can and cannot be tested

If licence-compliant access to 8773 and relevant 5050 files is obtained, test whether an existing insomnia PGS is associated with ELSA FI and physical frailty phenotypes. This can provide an independent cohort-level test of sleep genetic liability against frailty, subject to checking participant or relative overlap with the score's discovery GWAS. It **cannot validate the project's selected 15- or 22-variant scores**: a precomputed whole-genome score is a different predictor, and these files alone do not permit calculation of individual variants' contributions. The exact phenotype availability, matching identifiers, ancestry PCs, sample intersection, sleep items within the ELSA FI, and eligible licence terms must be verified before proceeding.

Proposed prespecified comparison: standardize the 2019 insomnia PGS within the analysis sample; model FI continuously and FFS separately, with age, sex and available ancestry PCs, documenting genotyping and relatedness handling. For repeat observations use a prespecified baseline or an appropriate longitudinal model with participant clustering. Report sample flow and score-associated effect with confidence intervals, not an individual clinical risk. Repeat using an FI excluding `pscedc`, then one also excluding `pscedb` and `pscedh`, if source items and missingness permit; distinguish both from the published FI. The PGS is genetic, so this is not literal reuse of an observed sleep exposure in a PGS–FI regression; it tests whether an association depends on the sleep and fatigue components of the outcome. If observed sleep symptoms are analyzed as exposures, item overlap is direct. Compare predictive increment against a covariate-only model in held-out data if sample size permits. Selection on complete data and European-ancestry construction limit generalizability; association is not proof of shared causality.

This route requires legitimate registered access; it is not an instruction to bypass registration or use another person's credentials. It advances the sleep–frailty question while access to individual genotypes for testing the project's actual scores remains unresolved.
