# SABE Brazil: older-adult genomic and sleep/frailty data lead

Checked 29 October 2026. The São Paulo SABE cohort is a potentially useful underrepresented-population lead, but **not a verified free public individual-level validation dataset**. [Naslavsky et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28648-3) report whole-genome sequencing for 1,171 unrelated SABE participants after that study's QC, with mean age approximately 72 years. They describe physical-function measures including frailty, balance and mobility. The cohort is admixed; transfer of predominantly European-derived score weights must be assessed, not assumed.

The [SABE cohort methods](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbepid/a/PqWZmwyKxw4Y3jT9zrD56TS/?lang=en) describe sleep evaluation and frailty phenotype cutoffs in the 2010 data-collection framework. The sequencing paper's [supplementary tables 1–2](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC8897431.1/41467_2022_28648_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) confirm that its sequenced sample was selected from this same 2010 wave: 1,335 people were enrolled in the three relevant cohort entry groups and 1,171 were retained in the unrelated whole-genome-sequenced sample after QC. These are recruitment and sequencing counts, not counts with complete sleep and frailty data. The 2010 measurement framework included questionnaires, anthropometry and home functional testing; a separately described hospital assessment subset must not be assumed to overlap the sequenced sample.

The [sleep and physical-performance study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39047526/) specifically reports self-reported sleep difficulty, daytime sleepiness and sleep quality alongside gait speed and the Short Physical Performance Battery in 2010 and 2015. Its analysis had 2,205 observations from 1,559 people across those waves, not 1,559 linked sequenced participants. This supports measurement feasibility in the same cohort wave, but the reports do not establish that the same 1,171 sequenced people have usable sleep responses, all five Fried components, a deficit-accumulation FI, or complete follow-up. Sleep difficulty and quality are not automatically clinical insomnia, and physical performance is not itself a complete frailty phenotype. Published definitions and overlap with exhaustion must be checked. Wave-specific consent, attrition, sampling weights, usable cases and individual/relative overlap with all discovery and selection data also remain unknown.

The sequencing paper's data statement distinguishes two products. [ABraOM](https://abraom.ib.usp.br/) provides aggregated cohort variant information; the paper describes academic download by direct request, with terms to be confirmed. Aggregated frequencies can help assess marker availability or ancestry context but cannot reconstruct participant-level genetic scores or their association with outcomes. Individual sequence/variant files are listed at the [European Genome-phenome Archive](https://ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00001008561) under study EGAS00001005052. The paper states that a data-access committee must evaluate requests, individual phenotypes cannot be uploaded to a public repository, and phenotype requests can also be evaluated through EGA. It described an approval timeframe of 180 days; this is a historical paper statement, not a current processing guarantee. The precise available files, linkage permission, access eligibility and any fees or secure-computing costs for this project are unverified.

A proper evaluation would require access to permitted individual variant calls and linked sleep and FI/FFS components, exact reference/alternate alleles at all 33 distinct project candidates, genotype quality, appropriately modeled ancestry and relatedness, assessment of the survey design and a sufficiently sized independent outcome sample. No application, external contact, participant-level download or association test was performed. Do not infer that missing or restricted access means the cohort lacks a phenotype or variant.
