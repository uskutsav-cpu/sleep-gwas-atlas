# Mak FI MAGMA Brown-approximation analysis

An exploratory MAGMA gene-property analysis of the **Mak frailty-index GWAS** found three of 102 cell-type variables with BH q < 0.05 across the six selected matrices: source-labeled human-cortex `hybrid` (p = 7.43 × 10⁻⁷, q = 7.58 × 10⁻⁵), human-cortex `neurons` (p = 2.38 × 10⁻⁶, q = 1.22 × 10⁻⁴), and mouse hypothalamus `Glu3` (p = 8.20 × 10⁻⁴, q = 0.0279). The precise biological identity of the `hybrid` source group needs review; the hypothalamus result is cross-species. These are exploratory expression–gene-association relationships, not proof that the cells cause frailty or mediate a specific variant. [All results](stage4_magma_cell_type_exploratory.csv); select trait `mak_fi_brown`.

This run uses MAGMA's **SNPwise Brown 1-df approximation**, an additional sensitivity model. The originally started SNPwise-mean Imhof run is still executing after spending a long time in a numerical integration step. The completed [Ye FFS analysis](stage4_ye_ffs_magma_cell_type_review.md) uses the Imhof model and found no cell-type q < 0.05 in its own 102-test family; **those p-values and apparent differences should not be treated as a direct FI–FFS comparison**. The traits, cohorts, sample-size handling and gene models differ, and they reuse or may reuse UK Biobank participants. A matched Brown FFS analysis is in progress. Even matched significance across two traits would not by itself demonstrate a shared genetic mechanism or a statistically significant difference.

## Inputs and testing

MAGMA v1.10 used the public Mak FI GWAS with per-variant N, the 503-person 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European GRCh37 LD reference, and Ensembl v92 coding genes extended by 1 kb on each side. Of 8,583,632 input rows, MAGMA reported 8,005,144 valid reference SNP p-values; rsID duplicates and reference synonyms were handled by the configured drop rules. A fixed sample of 7,887 directly matched Mak FI IDs agreed with their reference coordinates, but this does not authenticate all variants or synonyms ([partial audit](stage4_magma_reference_coordinate_sample_audit.json)). Gene-level sample sizes recorded in the output range from approximately 362,934 to 403,041; source QC, phenotype scale and ancestry matching require review. The [19,129 gene-level results](stage4_mak_fi_brown_magma_gene_results.csv) include BH q-values across those genes; 3,542 have q < 0.05 under this approximate model. These are gene-level statistical associations, not causal variant identifications.

For each selected expression matrix, the one-sided positive gene-property test conditions on its `Average` gene expression and MAGMA's internal gene size, density, inverse MAC and, where variable, sample-size terms. Models used 14,176–19,129 genes. BH was applied across all 102 cell-type variables from the six selected matrices for this trait and model; an independent R `p.adjust` calculation matched those q-values. The matrix family was selected after resource review and is exploratory; labels and experimental details are not uniform. Candidate genes may be expressed in multiple populations, but linking those expression edges to a cell-type enrichment does not prove that any individual gene drives the enrichment.

The workspace-local binary includes the narrowly scoped wide-integer correction to the `MEAN_SAMPLE_SIZE` output-header calculation. That correction's identical FFS regression rows were checked in the [validation receipt](stage4_magma_sample_sum_validation.json); FI results have not been validated against a completed Imhof run. Both biological interpretation and model robustness require further evaluation. The separate insomnia analysis and matched-model FFS analysis are in progress; no shared-risk cell-type inference is made here.
