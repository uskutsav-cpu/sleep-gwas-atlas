# Sleep–internalizing genomic SEM: lessons for insomnia–frailty

Checked 29 October 2026. This is methodological background, not a new analysis of our genetic scores.

## Relevant preprint

Şakiroğlu et al., [Insomnia and the genetic overlap of sleep with depression and anxiety: a genomic structural equation modelling study](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.21.26366134v1.full), medRxiv (23 October 2026), DOI 10.64898/2026.10.21.26366134. It is a preprint and has not established a frailty association. It uses genomic SEM of ten GWAS across sleep, internalizing, psychotic–bipolar and metabolic domains. The authors state that the input summary statistics are public; factor-level GWAS statistics are to be deposited on publication, and code is available on request, with public deposit planned. We have not verified a current public release of the factor-level results or code.

Among the authors' restricted single-link models, an insomnia cross-loading fit best; under that particular model the insomnia path represented 56.3% of sleep–internalizing covariance by their magnitude-weighted measure. A second pathway improved fit, but with only three sleep indicators its placement was not identifiable: equivalent models assigned the insomnia path between 0% and 127% of the covariance. Signed contributions can exceed 100% when paths offset one another. Thus 56.3% is model-specific, not a reliable estimate of the share caused by insomnia. Their FinnGen replication of internalizing leads also had partial sample overlap. Neither finding validates our scores or identifies a frailty mechanism.

## How to use this in our project

1. Treat FI, FFS and insomnia as observed phenotypes with potentially shared symptom content, not interchangeable measures of an underlying aging factor. An FI excluding sleep, and a further version excluding sleep and fatigue, are important sensitivity outcomes when participant-level data allow their construction. FFS exhaustion can still overlap with insomnia.
2. Before fitting an insomnia–frailty genomic SEM, inventory public GWAS for multiple distinct sleep and frailty indicators, their definitions, ancestry, effective sample sizes, overlap, summary-statistic availability and LD reference. Our current FI–FFS LDSC result alone cannot identify a latent model. Avoid presenting this proposed extension as already run.
3. Prespecify plausible cross-loadings and correlated residuals, assess model identification and fit, compare equivalent or competing models, and report how conclusions change. Use simulations or additional indicators where needed. Do not infer a unique pathway from a model that cannot distinguish alternatives.
4. Follow credible loci with independent variant-to-gene and cell-type evidence, including fine-mapping and colocalization where appropriate; model factors and nearby genes do not by themselves establish a causal gene or mechanism.
5. Freeze the score and test it in a separately ascertained cohort with linked genotype, phenotype, ancestry and relevant covariates. A genomic SEM on public summary statistics is complementary mechanism research, not individual score validation.
