Nearby DNA changes: repeatability of the experimental measurements

Checked 16,540 DNA examples from the established Kosicki neuron study.
The 16,329 nearby comparisons belong to 144 connected genomic groups; they are not independent people or independent discoveries.

The original published measurements from all three assay repeats were reconstructed.
Their values matched the stored replicate data exactly and reproduced the current effect-size targets.

Two individual repeats agreed on which nearby DNA change had the larger measured effect 56.3% to 57.3% of the time.
Comparing one repeat with the average of the other two gave 58.5% to 59.2% agreement.

These percentages describe laboratory repeatability, NOT AI prediction accuracy.
They are not a proven upper limit on what a model can predict and do not prove that noise caused the model's failure.

No significance, effect-size or error-size cutoff selected comparisons.
The original calibration regions and reserved new study were not used. No model, training target, split or ongoing experiment was changed.
