# NHANES device-classified sleep-wear time and grip: two-cycle exploration

Using the public CDC 2011–2012 and 2013–2014 daily accelerometer summaries and existing grip-test data, I ran the same age- and sex-adjusted survey-weighted linear model separately in each cycle, with a locally specified protocol written before loading these device files. One additional hour of mean algorithm-classified sleep-wear time per calendar day was associated with **−0.75 kg** combined grip strength in 2011–2012 (n=1,273; 95% CI −1.32 to −0.19; p=0.012) and **−0.22 kg** in 2013–2014 (n=1,322; 95% CI −0.89 to +0.45; p=0.48). These are separate cross-sectional estimates, not a formal test of a difference between cycles. The second interval is wide and does not establish absence of an association. Results are exploratory, and prior analyses in these cycles and related literature were known before this local protocol.

The CDC [daily accelerometry file](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/PAXDAY_H.htm) reports valid minutes classified as sleep wear by an algorithm; this is not insomnia, a clinical sleep diagnosis, or necessarily nighttime sleep alone. Calendar-day sleep wear can include naps, exclude periods marked unknown or non-wear, and be affected by sleep/wake classification error. We used days with at least 1,439 minutes of recorded data and 1,200 minutes classified valid wake or sleep wear, and required at least four such days per person. These are analytical QC choices, not established clinical or NHANES thresholds. The unweighted mean was 8.42 and 8.49 hours respectively among analyzed participants. Full-day and wear requirements and missing grip can select healthier or more adherent participants; examination weights do not necessarily correct this selection. Combined grip is a physical-function measure, not the FI or full Fried phenotype. Age is top-coded at 80, sex is the released survey variable, and age/sex adjustment does not remove confounding or establish cause, direction of time, or independence from other studies.

Requiring five days yielded estimates −0.81 kg/hour (95% CI −1.39 to −0.23; n=1,238) and −0.21 (95% CI −0.93 to +0.51; n=1,285). Of examined respondents aged ≥60, 1,687 and 1,785 were available before complete-case exclusions. The survey design used MEC weights, full examined-sample strata and PSUs, Taylor linearization, complete-case domains, and R `survey` 4.5. Design degrees of freedom were 17 and 15; model residual degrees were 14 and 12. The relationship could be nonlinear; the specified linear coefficient averages over the observed data and is not a clinical threshold.

This is an objective-device **phenotype** follow-up and does not test genetic variants, score discrimination or shared causality. It reuses the two NHANES cycles from the previous self-reported sleep–grip work, so it is not an independent cohort replication. The separate free NCI GGIR-processed release was identified, but its DataLumos file download redirected to sign-in; these results instead use the publicly downloadable CDC daily files and are not GGIR-derived.
