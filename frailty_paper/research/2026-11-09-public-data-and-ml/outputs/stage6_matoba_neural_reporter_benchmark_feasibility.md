# Neural-progenitor reporter data: independent-benchmark lead

[Matoba et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02454-2) report regulatory reporter assays in human neural progenitor cells using variants chosen from cortical-structure GWAS loci and chromatin-accessibility QTLs. The authors provide [supplementary results](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41593-026-02454-2/MediaObjects/41593_2026_2454_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx) and identify GEO accession [GSE289858](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE289858) for raw data. The article states CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; verify dataset-specific reuse permissions and restrictions before training, distributing derived material, or commercial use. This study was not used to fit the project's existing neuronal baseline, but overlap with its underlying sequences, loci, donors or pretraining data has not been assessed.

The supplementary table 3 baseline/vehicle regulatory-activity sheet contains 17,837 allele rows across 9,052 unique SNP identifiers. The vehicle allelic-effect sheet reports 661 unique SNPs, of which 330 have adjusted p < 0.05; stimulated-condition allelic-effect sheet reports 598, of which 287 have adjusted p < 0.05. These are counts of reported tests and thresholds, not counts of proven causal sleep–frailty variants or suitable negative labels. The reported allelic-effect subset is not interchangeable with the full activity-tested set; non-significant observations, missing tests and inactive constructs need distinct handling. The study's discovery selection, assay/condition, design and genomic coordinates must be understood before a benchmark is assembled. A direct condition-interaction result is different from comparing separate significance calls.

**Project candidate coverage:** no exact rsID from the union of the existing 15- and 22-marker lists appeared in any of the six table-3 result sheets. This exact-identifier screen does not establish absence from all study designs, proxy variants or unpublished results. The separate [table 2](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41593-026-02454-2/MediaObjects/41593_2026_2454_MOESM3_ESM.xlsx) labels its QTL constructs by chromosome/position rather than rsID: a tentative equality search against the candidate positions in both GRCh37 and GRCh38 found no matches. Its coordinate build was not established in the inspected source, and that screen is not an allele-harmonized determination of variant coverage. Absence from reported data is unassessed, not a negative result.

This source could support a **broader external experimental test** of a sequence-only baseline if a permissible full tested sequence universe can be constructed, matching, genomic-region overlap, assay and donor independence are established, and training/test leakage is addressed. It is not a participant-level sleep or frailty dataset and cannot validate the genetic scores. The current candidate-specific graph has too few tested labels to train a meaningful model. No AlphaGenome outputs were used or treated as labels.

## Sequence-pair feasibility and orientation issue

The GEO [barcode-statistics file](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE289nnn/GSE289858/suppl/GSE289858_variant_barcode_statistics.txt.gz) includes 150-base construct sequences and names. We matched the 661 reported vehicle allelic-effect SNPs to their activity-table chromosome and the named A1/A2 construct sequences. The prior Retallick baseline was trained on autosomes other than 1 and 2, so this exploratory check focused on Matoba's chromosomes 1 and 2 to reduce chromosome-level overlap with that training split. This is not a proof of full independence from training or pretraining data.

Of the 661 reported effects, 553 are on other chromosomes; the remaining 108 have one unique sequence for each A1/A2 construct and exactly one base difference. Comparing the differing construct bases to the table's Ref/Alt pair, **11 pairs match directly** (10 adjusted p < 0.05, one not significant), while **97 match only after complementing the two bases**. That systematic pattern is consistent with a potential strand representation difference, but the correct orientation has not been confirmed against the original design and reference genome. A simple complement match, especially at palindromic sites, does not prove the reference and effect-allele orientation. The 11 direct matches alone are too class-imbalanced and small for a useful performance estimate.

Do not proceed to an external model evaluation until the study's genome build, design orientation, source Ref/Alt conventions, duplicate constructs, labels and reference sequences are independently verified. Model features based on absolute k-mer differences may also behave differently under reverse complementation; silently flipping or combining unverified strands could produce a spurious result. Dataset license and model-training/test overlap remain open. No test performance is claimed.

## Six-variant reference-orientation pilot

As a diagnostic, we compared the first six qualifying chromosome-1/2 A1 constructs to 160-base windows around the activity table's numeric positions from the public UCSC Genome Browser sequence API for GRCh37/hg19 and GRCh38/hg38. For each 150-base construct, the scan tried both orientations and offsets within that window, allowing at most one differing base. All six had a reverse-complement match to the GRCh37 window: two exact and four with one mismatch. None had a match under this criterion at the same numeric position in GRCh38. This supports a GRCh37 and reverse-complement interpretation **for these six records**, conditional on the coordinate windows and match criteria. The records are an order-selected pilot, not a representative random sample; it does not verify the other 102 pairs, the source's Ref/Alt mapping, direction of the reported log fold change or study-to-study independence. A single mismatch may occur at the variant but the pilot does not establish that it always does.

The pilot makes the earlier complement pattern less mysterious but is insufficient to flip all sequences or report model performance. The existing k-mer model uses non-strand-symmetric feature names; any comparison must use a prespecified, consistent strand convention for both training and test or explicitly quantify orientation sensitivity. Before proceeding, verify full matched windows, the varying base's position and Ref/Alt for every test record, reference build, and the source of each label; also audit overlap and applicable data-use terms. The cohort-level score validation remains separate.

## Full 108-pair reference-alignment check

We extended the reference check to all 108 qualifying chromosome-1/2 vehicle allelic-effect records. For each pair, the script fetched a GRCh37/hg19 reference window from the UCSC sequence API, tested both orientations and offsets, required exactly one of the two 150-base sequences to match reference, required the differing base to fall at the reported chromosome position, and required its unordered allele pair to match the workbook's Ref/Alt. **All 108 aligned under reverse complementation**, with no unmatched or ambiguous records under those criteria. Of these, 64 had adjusted p < 0.05 and 44 did not. This verifies the local sequence/position/allele relationship under the assumed GRCh37 mapping; it does not prove the sign convention for the reported effect, establish statistical inactivity for nonsignificant calls, validate the complete tested universe, or prove permissions or independence.

The baseline's training excludes chromosomes 1 and 2, which reduces chromosome-level train–test overlap, but other sequence similarity, shared biological resources and model provenance remain to be audited. The baseline's original construct orientation and the effect of reversing the test sequences on its non-strand-symmetric k-mer features must be evaluated, with a fixed model and no tuning on the test set. Differences in construct length, cell model, assay and statistical selection also limit transportability. No participant-level genotype or frailty outcomes were used and no AI model performance or genetic-score validation is asserted.
