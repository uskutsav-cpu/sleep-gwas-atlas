# Scripps sleep-clinic genetic study: public evidence assessment

[Kripke et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2014.11.003) report a sleep-clinic cohort with questionnaire and polysomnography-derived sleep phenotypes, including insomnia complaints, and genotyping on a 768-SNP targeted panel. The article reports 751 participants of European ancestry; it is not an older-adult frailty study. The use of a targeted panel means project marker coverage cannot be assumed. The paper describes supplementary association results and phenotype definitions, but open access to the article does not establish access to linked participant-level genotypes and frailty outcomes.

A reproducible identifier scan of the Europe PMC full-text XML found 16 unique rsIDs in the article text and no exact matches to either the 15- or 22-marker score. This is **not a screen of all 768 chip SNPs**, the supplement, or nearby linked variants. The supplementary workbook described by the article was not obtained: Europe PMC's supplementary-files endpoint returned only the article figure, and one PMC file endpoint returned an HTML page rather than a workbook. No participant data were downloaded or analyzed, and current supplement/underlying-data availability, marker coverage, participant independence, FI/FFS outcomes, access conditions and cost remain unverified. The paper reports phenotype-level associations, not performance of the project scores.

Reproduce the limited text-only screen with `python3 work/kripke_article_rsid_screen.py` using the [Europe PMC full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4352103/fullTextXML); [aggregate receipt](stage6_scripps_sleep_article_rsid_screen.json) contains its SHA-256. The file hash is a local record, not a publisher checksum. This is a source to revisit if the complete supplement becomes available, not a demonstrated validation cohort.
