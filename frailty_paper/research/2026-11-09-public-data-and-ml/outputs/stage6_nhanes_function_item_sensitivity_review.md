# Sensitivity to functional items in the adapted NHANES index

This exploratory, post hoc analysis revisits the existing same-participant NHANES 2011–12 and 2013–14 comparison. The original 24-item adapted deficit index includes seven physical-function questions that overlap conceptually with the modified five-domain physical-frailty count. We removed all seven, leaving 11 self-reported conditions and six depressive-symptom items, and required at least 14 of the remaining 17 responses. The original index also omits direct sleep and fatigue items. The 17-item measure is a **nonvalidated sensitivity index**, not an established FI; it remains potentially related to sleep through its symptoms and diagnoses.

In the same complete participants as the original analysis, survey-weighted linear models adjusted for age and sex compare people reporting that they had ever told a health professional about sleep trouble with those who had not. Each outcome is expressed as a fraction of its items or domains. The paired contrast fits the within-person difference between the 17-item fraction and five-domain fraction, so its uncertainty incorporates their covariance.

| Cycle | N | 24-item association, difference (95% CI) | 17-item association, difference (95% CI) | 17-item minus five-domain association (95% CI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2011–12 | 1,479 | 0.071 (0.052, 0.091) | 0.078 (0.059, 0.097) | 0.032 (0.0003, 0.063) |
| 2013–14 | 1,575 | 0.088 (0.061, 0.114) | 0.090 (0.062, 0.119) | 0.022 (−0.012, 0.057) |

The positive sleep-trouble association persists after removing the functional questions. The paired contrast is imprecise, its 2011–12 interval only just excludes zero, and its 2013–14 interval includes zero. This exploratory pair of comparisons does not establish that either construct is more closely related to insomnia. Fractions of different items and domains are not equivalent clinical scales; removing questions changes the meaning, prevalence, denominator and reliability of the index. Reusing these cycles after examining prior results also prevents treating this as an independent replication. The five-domain measure is modified, not the original Fried phenotype, and includes a sedentary-time cutoff with ties; see the [parent review](stage6_nhanes_fi_ffs_comparison_review.md).

The change in the fitted sleep-trouble association after removing the seven items can itself be assessed with a paired model of `index17 − index24`, using the same people and design. It is 0.0065 (95% CI −0.0027 to 0.0157) in 2011–12 and 0.0027 (95% CI −0.0082 to 0.0136) in 2013–14. Both intervals include zero; this does not prove the index versions are equivalent. This is a further post hoc sensitivity, and the two fractions differ in item composition and denominator.

Sleep trouble is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis. These cross-sectional models do not establish causality, account for all confounding or validate genetic scores. Public NHANES data used in this analysis do not provide linked project genotypes. The [code](../work/nhanes_function_item_sensitivity.R), [results](stage6_nhanes_function_item_sensitivity.csv) and [run log](stage6_nhanes_function_item_sensitivity_runlog.txt) retain the item coding, complete-case rule, cycle-specific survey design and uncertainty estimates. This sensitivity was run after the earlier findings and is hypothesis-generating.
