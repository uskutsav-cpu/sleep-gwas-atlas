# MEIS1 score marker in restless legs with end-stage renal disease

[Risse et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpag095) studied restless legs syndrome (RLS) in patients with end-stage renal disease. Their exploratory case-control samples after genetic QC comprised 169 cases/332 controls in GER1, 74/164 in GER2, and 118/319 in GRK. This clinical phenotype is **uremic RLS**, not insomnia or FI/Fried frailty, and the dialysis population differs from an unselected aging population.

In their published two-German-cohort meta-analysis, the project's 15-marker variant **rs113851554** is reported at GRCh38 `2:66523432`, effect allele T and other allele G, OR **2.52** (95% CI 1.61–3.96), p = 5.73×10⁻⁵. The reported T/G pair and position match the project's provisional build-aware candidate map; exact imputed variant representation and multiallelic handling remain to be verified. The authors report this German result passes their Bonferroni threshold for 164 previously identified idiopathic-RLS lead SNPs (0.05/164). Their three-cohort meta-analysis reports OR **2.06** (95% CI 1.39–3.06), p = 3.51×10⁻⁴; this is above their applicable 0.05/165 threshold (3.03×10⁻⁴). The two meta-analyses share samples and must not be counted as independent confirmations. These figures are from the paper's selected-locus tables, not newly estimated project-score effects.

The T allele is also the allele counted for this marker in the project's 15-marker prototype, but this result concerns RLS among renal-failure patients. It supports a disease-specific follow-up hypothesis and does not identify the causal effector gene, demonstrate the same effect on insomnia or frailty, or validate a weighted score. The authors state the idiopathic-RLS PRS training excluded their four study datasets, which supports that particular PRS comparison; it does **not** establish absence of all participant or relative overlap with every discovery and selection source used in our project. The previously noted rs78206187 cross-study direction discrepancy is a different marker and is not resolved by rs113851554.

The authors provide [cohort-level GWAS summaries and code](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21234712). Their three filenames label coordinates hg19, while the published meta-analysis tables quote GRCh38. All three files passed their published size and MD5 checks; the complete cohort files were screened against project GRCh37 positions and allele pairs. Their small samples limit precision and any post hoc screen will be hypothesis-generating. Aggregate GWAS rows cannot calculate, calibrate or validate individual scores. No participant-level data were accessed.

## Completed cohort-level candidate screen

The three downloaded files contain 6,663,750 (GER1), 5,739,504 (GER2), and 6,605,011 (GRK) association rows. Each contained a position-and-allele-pair match for all **33 distinct** project markers. These expand to 59 score-specific rows per cohort because variants can belong to more than one score; repeated score rows are not independent observations. No matched score row was excluded for an invalid numeric value. Full results and integrity receipts are in the [allele-matched lookup](stage6_uremic_rls_candidate_lookup.csv) and [screen receipt](stage6_uremic_rls_candidate_receipt.json); the reproducible [screening script](../work/uremic_rls/screen_candidates.py) uses the publisher's GRCh37 files.

For the previously selected rs113851554 T allele, the source-cohort log-odds estimates (standard errors) are **0.834 (0.268)** in GER1, **1.181 (0.449)** in GER2, and **0.031 (0.426)** in GRK. These are per-cohort estimates for uremic RLS, not three independent confirmations or effects on frailty or insomnia; the Greek estimate is imprecise and is compatible with a range of effects. The source paper's two-cohort meta-analysis above combines the two German cohorts, and its three-cohort meta-analysis overlaps all of them.

For the distinct direction-discrepant marker rs78206187, the project-counted G allele has positive but imprecise cohort estimates: **0.142 (0.311), p=0.651** in GER1; **0.710 (0.452), p=0.118** in GER2; and **0.141 (0.405), p=0.730** in GRK. None is individually statistically significant at 0.05; direction alone cannot resolve the prior cross-study discrepancy. These estimates concern a different disease and are post-selection observations. The allele match does not establish exact multiallelic representation, imputation quality for every marker, causal genes, cohort independence from project discovery, or individual-level score performance. The paper reports an analysis filter of Minimac imputation R² > 0.3 and minor allele count ≥ 20 in each dataset; the released matched rows do not report marker-specific imputation R², so their precise quality is not established by this lookup.

The three large raw source files were removed locally after the checksum-verified scan to recover disk space. The source manifest, extraction script, candidate lookup and checksum receipt remain; download the public files again before rerunning the full screen.
