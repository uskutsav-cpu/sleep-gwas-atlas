# Public NHANES sleep question and grip strength: exploratory phenotypic analysis

In public NHANES 2013–2014 data, 1,558 examined participants aged 60 or older had both a valid sleep-question response and combined grip strength. A survey-weighted linear regression estimated that participants who had ever told a health professional they had trouble sleeping had combined grip strength **0.56 kg lower** than those who had not, after adjustment for age and sex (95% CI −2.22 to +1.09 kg; p=0.47). This estimate is statistically uncertain; it does not establish that there is no association.

This is a new **post hoc, cross-sectional phenotype analysis**, not independent validation of the genetic scores. The sleep exposure is the [SLQ050 question](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/SLQ_H.htm), which asks whether a person *ever told a doctor or other health professional* they had trouble sleeping. It is neither an insomnia diagnosis nor a current symptom measure and may reflect healthcare access and reporting. The outcome, [MGDCGSZ](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/MGX_H.htm), is the sum of the best grip measurements from each hand, in kg. It is one strength measure relevant to physical frailty, not the full Fried frailty phenotype, a frailty index, or a joint insomnia–frailty outcome. Age is top-coded at 80 in this public dataset. The analysis cannot establish temporal order or causality, and adjustment for age and sex does not remove other confounding or selection.

The analysis uses public [demographic](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/DEMO_H.htm), sleep and grip files linked by participant sequence number, full examination-sample design strata and PSUs, examination weights, a domain restricted to age ≥60 with valid responses, and R `survey` version 4.5 with Taylor linearization. Of 1,785 examined older adults, 226 lacked combined grip and two had an invalid or missing sleep response; exclusions overlap. Complete-case selection may bias the association, and examination weights do not automatically correct it. Of the analyzed 1,558, 470 reported ever telling a clinician about sleep trouble and 1,088 did not (unweighted counts). The design had 15 degrees of freedom; model residual degrees of freedom were 12. No marker genotypes are linked in these public files, and independence from all project discovery participants has not been established.
