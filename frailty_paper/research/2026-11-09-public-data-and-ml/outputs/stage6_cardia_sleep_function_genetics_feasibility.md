# CARDIA: no-data-fee sleep and physical-function validation lead

Checked 29 October 2026. CARDIA is a promising **controlled-access** cohort for evaluating project scores against sleep and physical function; it is not an openly downloadable linked-genotype dataset or a completed validation.

* The [CARDIA sleep-study methods](https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/5/1/zpae071/7777161) describe year-35 assessments and report 1,212 participants with valid Insomnia Severity Index responses, 1,159 with valid Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index responses, and 899 with valid wrist actigraphy. These counts do not identify an intersection with genetics, function, FI/FFS, or our variants. The sleep ancillary study's two phases and selection can affect representativeness.
* The separate [CARDIA Function paper](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article-pdf/194/10/2820/61265428/kwae463.pdf) describes year-35 grip strength, 4-m gait speed, balance, chair stands and six-minute walk. These are useful physical-function outcomes and overlap with pieces of Fried frailty, but the physical performance summary is not the complete five-component Fried phenotype or an accumulated-deficit FI. The ability to construct either frailty measure remains unverified. Sleep and function were different study activities; their person-level intersection is unknown.
* The [year-35 sleep questionnaire](https://www.cardia.dopm.uab.edu/images/more/pdf/Year35/Form122.pdf) includes `J12230NO` for difficulty falling asleep within 30 minutes and `J122WAKEEARLY` for night or early waking. These are distinct symptoms and do not constitute an insomnia diagnosis. Form labels and raw response values must be checked against the actual released data and missing-value codes.
* The [BioLINCC CARDIA page](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/studies/cardia/) says its requestable files include exam data through year 35 and 28 ancillary studies, but does not show whether the two specific ancillary datasets or linked genotypes are included. [BioLINCC's FAQ](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/faq/) says there is no cost to access its datasets, but registration with an institutional email and applicable approvals are required. It does not guarantee that all desired data or computing are free.
* A separate [dbGaP CARDIA GENEVA record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000309.v1.p1) describes controlled-access GWAS genotypes and notes important differences between the three historic call sets, recommending the QC-cleaned Beaglecall set. That page is an older version; verify the current release, allowed data-use groups, project variants, imputation and genotype QC. NIH controlled-access application rules apply. Do not assume BioLINCC and dbGaP participant identifiers are joinable or that one approval covers both resources.

Before undertaking score validation: verify whether the year-35 sleep and Function ancillary datasets are currently distributed through the permitted route; establish consent and authorized genetic linkage; obtain the joint count, ancestry, relatedness and data-quality information; audit all marker alleles and imputation; and check overlap with every score development and selection cohort. A continuous grip or gait analysis is distinct from validating FI, FFS or a joint insomnia–frailty outcome. A prospective design requires earlier sleep and later outcomes, rather than treating year-35 concurrent measures as prospective. No individual records were downloaded or used, no request was submitted, and cost-free access to the *complete linked package* has not been established.

## Public dictionary audit

BioLINCC's [2026a core dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/cardia/data_dictionary/CARDIA_2026a.pdf) lists year-35 dataset `jaf36.sas7bdat` with `J36EFFOT` (everything was an effort), `J36GOING` (could not get going) and `J36SLEEP` (restless sleep), plus a CES-D total. These are related to fatigue or sleep but are not equivalent to the seven-item ISI and do not themselves define Fried frailty. Using restless sleep within an FI while testing sleep associations would create item overlap; retain a sleep-excluded FI only if a defensible complete deficit inventory can be constructed.

An exact case-insensitive text search of the 2026a core dictionary and the separately linked [ancillary dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/cardia/Data%20Dictionary%20-%20Ancillary%20Studies.pdf) did **not** find `J12230NO`, `J122WAKEEARLY`, or an explicit “insomnia severity” label, and a targeted label search did not locate the year-35 grip or gait fields. The linked ancillary dictionary's PDF creation date is October 2024, whereas the core dictionary is dated February 2026. Text extraction, differing aliases, and the older document mean this is not proof that the relevant files are absent from the current requestable package. It does mean the prior published 1,212 ISI assessments cannot yet be equated with access through this particular release. Ask for the current ancillary file inventory and approved cross-repository linkage before designing an analysis around those fields.

The ancillary dictionary **does** list `cardiaa_sleep_finalv8.sas7bdat`, with PSQI sleep-latency, sleep-hours and trouble-falling-asleep fields for two waves. The names alone do not establish its time relative to year 35, participant overlap or exact coding. A [published earlier CARDIA sleep study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2785092/) used Chicago participants and two sleep-assessment waves beginning in 2003–2004, but the dictionary does not explicitly identify `cardiaa_sleep_finalv8` as that study, so this connection remains a hypothesis until the dataset documentation is verified. A permitted earlier-sleep/later-function analysis could be prospective if actual dates and linkage support it; its effective sample may be much smaller.

## Earlier core sleep assessments for a prospective design

The BioLINCC 2026a core dictionary also lists `faf67.sas7bdat` and `gaf67.sas7bdat`, which are sleep-question datasets from the [year-15 form](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/2/579/files/2026/03/FORM67.pdf) (2000–2001) and [year-20 form](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/2/579/files/2026/01/Year20_D.pdf) (2005–2006), respectively. The former includes `F67INSOM` (usually trouble falling asleep), `F67WAKEU` (waking several times), `F67EARLY` (waking far too early), `F67RESLP` (able to return to sleep easily), `F67SLPHR` (reported sleep duration) and `F67EXDAT` (exam date); corresponding G67 variables are listed at year 20. The questions are symptoms or self-reported duration, not a clinical insomnia diagnosis or the year-35 ISI.

For the relevant yes/no questions, the paper forms specify 1=no, 2=yes and 8=don't know. The return-to-sleep question has the opposite clinical direction from the difficulty questions and is conditional on waking; neither “don't know” nor a skipped conditional response should be coded automatically as no insomnia. Confirm recoded values and eligibility rules in the actual approved files. Since the core BioLINCC request covers exam data through year 35 and the core dictionary names these datasets, this is stronger evidence for availability of **earlier sleep fields** than the unresolved year-35 sleep-ancillary fields. It still does not prove a user's approval, complete responses, genetic linkage, or availability of later grip/gait.

This makes a potential forward-looking design concrete: fix a year-15 or year-20 sleep exposure, obtain year-35 Function measures if authorized, and compare frozen scores in participants with permitted genotype linkage. The interval and order should be calculated from actual dates, and attrition, death and selection need explicit handling. The outcome would remain physical function unless all components of an appropriate frailty measure can be verified. No such analysis was conducted.

## Core physical-function outcomes located

The same [2026a core dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/cardia/data_dictionary/CARDIA_2026a.pdf), page 773, lists `jaf65.sas7bdat` with an anonymized participant ID (`RID`), exam date (`J65EXDAT`), self-reported limitations in moderate activities (`J65MODA`) and climbing stairs (`J65CLIMB`), and a physical-component summary (`J65PCS12`). Earlier datasets `faf65.sas7bdat` and `gaf65.sas7bdat` list matching moderate-activity and stair-limitation fields, with dates, enabling a proposed baseline-adjusted year-35 analysis if the released data support consistent coding and linkage. The core dictionary also lists `RID` in the sleep forms; that supports a proposed within-release join, but does not establish usable data, consent, or a cross-repository genotype linkage.

This creates a feasible *phenotype-only* alternative that does not depend on the separately documented year-35 Function ancillary battery: prespecify an early sleep symptom from year 15, examine subsequent year-35 activity limitation, and adjust for the corresponding year-15 functional limitation and prespecified confounders. Confirm question wording, response coding, dates, completeness, selection and death/attrition before analysis. These self-reports are not objective gait or grip tests and are neither FI nor FFS; an association would not validate a genetic score. A score validation extension still requires approved person-level genotypes, legal ID linkage, sufficient joint sample and verified variant coding, and independent score-development samples.

## Verification of repeated physical-function questions

The original [year-15 Form 65](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/2/579/files/2026/03/FORM65.pdf) and [year-35 Form 65](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/2/579/files/2026/03/Form-65-Quality-of-Life-SF-12_SAS_09252019_wip.pdf) use the same wording and paper-response order for both proposed function measures: limitation in moderate activities such as moving a table, and limitation climbing several flights of stairs. At both visits the forms code 1 = not limited, 2 = limited a little, and 3 = limited a lot. Thus the public questionnaires substantiate item-level comparability more strongly than matching dictionary labels alone. They do not establish how the eventual released SAS variables encode missingness or whether actual records are complete; check release-specific documentation and distributions before recoding. One possible prespecified analysis would treat each outcome as ordered, retaining the three categories, with a binary sensitivity analysis only if event counts support it. Do not claim linear category spacing or use `J65PCS12` as a substitute for these two items without verifying its derivation and licensed scoring procedures.
