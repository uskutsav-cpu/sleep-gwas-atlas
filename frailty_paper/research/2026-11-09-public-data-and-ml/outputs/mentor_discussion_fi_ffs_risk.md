# Mentor discussion: insomnia, FI and Fried frailty

**Update:** This brief includes earlier findings. For the current synthesis, including the Mak FI–physical-phenotype comparisons and the 22-marker shared-direction research count, use [the updated assessment](mentor_risk_assessment_updated.md). The 15-marker set described below is a separate, earlier prototype; neither set is a validated predictor.

## The research question

Insomnia and frailty are associated in observational studies, but frailty can be defined as accumulation of health deficits (FI) or as five physical criteria (Fried frailty score, FFS). Are the genetic associations with insomnia shared across both measures, and can those associations ultimately improve prediction of frailty? Our first task is to establish what the existing data can answer before pursuing mechanisms or model building.

## What we found

1. **FI and FFS share substantial genetic signal.** The existing project's auxiliary LDSC estimate is genetic correlation r_g=0.757 (SE 0.021; 95% CI 0.715–0.799); an alternative SNP-support sensitivity gives 0.755. These reuse the same two frailty GWAS and are not independent replications. Genetic correlation is not the percentage of patients or causal variants shared and is not the observed correlation of FI and FFS in the same people. Source details and recalculation: `direct_fi_ffs_genetic_correlation.csv`.
2. **Insomnia–FI correlation is higher than insomnia–FFS in each checked source.** For UKB insomnia symptoms, r_g is 0.640 with FI and 0.483 with FFS; their paired difference (FFS−FI) is −0.156, 95% CI −0.213 to −0.100. The corresponding differences are −0.181 (−0.260 to −0.102) for FinnGen and −0.151 (−0.220 to −0.082) for MVP. Sources reuse the same frailty GWAS, so these are insomnia-source sensitivity checks rather than independent replications of the frailty side. Paired differences account for jackknife covariance in the archived analyses; the UKB and clinical analyses have different validation histories detailed in `insomnia_frailty_risk_briefing.md`.
3. **Score composition is one plausible reason for the difference, not its established explanation.** The UKB FI includes an insomnia question and a tiredness question; the five-part FFS includes exhaustion. A related 44-item SALT index used in Swedish research lists no direct sleep or fatigue item, though the exact Atkins TwinGene table still needs verification. Age, ascertainment, sample overlap, power and different FI definitions may also matter. The original scoring sources disagree on one tiredness coding value. See `fi_ffs_component_crosswalk.md`.
4. **A measured FI and a measured Fried score need not flag the same people.** In one separate cohort of 1,563 older adults, 103 were frail by both definitions, 125 by FI alone and 18 by Fried alone; 1,317 were classified non-frail by both. The observed overlap depends on the definitions and cutoffs and is a different quantity from r_g. [Toledo cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13264346/); derivation in `fi_ffs_same_cohort_comparison.md`.
A separate Spanish study reports a Spearman correlation of 0.542 between its 27-item FI score and the count of Fried criteria; its approximately 150 older participants and instruments differ from the project sources. [Article, Figure 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-025-01931-z/figures/2).

A separate Canadian study of 28,527 participants reported phi=0.38 for binary FI versus Fried classifications; it used a 93-item FI and specific clinical cutoffs. That is not a continuous score correlation, nor is it directly numerically comparable to the project's genetic correlation. [Pasquet et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40520-024-02706-w)

5. **Clinical and genetic risk are distinct.** A Chinese prospective cohort with an insomnia-excluded FI reported a prevalence ratio of 1.53 (95% CI 1.23–1.89) for later frailty among initially prefrail participants with baseline insomnia. This is observational, not an effect of treating insomnia. [Zhu et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-022-02557-0). A widely cited pooled OR of 1.95 came from 12 cross-sectional studies with substantial heterogeneity, so it does not by itself establish future frailty risk. [Wen et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/brb3.2793).

## What the 15 candidates mean

The later [22-marker candidate review](expanded_22_candidate_risk_gene_review.md) and [shared-direction count](shared22_score_research_guide.md) address a broader candidate set. Its count is an unweighted research construct selected using insomnia and hospital-frailty results; it has not been tested against observed outcomes. [The eight-source evidence review](shared22_eight_source_evidence_review.md) describes five post-selected candidates with nominal same-direction associations across eight result sets, including overlapping cohorts. These do not establish independent replication or causal genes.

`candidate_risk_gene_review_15.csv` joins selected variants to their trait-specific associations, source-cohort FI results, and nearby gene bodies. Selection was post hoc from published cross-trait results and filtered by availability; some candidates may be correlated and nine rsIDs are multiallelic in the reference. They are **not 15 independently verified shared-risk loci**. For instance, rs12536335 is within the FOXP2 gene body but has opposite estimated FI directions across UKB and TwinGene after the project's source-backed TwinGene allele adapter. Proximity does not establish FOXP2 as the acting gene, and the disagreement does not identify its cause. See `candidate_twingene_sensitivity.md`.

Three separate sets of exploratory trait-specific coefficients are in `unvalidated_15_variant_score_prototype.csv`. Their scales differ; they do not form a single calibrated score. No individual genotypes or outcomes were available for evaluation. Published FI and FFS polygenic scores provide comparators but do not validate this subset, a combined score, or prediction of insomnia–frailty outcomes. The paper and PGS Catalog report conflicting FI-outcome performance numbers for some score/cohort combinations; those estimates require reconciliation before using them in a comparison. The paper also reports physical-frailty outcome associations in LBC1936; see `fi_ffs_pgs_published_validation_audit.md`.

## Proposed next steps

- In a suitably powered, authorized cohort with both measures, construct the original FI, FI excluding insomnia, FI excluding insomnia and tiredness, and FFS in the same participants. Prespecify missingness, item coding, covariates and paired comparisons. Separately consider an FFS sensitivity phenotype without exhaustion, clearly labeled as modified.
- If genotypes are available, perform separate GWAS of the alternative FI definitions and compare genome-wide correlations with matched SNP support and covariance-aware uncertainty. A published quantile-normalized GWAS cannot be converted into a sleep-excluded one by subtracting a coefficient. The existing TwinGene LDSC precision gate and FI×sleep LAVA QC gates failed; they do not support a null or a local-sharing claim.
- Define a clinically meaningful incident-frailty outcome and time horizon. Train an LD-aware prediction model on independent development data and compare it with FI, FFS and insomnia PGS baselines. Validate calibration and incremental performance in a held-out, unrelated cohort independent of discovery GWAS. Do not assume that 15 or 30 selected SNPs will be sufficient.
- For supported regions, follow dense-QC, LD-aware and colocalization evidence with gene-regulation and cell-type analyses. Use nearest genes as leads, not conclusions.

## Additional check on external FFS-score validation

Ye et al. reported raw score AUC 0.57 in HRS, and 0.72 after combining the score with age, sex and genetic PCs. The latter is not the score’s standalone AUC. Conditional reconstructions expose discrepancies in the printed OR/CI/p and the reported 18-of-22 binomial sign test; see `ye_hrs_score_validation_audit.md` before quoting the precise statistics. The HRS result does not validate the 15-candidate prototype.

A separate body of clinical studies compares observed FI and Fried phenotype against mortality in the same people. A systematic review found broadly comparable, modest discrimination across heterogeneous cohorts; this is not a comparison of the two genetic scores. See `fi_ffs_clinical_mortality_comparison.md`.
