# MEIS1: prior functional evidence in neural cell models

[Lam et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddab355) investigated the MEIS1 intronic region associated with insomnia and restless legs syndrome (RLS). This is particularly relevant because rs113851554, a 15-marker prototype variant, strongly influenced the project's exploratory FI-versus-FFS-weight contrast for insomnia. The paper is prior molecular evidence, not a new analysis or validation of our genetic scores.

The authors examined chromatin accessibility, promoter contacts, methylation and expression data across neural cell models and developmental stages. They report that the intronic element they call **602**, which carries the lead RLS-associated variant, was accessible in their assayed GABAergic neurons and GABA-enriched fetal tissues, but not in all the other tested cell models and tissues. The paper identifies rs113851554 as the leading insomnia/RLS variant; the earlier [reporter supplement](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/suppl/2014/02/10/gr.166751.113.DC1/Supplemental_Material.pdf) also lists that rsID within element 602. This supports a testable inhibitory-neuron regulatory-context hypothesis. Cell lines, developmental state and bulk tissue are not interchangeable with all adult brain cell types.

The study also used CRISPR interference to suppress three regional regulatory elements, including 602, in human **neural stem cells** and measured MEIS1 expression. It reports roughly 20–30% transcriptional suppression relative to its control guide, with four replicates per guide. This is perturbation of an element, not a precise edit or functional comparison of the rs113851554 G and T alleles. It supports a regulatory role for the element in that cellular setting; it does not demonstrate that this exact SNP causes the observed sleep association, that the mechanism operates in adult inhibitory neurons, or that insomnia and frailty share a causal variant. The study's forebrain eQTL findings concern the region and should not be conflated with our [separate GTEx v10 exact-variant query](stage6_meis1_gtex_v10_exact_variant_review.md), which returned no rows for G/T; differing versions, variant coverage, tissue and filtering could account for different results.

For future work, link a fully harmonized insomnia and frailty credible set to cell-specific chromatin and QTL data, explicitly examine RLS/movement as a competing explanation for sleep associations, and consider allele-specific reporter or base-editing validation. Avoid treating inferred cell type, nearest-gene position or element suppression as patient-level risk evidence. The sleep/frailty scores still need independent linked participant-level validation.
