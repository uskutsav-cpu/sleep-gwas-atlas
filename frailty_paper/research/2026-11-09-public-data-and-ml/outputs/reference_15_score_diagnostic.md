# Technical test of the three 15-marker research sums

I applied the existing `unvalidated_15_variant_score_prototype.csv` to 503 European-labeled 1000 Genomes phase 3 reference individuals. For each participant and each trait separately, the calculation summed the number of the listed counted alleles (0, 1 or 2) times that trait's published-source coefficient. All 15 positions, rsIDs and two-allele contrasts matched biallelic records in the GRCh37 reference VCF, with complete calls for the 503 individuals. This verifies that the arithmetic can be applied to this specific panel; it does not verify all GWAS effect-allele conventions or other representations at the nine rsIDs that are multiallelic in broader reference data. The FFS effect orientation remains inferred.

The resulting **sample correlations of the three constructed sums**, not clinical outcomes or genome-wide genetic correlations, were:

| Pair of research sums | Pearson r in this panel |
|---|---:|
| FI and FFS | 0.801 |
| FI and UKB-insomnia | 0.859 |
| FFS and UKB-insomnia | 0.523 |

These values follow mechanically from the selected markers, their three sets of weights, reference allele frequencies, LD and population structure. The markers were selected using related GWAS results; the correlations do not independently establish shared risk or compare prediction performance. In particular, the 0.801 correlation of these **15-marker sums** is not the project's genome-wide LDSC estimate of approximately 0.75 and should not be interpreted as its replication. The weight scales differ, so the three raw sums should not be added.

## Sensitivity to a single marker

I recalculated all three correlations after omitting each marker in turn, without reselecting or retraining. Removing rs113851554 changes the FFS–insomnia correlation from 0.523 to 0.671, illustrating that this small candidate set is sensitive to its composition. Across the 15 single-marker omissions, the FI–FFS correlation ranged 0.769–0.834, FI–insomnia 0.841–0.911, and FFS–insomnia 0.471–0.671. These are descriptive perturbations, not confidence intervals or causal influence estimates. The 503 participants comprise five European subpopulations and may be related; no ancestry-PC adjustment or uncertainty analysis was applied.

## Within the reference subgroups

As a descriptive check on the pooled result, the same correlations within each phase 3 EUR-labeled subgroup were:

| Reference subgroup | n | FI–FFS | FI–insomnia | FFS–insomnia |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CEU | 99 | 0.792 | 0.889 | 0.571 |
| FIN | 99 | 0.820 | 0.855 | 0.518 |
| GBR | 91 | 0.756 | 0.864 | 0.451 |
| IBS | 107 | 0.791 | 0.851 | 0.532 |
| TSI | 107 | 0.823 | 0.846 | 0.525 |

All three correlations are positive within each subgroup, so the pooled positive pattern is not solely generated by differences in subgroup means. These small, potentially related reference samples do not support claims that populations differ or that the scores transfer clinically. They have no outcome measures, and no inferential comparison or adjustment for finer structure was performed.

## What remains untested

The reference file supplies genotype calls, **not FI, FFS, insomnia, age, follow-up outcomes or clinical risk** for this analysis. No discrimination, calibration, prospective performance, score significance, individual prediction or improvement over existing scores has been evaluated. A real study must fix an outcome and time horizon, resolve source alleles, develop and tune weights with appropriate LD and independent data, and test in a separate cohort. Neither reference-panel correlations nor technical allele matching validate a genetic risk score.

`reference_15_score_diagnostic.json` records sample/subpopulation counts, all allele checks, correlations and leave-one-out results, plus the input checksum. `work/reference_15_score_check.py` reproduces the calculation from indexed public VCFs and the phase 3 sample panel. No person-level genotypes or scores were saved.
