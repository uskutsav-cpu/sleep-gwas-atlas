# Estonian sleep-diagnosis follow-up for a project marker

A [sleep-genetics preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12668095/) reports an Estonian Biobank follow-up of selected sleep-disorder GWAS leads. In its [public supplementary workbook](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12668095.1/media-2.xlsx), sheet ST5, one of the project's 15 weighted-prototype variants appears by rsID: **rs113851554**, with T versus G at GRCh38 position 2:66523432. T is also the project's counted allele. This locus is multiallelic in other references, so exact allele-specific encoding and build harmonization remain important. None of the 22-marker count's rsIDs appeared in this selected-lead table. An absent row means only that a variant is not in this table, not that it is unassociated or absent from the underlying GWAS.

| Estonian outcome | Reported beta (effect allele convention unverified) | SE | p | N |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Insomnia | 0.0551 | 0.0291 | 0.0583 | 205,165 |
| Restless legs syndrome | 0.5799 | 0.0677 | 1.05 × 10⁻¹⁷ | 205,165 |
| Sleep apnea | −0.0224 | 0.0320 | 0.484 | 205,422 |

The source reports a positive insomnia beta, but its beta-to-allele convention has not been independently verified; its confidence interval crosses zero (approximate log-odds 95% CI −0.002 to 0.112), so it is **not a statistically clear replication**. The paper reports 12,962 insomnia cases in its Estonian replication sample. The much stronger restless-legs association is consistent with this locus's established sleep-movement phenotype and reinforces the need to distinguish restless legs from insomnia, rather than attributing the finding to a single insomnia mechanism. Differences in significance alone do not prove that effects differ across phenotypes. The reported per-allele estimates concern diagnoses, not individual clinical predictions.

This is a selected genome-wide lead examined after the project's marker selection and prior locus investigation, not a prespecified test of the 15-marker score. The preprint's discovery meta-analysis contains UK Biobank, MVP and FinnGen, which were also used in project work; its discovery effect is not independent. Estonia is a separately named follow-up cohort, but participant and close-relative overlap with all project sources has not been established. The table does not test FI, FFS, joint sleep–frailty outcomes, score discrimination or calibration. The report is a preprint and its results are subject to revision. [Earlier MEIS1 review](stage6_meis1_independent_sleep_phenotype_review.md) provides additional context on sleep movement phenotypes.

Using `openpyxl` in read-only mode, ST5 SNPID entries were matched to rsIDs in `unvalidated_15_variant_score_prototype.csv` and `unvalidated_22_shared_direction_score.csv`; all three phenotype rows for the matching rsID are shown above. A different representation of a candidate would not be found by this rsID-only check.

The [later source audit](stage6_kunorozva_estonian_selected_variant_review.md) repeats these same selected-table results and does not constitute a new cohort or replication. A subsequent [position-based screen](stage6_estonian_coordinate_screen.py), using prior positive-strand GRCh37-to-GRCh38 mappings plus two checked Ensembl mappings, matched all 15 and 22 marker coordinates against the 490 structured rows in ST5. It found the same three rs113851554 phenotype rows and no other positions. See the [machine-readable result](stage6_estonian_coordinate_screen.json). Coordinate agreement alone does not establish matching allele contrasts; this remains a selected-lead table, not a complete GWAS.

A subsequent [exploratory nearby-proxy check](stage6_estonian_insomnia_proxy_review.md) found that rs1093463 is perfectly correlated with 22-marker candidate rs817509 in a 503-person European reference panel, but the Estonian proxy association is uncertain (p = 0.539). The panel does not establish study LD or score validation.
