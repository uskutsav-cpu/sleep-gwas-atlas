# RLS regional discrepancy: allele-frequency spot check

The [regional source comparison](stage6_rls_region_source_concordance_review.md) found opposite effect signs for multiple matched chromosome 2 variants in the Akçimen and FinnGen RLS summary files. To investigate a possible coding explanation, we queried five variants selected from that region and expressed both sources' reported frequency and beta for the same exact GRCh38 ALT allele. Four had opposite reported signs in the two sources; rs113851554 was included as a concordant-direction comparator.

For the two common discordant variants rs62144055 C and rs12469063 G, the Akçimen/FinnGen ALT frequencies were 0.321/0.354 and 0.237/0.217, respectively. The reported frequencies are closer to one another than to their complements, so a **simple whole-file ALT-frequency reversal is not an obvious explanation for these rows**. This is a plausibility check, not proof of correct effect-allele coding. Allele frequencies can differ between populations, between case–control samples and meta-analyses, and with imputation or QC; neither source's total frequency verifies every contributing cohort's effect encoding. This observation does not rule out a beta sign error, another harmonization issue, different LD or phenotype ascertainment, or genuine heterogeneity. The checks were selected after seeing the discrepancy.

The three lower-frequency variants also show cross-source frequency differences: rs187012067 C 0.0117/0.0385, rs113851554 T 0.0512/0.0698 and rs78206187 G 0.0509/0.0277. These differences do not by themselves identify an error, especially because the FinnGen sample is Finnish and the studies have different designs. No participant-level genotypes, per-component Akçimen estimates or allele counts were inspected. Both exact pair matches and the Akçimen harmonization codes are recorded in the [spot-check table](stage6_rls_region_allele_frequency_spotcheck.csv); source URLs and the output hash are in the [receipt](stage6_rls_region_allele_frequency_spotcheck_receipt.json). Reproduce with `python3 work/rls2024/allele_frequency_spotcheck.py`. Queries retrieved indexed ranges rather than full files, so full-file checksums were not verified.

The direction conflict remains unresolved and cannot be used to claim a causal sleep–frailty mechanism or validate a score. The next decisive evidence would be original per-cohort association outputs and effect-allele/QC documentation, with sample overlap and phenotype definitions checked.
