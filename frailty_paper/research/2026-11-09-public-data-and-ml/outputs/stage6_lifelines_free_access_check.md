# Lifelines: free dataset does not mean free access

Lifelines is relevant to sleep and ageing research, and a [published longitudinal analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7869455/) uses a 64-deficit frailty index. Its potential genotype–sleep–frailty overlap, marker coverage, frailty item definitions and independence from the discovery data would still need to be established. These facts alone do not validate either project score.

An external directory claims no cost for academic access, but the [official 2026 Lifelines fee schedule](https://www.lifelines-biobank.com/researchers/what-we-offer/fees-2026) distinguishes the dataset itself, which is free, from the secure environment and supporting services, for which standard one-year academic access is listed at €3,000 for a basic workspace, €3,500 for a premium workspace or cluster, and €4,500 for a combined workspace and cluster, excluding VAT. The page specifies a €500 service discount for three named partner institutions. We have not established a waiver or an alternative no-cost linked participant-level access route for this project. Accordingly, Lifelines does **not** currently satisfy the user's practical free-access requirement; its directory's general no-cost claim should not be used as a budget estimate. A controlled EGA subcohort is not automatically a free linked phenotype alternative.

For comparison, the [Framingham route](framingham_no_fee_validation_route.md) remains a no-data-fee but controlled-access lead, subject to eligibility and the unresolved genotype–phenotype intersection. The [main validation summary](stage6_score_validation_evidence_summary.md) distinguishes these leads from true individual-level score validation.
