# Look AHEAD: FI versus physical frailty and sleep overlap

Look AHEAD supplies a useful published comparison of frailty definitions, but is **not an identified free public genotype-validation dataset**. It enrolled adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity into a randomized lifestyle-intervention trial. Phenotypic data from its primary trial are [available by request through the NIDDK repository](https://repository.niddk.nih.gov/study/61); a [2025 genetics paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03827-z) explicitly states that Look AHEAD genetic data are not publicly available because of informed-consent restrictions. We have not established an eligible no-fee linked-genotype route; data availability by request does not prove anonymous download, approval, absence of fees, or access to genetic data.

## What the published results add

* The [deficit-accumulation FI analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7518562/) reported mean FI scores 5–6% lower in the intensive lifestyle intervention group across follow-up. This is a relative group difference as reported in the paper, not a five-to-six percentage point difference.
* A separate [long-term physical-frailty analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536442/) assessed 2,979 extension participants on average 14 years after randomization and 4.6 years after the intervention ended. Its modified Fried phenotype used four criteria—weakness, slowness, low activity and exhaustion—omitting weight loss because the trial deliberately targeted it. It defined frailty as at least three of the four criteria and reported 10.9% versus 11.6% frailty in the intervention and control groups, respectively, without a detected group difference.
* These results illustrate why FI and physical frailty can answer different questions: the FI aggregates a broad selection of health deficits, whereas this modified phenotype concentrates on physical function. They are **not a matched, same-time head-to-head estimate**: outcome definitions, assessment timing, selected participants, scale and statistical precision differ. A nonsignificant difference is not proof of equivalence. Nor is this four-item adaptation interchangeable with the project's five-component FFS GWAS.

## Sleep item and an implementable sensitivity

The public [NIDDK source-variable documentation](https://repository.niddk.nih.gov/public/study_document/Look_AHEAD_V9/Data/LA_Frailty_Index_-_Source_Variables.pdf) identifies component 11 as a sleep item derived from the psychosocial depression dataset (`Bdsleep1`, `Bdsleep2`, `Bdsleep3`, `Bdsleep4`), scored 0, 0.33, 0.67 or 1 across worsening responses. It says the sleep component is available in years 0–4 and 8 and was not collected in years 5–7. The augmented index also includes sleep apnea, a distinct clinical deficit; omitting only the insomnia-like sleep item does not eliminate all sleep-related overlap. An association between that sleep question and an FI that includes it is partly mechanical.

If the relevant person-level component values and denominator are available and this item contributed to the reported score, the sleep-excluded FI is `(n × FI − sleep_component) / (n − 1)`, where `n` is the **actual number of evaluated components for that person and visit**, not automatically 38. Apply only when n > 1 and verify source-specific missingness, imputation, derivation and rounding. If FI was imputed, rounded or the sleep component was not included at that visit, recompute the score from observed components and the documented rules rather than blindly applying the formula. Define separately whether to exclude sleep apnea and fatigue/exhaustion; those answer a broader question and change the construct. Analyze complete and missing-data patterns and paired participants when comparing FI and physical frailty.

No individual records were accessed, no intervention effects were re-estimated, and no genetic score was calculated. This source is useful background and a reproducible phenotype-audit example, not evidence of a shared genetic mechanism or validation of our project scores.
