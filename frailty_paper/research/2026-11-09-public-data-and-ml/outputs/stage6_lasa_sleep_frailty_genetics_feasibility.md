# LASA: potential FI, physical-frailty and genetic validation cohort

Checked 28 October 2026. The [Longitudinal Aging Study Amsterdam cohort profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7058575/) describes genome-wide array data for 2,279 unrelated European-ancestry participants following QC, using Axiom-NL or Illumina GSA v1.0 and Haplotype Reference Consortium imputation. The number pertains to the profile's 2019 report, not a verified joint sample with complete insomnia, FI, physical frailty and candidate variants. Other LASA participants and ancestry groups may not be represented in this specific genetic release.

LASA has used both FI and a Fried-style physical phenotype, including in [a study comparing functioning measures and mortality](https://bmcgeriatr.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12877-022-02876-0). Its cohort profile also describes a one-week calendar with self-rated sleep for a face-to-face subset; this measure is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis and its overlap with the genotyped frailty sample is not established. A published [grip-score study](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/files/139342043/The-phenotypic-and-genotypic-association-of-grip-strength-with-frailty-physical-performance-and-functional-limitations-.pdf) demonstrates that genetic scores have been studied alongside frailty, but it uses a different score and does not validate our prototypes.

Access is by research proposal and agreement according to the [LASA access page](https://lasa-vu.nl/en/request-data/), including a data-sharing agreement for external use. The page does not establish that a specific linked genotype–phenotype request is no-fee or open for immediate download. No access request was made. Before calling it a validation cohort, verify cohort and relative overlap with every score-selection and discovery study, actual genotype/imputation records for every allele, available sleep and frailty timepoints, definitions and event counts, allowable linkage, genetic batch, and cost. FI item content and sleep/fatigue overlap need review. The [generic GSA v1 reference screen](stage6_nshap_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md) is product/reference information only and does not establish LASA's actual variant quality or the Axiom-NL batch's coverage.

This is a useful controlled/request-access lead and possible paired FI-versus-physical-phenotype evaluation, not completed independent validation or an established free public dataset. Any use of phenotypic results from other published PRSs as if they validated our own scores would be incorrect.

## Sleep-symptom and FI item follow-up

A [LASA frailty-prediction study](https://d-nb.info/1226930808/34) describes three self-reported sleep problems: difficulty falling asleep, waking during the night, and early awakening. Each has four frequency categories; the summed score ranges from 3 to 12. This is more directly relevant to insomnia symptoms than the one-week sleep calendar, but the paper does not establish a clinical insomnia diagnosis or that these measures are present in the genotyped sample. Its 569 participants were aged ≥75 and selected for a separate observational analysis; this is not the number available for our genetic evaluation. It used multiple imputation and model selection, so its predictive findings do not validate our scores.

The original [32-item LASA-FI development study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5589777/) lists chronic disease, function, self-rated health, depressive-symptom items, activity, memory/cognition and gait among its components. Its published list contains no explicitly named sleep item, but includes “everything is an effort” and “could not get going,” two fatigue-related items. Other FI versions and wave-specific coding require verification. A [later LASA Fried-style phenotype study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12021940/) also describes using two CES-D items for exhaustion; confirm whether the precise items and waves match the FI before treating the two outcomes as independently measured.

For a future authorized paired comparison, prespecify the published 32-item FI, an FI recomputed without the two fatigue items with its denominator and missingness rule updated, and the study-specific physical phenotype. Compare the three-item sleep-symptom score at appropriate exposure and outcome times. These are alternative outcome definitions; a modified FI is not automatically validated by the published 32-item FI, and removing fatigue does not remove confounding or indirect sleep-related content. The cohort availability, source permissions, exact genotype markers, sample overlap and costs remain unverified.
