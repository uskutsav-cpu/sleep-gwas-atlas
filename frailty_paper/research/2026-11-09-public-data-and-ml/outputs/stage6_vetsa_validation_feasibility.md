# VETSA public-use data: a validation lead

The [Vietnam Era Twin Study of Aging public-use collection](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/NACDA/studies/38836) is a potential outcome-data lead. It follows community-dwelling male veteran twins across three waves, with mean age approximately 56 at baseline and 68 by the third wave. The archive says the public-use files do not require membership in an ICPSR institution. The cohort is male-only and includes related twins, so any analysis would require attention to sex generalizability and within-family correlation. The study description also states that it is not a Veterans Affairs sample; this does not by itself establish absence of participants or relatives from the project's discovery datasets.

The [VETSA public variable concordance](https://psychiatry.ucsd.edu/research/programs-centers/vetsa/_files/VETSAComposite-concordance_2023_12_01.xlsx) lists a past-month question about being unable to fall asleep within 30 minutes (sheet `Med Hist Concordance`, row 570), and a right-hand grip-strength trial in kilograms (sheet `DES`, row 755). It also lists other sleep, physical-function and activity questions. These are promising measurements, but they do **not** establish a clinically diagnosed insomnia outcome or a complete Fried frailty score. The word-search inventory found no exact weight-loss, frailty or genotyping keywords; missing search terms do not prove that such data are absent, because the concordance is not a complete inventory and labels may differ.

A prior [VETSA methods report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31933447/) says genome-wide genotyping was performed in wave 3. However, neither the concordance search nor the archive's general public-use statement establishes that the public release includes those genotypes, the score variants, suitable linkage IDs, or adequate participant numbers. [UC San Diego](https://psychiatry.ucsd.edu/research/programs-centers/vetsa/researchers.html) states that data not on the public archive require a data request and agreement. The archive documentation page returned a security-verification challenge in this environment, so its file list could not be inspected. No assertion is made that the genetic data are unavailable or restricted in this specific release. No participant-level data were downloaded, no access request was sent, and score validation has not been performed.

Before treating VETSA as a usable validation cohort, inspect its public data dictionary and file list for genome-wide genotypes or the exact marker dosages; confirm a permitted linkage, usable FI/FFS or prespecified physical and sleep outcomes, marker representation, sample/event counts, family structure, and non-overlap with discovery cohorts. If only survey variables are public, they cannot be combined with inferred or simulated genetic dosages to validate the score. If full score variants require separate authorized access, that is outside the current public-data route.

`stage6_vetsa_variable_screen.py` inventories targeted keywords in the downloaded concordance; `stage6_vetsa_variable_screen.json` records matching sheet rows and the locally calculated SHA-256. The source file was not checked against a publisher checksum, and this keyword screen is not an exhaustive semantic review of the dataset.
