# Mak physical-frailty score and the Hanlon UK Biobank adaptation

Mak et al. cite the UK Biobank frailty phenotype developed by [Hanlon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(18)30091-4). Comparing Hanlon's published Table 1 with Mak's Supplementary Table 5 confirms that the descriptions and scoring rules match for four domains: **weight loss, exhaustion, slowness and low physical activity**. Both use the same sex- and BMI-adjusted Fried approach for grip strength, but Hanlon's Table 1 does not enumerate the specific grip cutoffs and cannot resolve the discrepancy in Mak's supplement.

| Domain | What both published tables describe | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Weight loss | A self-reported comparison with the prior year; reporting weight loss receives one point. | The question does **not** distinguish intentional from unintentional loss. Hanlon explicitly notes this limitation. |
| Exhaustion | Self-reported tiredness or low energy over two weeks; more than half the days or nearly every day receives one point. | The question matches UKB field 2080, used as fatigue in a published FI derivation script; Mak's FI table does not supply the field or coding, so exact field identity remains unconfirmed. See [the detailed comparison](ukb_fi_fp_exhaustion_overlap.md). |
| Slowness | Self-reported usual walking pace; slow receives one point. | A reported pace, not a timed walk. |
| Low activity | The same four-category self-reported activity scheme and low-activity cutoff. | The definition differs from the original Fried leisure-activity questionnaire. |
| Weakness | Measured grip strength with sex- and BMI-related cutoffs based on Fried. | Hanlon Table 1 cites the Fried cutoffs without printing each numerical boundary. Mak's last male cutoff row is internally inconsistent. |

Mak's supplement prints BMI >28 as “≤32 kg=1; <32 kg=0.” The first part matches the familiar positive-case Fried threshold reported in the primary [Searle et al. frailty-index methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2573877/); the printed zero category overlaps it. If these are intended to be mutually exclusive exhaustive binary categories, **>32 kg** would be the logical complement of ≤32 kg. That is an inference, not proof of the actual Mak GWAS coding. The original Hanlon description does not establish the exact zero-category implementation, and Mak's phenotype-construction code or author clarification is still needed. No source file or GWAS effect was changed.

Ye's original FFS study cites the Hanlon UK Biobank adaptation and describes the same five physical domains, but the complete source-specific item and cutoff implementation has not been verified. Similar published descriptions are not proof of identical samples, derived values or GWAS models. The project's statistical comparison therefore refers to the named study phenotypes, not one universal FFS. The FI–FP genetic correlations cannot quantify the contribution of the exhaustion or insomnia item; that requires explicit phenotype reconstruction and new GWAS or appropriately matched released summary statistics.
