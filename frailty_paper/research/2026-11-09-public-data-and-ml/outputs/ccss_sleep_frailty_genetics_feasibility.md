# Childhood Cancer Survivor Study: sleep, frailty and genetics

The CCSS is a promising **secondary-population** lead for testing the project's scores, not an established accessible validation sample. The [dbGaP study description](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs001327.v3.p1) describes genome-wide array, exome and whole-genome sequencing data, plus longitudinal health surveys. Genome-wide genetic data are controlled access. The 30,717 consented participants shown in the study description are not the number with all three of genotypes, sleep and frailty.

In a [published CCSS analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11807945/), 9,044 childhood-cancer survivors (mean age 40.8 years) had a modified physical frailty measure in 2014–2016 and sleep quality in 2017–2019. This is population-level evidence that the phenotypes were collected in the same people, **not evidence that those 9,044 are all genotyped**. The adapted frailty measure uses weight/BMI, SF-36 vitality, physical activity, reported walking limitations and clinician-told weakness; it is not identical to the UK Biobank FFS. Sleep is assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, not an insomnia diagnosis. Cancer and treatment effects, selection of survivors, young mean age and attrition limit generalization to community-dwelling older adults.

A [published CCSS genomic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11029534/) reports genome-wide array genotypes for survivors diagnosed in 1970–1986 through dbGaP, while its [data availability statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02837-7) describes later-diagnosed participant genotypes through the St. Jude Cloud. Release versions and cross-portal linkage need verification: neither source establishes that the sleep/frailty sample has the project's 15 or 22 exact allele contrasts, their imputation quality or adequate outcome counts. Discovery-sample and relative overlap also require checking.

## Access and next checks

The [v2 data-use agreement](https://dbgap.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/aa/wga.cgi?stacc=phs001327.v2.p1&view_pdf=) specifies health/medical/biomedical use and says public posting of genomic summary results is not allowed. Check the current agreement for the exact release, applicable consent groups, permitted outputs, and any CCSS or portal requirements before requesting access or planning publication. Standard dbGaP application and institutional authorization apply; no study-specific data or computing cost was established. Do not assume cloud analysis is free. The [study site](https://ccss.stjude.org/public-access-data.html) offers public tables, which are aggregate rather than genotypes linked to individuals.

Priority feasibility checks: identify available release-specific 2014–2016 frailty variables and 2017–2019 sleep measures, assess participant overlap with the authorized genotype files and consent, then verify exact variant alleles, assembly, dosage and quality. Compare the frozen scores with the same prespecified physical frailty and sleep outcomes, checking ancestry and relatedness and reporting effect uncertainty, and treat this as survivor-specific evaluation. Approval, usable data, sample size and marker coverage remain unverified; no participant analysis has been performed.

## Open portal check, 29 October 2026

The [St. Jude Survivorship Portal](https://survivorship.stjude.cloud/) is described in its [methods paper](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerdiscovery/article/doi/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-23-1441/746346) as free and open for interactive aggregate browsing, with controlled-access individual data downloads. In the live portal I selected CCSS (December 2023 data freeze; 25,735 questionnaire-completing survivors, not all necessarily genotyped). Searching the Data Variables dictionary for “insomnia” returned existing scores including PGS001932; selecting its European-ancestry-computed version displayed a distribution for 4,083 samples. This is an existing external score, not our 15- or 22-marker predictor; its count does not establish matching frailty outcomes, discovery independence or usable covariates.

The dictionary also lists a question labelled “Had trouble sleeping in the past 7 days,” but its CCSS display categorizes all 25,735 as “N/A: survey version.” Therefore this **specific portal variable cannot be used as a CCSS outcome in this displayed release**, despite its search hit; it does not contradict the published sleep study or prove that all other sleep variables are unavailable. Searching the dictionary for the exact word “frailty” returned no match; this does not rule out building a measure from component variables or accessing the paper's separate analytical dataset. No genetic association or individual-level score validation was performed. The older portal paper's WGS counts refer to an earlier release and should not be equated to the current CCSS questionnaire total.

The portal's [version 2 release notes](https://github.com/stjude/proteinpaint/wiki/St.-Jude-Survivorship-Portal-Data-Releases) say 4,803 CCSS survivors have genetic data, up from 2,688 in version 1; some are also SJLIFE participants. This is not the number with usable sleep, frailty and all score variants. The notes say the 2018 SJLIFE freeze is retained and the earlier release describes phenotypes as baseline except chronic-health-condition data, which gives useful timing context for the separate SJLIFE grip exploration without proving an exact date match.

In the separately selected SJLIFE cohort (2018 data freeze; 5,053 listed, including 2,288 also in CCSS), the portal lists clinically measured right-hand grip strength, first trial: 4,557 nonmissing measurements. Its interactive linear-regression setup shows **3,554 records** with that grip measure and the European-ancestry-computed PGS001932 insomnia score together. This is a real score–phenotype intersection, not the 4,083-person CCSS score count above, and grip strength alone is not FFS or FI. The portal initially binned the score; no regression was run. Before an exploratory association, confirm score provenance, continuous encoding, age/sex and ancestry covariate availability, relatedness and sample overlap; avoid treating the analysis as prespecified or validation of our different scores. The portal cohort labels and component counts should not be added because the two cohorts overlap.

The [PGS Catalog record](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS001932/) describes PGS001932 as a 37,712-variant penalized-regression insomnia score trained in 391,124 European-ancestry UK Biobank participants (Privé et al., 2022). This is external to the project's selected-marker prototypes, and UK Biobank is shared with several project discovery inputs. Whether individual SJLIFE participants or relatives overlap any score development data remains unverified. In the live SJLIFE data dictionary, searching `PC1` revealed cohort-specific European PC1 and PC10 entries, and the unfiltered EUR SJLIFE PC1 chart displayed 3,554 records. That count coincidentally equals the score–grip intersection count but does **not** prove they are the same people; check the combined complete-case sample and intended ancestry subset in the actual model. A search for the longer phrase `principal component` returned no match even though `PC1` did, illustrating why a single empty search should not be treated as absence. Remaining PCs, age and sex, and the final model have not yet been verified.

Subsequent portal work confirmed that PGS001932 can be changed from its default discrete bins to a continuous predictor (select the variable, Edit, Continuous, Apply); the setup retained 3,554 score–grip records. Searches for `age at assessment`, `age at evaluation`, `age at visit` and `current age` did not yield a matching age field in the visible dictionary search, so no age-adjusted model has yet been fitted. These unsuccessful search terms are not proof that age is unavailable. The unfinished portal session is retained for further inspection. The score's unit is the portal's original scale, not automatically one standard deviation.

Update: browsing the Demographic Variables → Age (years) tree revealed `Age (years) at Baseline Assessment`, which did not appear in those text searches. A model with sex, this age variable and one ancestry PC, followed by a two-PC sensitivity, is documented in the [exploration report](sjlife_insomnia_pgs_grip_exploration.md). Whether the baseline-age and grip measurements correspond to the same visit remains unverified.

The open SJLIFE SNP browser also returned A/G genotype charts for two markers shared by both project prototypes, rs12536335 and rs4886140. Both charts total 4,481 observations, but this is not evidence of complete score–outcome overlap or validated multiallelic handling. [Exact screen and caveats](sjlife_project_variant_portal_screen.md).
