# SOF: older-women sleep, frailty and genetics lead

Checked 29 October 2026. The Study of Osteoporotic Fractures (SOF) provides a potentially complementary cohort to the male-only MrOS study. This is a controlled-access lead, not completed score validation.

* [dbGaP phs000510.v1.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000510.v1.p1) describes QC-cleaned PLINK data and GWAS data on 3,625 Caucasian participants. The cohort originally enrolled older women with substantial racial exclusion; findings would not represent all older women. The [public configuration report](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000510/phs000510.v1.p1/manifest/Study_Report.phs000510.OsteoporoticFractures_SOF.v1.p1.MULTI.pdf) lists 3,625 participants in consent group c1, with disease-related aging restrictions that include sleep and decline of physical function. Actual project eligibility must be adjudicated; the report count is not the count with all required outcomes or variants.
* [Evans et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3571755/) report 1,407 SOF women with actigraphy and genotype data in a circadian-gene analysis. The genotyped sleep sample is not proven to overlap the phenotype or required consent/variant set for our analysis.
* [Miner et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10089942/) studied 3,044 SOF women with usable self-reported sleep and actigraphy and assessed a three-component SOF physical frailty measure. The insomnia-or-poor-sleep definition includes sleep medication as well as symptoms and is combined with actigraphy-derived short sleep in the paper. Its SOF frailty measure is **not** the five-component Fried score or a cumulative-deficit FI. These published sleep and genotype sample counts cannot simply be intersected without participant-level linkage.
* [NCBI guidance](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/ncbi/workshops/2023-05_genetic-disorder-mechanisms/supplemental-material/Flyer_Pharmocogenetics_CaseStudy.pdf) describes dbGaP data as no-cost but controlled; [NIH access rules](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/accessing-data/dbgap) require an eligible investigator and institutional sign-off. Check release-specific phenotype files, authorized joins, DUC restrictions, project fees and computing costs. NSRR and other repositories may have different terms.

The [CHARGE gait meta-analysis](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58468961/2J9vAuiscdt8bWsNb.pdf) previously inspected by the project included SOF. This is cohort-level overlap in prior follow-up, not proof that score selection used SOF. Audit score decisions, source studies, individual and relative overlap, and exact measurement timing before describing any SOF result as held-out validation. Confirm 15/22-marker contrast and imputation quality and the number of participants with sleep, frailty and genotype together. A three-item SOF measure could serve as a separate physical-frailty endpoint, but it cannot replace the requested paired FI-versus-FFS comparison.

## Public release metadata check

The [2020 dbGaP phenotype dictionary](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000510/phs000510.v1.p1/pheno_variable_summaries/phs000510.v1.pht010697.v1.SOF_Subject_Phenotypes.data_dict.xml) contains 110 listed variables, including visit-8 sleep efficiency (`V8SEFFMP`), sleep latency (`V8SLATMP`), chair-stand inability (`RSTNDARM`), grip (`RGRIPAVG`) and walking speed (`RWLKSPED`), plus harmonized SOF/MrOS variables. The [public summary report](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000510/phs000510.v1.p1/pheno_variable_summaries/phs000510.v1.pht010697.v1.p1.SOF_Subject_Phenotypes.var_report.xml) lists 1,523 observations for each of the two sleep measures, 3,624 for grip and 3,623 for walking. It reports zero nulls for those variables despite a 3,625-subject total, so the reason for the different denominators and actual overlap cannot be inferred from these summaries. The dictionary does not name an explicitly labeled frailty variable, energy variable or insomnia variable in the checked descriptions; this does not rule out aliases, components, other releases or measurements. Ascertain visits, coding, joined sample size and permitted data access before using them. These are aggregate metadata, not individual observations or score validation.

**Metadata discrepancy:** The dictionary and matching total-sample report disagree materially on the numeric ranges of several variables. For example, `V8SEFFMP` is labeled sleep efficiency in both, but the dictionary's logical maximum is 77.60 while the summary report gives median 86.38 and maximum 98.6. `V8SLATMP` is labeled sleep latency, with dictionary range 24.44–97.12 and report range 1.333–373 minutes. A revised scan found **nine variables** whose reported extrema are outside dictionary limits even allowing half a unit of the report's last displayed digit; see [this table](sof_dbgap_dictionary_range_discrepancies.csv). The earlier count of 12 used a fixed 0.1-unit tolerance and included three differences consistent with rounding; it has been corrected. Several `V8` dictionary bounds closely resemble the summary ranges of *other* `V8` variables, including `V8SMINMP` bounds resembling the report range for `V8SLATMP`. This is a metadata pattern, not proof of how it arose or that underlying individual columns are swapped. Do not discard observations based on these logical bounds or swap columns without source verification; confirm with the study documentation/data custodian after authorized access. The exact visit for `RGRIPAVG`, `RWLKSPED` and `RSTNDARM` remains unverified, so same-visit overlap with `V8` sleep measurements cannot be claimed.
