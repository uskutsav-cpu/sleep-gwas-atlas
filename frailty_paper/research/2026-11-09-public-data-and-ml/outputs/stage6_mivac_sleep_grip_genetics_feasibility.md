# MIVAC: sleep, physical function and genetic-sample lead

29 October 2026. The [MIVAC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13424306/) is a longitudinal sub-study of the NKI Rockland Sample with 348 baseline participants aged 38–71 and 219 completing three annual timepoints. The published protocol describes sleep/activity assessments, grip strength and a walking task, as well as blood samples. The [end-user protocol](https://rocklandsample.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/MIVAC-Full-End-User-Protocol.pdf) says additional genetic samples were sent to the NIMH Genetics Repository. The [Rockland Sample I description](https://rocklandsample.org/for-researchers/rockland-sample-i-2011-2022) describes genetic information in its broader 1,500-person resource. These are encouraging leads for linkage, not evidence that genomic calls and the full project outcomes are presently downloadable together.

The published study distinguishes its open Lite imaging release, with limited demographic information, from detailed phenotype data available to approved users under a data-use agreement. The published data-availability statement provides LORIS instructions for the latter and an open S3 location for images. The exact fee, approval and eligibility requirements for detailed phenotypes, genomic dataset accession, actual genomic assay and data release, ability to link genotype IDs to MIVAC visits, participant-level outcome completeness and our 33 candidate variants are not established. Collection and deposition of a biological sample do not establish that genome-wide genotyping has been performed or released.

MIVAC has a modest sample, includes middle-aged adults, and the article does not identify a ready-made FI or Fried frailty score. Grip and walking are relevant physical measures, not a frailty diagnosis; sleep quality and actigraphy need not equal insomnia. A credible score evaluation also needs case counts, age distribution, source-cohort and relative nonoverlap, exact allele representation, ancestry and uncertainty. Even a fully linked 348-person sample would have limited statistical power and cannot automatically validate clinical performance.

**Next check:** identify the NIMH Genetics Repository accession and a public file manifest or access documentation for this specific sub-study, then assess free-access status and candidate dosage coverage. Review approved LORIS terms before using phenotypic files. No participant data were downloaded, no application was made, and no score or association was computed here.

The [paper's XML](../work/mivac_paper.xml) is a public article copy retrieved through Europe PMC; it is not participant data. Relevant text identifies the study-wide physical measures and genetics repository. The published release explicitly distinguishes no-agreement Lite access from detailed phenotype access, so the paper's broad statement that data are open should not be read as proof that all genomic and phenotype data are anonymously available.

## Public measurement-dictionary and access check

The paper's assessment table lists the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), grip strength and Walking While Talking at baseline and follow-up. The publicly downloadable [Rockland data dictionaries](https://rocklandsample.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Rockland-Sample-LORIS-data-dictionaries.zip) provide item labels: `psqi_05a` asks about inability to fall asleep within 30 minutes; `psqi_05b` asks about waking during the night or early morning; `psqi_02` records sleep latency. `grp_02` and `grp_03` are left and right hand average strength in kilograms; `wwt_walktime1` and `wwt_walktime2` describe single-task walking times. The study-wide dictionary establishes questionnaire definitions, not actual nonmissing MIVAC records, a clinical insomnia diagnosis, a directly measured habitual gait-speed value or complete Fried frailty classification. Its walking-task protocol and scoring require review before comparing it to FFS.

The provider's [access instructions](https://rocklandsample.org/for-researchers/step-2-data-usage-agreement) specify that the full phenotype DUA requires signature and notarization by an institutional representative; the [phenotype page](https://rocklandsample.org/phenotypic-data) says an account request follows the DUA. Their link labeled as genetic linking information currently resolves to a biosample-ordering page that returned 404 when checked, not a documented downloadable genotype accession. The [published paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13424306/) describes whole blood and cell-line samples as available on request. This does not establish that the corresponding SNP genotypes exist or are no-cost. Individual-level score validation remains unavailable here; we did not request access or download study data.
