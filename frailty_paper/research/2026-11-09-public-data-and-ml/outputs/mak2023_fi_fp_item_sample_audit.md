# Mak FI and physical-frailty measures: items and source-sample audit

The new Mak FI and modified physical-frailty GWAS represent related, but not identical, constructs. The study's Supplementary Table 4 lists **insomnia and fatigue as FI items**. Supplementary Table 5 defines physical-frailty exhaustion using a self-reported tiredness or low-energy question over the preceding two weeks; responses of more than half the days or nearly every day receive one point. The source table labels the FI item “fatigue” without providing its full question or coding in that table, so exact identity of the FI and FP fields is not established by these cells alone. Both constructs have sleep/fatigue-related content, and the FI directly includes insomnia.

The Mak physical score counts five domains: weight loss, exhaustion, slowness, low activity and weakness. Weight loss and slowness are self-reported; weakness is based on grip strength with sex- and BMI-specific cutoffs. Its supplement says participants missing any physical-phenotype component were excluded. The FI is the proportion of 49 items present among nonmissing items, excluding participants with more than 20% missing items. These differing rules make complete-case assumptions important even though the released FI and physical-phenotype files each report the same nominal 403,041 participants. The original Ye FFS also uses five Fried domains, but its precise original field and threshold crosswalk remains unverified; do not assume every Mak cutoff matches Ye.

**A source-table issue requires clarification before reconstructing the Mak physical score:** Supplementary Table 5 prints the final men's grip-strength row as BMI >28, “≤32 kg=1; <32 kg=0.” Those ranges overlap. It may be a transcription error, but the correct zero category cannot be established from this table alone and should not be silently changed. The published summary statistics used here are not affected by our inability to reconstruct the phenotype, but the exact scoring in the source pipeline needs verification. The table's exhaustion and missingness wording is internally clear. A later [Hanlon-source comparison](mak_hanlon_physical_frailty_crosswalk.md) supports the ≤32 kg positive threshold and suggests the logical complement, but cannot verify the actual Mak code.

## Source file sample-field check

The checksum-verified Mak FI and FP files each contain **8,983,921 rows**. In a full row-by-row comparison, chromosome, variant ID, position, A1 and A2 fields were identical, as were the per-variant N text and A1-frequency text in every row. This provides stronger consistency evidence for a shared underlying genotype panel than the equal nominal total alone. It does **not** verify individual IDs, prove exactly the same people were analyzed for both outcomes, or establish how missing phenotypes were treated. Genotype frequencies can be rounded or derived from a common reference, and source processing is unknown. Interpretation of the paired LDSC results remains conditional on source documentation, model and participant overlap.
