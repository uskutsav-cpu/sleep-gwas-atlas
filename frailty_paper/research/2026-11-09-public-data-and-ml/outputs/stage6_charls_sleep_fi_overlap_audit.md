# CHARLS sleep–frailty literature: hidden sleep overlap in a depression item

[Tang et al. (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40019149/) examined sleep duration and frailty longitudinally in CHARLS, with 15,333 participants in the baseline sample. Their [Supplementary Table S2](https://jogh.org/documents/2025/jogh-15-04059-s001.pdf) lists a deficit based on whether the CESD-10 depression score exceeds 10, along with chronic illness, daily-function and cognitive items. Sleep duration is the exposure, not a named individual FI item in that list.

The [Harmonized CHARLS documentation](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/harmonized_charls/application/Harmonized_CHARLS_D.pdf) describes its 10-item CES-D, including questions about restless sleep and everything requiring effort. Therefore, **if the published study used the standard CHARLS CESD-10 without removing items**, its single depression deficit indirectly includes sleep and effort symptoms. This is an inference from the supplement and CHARLS documentation; the study's executed scoring code and item exclusions have not been checked. The duration question and restless-sleep question are different measures, so this is not the exact same-question arithmetic overlap illustrated in our NHANES analysis. It does not invalidate the paper's findings or establish the size of any bias.

The study reports that, compared with seven to nine hours of sleep, shorter sleep was associated with subsequent frailty (for <6 hours, HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.34–1.55). These are observational, non-genetic associations; confounding and sample selection remain possible. CHARLS data and this paper do not independently validate our genetic scores. The newer 2026 CHARLS paper uses a different 44-item FI; do **not** assume this 2025 component list applies to it.

For a proposed CHARLS genetic validation, request the item-level construction code and score definitions. Prespecify a primary FI that omits the composite depression item or reconstructs it without the sleep/effort items where justified, alongside the published FI as a sensitivity analysis. Removing the entire depression deficit changes more than sleep content; recalibrated scoring or thresholds would need validation. Compare matched individuals and models and avoid attributing any difference solely to sleep. Genetic data remain controlled access ([access review](stage6_charls_genetic_access_2026.md)).

The source PDF was retrieved from the Europe PMC supplementary archive for PMC11869517 and inspected using `pdftotext -layout`; [publisher supplement](https://jogh.org/documents/2025/jogh-15-04059-s001.pdf) is the original source. No participant data were accessed.
