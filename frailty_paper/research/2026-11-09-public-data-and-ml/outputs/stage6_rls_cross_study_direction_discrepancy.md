# Conflicting restless-legs associations at rs78206187

A focused follow-up changes the interpretation of the [earlier RLS screen](stage6_rls2024_candidate_followup_review.md). In that Akçimen et al. meta-analysis, the harmonized summary file reported **negative** rs78206187 G-allele beta. Two separately released discovery-cohort GWAS from [Schormair et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01763-1) instead report **positive** G-allele beta. Therefore the earlier result should not be treated as an established cross-study direction or proof of a biologically opposing sleep–RLS effect.

| Public RLS source | Cohort and sample | rs78206187 G beta (SE) | p | rs113851554 T beta (SE) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| [GCST90435387](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST90435387), Akçimen | All of Us, CLSA and CARTaGENE meta-analysis; 9,851 cases, 38,957 controls | −0.1584 (0.0350) | 6.19 × 10⁻⁶ | +0.5044 (0.0319) |
| [GCST90399568](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST90399568), Schormair | EU-RLS-GENE; 7,248 cases, 19,802 controls | +0.5353 (0.0497) | 4.54 × 10⁻²⁷ | +1.3116 (0.0442) |
| [GCST90399571](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/studies/GCST90399571), Schormair | INTERVAL; 3,491 cases, 23,741 controls | +0.2597 (0.0563) | 3.93 × 10⁻⁶ | +0.6111 (0.0601) |

All entries matched GRCh38 coordinate, source rsID and G/A pair, with the source's harmonized file reporting beta for G. The source reports rs78206187 G frequency ~0.051, 0.059 and 0.059, respectively. The Schormair EU-RLS-GENE row has GWAS Catalog harmonization code 11, which its [pipeline log](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90399001-GCST90400000/GCST90399568/harmonised/GCST90399568.running.log) describes as an orientation flip; the INTERVAL and Akçimen rows have code 10. Harmonization is not a guarantee that the original input coding, phenotype or meta-analysis is correct. We did not verify the complete original unharmonized files or each contributor's allele semantics. The discordance could arise from data or harmonization differences, phenotype ascertainment, selection or other heterogeneity; we cannot determine the cause here. Case/control proportions and study designs differ. Do not pool the estimates, pick a preferred sign, or infer direction differences are biological without a source-level audit and overlap assessment.

The studies are separately released but their participant and close-relative overlap with each other and with all project discovery data has not been established. Both candidate variants and RLS were selected after examining related findings; these are targeted lookups, not an independent prospective hypothesis test. The consistency of the rs113851554 sign is descriptive, not validation of the whole score. Neither source measures FI or FFS in these summary rows, and none evaluates risk discrimination or calibration for our scores.

[Exact four-row lookup](stage6_rls_schormair_two_variants.csv) and [reproducibility receipt](stage6_rls_schormair_two_variants_receipt.json); run `python3 work/rls_schormair/query_two.py`. The GWAS Catalog identifies the two Schormair files as CC0. The query uses indexed remote ranges and does not verify full-file checksums. The next necessary check is source-specific original allele coding and cohort-level estimates, followed by a formally harmonized and appropriately independent analysis; until then treat the direction of rs78206187's RLS association as **unresolved across sources**.

## Original-file allele check

A bounded lookup of the original unharmonized public files found both exact target rows in each of the three sources. The [Akçimen original file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90435001-GCST90436000/GCST90435387/GCST90435387.tsv) directly reports rs78206187 at GRCh38 chr2:66795102, effect allele G, other allele A, beta −0.1584. The [EU-RLS-GENE original file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90399001-GCST90400000/GCST90399568/GCST90399568.tsv) reports the GRCh37 counterpart chr2:67022234, effect allele A, other allele G, beta −0.5353; changing the effect allele to G reverses that to +0.5353. The [INTERVAL original file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90399001-GCST90400000/GCST90399571/GCST90399571.tsv) directly reports G/A beta +0.2597. Original and harmonized rs113851554 rows also agree on the positive T-allele sign in all three.

This verifies that the **reported direction disagreement was already present in the original public summary files**, after the stated allele conversion. It rules out a sign change introduced solely by the GWAS Catalog harmonization step for these rows; it does not independently audit the underlying association models, cohort-level coding, imputation or source data. Nor does it establish biological heterogeneity, independence of samples, or a correct common effect. Raw-file content was retrieved in bounded byte ranges and its full-file MD5 was not checked. The targeted records, probe traces and range hashes are in [Akçimen lookup](stage6_rls2024_unharmonized_bounded_lookup.json) and [Schormair lookups](stage6_rls_schormair_original_allele_lookup.json); scripts are `work/rls2024/source_bounded_lookup.py` and `work/rls_schormair/unharmonized_bounded_lookup.py`. Both use observed file ordering to find an exact row and stop with an error if that row cannot be uniquely located. They do not establish global sortedness, complete variant coverage or negative results for absent rows.

A later [current All of Us RLS browser check](stage6_aou_rls_cohort_direction_check.md) found a positive rs78206187 G association, conditional on the documented ALT-effect assumption. The earlier meta-analysis used an older All of Us component, but exact participants, case definitions and releases differ or are unverified. This finding does not identify the cause of the negative meta-analysis sign and should not be counted as an independent replication of the meta-analysis.

## Component availability and meta-analysis methods

The [Akçimen paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.27040) describes four component analyses (All of Us sequencing, All of Us genotyping, CLSA and CARTaGENE), says it used inverse-variance meta-analysis, and reports restricting the final meta-analysis to variants present in all datasets. Thus, assuming the public result corresponds to the described analysis, the reported rs78206187 meta-analysis row was not intentionally based on only a single component; that does **not** disclose the component-specific alleles, direction, effect estimates, precision, or phenotype overlap. The separate current All of Us browser result still cannot stand in for the earlier sequencing or genotyping component.

A [GWAS Catalog v2 publication query](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/rest/api/v2/studies?pubmed_id=39078117&size=100) returned one study accession, GCST90435387, rather than separate component accessions. This narrow Catalog lookup cannot rule out component results hosted elsewhere or unpublished access routes. The paper lists supplementary workbooks, but we did not retrieve and verify them in this check; do not infer that they contain or lack the candidate's component estimates. [Source and query hashes](stage6_rls2024_component_availability_receipt.json). The cause of the direction conflict remains unresolved.

A [FinnGen R13 RLS check](stage6_finngen_r13_rls_direction_review.md) also reports a positive rs78206187 G effect. Its register-defined phenotype and Finnish population differ from the other sources, and Finnish participant overlap with the EU-RLS-GENE study is unresolved. This does not explain the negative Akçimen meta-analysis or establish that any source is incorrect.

A [regional audit](stage6_rls_region_source_concordance_review.md) found 12 opposite-sign variants among 42 exact, non-palindromic SNVs passing genome-wide significance in both the Akçimen and FinnGen RLS files within a postselected chromosome 2 interval. These variants are correlated, and the comparison cannot determine the origin of discordance or establish independent evidence.
