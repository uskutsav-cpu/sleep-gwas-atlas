# LIFE-Adult Leipzig: sleep and genotype lead, not an open validation cohort

Checked 29 October 2026. LIFE-Adult in Leipzig is **not** the US Lifestyle Interventions and Independence for Elders trial, which also uses the acronym LIFE. The [Leipzig cohort profile](https://doi.org/10.1093/IJE/DYAC114) and [LIFE-Adult overview](https://www.health-atlas.de/projects/5) describe a population-based cohort of around 10,000 adults with extensive genomic and phenotypic measurements; participants aged over 60 were invited for deeper assessments. An [actigraphic sleep GWAS](https://openalex.org/W2344375774) reports data from 956 middle-aged and older LIFE-Adult participants, documenting an overlap of objective sleep traits and genotypes in that study. Actigraphic sleep traits are not necessarily insomnia, FI or Fried frailty, and the 956 is not a count with all three project outcomes or score variants.

The cohort profile says access to individual data requires a detailed proposal and approval by its use-and-access committee; [an independent 2025 analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-90703-y.pdf) says individual LIFE-Adult data are not publicly shared and require a project agreement. The Health Atlas lists public summary-level resources, but that does not establish public participant genotype–outcome data or support calculating our scores. A no-fee route, institutional conditions, specific insomnia and FI/FFS instruments, relevant participant overlap, variant coverage and discovery independence remain unverified. No contact, application, participant-data access or score validation occurred. Given these unresolved conditions and small published actigraphy-genotype subset, WHI and other routes with documented physical-frailty assessments remain more concrete.


## Sleep and genetics beyond the actigraphy subset

The [original study protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4509697/) says sleep questionnaires, including the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and Epworth Sleepiness Scale, were administered to all participants; fatigue and morningness–eveningness questionnaires were also planned. The [2022 cohort profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9908058/) reports genome-wide array genotyping for 7,669 people at baseline. These are **separate descriptions**, not a count of participants with valid sleep, all score variants and frailty measures, and the PSQI is sleep quality rather than a clinical insomnia diagnosis. A completed FI or Fried phenotype was not established in the sources inspected.

The original [actigraphy GWAS abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27126917/) says it tested 14 actigraphic/rest phenotypes and 7,141,204 post-imputation/QC variants among 956 participants; its reported single-trait genome-wide threshold was not corrected for the multiple sleep traits. In this search no full openly downloadable cohort-specific result file or participant data for this GWAS was identified. Therefore the selected 33 variants have not been tested here in LIFE-Adult, and the abstract's highlighted loci are not evidence for or against our scores. Investigate specific outcome definitions, access fees and genotype–sleep–frailty overlap before prioritizing a request.

## GWAS Catalog availability check

On 29 October 2026, the [current GWAS Catalog API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/rest/api/v2/studies?pubmed_id=27126917&size=100) returned two studies from this paper: GCST003518 (daytime rest, discovery n = 874) and GCST003542 (nighttime rest, discovery n = 941). Both explicitly report `full_summary_stats_available: false` and no replication sample. These are trait-specific discovery sample sizes, distinct from the 956-person overall actigraphy sample in the paper. The [captured API response](stage6_life_spada_gwas_catalog_v2_studies.json) documents the lookup. Catalog metadata alone does not rule out a file hosted elsewhere, but its curated associations cannot provide association estimates for every score marker. This source cannot calculate or validate either individual-level score, and no association-based replication was performed.

The separate v2 association-list request timed out after 35 seconds without receiving data; curated association overlap with our markers was therefore not assessed in this lookup.
