# TOPMed handgrip GWAS: public-data feasibility

[Sarnowski et al. (2021)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253611) used whole-genome sequence data from multiple US cohorts to study handgrip strength in a multi-ancestry sample. It could offer a useful external **component-phenotype** comparison: grip strength is relevant to physical frailty, but it is not an FI, Fried frailty score, insomnia measure or predictive test of the project's scores. Cohort overlap with every discovery, selection and molecular study has not been established.

The article's data-availability statement says its full association summary statistics were to be released through dbGaP accession phs001974 under controlled access, and individual-level data require approvals. This statement does not establish an immediately downloadable free public whole-GWAS file; nor does it prove no alternative public copy exists. No application was made.

I downloaded the article's public [S1 supplementary document](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253611.s001&type=supplementary) and [S4 workbook](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253611.s004&type=supplementary). A literal rsID scan of S1 paragraphs and all 13 tables found 42 unique rsIDs; scanning both S4 sheets found 27. None exactly matched the 22 rsIDs in the current project candidate table. S4 tables contain a selected set of skeletal-muscle eQTL/meQTL results filtered at p<1e-6 for selected grip variants, and S1 reports selected results and context. **Absence from these selected materials is not an association failure, zero effect, or proof that a variant was not tested.** This scan does not examine proxy variants, alternate IDs, all genome-wide results or genomic coordinates. It cannot validate a genetic score or establish lack of genetic sharing.

Reproducibility: source files `work/topmed_grip_s1.docx` and `work/topmed_grip_s4.xlsx` have SHA-256 `6116b366b16f5c2b42c7a034316de18447c5890127d8df6a941377c62b6991a0` and `3a2e86235ef32e6207156ffa80751a4ae1f8d5d4295df1d335e20f2621910d44`; the candidate CSV has SHA-256 `d500617af1a76f8d7408c26a2b4ba277a3f736335aab7efd3f364459872fc874`. Hashes identify retrieved copies, not publisher-supplied checksums. Public unselected signed full summary statistics, exact allele harmonization, variant coverage, and independent cohort membership would be needed for a defensible candidate-level comparison; linked genotypes and appropriate outcomes would still be needed for score performance.
