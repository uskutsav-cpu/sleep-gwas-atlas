# HUNT4 FI–Fried comparison: sleep overlap and correction

Checked 29 October 2026. The HUNT4 70+ study reported FI and Fried phenotype analyses on the same parent sample, not a genetic-score validation. The [main article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40520-024-02839-y) reports 9,956 participants, 9,318 with enough information for the 35-item FI and 9,330 with enough for the Fried analysis. These are not necessarily the same people, and neither is a genotype–sleep–frailty complete-case count.

## Phenotype overlap

[Supplementary Tables 3–4](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs40520-024-02839-y/MediaObjects/40520_2024_2839_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) show that the 35-item FI explicitly includes **insomnia** and **current exhaustion/tiredness** as separate deficits. For community-dwelling participants, insomnia is coded positive when at least one of three sleep problems—difficulty falling asleep, repeated waking, or early waking—is reported at least three times a week; nursing-home staff report sleep/night behavior using a different item. The Fried phenotype includes an exhaustion/tiredness component; the supplement describes low energy/exhaustion questions for HUNT participants, with different instruments from the original Fried protocol.

Consequently, the FI and a sleep-trouble exposure may share questionnaire information directly, and FI and Fried measures also share fatigue-related content. A stronger FI-versus-Fried comparison would use the same eligible participants, prespecify an FI excluding insomnia and potentially an additional exhaustion-excluded sensitivity, recalculate the observed-deficit denominator and missingness criterion, and separately analyze the unchanged five-component Fried phenotype. Thresholds for a modified FI must be specified and its performance assessed; they cannot automatically be assumed to retain the standard index's meaning. A four-component exhaustion-excluded count is a sensitivity measure, not the original Fried phenotype. Different ascertainment for nursing-home residents and missingness need explicit handling. None of these changes retrospectively quantifies the effect of overlap on the published GWAS genetic correlation.

## Source correction

The authors' [2025 correction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40520-025-03165-7) reports that a data-provider merging error overestimated grip strength for about 2,800 people by 1–9 kg. After correction, reported Fried frailty prevalence changed from 10.6% to 11.7%, and FI prevalence from 35.8% to 36.0%. Use the corrected results and confirm the corrected grip data version if a future access request is approved. These are published phenotype prevalence estimates, not model calibration or genetic-score performance.

HUNT individual-level access has published fees and requires approval; the sleep–FI–Fried–genotype intersection, project variant coverage and sample independence are not established. No restricted records were acquired. The public supplement and extracted text are retained in `work/hunt_frailty_supp.pdf` and `work/hunt_frailty_supp.txt`.

## Cross-tabulation quality check

The original and [correction supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs40520-025-03165-7/MediaObjects/40520_2025_3165_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) both print the same Supplementary Table 6. Its Fried-by-FI category rows sum to the reported total of 8,999, but the printed FI-robust column total is 3,085 while its three displayed cells (2,554 + 520 + 4) sum to **3,078**. The three printed column totals sum to 9,006, not the printed grand total 8,999. We confirmed the numbers against the rendered source, not only PDF text extraction. The published correction replaced the supplement but the displayed cross-tab is unchanged; the source does not establish whether these cells were recomputed after the grip correction or which entry is wrong.

Do not report an exact agreement percentage from this table as a corrected result without clarification or independent recomputation. The displayed off-diagonal counts nevertheless illustrate that the two classification systems can give different categories, subject to these source uncertainties. This discrepancy is separate from the known grip-strength correction, and should not be attributed to a particular cause without evidence. The corrected public supplement is stored at `work/hunt_frailty_correction_supp.pdf`; the rendered original page used for visual checking is `work/hunt_supp_table6.png`. No participant data or genetics were accessed.
