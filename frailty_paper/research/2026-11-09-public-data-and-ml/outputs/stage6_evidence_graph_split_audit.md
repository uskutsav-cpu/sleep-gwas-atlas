# Evidence-graph split feasibility

The current selected-candidate graph contains 33 variants on 15 chromosomes. A descriptive single-linkage grouping joins consecutive variants on the same chromosome when they are at most 1 Mb apart; this gives 25 groups, seven containing more than one variant. For example, the three FOXP2-region candidates on chromosome 7 fall in the same group. These are proximity groups, **not measured LD blocks**, and 1 Mb is not a guarantee of genetic independence. The graph uses inferred GRCh37 coordinates that require verification before a definitive split.

All 15 chromosomes connect to the FI, FFS, UKB insomnia, MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital-frailty study nodes through marginal-association edges. Nine connect to FinnGen insomnia. Sharing a study node across chromosomes is not by itself proof of outcome-label leakage: the risk depends on the task, information available at prediction time, and the model. But a random edge split or transductive graph embedding can expose held-out variants' association edges or their study and neighborhood information during training. Chromosome separation alone also does not remove overlap among GWAS participants or fix selection of variants using the target association.

For an actual benchmark, first obtain independently ascertained labels and an eligible tested universe; prespecify the prediction task and permissible input data. Split at chromosome or appropriately defined LD-region level before fitting any preprocessing, feature selection, model or hyperparameter choice. Remove held-out outcome edges and derivatives from the training graph, and state explicitly whether held-out variant features and shared study or gene nodes are visible to the model. Compare models with the same allowed information, including simple position/nearest-gene and association baselines. Use an external experiment and independent cohorts where available, and report coverage and the source of negative labels; unmeasured edges are not negatives. Even a clean molecular-label benchmark would not validate patient-level genetic scores.

The present audit measures feasibility and possible information paths only. It performs no model fit, no performance evaluation and no independent validation. Reproduce with `python3 work/stage6_graph_split_audit.py`; [machine-readable output](stage6_graph_split_audit.json) includes per-chromosome counts, proximity groups, shared targets and input hashes.

## Practical consequence for the new neuronal assay

The [Salomon reporter scan](stage6_salomon_neuronal_reporter_feasibility.md) found no exact coordinate-and-allele match between its processed tested table and the 33 distinct project candidate contrasts. Therefore that assay's 6,573-pair sequence-baseline experiment is **not** an external test of the current 33-node candidate graph, even though it tests a related sequence model. Filling in graph labels for the absent variants as negatives or transferring labels from merely nearby variants would create false targets. A genuine graph benchmark requires either an independently labeled broader tested universe with compatible non-outcome graph features or a separate assay directly testing the graph candidates. Obtain and evaluate complete tested variants, establish genome build and allele correspondence, group by LD/region, check that donor/sequence and selection overlap do not leak information, and apply the same held-out splits to non-embedding baselines. The present postselected graph alone cannot supply unbiased negatives or an independent clinical outcome.
