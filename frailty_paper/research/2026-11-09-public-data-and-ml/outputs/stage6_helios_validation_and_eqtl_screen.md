# HELIOS Singapore: restricted cohort and public blood eQTL follow-up

The multi-ethnic [Health for Life in Singapore study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12764833/) describes genome-wide sequencing and extensive clinical and questionnaire phenotyping in adults aged 30–84. A [separate sleep study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11999866/) used the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index; sleep quality is not automatically an insomnia diagnosis. The official [participant consent form](https://www.healthforlife.sg/PDF/bookingpdf/ICF_English_Consent_V5.5.pdf) refers to questions on frailty and activities of daily living. These sources do not establish a completed, reproducible FI or Fried phenotype, contemporaneous measures, a linked sleep–frailty–genotype sample, or project-marker quality. The cohort paper describes a 10,000-sample merged WGS file, not 10,000 complete cases with all outcomes. Its data statement says individual participant data require a restricted-access request; no fee, eligibility, approvals, infrastructure requirements or overlap with project GWAS sources have been established. No participant records were accessed or request submitted.

The linked **PRECISE-SG100K** resource is a related but distinct access route: [current access principles, updated 16 October 2026](https://www.npm.sg/our-data-users/data-access/data-access-to-precise-sg100k/), report completion of sequencing for 100,000 recruited participants and say routine new proposals are not actively being accepted after two Singapore public-researcher calls. During 2024–26, proposals involving overseas collaborators are described as case-by-case and expected to demonstrate tangible benefit to Singapore. The page anticipates broader access for Singapore public academic and clinical researchers from 2027, with overseas access contingent on a Singapore-led application; this is a stated future plan, not current open access or a guarantee. Extra review applies to linked electronic health records. Whether this project is eligible, would have no fees, and could obtain its required phenotypes and exact genotypes remains unknown. These PRECISE conditions must not automatically be imposed on the separate HELIOS data-access route, which has its own committee and terms.

As a public molecular follow-up, we downloaded the study's [HELIOS-ciseQTLs-novel.txt.gz](https://github.com/HELIOS-SG100K-LKC/HELIOS-Study-design-and-baseline-characteristics/tree/7a518903d345645eeaa6180047cd85ba68862cea/Data-Blobs), a **selected whole-blood cis-eQTL file** from the authors' public repository. We scanned all **388,582 rows covering 76 gene identifiers** against the 33 distinct rsIDs in the project score lists. There were **zero exact-ID hits**; a supplementary check for either provisional GRCh37 or GRCh38 coordinate also returned no rows. The source's actual build and allele conventions were not independently authenticated, so the coordinate screen is only a sensitivity check.

This file covers source-selected genes rather than the whole transcriptome or all variants, and includes association rows across those genes even when an individual row has a large p-value. Missing markers may lie outside the selected gene regions, use another representation, be unavailable or fail source criteria; **absence is not evidence of no eQTL effect**. Whole blood is not a single-cell or brain-specific assay. This scan cannot establish colocalization, causal genes, shared sleep–frailty biology or genetic-score prediction. Data-use terms and provenance must be checked before combining these results with other analyses.

The 76 source gene IDs also have no exact Ensembl-ID overlap with the 166 distinct genes in the existing [project FUMA expression inventory](stage4_fuma_candidate_gene_expression.csv). This is a comparison of two selected gene lists, not a gene-level association test: it does not imply that HELIOS measured no relevant genes elsewhere or that any of the 166 genes lacks a blood eQTL. No functional or disease conclusion follows from list non-overlap. The updated receipt records the inventory hash and exact-ID rule.
