# Insomnia–frailty scores: validation evidence and outstanding work

**New CLSA public follow-up, 6 November 2026:** The reopened browser allowed an exact GRCh38 coordinate, allele-pair and rsID screen of all 33 distinct prototype candidates against average grip, maximum grip and four-metre walking time (99 combinations). Five source p values were below 0.05, but none survived Holm correction across the 99-test family. These rounded marginal statistics concern physical components, not FI, FFS, a combined outcome or score performance. Participant/relative overlap with all discovery/selection sources remains unverified; no claim of independent score validation is made. See [results and caveats](clsa_public_followup_20261106/README.md).

**HPP public PRS matrix, 29 October 2026:** Five insomnia/sleep-score columns contain only corrected values of 1 across 729 clinical phenotype rows. This aggregate file does not establish cell-wise testing, effects or a lack of association and does not test the project's scores; see [the matrix review](stage6_hpp_prs_matrix_review.md).

**Public HPP sleep files, 29 October 2026:** The dashboard source repository contains public sleep-latency and total-sleep-time association files omitted from its current visible domain menu. Both appear to be selected subsets, and neither contains exact rsIDs for the project markers; absence is not a null finding. They cannot evaluate complete scores. See [the bounded file check](stage6_hpp_public_sleep_subset_review.md).

**HPP sleep follow-up, 29 October 2026:** Published HPP work linked genetic scores to sleep measures including trouble falling asleep; an EGA record describes an imputed genotype dataset. Access is controlled and costs, our marker coverage and FI/FFS availability are unverified. Its competitive academic program advertises an application window ending 31 December 2026 but contains conflicting onboarding dates; see the [HPP feasibility review](stage6_hpp_insomnia_score_validation_lead.md).

**General-frailty comparator, 29 October 2026:** The public 943,126-row PGS005221 score was evaluated against FI in three cohorts in its source publication. Its genomic-SEM training traits include insomnia and UK Biobank contributes, so neither its published performance nor exact variant matches validate our prototypes or establish independent sleep–frailty sharing. See [frailty comparator review](stage6_frailty_general_pgs_comparator.md).

**HRS insomnia-index follow-up, 29 October 2026:** The updated PGI Repository's supplement assigns an insomnia index to HRS, but the HRS product page currently describes version 1 rather than the new version 2. Distribution, fees and linkage remain unverified. This would support a separate genome-wide-index analysis, not validation of the project's prototypes; see the [HRS insomnia index review](stage6_hrs_insomnia_pgi_v2_lead.md).

**HELIAD follow-up, 29 October 2026:** A Greek cohort has published sleep, FI, Fried frailty and genome-wide genotyping assessments, including a genotype–frailty analysis. Access is by inquiry and free access and the sleep–frailty–genotype intersection are unverified; see [HELIAD feasibility](stage6_heliad_sleep_frailty_validation_feasibility.md).

**Reference tag sensitivity, 29 October 2026:** Selection in non-Finnish 1000 Genomes Europeans and evaluation in Finnish reference individuals gives descriptive cross-group tag correlations for the 15 and 22 candidates. This is not a Czech-cohort imputation test or score validation; see [the holdout review](stage6_hapiee_tag_holdout_review.md).

**INSPIRE-T follow-up, 29 October 2026:** A [French longitudinal cohort review](stage6_inspire_t_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md) found documented sleep and Fried assessments, but its listed APOE genotyping does not establish availability of the genome-wide variants needed for our scores. The data are controlled access; fees and usable genetic coverage are unverified.

**WHI follow-up, 29 October 2026:** The Women’s Health Initiative has published repeated insomnia and adapted physical-frailty measures, and its virtual data enclave has no access fee for approved projects. Genotype availability in that enclave, permissible linkage, complete sample, consent, allele coverage and total costs remain unverified. See the [WHI feasibility review](stage6_whi_sleep_frailty_genetic_validation.md); neither score has been tested.

**MrOS follow-up, 29 October 2026:** Published studies document sleep with a Fried-style frailty phenotype and, separately, sleep with genotypes in older men. The controlled dbGaP release describes QC-cleaned PLINK genotypes, but consent restrictions differ by group and the required genotype–sleep–frailty intersection, marker coverage, permitted linkage and total costs are unknown. The NIA data packages are generally no cost subject to application and institutional requirements; no score has been validated. See [MrOS feasibility](stage6_mros_sleep_frailty_genetic_validation.md).

**NHATS follow-up, 29 October 2026:** The study's genetic QC report explicitly describes an NHATS application for linking NIAGADS genotypes to survey data; NHATS currently states that restricted access has no data fee. Round 7 contains the biospecimen and sleep/frailty measures, and a published Round-8 physical-frailty assessment offers a possible later outcome. The linkage needs approvals, the eligible joint sample and genetic coverage are unknown, and no imputation-quality r² threshold was applied in the release. The detailed [NHATS access and QC review](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md) and [Round-7/8 design](stage6_nhats_round7_sleep_frailty_crosswalk.md) distinguish these facts from a completed validation.

**MESA follow-up, 29 October 2026:** NSRR says its sleep data have no access fee and its MESA records use the BioLINCC `mesaid` identifier. A separate MESA dbGaP record describes molecular-data linkage. Exact genetic-ID mapping, linked frailty fields, permission for the proposed joint use and complete computing costs remain unresolved; MESA is an opportunity, not an available validation dataset. [MESA feasibility](stage6_mesa_sleep_frailty_validation_feasibility.md).

**CARDIA follow-up, 29 October 2026:** Public core questionnaires document early sleep symptoms and comparable self-reported activity limitations at years 15 and 35. These support a possible longitudinal phenotype analysis, subject to approved data access, but neither establish FI/FFS nor provide a score validation dataset. Access to linkable genotypes, joint sample size and score-marker coding remain unverified. See the [CARDIA feasibility review](stage6_cardia_sleep_function_genetics_feasibility.md).

The [allele input gate](stage6_score_allele_input_gate.md) identifies 9/15 and 14/22 reference-multiallelic sites in the two principal prototypes. Matching an rsID is insufficient to establish the original or target allele contrast. Reversed dosages fail by default in the research scorers; the separate reversal option is appropriate only when the biallelic diploid contrast has been verified independently. The synthetic implementation checks pass for all three scorers, but are not phenotype validation.

**Update, 29 October 2026:** A checksum-verified [three-cohort uremic restless-legs summary screen](stage6_uremic_rls_meis1_followup.md) matched all 33 distinct project variants by reported GRCh37 position and allele pair. The condition and population differ from insomnia and frailty; these are variant-level associations, not score validation.

The [Semmelweis Study protocol](stage6_semmelweis_validation_feasibility.md) describes planned sleep-quality, grip-strength and genetic assessments, but available linked genotypes, a complete frailty phenotype, access terms and fees have not been verified. It is a future lead, not an established free validation dataset.

**Update, 29 October 2026:** Published [HUNT and Partners insomnia follow-up](stage6_lane2019_candidate_replication_review.md) supplies selected-locus results for six of 33 unique project variants, including rs113851554 near MEIS1. This supports individual insomnia associations only; it does not evaluate either complete score or frailty, and cohort overlap with selection datasets remains to be checked.

**Update, 28 October 2026:** The public TPMI browser includes [an insomnia-specific diagnosis phenotype](stage6_tpmi_insomnia_specific_lead.md), in addition to the broad sleep-disorder phenotype previously screened. Twelve of 15 weighted-prototype markers and 17 of 22 count-prototype markers have exploratory rounded lookups; these do not validate either score. Remaining markers, effect-allele checks, full-score evaluation, and individual-level validation remain incomplete. See the [22-marker review](stage6_tpmi_shared22_insomnia_review.md).

The six published HUNT/Partners candidates have also been [compared with existing All of Us insomnia and weakness associations](stage6_lane_aou_six_variant_comparison.md); this remains a postselected, variant-level comparison rather than score validation.

A [Genes & Health access check](stage6_genes_health_public_data_feasibility.md) identified a potentially useful public South Asian GWAS summary-data release; its phenotype inventory was not obtained because anonymous bucket listing returned a policy-related 403. Its individual-level access is controlled and fee-based, so it is not a free open score-validation cohort.

A [fatigue weighted-Z sensitivity](stage6_aou_eur_fatigue_weighted_z_review.md) applies the existing score weights to public All of Us EUR marginal associations for coded malaise/fatigue. This is postselected summary evidence for an overlapping symptom, not a measured score effect or FI/FFS validation.

A later [conditional weighted-Z sensitivity](stage6_tpmi_17_marker_exploratory_z_review.md) combined the 17 available count-prototype markers using EAS reference LD. It is postselected, assumes browser ALT-effect coding, and is not a full-score or individual-level validation.

A separate [12-marker FI/FFS/insomnia-weight sensitivity](stage6_tpmi_12_weighted_z_review.md) uses the same assumptions for the weighted prototypes. The resulting Z values cannot rank predictive performance; FFS source orientation has supporting evidence but remains without record-by-record author confirmation ([audit](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md)).

The checksum-verified Japanese [knee-strength candidate screen](stage6_japanese_knee_candidate_review.md) provides a small separate-population muscle-strength association follow-up for 12/15 and 14/22 markers. It is not an FI, FFS or score-prediction validation; participant overlap has not been exhaustively verified.

**Neither project prototype has been independently validated for prediction.** The current public-data analyses provide exploratory association and biological context; they do not show calibration, discrimination, incremental benefit over clinical information, or performance for a joint sleep–frailty outcome.

## Score definitions and their variants

The [15-marker prototype](unvalidated_15_variant_score_prototype.csv) supplies three alternative sets of per-allele weights derived from FI, Fried frailty score (FFS) and insomnia associations. A research-only [scorer and usage guide](score_15_research_readme.md) now implement these sums, with reference-genotype arithmetic and input-error checks; those checks are not phenotype validation. They are three different weighted sums, not a fitted joint-outcome score. The FFS source effect-allele convention is supported by 37 matching published COJO lead records and BOLT-LMM documentation, but its exact conversion into the distributed file is not documented ([source audit](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md)). Candidate-specific allele contrasts still require verification; several rsIDs have multiallelic reference representations.

All three research scorers now reject reversed-allele dosage by default; an explicit `--allow-verified-reversal` opt-in is available after independent biallelic and diploid checks. The flag is an analyst assertion, not verification. [Synthetic integration checks](../work/test_scoring_reversal_safeguard.py) cover direct and reversed inputs, and the existing reference-genotype arithmetic checks were rerun. These safeguards do not confirm target-data coding or validate predictive performance.

The [22-marker shared-direction count](unvalidated_22_shared_direction_score.csv) is a separate **equal-weight allele count**. A second 22-marker file also defines [three exploratory FI-, FFS- and insomnia-weighted sums](unvalidated_22_variant_score_prototype.csv), with its own [research scorer](score_22_research_readme.md). The weighted file and equal-weight file contain the same 22 rsIDs but use opposite counted alleles at eight markers; their weight and allele conventions must not be mixed. These weighted sums are likewise postselected and unvalidated. Its candidates were selected from results that included MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital frailty; testing those same results is not independent confirmation. It and the 15-marker list have four rsIDs in common; even for these, a shared rsID does not establish an identical counted allele. The separate [guide](shared22_score_research_guide.md) records the allele and selection conventions. Both are uncalibrated research constructs.

## What the public evidence does and does not say

| Evidence | Finding | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| 15-marker weighted marginal-Z check against FinnGen hospital frailty | FI, FFS and insomnia weights give positive exploratory statistics using Finnish reference LD | Held-out score performance; HFRS is a diagnosis-based outcome distinct from FI and FFS, and selection or overlap remains possible. |
| Same 15-marker weights against MVP and FinnGen insomnia summary results | Positive exploratory weighted statistics across the three existing weight sets | Patient-level prediction, a joint frailty endpoint or a prespecified replication. Candidate associations had already been inspected; target LD and sample overlap remain uncertain. |
| 22-marker eight-source candidate table | Some alleles have concordant nominal estimates across listed traits | Independent replication of the 22-marker count; selection used some of those same sources and several GWAS cohorts overlap. |
| Published broad FI- and FFS-derived scores | Separate papers report associations with observed frailty in outside cohorts | Validation of either project prototype or a fair paired comparison between their predictive performance. |
| Genomic colocalization, eQTLs and AlphaGenome | Candidate locus, gene and cell hypotheses with substantial sensitivity to inputs | Accuracy of the prototypes in individuals, a causal gene or proof that sleep causes frailty. |

The underlying sources and assumptions are documented in the [hospital-frailty weighted-Z review](stage6_hfrs_sparse_score_summary_review.md), [insomnia weighted-Z review](stage6_insomnia_sparse_score_summary_review.md), [22-marker evidence review](shared22_eight_source_evidence_review.md), [published-score audit](fi_ffs_pgs_published_validation_audit.md), and [mentor synthesis](mentor_insomnia_frailty_synthesis_2026-10-28.md). The weighted-Z statistics are approximate tests of weighted marginal associations using external LD, not measured score effects. They cannot be used to rank scores by predictive ability. Tests and sensitivity analyses were postselected; the nominal p values are not prespecified replication evidence.

## Data and analysis still needed

A real out-of-sample evaluation requires a prespecified primary outcome and a cohort with linked participant genotypes, demographics, outcomes, adequate events and genotype coverage. Freeze the score and allele coding before accessing test outcomes; resolve multiallelic contrasts and the inferred FFS direction. Assess discovery, candidate-selection and target participant/relative overlap. Compare the score against an age/sex and relevant clinical baseline, estimate discrimination and calibration with uncertainty, and compare increments on the same participants. FI, Fried physical frailty and hospital-frailty outcomes need separate definitions; an insomnia-containing FI cannot independently establish that the score predicts sleep-free frailty. Use of additional sleep and fatigue items should be specified explicitly.

No suitable freely public dataset with the required genotype and outcome coverage has been established. The [public-cohort feasibility review](independent_outcome_validation_cohort_feasibility.md) explains the checked leads and limits; neither the old PGP snapshot nor the limited Open Humans metadata scan established sufficient FI/FFS outcomes. The project should not label the requested validation complete or deploy the prototype based on the current evidence.

## Reproducibility check

`stage6_score_evidence_audit.py` verifies local score row counts, uniqueness, weight columns and the prototype hash used in the insomnia analysis; it reproduces the stored 15-marker summary statistics into `stage6_score_evidence_audit.json`. It is an artifact-consistency check only. It does not independently verify source alleles, participant independence, study LD, phenotype definitions or score performance. The 22-marker hash records the current file; it does not mean that the score was preregistered or frozen before the prior exploratory work.

A subsequent selected-locus screen found a strong 1000 Genomes European reference proxy between the 22-marker candidate rs28528997 and a published HRS FI signal at rs62444881. The HRS signal was selected, the ELSA estimate was not statistically clear, and source-strand uncertainty prevents assigning its direction to the candidate allele. This does not validate either score. See [MAD1L1 proxy review](stage6_mad1l1_hrs_proxy_review.md).

A checksum-verified low-grip GWAS comparison produced exploratory positive weighted marginal-Z signals for the 15-marker prototype, but the study includes UK Biobank and is not independent; its phenotype is one physical component, not FI or FFS. See [grip comparison](stage6_grip_sparse_score_review.md) for assumptions and limitations.

The separate [22-marker count was also assessed](stage6_grip_shared22_weighted_z_review.md) using all markers in the same low-grip GWAS. Its approximate postselected Z is 2.51, with the same overlap and phenotype limitations; it is not predictive validation.

The [Korean and Japanese catalog screen](stage6_koges_bbj_phenotype_feasibility.md) located a small BioBank Japan insomnia GWAS but no catalog label for frailty or grip in those browsed catalogs. The [completed candidate extraction](stage6_bbj_insomnia_candidate_review.md) matched 13/15 and 18/22 score markers; it is an exploratory single-outcome SNP screen, not individual score validation. A rare rs12524473 effect is unstable, and participant overlap remains unverified.

The [BBJ matched-14 sensitivity](stage6_bbj_insomnia_matched14_z_review.md) yields an approximate positive insomnia weighted-Z under source coding and external-LD assumptions. It is postselected, not a full-score test or a measured score effect, and does not validate either prototype.

A new [reference-panel coverage comparison](stage6_bbj_subset_reference_correlation_review.md) quantifies how those partial count subsets track the full 22-marker count in JPT, pooled EAS and pooled EUR reference samples. The correlations differ by group and cannot substitute for BBJ participant-level validation or establish equivalent prediction.

The [Japanese cohort and reference audit](stage6_japanese_source_independence_audit.md) distinguishes the BBJ-only insomnia file from the same paper’s BBJ/UKB/FinnGen meta-analysis, and shared imputation resources from actual overlap. Cohort and relative non-overlap remains unverified.

A [public All of Us browser check](stage6_aou_public_variant_screen.md) located insomnia and generalized muscle-weakness analyses, but its selected-association endpoint returned no row for the 22 candidates. Two positive controls returned data. The endpoint does not return arbitrary variant results, so the blanks are not null findings and cannot be used to validate a score.

The [BBJ FI/FFS weighted sensitivity](stage6_bbj_fi_ffs_weight_sensitivity.md) uses the same 13 available 15-marker prototype variants and gives uncertain estimates for each weight set. It cannot rank prediction or be directly compared with the differently selected 22-marker count.

A published [MGI insomnia-score PheWAS](stage6_mgi_published_insomnia_prs_phewas_review.md) reports uncertain associations for EHR weakness and fatigue. It uses a different score, contains no explicitly labelled FI or FFS outcome, and cannot validate the project prototypes.

A new [NHATS feasibility review](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md) identifies a potentially suitable **no-fee but controlled-access** cohort: 4,006 genotyped participants and published insomnia-symptom and physical-frailty measures. Linkage, eligibility, phenotype completeness, discovery overlap and marker coverage remain to be verified. Access requires an approved project and the relevant IRB and enclave conditions; no validation has been run.
