# Candidate-gene expression across four public cohorts

This table brings together the project's postselected checks of FOXP2, SMAD5, RBM6 and MEIS1. It is designed to prevent a single favorable comparison being mistaken for independent confirmation. All are bulk-expression comparisons, not GWAS, variant-to-gene colocalization, insomnia analyses or evaluations of the proposed genetic scores.

| Source and phenotype | Participants compared | Findings across the four genes | Major limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [GSE117525](stage6_gse117525_muscle_candidate_expression_review.md): muscle, pre-frail/frail vs healthy older | 61 vs 73 | SMAD5 higher and RBM6 lower passed BH 0.05 within the four-gene screen; other two did not. | A sample-title study label is perfectly aligned with group; published work reports batch differences. |
| [GSE144304](stage6_gse144304_muscle_candidate_expression_review.md): muscle, pre-frail/frail vs fit older | 24 vs 30 | None passed BH 0.05 in either the original four-gene screen or the subsequent edgeR count model. | Small RNA-seq sample; incomplete control of batch and muscle cell composition. |
| [GSE167186](stage6_gse167186_sarcopenia_expression_review.md): muscle, sarcopenia vs healthy older | 24 vs 29 | The earlier log-CPM model found no BH-significant gene; a subsequent edgeR model found higher FOXP2 with BH p=0.029 across four genes. | Sarcopenia is not FI/FFS; cell composition and batch are unresolved. |
| [GSE40366](stage6_gse40366_frailty_expression_review.md): PBMC, frail vs non-frail nonagenarians | 29 vs 38 | None passed BH 0.05 in the four-gene screen. | Different tissue, very old survival-selected cohort and limited covariates. |

The significant GSE117525 observations are **not replicated** by the other comparisons, but their non-significance does not demonstrate absent effects or statistical heterogeneity: sample sizes, phenotype definitions, tissues, assays and covariate sets differ, and overlap among participants has not been verified. Separate q-values adjust only the four genes tested within each analysis, not the many project analyses. These descriptive checks cannot establish direction or mechanism for the insomnia–frailty shared genetic signal; for example, SMAD5 blood-cell regulatory QTL evidence and muscle expression are different measurements.

Detailed estimates and standard errors are consolidated in `outputs/stage6_expression_four_study_summary.csv`, generated by `work/geo_muscle/build_cross_study_evidence.py` from the four source JSON receipts. No meta-analysis or cross-platform coefficient pooling was performed. Each linked review records the specific methods and caveats.

The later edgeR analysis of [GSE167186](stage6_gse167186_skeletal_muscle_frailty_review.md) found a FOXP2 association (log2 fold change +0.402, BH p=0.029 across four); the matching-method follow-up of GSE144304 estimated −0.00565 (p=0.959). This is no observed replication, not a formal test of between-study heterogeneity, and subject overlap remains unverified.

A further [male-only GSE144304 sensitivity](stage6_gse144304_muscle_candidate_expression_review.md) aligned sex with the reported male GSE167186 cohort: 13 frail/pre-frail and 15 fit males gave an estimated FOXP2 log2 fold change −0.226 (p=0.077; BH p=0.309 for four genes). This small post hoc analysis is imprecise; differing point-estimate signs alone are not a formal heterogeneity or sex-interaction test. It provides no genetic-score validation.

A separate [older-adult insomnia PBMC screen](stage6_gse208668_insomnia_candidate_review.md) found four project-gene contrasts in a 17-versus-25 submitted matrix. Insomnia and control participants came from different recruitment studies; the original authors report a shared assay batch, but study-source confounding persists. This is not a joint insomnia–frailty cohort, genetic mechanism or independent score validation, and it should not be pooled with the frailty comparisons above.

The [GSE275071 human single-cell detection screen](stage6_gse275071_sleep_sc_detection_review.md) adds tentative immune-cell context. Four independent donors per sleep condition contribute four wells each; simple marker gates detect SMAD5 and RBM6 more often than FOXP2 or MEIS1. It is a coverage summary, not a differential-expression test or evidence of insomnia, frailty or a causal genetic mechanism.

A further [paired prolonged-wakefulness comparison](stage6_gse37667_sleep_wake_candidate_review.md) analyzed the same four genes in nine men. No test passed the four-gene BH threshold; this is not an independent replication of insomnia or frailty, and the small within-person experiment does not establish a null effect.
