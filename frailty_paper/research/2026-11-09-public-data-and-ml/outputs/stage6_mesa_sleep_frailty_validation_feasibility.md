# MESA: genetic, sleep and later physical-frailty candidate

Checked 28 October 2026. The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) is a further potential participant-level validation cohort, but it is controlled access, not an openly downloadable genetic dataset, and its cost and project eligibility have not been verified. No MESA participant data were downloaded or analyzed.

## Relevant evidence

* A [MESA sleep study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434554/) describes 2,230 people aged 54–93 with self-reported sleep disturbance and objective measures including polysomnography and actigraphy at the fifth exam (2010–2013).
* A separate [MESA frailty paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11200193/) constructed a modified questionnaire-based FRAIL scale from exam 6 (2016–2018) and used a six-minute walk test. Its analyzed imaging subgroup included 1,743 people and only 32 frail individuals; those numbers are not the intersection with sleep and genotype. FRAIL is a different construct from the five-component Fried phenotype and from the project's FFS discovery score; it includes fatigue, self-reported function and illness. Any four-item sensitivity excluding fatigue would be unvalidated.
* A recent [sleep PRS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13652048/) reports genetic/sleep analyses for 2,085 MESA participants and lists controlled dbGaP accessions phs000209 and phs001416. This documents that a sleep/genotype overlap exists for that study; it does not establish the number with exam-6 frailty or the project’s marker coverage.
* The [MESA BioLINCC dbGaP record phs003288.v2.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003288.v2.p1) describes requestable exam 1–6 data and the exam-5 sleep ancillary study, with potential linkage to molecular datasets. It explicitly directs users to authorized-access instructions. Confirm the current mechanism, scope of linkage, access costs, data-use limits and research-site requirements before assuming this is a practical route.

## Implications for this project

This could support a prospective analysis of sleep at exam 5 and a modified FRAIL outcome at exam 6, alongside genetic scores, if data access and participant linkage are approved. Before interpretation, check that exact score variants are available and correctly coded; ascertain participant and relative overlap with every score-development and candidate-selection cohort; quantify complete-case numbers, genetic ancestry coverage and attrition; and address sleep/fatigue overlap. A small number of frail participants may limit binary model calibration, discrimination or subgroup analysis. The exam-5 to exam-6 weight-change definition itself spans exams and may incorporate earlier health status. The data do not establish a valid directly measured FFS or FI without further work.

Potential access and a published modified scale do not constitute validation. An approved investigator would need to confirm dbGaP/BioData Catalyst conditions and fees, linkable records, institutional permissions, ancestry PCs, genotype quality and missingness before a pre-specified analysis. Compare this lead to [NHATS](stage6_nhats_genetic_validation_feasibility.md), whose custodian explicitly states that restricted-data access currently has no fee and which has a published physical frailty phenotype.

## Linkage and computing clarification

The public [phs003288.v2.p1 study description](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003288.v2.p1) explicitly states that its BioLINCC-origin MESA phenotypes include exam-5 sleep ancillary data and are intended to be merged with posted MESA phenotype and molecular datasets, including genome-wide genotyping and WGS. This supports cohort-level linkability more directly than separate papers alone, but does not establish that a particular request is approved, that every consent group can be merged, or that the resulting individuals have exam-6 frailty and all score variants. Confirm the current accession versions and permitted fields for the proposed study.

[NIH's dbGaP instructions](https://www.grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/accessing-data/dbgap) require an eligible institutional investigator, a signing official and data-access committee approval. The [BioData Catalyst guide](https://sb-biodatacatalyst.readme.io/docs/getting-started) describes charges for cloud computation and storage and mentions pilot credits; it is not proof that the complete analysis will be free. A project-specific access fee and compute budget remain unverified. No application has been submitted and no participant data accessed.

## Published FRAIL components versus public core dictionary

Inspected BioLINCC's [MESA 2025a core dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/mesa/data_dictionary/MESA_2025a.pdf), dated September 19, 2025 in the extracted text (source SHA-256 `57c92c2cbe83c89d398b2b7ae503c4af90abb88dfe9bbad80a9f786c406a8b3b`). The listed exam-6 core dataset `mesae6_drepos_20250102.sas7bdat`, PDF page 175, includes `climb6`, labeled as health limiting climbing one flight of stairs, which is consistent with the resistance item in the [published modified FRAIL measure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11200193/). It also lists `walk6`, but this asks about walking **more than a mile**, whereas the published ambulation item asks about walking **one block without assistance**; these should not be treated as interchangeable.

A text search for fatigue, walking one block and six-minute walk did not locate explicit corresponding labels in this dictionary. That is a search result, not proof of absence: abbreviations, different descriptions, supplementary files or dataset versions could contain them. The current dictionary and study page establish requestable exam-6 data in general, not enough exact field/coding evidence to reconstruct the full published five-component outcome. Obtain the release-specific question forms and detailed data dictionary, verify each of the 11 illness definitions, the two weights and dates, missingness, the 10% boundary in the published description, and all other item coding before scoring. The six-minute walk is separately useful but its field was not confirmed in this audit. The paper combines prefrail with frail owing to 32 frail participants in its selected sample; that number cannot be assumed for our sleep-and-genotype overlap.

The source PDF and extracted text are retained under `/Volumes/Extreme SSD/codex-sleep-frailty-work/`; no individual data were downloaded or analyzed.

## Exam-6 functional measurement provenance

The [MESA Early Heart Failure study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11073782/) describes the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) and six-minute walk test at exam 6. These are additional places to trace the fatigue and one-block items used in the modified FRAIL paper, but identifying the precise release file and response variables is still required; a study publication or form does not prove that the individual questionnaire fields are in the currently requestable core package. The [exam-6 six-minute walk form](https://mesa-nhlbi.org/PublicDocs/MESAExam6Forms/V6_SixMinuteWalk.pdf) explicitly records distance in metres and lists exclusion criteria such as use of a wheelchair, crutches or walker and inability to walk due to musculoskeletal problems. Thus observed walk distances are selectively missing for some participants with greater functional limitation; restricting to completed tests can bias estimates. Record exclusions, noncompletion and reasons, and prespecify an estimand and sensitivity analysis rather than coding missing distances as zero. Test availability, linkage and joint sample remain unverified.

## Separate exam-6 accession checked

The search located [dbGaP phs003702.v1.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003702.v1.p1), a separate MESA exam-6 **echocardiogram image repository**. Although its cohort description and associated publications reference Early Heart Failure, its description concerns images and does not establish that it distributes the KCCQ item responses or six-minute walk variables. The 6,814 consented subjects shown on the study page are not a count of eligible images, questionnaire responses, genotypes, or the joint validation sample. Do not request or interpret this imaging accession as a substitute for the missing questionnaire dataset without confirming its contents and study-specific data-use conditions. The [Early Heart Failure methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11073782/) report administration of KCCQ-12 at exam 6, strengthening the field-location hypothesis but not establishing its availability in a particular release.

## Ancillary dictionary audit: baseline sleep and missing outcome files

BioLINCC's [MESA ancillary dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/mesa/Data%20Dictionary%20-%20Ancillary%20Studies.pdf), dated September 19, 2025, includes dataset `mesaaire1_drepos_20240603.sas7bdat` with `trblslp1` (trouble falling asleep), `wknight1` (night waking and difficulty returning to sleep), `wkearly1` (early waking and inability to return to sleep) and `insomn1` (labeled insomnia). **Correction:** the `1` suffix does not establish baseline or core-cohort membership. The corresponding [dbGaP variable record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/variable.cgi?phv=83418&study_id=phs000209.v10.p2) identifies the MESA Air non-reference exam, labels its collection interval exam 4, and says MESA Air and classic exam datasets do not overlap in subjects. The record is from a superseded release, and its precise applicability to this BioLINCC file and later core follow-up needs confirmation. Do not treat these records as earlier observations on the core exam-6 frailty sample. The separate exam-5 sleep ancillary `mesaas113_ps_drepos_20190301.sas7bdat` lists participant ID, PSG/actigraphy availability and study timing variables, offering a different route once access and linkage are authorized.

A case-insensitive text search of this ancillary dictionary for `kccq`, `kansas city`, `fatigue`, `six minute`, `6 minute` and `walk ... block` did not find relevant KCCQ or six-minute walk outcome labels; the `6 minute` hits were actigraphy bin widths. This complements the core dictionary audit but does not rule out aliases, another release or an additional data distribution. Source PDF SHA-256: `50a43101111092d0b43602b3dd43f2b1a5d591c2bab8cf90b9ac8b69361d472a`. The PDF and extracted text are retained on the external working disk. No participant data were accessed.

The exam-5 [sleep questionnaire](https://sleepdata2.org/datasets/mesa/files/m/browser/forms/MESA_Sleep_Questionnaire.pdf) asks symptom frequency over the past four weeks using five categories, and identifies `trbleslpng5` for trouble falling asleep. The separate [Air sleep form](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetPdf.cgi?id=phd001846.1) uses monthly frequency categories and labels its fields with an `A` suffix. These are different forms and populations: do not assume identical raw codes or directly pool their records. Confirm released values, dates, missingness and target linkage.

## Exam-5 sleep questionnaire field mapping and scoring

The public [MESA Sleep questionnaire variable guide](https://www.sleepdata.org/datasets/mesa/files/documentation/dataset-descriptions/MESAe5a113SleepQuestionnaire_20140617.pdf) and [NSRR variable catalog](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/mesa/variables?folder=Sleep+Questionnaires) identify `trbleslpng5`, `wakeup5`, `wakeearly5`, `bcksleep5`, `typicalslp5` and a derived `whiirs5c`. The first four use 1–5 symptom-frequency codes, whereas typical sleep quality uses 0–4; the guide explicitly requires recoding the four 1–5 items to the scale's values. A raw sum without recoding is therefore incorrect. The questionnaire has a skip instruction for the early-waking follow-up, so structural skips must be handled according to the full official scoring/derived-variable documentation and actual release coding, not automatically treated as ordinary missingness. Confirm the derived `whiirs5c`, score completeness criteria, and permissions before use; do not label the score as a clinical diagnosis.

These exact questionnaire variable names were not found by case-insensitive search of the downloaded 2025a BioLINCC core and ancillary dictionary texts. Their public NSRR documentation is strong evidence of collection and documented variables, **not proof that this specific BioLINCC release contains the questionnaire**. A [published MESA analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12889833/) says Sleep Data access requires a data-use agreement and compliance with applicable terms. Verify current NSRR access rules, exact release, permitted crosswalk to MESA genotype/frailty data and any costs before designing a linked analysis. None of the above provides participant-level data or score validation.

## NSRR-to-BioLINCC identifier evidence

The [NSRR MESA dataset documentation](https://github.com/nsrr/mesa-documentation/blob/master/pages/dataset-introduction.md) explicitly states that its processed sleep questionnaire, actigraphy and polysomnography datasets and raw files use `mesaid`, the identifier used in MESA BioLINCC releases. This is direct documentation for an NSRR–BioLINCC phenotype join when the relevant data-use agreements allow it. It is not proof of approval, complete overlap, or an NSRR–dbGaP genetic crosswalk; the latter still requires authorized mappings and permission under all agreements. The [published sleep-PRS study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sleep/zsag200/8739310) lists MESA genetics at phs000209 and phs001416 and sleep data at NSRR, providing precedent for combined genetic/sleep research without establishing this project's sample or authorization.

NSRR's [MESA page](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/mesa) describes the available sleep data, and its [repository publication](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocy064) describes dataset-specific data-use agreements and IRB review. Confirm current application requirements and costs rather than assuming that access is immediately open or free. This is an identifiable practical route, not a completed join or validation.

The older [dbGaP exam-2 documentation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/document.cgi?phd=1742&study_id=phs000209.v10.p2) also names `MESAID` as a key linking exam 1 and 2 phenotype records. Together with the NSRR documentation, this substantiates the phenotype identifier across those documented releases, but it does **not** identify the current genetic sample ID mapping or authorize joining outside the approved data-use terms. The current [BioLINCC-derived accession](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003288.v2.p1) signals that its access procedure differs from many dbGaP studies, while its accessible public text does not resolve the precise delivery and crosswalk procedure. Confirm both with the current controlled-access manifest and agreements. BioLINCC states no dataset access cost; cloud computation may still cost money, and I did not locate a primary-source confirmation that the full NSRR-plus-genetics workflow has no fees.

## NSRR access fee confirmed

[NSRR's own data-security page](https://sleepdata.org/about/data-security) states that it provides its data cost-free to users who complete an online request, and that each data-use agreement is reviewed. This resolves the **NSRR sleep-data access fee**: it is no fee according to the repository. It does not establish free dbGaP genetic delivery or compute/storage, approval of the requested cross-repository linkage, or the exact terms for training and sharing an ML model; those remain project- and dataset-specific. No application has been submitted.
