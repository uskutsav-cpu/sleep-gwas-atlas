# Berlin Aging Study: sleep, frailty and genetics feasibility

The original [Berlin Aging Study (BASE)](https://rdc-psychology.org/en/reuse-data/base) enrolled a core sample of 516 people aged 70–103 at baseline. It is distinct from BASE-II; ZPID states that BASE-II data are not included in its BASE collection. The Max Planck [study page](https://www.mpib-berlin.mpg.de/research/research-centers/lip/projects/aging/base) says DNA from approximately 380 BASE participants was retrieved and analyzed, but it does not specify genome-wide coverage, the two project scores' markers or whether genetic files are part of the data currently distributed through ZPID.

Public [BASE documentation](https://rdc-psychology.org/en/reuse-data/base/material) identifies a baseline complaint-list item labelled insomnia (`q1bl20`, derived `q1kbl20`) and an observed frailty construct (`k1z5sum`) defined as a sum of signs of need for help. The latter is **not established as the frailty index or Fried score** used in our project. Observation of help needs and an insomnia complaint are scientifically relevant but are not necessarily a validated frailty or clinical-insomnia diagnosis. Their completeness, timing, response coding, and intersection with genotyped participants are unknown. The archive's page called “Genetic Determinants” describes family history, so its title is not evidence of genome-wide genotype files.

The institute describes BASE as available for specific research projects and the [documentation page](https://rdc-psychology.org/en/reuse-data/base/material) describes applying for data. A historical [data-protection document](https://www.base-berlin.mpg.de/17187/dataprotection.pdf) restricts use to an agreed scientific project. The current research-data-center terms, potential fees and the genetic data's exact access path are unverified; an independent directory lists no fee but its current applicability is not confirmed. Open documentation and discoverability do not establish an unrestricted linked-genotype download. The separate BASE-II has published genome-wide genotypes and frailty research but is not in this BASE release, and the availability and cost of linked BASE-II data remain unverified.

The [documentation ZIP](https://rdc-psychology.org/media/56/download/BASE%20DOKU.zip?v=1) was read without downloading participant data. Its `Psychia/Complaints.htm` and `ZPK/Frailty.html` describe the named items; `Geriatrics/riskprofile.genetic.determ.htm` describes family history. The downloaded archive SHA-256 is `4e6811875f5f5ac2710662ffb29344b16779580cedd399752fec88336123614b` (local integrity record, not a publisher checksum). It is old documentation and may not cover later genetic additions or the current release. No study records, genotypes, outcomes or scores were analyzed, and participant/relative independence from all discovery datasets has not been established.

**Assessment:** a promising phenotype lead for older adults, but not yet a freely and openly usable score-validation cohort. At most roughly 380 analyzed DNA samples are reported, before linkage and missingness; even a usable subset could have limited precision. Exact allele coverage, outcome definitions and data-use conditions must be established before any score evaluation.

A 2026 [BASE-II publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13031708/) states that its raw participant data are not public and describes project-specific access under a negotiated agreement. This confirms that the public BASE documentation cannot be treated as an open download of BASE-II genotypes. Fees, availability of project alleles and linked sleep/frailty measures remain unverified.
