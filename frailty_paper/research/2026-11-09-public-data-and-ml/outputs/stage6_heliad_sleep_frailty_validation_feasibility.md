# HELIAD: candidate cohort for sleep–frailty score validation

**Status, 29 October 2026:** promising request-based lead, **not established free or publicly downloadable individual-level data**. No participant data obtained and no score validated. The cohort has unusually relevant measures; its fee, permission conditions, usable sample, variant coverage, and independence require verification.

## Evidence

* The Greek [HELIAD sleep–frailty study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1525861020307027) reports 1,984 older participants, sleep quality assessed with the nine-item MOS Sleep Index II, and three frailty definitions including FI. This sleep measure is not identical to the discovery GWAS insomnia phenotype.
* A [HELIAD longitudinal analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.14283/jfa.2023.28) reports both Fried frailty and a 70-deficit FI, and describes sleep questionnaire collection. Its analytic sample and follow-up exclusions differ from those of other publications; these counts cannot be assumed to represent the genetic analysis sample. Its Fried measurement definitions must be compared with the project's discovery definition.
* A [HELIAD genetic study](https://academic.oup.com/ageing/article/52/1/afac318/7008635) reports 1,171 unrelated participants with genotype and frailty assessment, genome-wide genotyping and TOPMed imputation. This directly establishes genotype–frailty linkage for that study, but does not establish how many of these participants have usable insomnia data or all project variants. Another [study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9616333/) specifies the GSAsharedCUSTOM_24+v1.0 array, which is **not** the v3 manifest used in the HAPIEE reference-tag exercise.
* The [cohort profile](https://www.maelstrom-research.org/study/heliad) describes quantitative and qualitative sleep questions and age 65+. A prior [sleep and genetics analysis](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4425/13/1/63) also motivates checking participant-level linkage and insomnia-specific questions.
* The [Atlas of Longitudinal Datasets](https://atlaslongitudinaldatasets.ac.uk/datasets/heliad) directs researchers to inquire about access and reports no fee information. A related [published data statement](https://pub.dzne.de/record/163660/files/DZNE-2022-00406.pdf?subformat=pdfa) discusses sharing anonymized data for replication with qualified investigators; it does not promise release for this proposed project. No contact has been made.

## Checks needed before ranking as usable

1. Confirm whether access is offered at no charge, who is eligible, approval requirements and whether sleep, individual FI/FFS components and genotype dosages can be jointly analyzed for this research.
2. Obtain counts for participants with all outcomes and genotype, case prevalence, visit alignment, missingness, ancestry and relatedness. Confirm discovery-cohort independence and whether any HELIAD participants contributed to relevant discovery studies.
3. Check exact genome build, alleles, imputation INFO and allele frequencies for each locked 15- and 22-variant prototype, including the known ambiguous/multiallelic contrasts; do not infer coverage from array name.
4. Compare cohort definitions with discovery phenotypes; check whether sleep appears in FI deficits and perform a prespecified FI-without-sleep sensitivity analysis if applicable. Prespecify score construction, ancestry-adjusted models, uncertainty and a published insomnia PGS comparator. Sample size may support only exploratory estimates with wide confidence intervals.

If access is approved and genuinely cost-free, analyze FI and FFS separately alongside the best available insomnia phenotype; otherwise retain this as a conditional lead. Public summary statistics can support directional biological investigation but cannot establish individual-level prediction.
