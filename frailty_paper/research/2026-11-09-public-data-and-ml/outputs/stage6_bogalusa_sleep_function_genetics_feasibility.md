# Bogalusa Heart Study: sleep, genetic data and physical-function lead

Checked 29 October 2026. This is a potentially useful **middle-aged physical-function** cohort, not a demonstrated free, open frailty-score validation dataset. No participant data were obtained and no score has been tested.

The 2026 [multi-cohort sleep-PRS study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sleep/zsag200/8739310?searchresult=1) reports 1,298 BHS participants with comprehensive sleep questionnaires at the 2013–2016 BiCEPS visit; 1,078 with sleep information also had genetic data for that study. The sleep-genetic analytic sample's mean age was 48.2 years (SD 5.2). Its evaluated polygenic scores were not our custom 15- and 22-marker scores.

A separate [physical-performance study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7343486/) used 2013–2016 BHS data and reported grip strength and walking speed, with 1,227 and 1,164 participants respectively remaining in its selected cross-sectional metabolomics analyses. These studies describe the same BHS visit period and similar total cohort size, which suggests a possible intersection of sleep, genotype and physical measurements. **The reports do not establish that those individual participants overlap**, what the complete joint sample is, or that all five components of a Fried phenotype or an adequate deficit FI can be derived. Grip and gait are components, not complete frailty outcomes. The age distribution makes this a complement to, not a replacement for, an older-adult frailty cohort.

The sleep-PRS paper says de-identified BHS data are confidential and available on request to the BHS steering committee, subject to approval; it does not establish a fee waiver, access for this project, marker-level coverage or permission to link all necessary data. The [BioLINCC BHS study page](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/studies/bhs/) describes a separately requestable historical dataset with pediatric exams and a 1995–1996 adult exam, last updated in 2018. That page does **not** establish that it includes the later BiCEPS sleep, genotypes and physical measurements. Do not treat the earlier package as a free linked validation release.

For an authorized route, confirm the exact BiCEPS variable dictionary, allowed sample IDs and consent, cost and secure environment, ancestry/relatedness and project-source overlap, and all marker dosages and quality. Determine whether the five frailty items or a sleep-free deficit FI exist; otherwise prespecify separate grip and gait outcomes and label them physical-function analyses. Obtain the actual intersection and missingness/event counts before assessing power. A UK Biobank/MVP-derived sleep score evaluated in BHS is not independent evaluation of our scores. Potential associations in this cohort would require age, sex, ancestry structure and cohort-specific confounder handling and should not be generalized to older or clinically frail adults without evidence.

## Sleep definitions and genotyping in the published subset

The [2026 paper](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sleep/zsag200/8739310) identifies five WHIIRS sleep-symptom questions in BHS and considers both five-question and three-question definitions, alongside a separate question about ever being told by a doctor of insomnia. In its 1,078-person BHS sleep–genetic analytical cohort, 1,067 had a WHIIRS score, 410 met the paper's five-question WHIIRS ≥10 cutoff, and 90 reported a doctor-told insomnia diagnosis. These are **study-specific sleep/genetics counts**, not a count of people with our variants, grip, gait or frailty outcomes. WHIIRS ≥10 is a symptom-score threshold, not a clinical insomnia diagnosis. The underlying response codes, missingness, and exact score definition must be checked if a future data-access request succeeds.

The authors report Human610 array genotyping, sample/variant quality control and TOPMed-server imputation for their analysis. This is direct evidence that genotypes and imputed data existed for that research, not proof that the same imputed dosages are available to other investigators, are no-fee, cover all project variants or are permitted for the intended linkage. Record source build and imputation quality and independently resolve score alleles before use.

The paper's Table 2 appears internally inconsistent for the *ARIC* three-question WHIIRS rows (the ≥6 count exceeds ≥5), so its tabulated values require cautious interpretation; that discrepancy is not in the BHS five-question rows cited above and does not establish an error in BHS data. It reinforces the need to check underlying coding rather than copy published thresholds blindly.
