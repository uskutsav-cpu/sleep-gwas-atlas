# NHATS: a no-fee, controlled-access validation candidate

Checked 28 October 2026. NHATS appears to be a particularly relevant candidate for individual-level validation, subject to eligibility and approval. It is **not openly downloadable genotype data**, and no project validation has yet been performed.

## Evidence

* The [NIAGADS NHATS GWAS record NG00134](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00134/) describes 4,006 QC-passing people, genotyped on the Illumina GSA v3 (700,009 variants after QC), with TOPMed-imputed genotypes. Array data are GRCh37 and imputed data GRCh38; ancestry-specific groups and filtering information are available, while ancestry PCs are available by request. Self-reported group counts in the record do not establish sufficient statistical power in each genetic ancestry.
* A published [NHATS insomnia study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303065/) describes questions on sleep initiation and difficulty returning to sleep in Round 1, and objective physical-function assessments. A separate published [NHATS frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4723664/) implements the five Fried phenotype criteria. Both are evidence that measures exist in NHATS, **not** that all relevant data are available for every genotyped participant or the same interview wave.
* [NHATS restricted data guidance](https://nhatsannex.org/restricted-data) says there is currently no cost for restricted-data access, but projects must be approved, the genetic data are accessed in the MiCDA enclave, and genetic and other categories of restricted data cannot be combined. It requires full or expedited IRB approval for restricted NHATS data and directs applicants to request the current genetic application instructions from the study. The [output vetting handbook](https://www.nhats.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/NHATS%20Vetting%20Handbook_v1.4_public_12122025.pdf) says enclave outputs require review before release. These conditions may change; cost-free access does not mean unrestricted use or no institutional expenses.
* NIAGADS offers its own controlled-access request for NG00134. The exact relationship of that route to the currently described NHATS enclave and the permissions required to link longitudinal NHATS phenotype files must be confirmed with the data custodians before applying; do not assume one approval authorizes the other.
* [NHATS conditions of use](https://nhats.org/conditions-of-use) prohibit uploading NHATS/NSOC data to public LLM or AI platforms. No NHATS participant records were downloaded or used in this review. Authorized analysis would need to remain within the approved environment and data-use terms.

## What to verify before interpreting any result

1. Whether the specified sleep and physical frailty questions are present, linkable and complete at a chosen common wave for the genotyped sample, and whether sampling weights cover the biospecimen subset. Genotyping occurred using Round 7 dried blood spots, so analysis conditional on survival and participation through 2017 can introduce selection.
2. Whether any participants or close relatives overlap the FI, FFS and insomnia discovery or candidate-selection studies. Cohort independence is unverified. Sample sizes for the actual complete-case, ancestry-specific and event strata are unknown.
3. Coverage, imputation quality, exact alleles/build and ambiguous multiallelic variants for each score. The FFS source A1 effect convention has supporting published-lead comparisons, but the file conversion and candidate-specific multiallelic contrast still require verification ([audit](ffs_effect_allele_source_consistency_audit.md)).
4. Presence of or permission to derive a deficit FI with sleep/fatigue excluded, and whether the source items support a meaningful comparison with the Fried physical phenotype. Published NHATS FFS construction is not proof that all FI components are available or valid in this cohort.
5. Formal power, sparse-event limitations, availability of principal components and relatedness, external score-file approval, and release rules. A nominally large genotyped sample does not imply adequate numbers for a joint phenotype or predictive validation.

## Application-ready research outline (draft; not submitted)

**Objective:** Evaluate two previously developed research-only insomnia–frailty genetic scores in a separate cohort with measured sleep and physical frailty. Assess whether either score adds out-of-sample information to prespecified age, sex and ancestry-adjusted baseline models. Scores are not clinical tests.

**Population and endpoints:** Approved NHATS participants with QC-passing genotypes and linked survey measures. Specify a single common time point after checking available documentation and before inspecting outcome associations. Primary outcome: physical frailty or a continuous physical frailty component score using published NHATS definitions, excluding exhaustion in an explicitly defined sensitivity measure where symptom overlap is a concern. Secondary outcomes: insomnia symptoms and, if feasible, a separately specified sleep/fatigue-excluded deficit FI. State all item coding, thresholds, missingness rules and sample-selection rules in advance. Do not equate insomnia symptoms with clinical diagnosis.

**Genetic analysis:** Freeze score files and source directions before enclave outcome analysis; map and harmonize exact alleles and genome builds, examine imputation quality, and apply prespecified missing-marker rules. Remove or account for relatives, and model ancestry using approved PCs. Verify any external files are allowed into the enclave. Explicitly flag previous exploratory marker selection and discovery overlap.

**Evaluation:** Estimate score associations and uncertainty and compare each score against the same covariate baseline on the same individuals. Where sample size and outcome type permit, report out-of-sample discrimination, calibration and incremental performance with confidence intervals; avoid fitting new flexible models or multiple interaction tests on a small target sample. Distinguish FI, FFS, insomnia and any joint endpoint. Record missingness and sample flow. Complete disclosure review before releasing results.

**Questions for custodian:** Is NG00134 or a current imputed equivalent inside the MiCDA NHATS genetic enclave? Which applications are required for linkage to public and sensitive NHATS survey files? Which rounds, PCs, relatedness/QC and genetic-sample weights are available? Is importing a small non-identifying score/variant file permitted, and what output-review rules apply? What project and investigator qualifications, IRB wording, processing times, fees (if any) and study-use restrictions apply today?

A [representative GSA v3 manifest screen](stage6_nhats_gsa_v3_manifest_review.md) found no direct coordinate matches for the score markers. The manifest's 654,027 assay rows differ from the 700,009 variants reported for NHATS, so exact array content is unresolved; TOPMed-imputed coverage and quality must be checked in the approved data before accepting or rejecting feasibility.

NHATS now provides a [2026 technical paper and multi-language frailty-construction code](stage6_nhats_frailty_2026_implementation.md). Its definite/indefinite classification makes missing-data handling consequential; the linked review also flags a low-activity coding question to resolve before execution. This is public documentation, not confirmation of access to linked records.

A [Round 7 field-level crosswalk](stage6_nhats_round7_sleep_frailty_crosswalk.md) now identifies public-file variables for sleep initiation and maintenance in the same wave as the biospecimen collection, plus energy and activity fields used in the frailty definition. This supports a specific prospective analysis plan, not proof of complete linked records.

The manifest names four population-labeled imputed archives of approximately 19.9, 46.7, 107.0 and 9.8 GB compressed, alongside a 701 MB array BED and a 26 MB BIM. Their sum is roughly 183.4 GB compressed; values are not an estimate of the storage or compute needed after extraction. The manifest marks `requires_requester_pays=false` on these entries. This is a file-delivery flag, **not** proof that the whole NIAGADS/NHATS linked analysis has no fees or that data may be moved outside a permitted environment.

No genotype or phenotype records were downloaded; the public manifest contains file metadata only.

The notes identify 4,006 samples after missingness QC and state within-ancestry principal components are available by request. This is not proof of marker-specific imputation quality, linked outcome availability or independence. The notes contain an internal heading referring to `ng00132.v1` while their title and accession table identify NHATS as `NG00134.v1`; use the accession confirmed by the dataset record and seek clarification if the mismatch affects an application. ## NIAGADS consent restrictions

[NIAGADS NG00134](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00134/) lists all 4,006 samples under `GRU-IRB-PUB-NPU`. Its [definitions](https://niagads.scrollhelp.site/support/data-use-limitations) specify general research use subject to the data-use certification, local IRB documentation, an agreement to make study results available to the scientific community, and use limited to not-for-profit organizations. These are dataset-level conditions, not evidence that our project has approval or that it may link all NHATS data categories. The same page defines `MDS` as a methods-development modifier, but NG00134's displayed code does not include it; do not infer either permission or prohibition for the proposed ML work from that omission alone. Check the actual certification and project approval before model training or releasing derived models.

## Imputed dosage quality: an important release detail

The current [NHATS genetic QC and imputation report](https://nhats.org/sites/default/files/public-documentation/NHATSGeneticsQCandImputationReport_08282023.pdf), pages 18–19, says no imputation r² quality threshold was applied to the server output. Thus membership in an imputed VCF is not sufficient evidence of an accurate dosage. The release uses `GT:DS:HDS:GP` with `DS` described as estimated **alternate-allele** dosage; the score's counted allele can instead be the reference allele, and the multiallelic/source-contrast safeguards remain essential. Genotyped variants are marked `TYPED`, but the report says missing observed calls at those sites were imputed too, and some SNPs that failed pre-imputation filtering can reappear as imputed variants. A `TYPED` label should not be assumed to mean every participant has a directly observed genotype.

Imputation and phasing were stratified by self-reported race/ethnicity; the report also describes genetic-ancestry analytic groups and within-group PCs. The two types of group are not interchangeable. In an approved environment, prespecify variant-level imputation-quality criteria, inspect metadata and genotype/missingness by relevant group, examine rare variants and effective sample size, and document missing or excluded score components. Decide thresholds before outcome analysis and compare scores on the same eligible participants. No quality metric or genotype for any project marker was inspected. The report was retrieved from the current NHATS public-documentation path after an older search-result URL returned 404; source PDF stored on the external work disk.

## Direct linkage instruction and QC count discrepancies

The [NHATS genetic QC report](https://nhats.org/sites/default/files/public-documentation/NHATSGeneticsQCandImputationReport_08282023.pdf), page 2, explicitly states that an NHATS application is required to link the NIAGADS genetic data to NHATS study data; it also describes a separately available sample-filtering document. This is stronger evidence of an intended linkage pathway than the repository records alone. The exact current application, approved location and terms still require confirmation, especially alongside NHATS's current enclave guidance. No application has been made.

The same QC report describes 4,015 minimally QCed samples and lists analytic-group counts of 2,791, 667, 393 and 164 (which sum to 4,015), whereas the current NIAGADS release page lists 4,006 samples. These figures refer to differently described stages or releases and cannot be silently treated as the same denominator; reconcile using the actual versioned sample-filtering and subject files. The report's relatedness table lists 3,838 remaining after third-degree filtering while nearby text reports 3,839, another source inconsistency to resolve with the actual filtering file. It recommends against pooling heterogeneous ancestry groups; within-group PCs and the filtering document are available by request. Self-reported race used for imputation batches, the report's genetically defined analytical subsets and the target sample for the phenotype model are distinct concepts. Do not treat these public aggregate figures as our eventual score-validation sample.

## Existing NHATS polygenic-score files

NHATS publishes a [2024 PGS user guide](https://nhats.org/sites/default/files/public-documentation/NHATSPGSUserGuide_03152024.pdf) describing precomputed sensitive-data scores from the Round-7 genetic sample. The guide's trait inventory includes BMI, smoking, depressive symptoms, cognition, and longevity among others; a full text search of this version found no insomnia, sleep, frailty or grip trait. This does not rule out later releases or unlisted externally computed scores. These existing scores are **not our custom 15- or 22-marker prototypes** and cannot by themselves validate them or replace a target-matched insomnia/FI/FFS PGS comparator.

The guide documents source GWAS, ancestry-specific groups and standardized scores for its listed traits, so relevant precomputed scores could be exploratory comparators or covariates only with an explicit scientific rationale, appropriate permissions, and careful disclosure of ancestry and source-cohort overlap. A BMI or depression PGS is not an insomnia or frailty PGS. Verify the current file inventory, permitted linkage, score standardization and sample overlap; avoid selecting comparators based on observed target associations. Source PDF is retained on the external working disk; no sensitive files were accessed.

## Published FI/FFS comparator readiness

The [Foote et al. published FI and Fried-score PGS assessment](fi_ffs_pgs_published_validation_audit.md) identifies PGS005228 and PGS005229 as relevant genome-wide comparator candidates, subject to discovery/target overlap and ancestry checks. The project's existing [scoring-file audit](published_fi_ffs_pgs_source_audit.json) records that the saved PGS005228 file had identical entries in `effect_allele` and `other_allele` for all 2,379,429 rows, and the saved PGS005229 file had a blank `other_allele` in 388,036 of 2,761,868 rows. These findings concern the previously downloaded file versions, not necessarily current Catalog files. They make naïve two-allele matching unsafe; retrieve the current version, inspect its metadata and QC, and seek an authoritative representation or explicitly justified source-specific harmonization. Do not silently repair allele pairs or assume absence of an other allele means any unmatched allele is valid. The separate [15-marker sampled FI audit](fi_pgs_allele_sample_summary.json) provides partial reference support but does not correct the score file or prove the interpretation for every row.

Once source-file semantics and target genotype handling are resolved, evaluate published scores and our fixed prototypes against the same prespecified NHATS outcomes on the same people, reporting uncertainty and missing-variant coverage. Published external associations or catalog entries do not validate either prototype in NHATS. The PGS file construction and NHATS dataset permissions also do not establish that the proposed pair is independent of every participant or relative; audit actual source cohorts and target overlap.

**Current comparator-file checksum check (29 October 2026):** The official Catalog checksum files for [PGS005228](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/pgs/scores/PGS005228/ScoringFiles/PGS005228.txt.gz.md5) and [PGS005229](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/pgs/scores/PGS005229/ScoringFiles/PGS005229.txt.gz.md5) currently report MD5 `584c4839ba4d8caa299ff558ed161259` and `168e5c11469c0b27668a4c4d733276b1`, respectively. They match the locally saved checksums in the earlier audit. Thus the publisher-reported checksums have not changed since that audit; this check did not independently redownload the files, verify their content now, or determine whether the observed allele fields are errors versus intentional source representation. The comparator allele-harmonization gate remains unresolved.

**Full GRCh38 harmonized-file audit:** The PGS Catalog harmonized files for [PGS005228](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/pgs/scores/PGS005228/ScoringFiles/Harmonized/PGS005228_hmPOS_GRCh38.txt.gz) and [PGS005229](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/pgs/scores/PGS005229/ScoringFiles/Harmonized/PGS005229_hmPOS_GRCh38.txt.gz) were downloaded and their MD5s matched the Catalog's checksum files (`8367420d90d676dd7cb0a7d0a483b89d` and `b80a6f48272963438c8613d752e535f2`). Complete gzip decompression and row counts succeeded. In the harmonized FI file, all 2,379,429 rows still have effect allele equal to other allele and none has `hm_inferOtherAllele` populated; 32 rows lack harmonized positions. The FFS file has 388,036 rows with missing original other alleles and 387,988 rows with a populated inferred-other field; 79 rows lack harmonized positions. The latter are overall field counts, not a verified one-to-one classification of all missing-allele rows. Catalog's [format documentation](https://www.pgscatalog.org/downloads/) describes inferred alleles as reference-based annotations, not author-confirmed original allele contrasts. Neither harmonized file by itself resolves source coding, multiallelic matching, target marker availability or score validation. [Machine-readable audit](stage6_foote_hm38_allele_audit.json), [script](../work/audit_harmonized_foote_scores.py); verified files reside on the external work disk.

**FFS inference breakdown:** A subsequent full scan matched the original-other and inferred-other fields row by row. Among the 388,036 rows with a missing original other allele, 235,335 have one inferred alternative, 152,653 have multiple slash-separated inferred alternatives, and 48 have no inferred alternative. All 235,335 single-alternative rows form A/T or C/G pairs with the reported effect allele. These counts expose strand or multiallelic matching challenges; they do not establish that all such records are wrong or unusable. Use verified build and strand plus target reference data and explicit ambiguity rules; frequency checks alone may be unreliable near 0.5 or across populations. The [audit receipt](stage6_foote_hm38_allele_audit.json) and [script](../work/audit_harmonized_foote_scores.py) preserve the breakdown. No NHATS calls or phenotype outcomes were examined.

A follow-up arithmetic diagnostic found that the 388,036 FFS rows lacking an original other allele contribute 13.28% of the sum of squared published weights and 13.96% of the sum of absolute weights across all FFS rows in the downloaded file. These are **weight-file summaries**, not proportions of genetic variance, score variance, prediction, or variants actually missing in NHATS; they ignore allele frequencies and LD. They show that a blanket exclusion would change the published score definition and merits a prespecified sensitivity analysis, not that exclusion necessarily causes a particular performance loss. [Reproducible receipt](stage6_foote_hm38_allele_audit.json) and [script](../work/audit_harmonized_foote_scores.py). The script's `reported_other` label means only that the column is nonempty; for PGS005228 those nonempty values equal the effect allele and must not be interpreted as independently verified other alleles.

## Release-access identifier clarification

The [NIAGADS dataset page](https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/ng00134/) identifies this genetic release as **NG00134.v1**, and its filesets as `fsa000044` (array) and `fsa000045` (TOPMed imputation). Its linked [2023 release notes](https://st1.niagads.org/portal/download-public/NG00134.v1/rn) contain a conflicting heading that mentions `ng00132.v1`; the dataset accession table in the same document and NIAGADS page both say NG00134. Treat the heading as a discrepancy to confirm, not a reason to request NG00132. Both releases and any phenotype-linkage permission must be checked in the actual application. The existing [33-variant coordinate request table](ncds_score_variant_request.csv) includes candidate GRCh38 coordinates for an initial NHATS marker inventory; it was created for a different cohort, so its build, allele and multiallelic warnings still apply and it is not an authorized NHATS score or proof of coverage.
