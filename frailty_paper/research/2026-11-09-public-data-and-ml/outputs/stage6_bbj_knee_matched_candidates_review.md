# Matched candidate associations across Japanese and Taiwanese studies

The same **14 of 22** count-prototype markers have an exact position-and-allele-pair match in all three available sources: the completed BioBank Japan insomnia summary file, the Japanese knee-extension-strength GWAS, and the previously transcribed Taiwan TPMI insomnia browser inventory. These are the same 14 markers as the earlier [Taiwan–Japan comparison](stage6_cross_population_matched14_review.md). The two Japanese sources also share 12 of 15 markers from the separate weighted prototype. An exact pair and coordinate is a necessary check, not confirmation of source strand, phenotype equivalence or a shared causal variant. The [joined table](stage6_bbj_knee_matched_candidates.csv) and [input-hash receipt](stage6_bbj_knee_matched_candidates.json) are reproduced with `python3 outputs/stage6_bbj_knee_matched_candidates.py`.

This table facilitates a **descriptive SNP-level comparison**, not a test of a joint phenotype or the predictive performance of either score. The 14 markers were selected after earlier results and availability had been inspected. Japanese insomnia has 389 cases and 177,864 controls; the separate knee GWAS has 3,452 participants and measures strength, not FI or FFS. Taiwan's displayed clinical insomnia estimates are rounded. The source studies need not include the same participants; overlap or relatedness with discovery/selection data and across sources have not been exhaustively excluded. Do not compare beta magnitudes across the differently defined outcomes or infer heterogeneity from whether individual p-values pass 0.05.

For a concrete illustration, rs12671330 counts C in the 22-marker score. Conditional on each source's allele coding, the Japanese insomnia beta is 0.327 (SE 0.152; source p=0.0318); the Taiwan insomnia browser estimate is 0.022 (SE 0.013; p=0.11, rounded); and the Japanese lower-knee-strength beta is 0.0076 (SE 0.0535; p=0.8877), with an approximate Wald interval −0.097 to 0.112 on the knee-study scale. This example is postselected and none of these results establishes an association with both traits, a null association in the knee study, or a causal locus. The extremely low-count rs12524473 warning from the broader BBJ screen does not enter the 14-marker intersection because the knee source had no exact pair there; dropping it from this intersection is due to availability, not a prespecified quality filter.

For BBJ, the calculation assumes standard SAIGE Allele2 effect coding; for Taiwan, it assumes the browser beta is the ALT effect, with source effects rounded and independent verification of each target allele incomplete. For knee strength, the source beta is oriented to the score's allele and negated so a positive value refers to lower strength. The matched effects have different scales and models. The data join validates each listed candidate's declared pair against both inventories, and the earlier [knee source/reference audit](stage6_japanese_knee_allele_frequency_audit.md) checks reference representation; neither audit establishes causality, target LD or participant independence. A subsequent [matched 14-marker BBJ weighted-Z sensitivity](stage6_bbj_insomnia_matched14_z_review.md) compares marginal p-value conventions and reference LD; it is not a target score regression or a score-level saddlepoint test. No Wald interval from this table should be substituted for the source saddlepoint p-value.

Source: [BioBank Japan insomnia page](https://pheweb.jp/pheno/Insomnia) and [Sakaue and Kanai et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00931-x); [Ito et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06108-6); Taiwan TPMI requests acknowledgement and citation of [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09350-y) and [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09680-x).
