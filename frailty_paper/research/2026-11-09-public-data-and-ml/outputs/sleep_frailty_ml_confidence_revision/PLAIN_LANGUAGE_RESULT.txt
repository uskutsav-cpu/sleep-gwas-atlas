Confidence-method experiment: completed, rejected

We tested one specific revision to the earlier model’s confidence method.
It chose eligible cases before estimating their error ranges, instead of imposing
one broad minimum range across all cases in a cell type.
The comparison kept the same earlier predictions, fitted error models,
training partitions and original control rules. The original control results reproduced.

None of seven development studies passed all usefulness checks.
The method answered cases in three studies. Two had coverage below the 90% target;
the other had less than a 1% error reduction versus a simple typical-effect guess.
It gave no answers in four studies. Those empty results do not count as success.
Independent saved-data calculations checked every completed study’s reported errors and coverage.
These checks do not prove a statistical guarantee across laboratories.

The revised method is not released as a trustworthy confidence system.
The separate model-weight adaptation comparison continues unchanged.
No untouched-study outcomes were used in this experiment.
