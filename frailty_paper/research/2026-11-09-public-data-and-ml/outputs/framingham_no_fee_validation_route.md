# Framingham: a no-data-fee route to score validation

**Release update:** dbGaP now lists `phs000007.v36.p17`; exam 8 is `pht000747.v11.p17`. The checked v36 marginal counts match the historical v35 values below. See the [mentor proposal and checklist](framingham_mentor_access_proposal.md) and [v36 extract](stage6_framingham_exam89_dbgap_v36_aggregate.csv). References to v35 as current below describe an earlier snapshot.

Checked 29 October 2026. This is a **controlled-access opportunity**, not an openly downloadable individual-level dataset, and no score has yet been validated in it.

The [Framingham Heart Study data overview](https://www.framinghamheartstudy.org/fhs-for-researchers/data-available-overview/) states that its external dbGaP and BioLINCC repositories charge no data-access service fees, and directs new applications for genetics linked to phenotypes to dbGaP. This does not guarantee that an investigator is eligible, that access will be approved, or that computing and institutional costs are zero. Internal-repository requests may incur fees.

## Why it is promising

* [Framingham's genetic data inventory](https://www.framinghamheartstudy.org/fhs-for-researchers/data-available-overview/genetic-data/) describes linked genotype, phenotype and family-structure files; SHARe includes 3,753 Offspring participants, plus 99 Offspring spouses, among its 9,274 unique participants. These are genotyped totals, not the usable sample for this question.
* A [published study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29917058/) evaluated frailty in Offspring and Omni participants aged at least 60 at the 2005–2008 exam, including a Rockwood cumulative-deficit sensitivity analysis. Confirm the exact Fried phenotype and FI definitions, variable availability and scoring before implementing either outcome.
* An [earlier sleep genetics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1995620/) reports sleep-habits questionnaires from 2,848 Offspring participants at exam 6 and a genotyped family subset. This is evidence that sleep and genetics were studied in the cohort, not a count with the project scores and both outcomes.
  The [published methods](https://bmcmedgenet.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/1471-2350-8-S1-S9) give 749 individuals with both sleep-phenotype and 100K-genotype data. This is a historical exam-6 analytic subset, not the exam-8 frailty intersection or the available size in newer releases. The much denser [TOPMed FHS release](https://dbgap.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000974.v6.p5) describes approximately 4,100 selected for whole-genome sequencing and imputation to array participants, while comprehensive phenotypes and pedigrees are under `phs000007`. Confirm which current dataset and consent groups contain project-specific genotype dosages and whether cross-study linkage is authorized; neither the old 749 nor the 4,100 is a joint validation sample.
* The publicly indexed [Offspring data dictionary](https://biolincc.nhlbi.nih.gov/media/studies/framoffspring/data_dictionary/Framingham_Offspring_2025a.pdf) lists exam 8 insomnia variable `h745` and exam 9 variable `j944`. Both forms ask whether a health professional ever said the participant had insomnia; the exam 9 form explicitly gives 0 no, 1 yes and 9 unknown. Denominators, completeness, release coding and availability within dbGaP still require verification. See the [exam 8–9 variable crosswalk](stage6_framingham_exam8_exam9_variable_crosswalk.md). Physician-diagnosed insomnia is not interchangeable with the UK Biobank insomnia-symptom phenotype.
* The [Framingham SHARe Sleep Heart Health Study entry](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000137.v8.p5) describes detailed sleep questionnaires and polysomnography and directs researchers to authorized access. Its displayed 6,441 participants refer to the multicenter SHHS cohort, not necessarily genotyped Framingham participants.

## Public dbGaP response counts

In the current v35 public exam 8 metadata, H745 recorded 107 affirmative insomnia responses among 2,856 nonmissing responses; each of the three symptom variables had over 2,890 nonmissing responses. The release also lists an exam 9/Omni 4 clinic file containing grip and walking measurements. These are marginal counts, not the usable genotyped or prospective sample. The metadata also confirms that unknown values were represented as missing in that release. [Aggregate counts and release caveats](stage6_framingham_dbgap_exam8_aggregate.md).

## Same-exam FI, Fried frailty and sleep feasibility

The [Offspring exam 8 form](https://www.framinghamheartstudy.org/files/2017/08/Offspring-Exam-8.pdf), pages 23–24 of the form, includes past-month frequency questions about trouble falling asleep, waking during the night and difficulty returning to sleep, and waking too early. Each has five ordered responses from never to almost always; the form separately asks whether a health professional has ever said the participant has insomnia. These are distinct endpoints, not an established clinical insomnia diagnosis or the exact UK Biobank question. The [dbGaP exam protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/GetDocumentPart.cgi?phd=1514&section_id=phd001514&version=2) says cognitively impaired participants were not asked the sleep questionnaire, which may induce selection in an older and frailer sample.

The [published same-period frailty study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29917058/) included 2,171 Offspring and Omni participants aged at least 60 after additional exclusions for its arterial-stiffness analysis; it reported 1,046 nonfrail, 976 prefrail and 149 frail by Fried phenotype, alongside a cumulative-deficit FI. Those are **not** counts of people with sleep responses, usable genotypes or access permission, and they should not be treated as the sample size available for our project. The paper's selected, predominantly white sample and its exclusions also limit generalization. The same exam makes a paired FI/FFS comparison plausible if the files and participants can be linked, but availability of the complete FI item list, genotype coverage and an insomnia-free FI must still be verified. The FFS includes exhaustion; its overlap with sleep-related constructs must be described, and a sleep-excluded FI sensitivity must be defined from the actual items rather than assumed.

The [paper describes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6599281/) a **37-variable FI**, dividing accumulated coded deficits by possible deficits. Its methods describe FI categories 0–0.1, >0.1–0.21 and >0.21, while [Table 1’s footnote](https://academic.oup.com/view-large/137290243) instead gives FI <0.1, 0.1–0.2 and ≥0.2. These descriptions disagree at the thresholds and the footnote is ambiguous at 0.2. The actual analysis coding is unresolved; confirm source code or definitions before reproducing categories. Neither set of FI thresholds is the Fried 0, 1–2 and ≥3 component categorization. The accessible paper text describes broad domains but does not establish the full 37-item list or whether a sleep item appears in it, so the proposed sleep-excluded FI cannot yet be specified. A [later analysis of this cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7955470/) found participants missing complete Fried scores were older and had more cardiovascular illness than those with complete scores. This makes missing-data selection a practical concern in addition to the documented cognitive-impairment exclusion for the sleep questionnaire. Exact overlap and the direction of any resulting bias in our analysis remain unknown.

For analysis after approved access, prespecify (1) each sleep symptom's ordinal outcome or a justified binary threshold, (2) Fried score and FI outcomes measured in the same exam, and (3) paired comparisons on the same complete participants with family-aware uncertainty. A contemporaneous association does not establish prediction over time or causal direction. The form alone does not establish release-specific variable identifiers, completeness or coding in the linked genotype release.

## Array marker feasibility

A public NA12878 report from a related Omni5 revision contains same-position probes for 9/15 and 12/22 score markers, conditional on the assumed coordinate build. The actual Framingham release revision, variant identity, allele orientation, QC and imputed coverage remain unverified. [Array screen](stage6_framingham_omni5_nexus_review.md). Nearby probes also have high reference-panel LD with each candidate lacking a same-position probe, but their target-cohort performance and allele direction are unverified; this does not validate a proxy or the score. ## Before calling it independent validation

### Preliminary overlap screen

The [FFS source study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/) describes UK Biobank as discovery and HRS as its replication cohort; Framingham is not identified as a discovery cohort in that description. The [Song cross-trait study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2024.1358996/full) identifies UK Biobank and TwinGene for the FI input and UK Biobank for its insomnia input. Those named cohorts are distinct from Framingham, which makes it a plausible lead, but study-level names do not rule out individual or relative overlap. The 22-marker selection additionally used MVP and FinnGen, and all weight sources, follow-up choices and relatedness still need a complete audit.

There is a specific overlap issue: the project has already inspected a [CHARGE gait GWAS](stage6_charge_gait_song124_review.md), and the [original gait paper](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58468961/2J9vAuiscdt8bWsNb.pdf) explicitly lists Framingham among its discovery cohorts. It describes usual gait at Offspring exam 8, which is also the exam period used in the published frailty study. This confirms cohort-level overlap in prior exploratory evidence, not that the same participants are in a future evaluation or that gait results determined score selection. Audit the decision history and participant overlap before calling an evaluation strictly held out; do not tune scores using the Framingham-overlapping gait evidence and then present Framingham outcomes as an untouched test.

The existing project documentation describes the [15-marker selection](candidate_selection_and_sleep_score_context.md) as Song-ranking and FI/insomnia/FFS source availability, and the [22-marker selection](shared22_score_research_guide.md) as a joint MVP-insomnia/FinnGen-HFRS filter. The [gait lookup](stage6_charge_gait_full_candidate_review.md) calls the 22 candidates previously selected and reports results as post-selection follow-up. This documented chronology is reassuring, but it is not a complete audit of every decision, weight, comparator, or participant and relative overlap; preserve that distinction.

1. Confirm the investigator and institution satisfy dbGaP access rules, required ethics review, consent and data-use restrictions. Identify the current dbGaP releases containing compatible participant IDs, genotype dosage, frailty components and sleep variables; do not assume BioLINCC and dbGaP IDs can be joined without permission.
2. Establish participant and close-relative overlap with **each** discovery GWAS, including the FFS, FI and insomnia sources and their contributing cohorts. If there is overlap, exclude affected participants/relatives or use an independent discovery GWAS; otherwise call the analysis non-independent.
3. Audit the 15- and 22-marker score alleles, build and imputation quality in the approved genotype release. Resolve the existing eight-marker orientation differences before computing either score. Establish sample counts after genotype, sleep and frailty linkage, missingness and age filters.
4. Prespecify separate outcomes: diagnosed or symptom-based insomnia and FFS/FI. Avoid shared questionnaire items creating a mechanical sleep–FI association, and account for family relatedness, ancestry, sex, age, genotyping batch and ancestry PCs. Compare a clinical baseline with and without each frozen score; report uncertainty, calibration and discrimination, and treat modest sample size and event counts as limits.

The dictionary and published cohort descriptions are evidence of feasibility, not proof the necessary records are jointly available to this investigator, that the scores will be predictive, or that this cohort is independent of the project's discovery data.

## Later frailty outcome for a prospective evaluation

A separate [Framingham Offspring analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7955470/) reconstructed Fried physical frailty at examination 9 (2011–2014), after the examination 8 sleep questionnaire (2005–2008). This establishes that the published cohort has been used to study a later physical-frailty assessment and makes a forward-looking validation design more concrete: if permission and linkage are confirmed, freeze the genetic scores and define baseline sleep and covariates at examination 8, then assess the prespecified frailty outcome at examination 9. A binary new-frailty endpoint would require clearly defining baseline nonfrailty, event counts and attrition; the original paper states that frailty is measured at visits, so its exact onset time is not observed. It used logistic regression for its later-visit outcome, not a known-time survival analysis.

The published incident-frailty analysis included 1,566 selected participants after exclusions including 572 who did not attend exam 9, 72 with missing or incomplete later Fried scores, and 52 with prevalent frailty at baseline. These counts apply to that paper's particular analysis and are **not** the number with the project variants, sleep responses, valid genetic QC or access permissions. Selection through survival and follow-up attendance and the sleep-questionnaire cognitive exclusion may change the target population. Exact sample overlap with our discovery and prior selection work remains a separate audit. If FI is also evaluated at exam 9, verify its actual item-level availability, validity and overlap with sleep and fatigue; the paper's secondary use of a Rockwood definition does not establish every variable needed for our preferred sensitivity analyses.

The later article repeats the methods-style FI thresholds of 0–0.1, >0.1–0.21 and >0.21. This corroborates how a separate analysis described its categories but does not resolve the conflicting Table 1 footnote in the earlier Framingham paper or confirm its underlying implementation. No participant records were accessed, application submitted, or genetic score evaluated.

### Event counts and paired-comparison constraint

In that study's [Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7955470/), the age/sex-adjusted Fried analysis reports 111 later frail participants among 1,566; the separate Rockwood FI analysis reports 238 among 1,343. It excluded 52 people with baseline frailty under Fried criteria versus 241 under Rockwood criteria. These are different outcome definitions and differently selected analytic samples, not paired counts and not participants known to have our sleep and genetic data. Their intersection, missingness, sample/relative overlap and actual number of genetic-score events are unknown. The later-visit sample will be at most the number with eligible genotypes, sleep and frailty data, which has not been established; do not use 111 or 238 as project event counts.

A defensible paired incident-outcome comparison requires defining a common eligible baseline population and complete outcome/genetic data for both measures, then evaluating the same participants and accounting for their paired outcomes and family structure. A separate analysis may use each definition's own eligible population, but differences in associations across those populations cannot be attributed solely to FI versus Fried measurement. Sparse events, attrition and the approximate 5.8-year observed follow-up interval constrain model complexity and precision. This is a prospective analysis plan, not an accomplished cohort validation.
