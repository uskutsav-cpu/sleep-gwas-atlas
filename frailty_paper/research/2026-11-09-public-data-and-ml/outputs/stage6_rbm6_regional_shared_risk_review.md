# RBM6 region: public insomnia and frailty comparison

To investigate whether the gene-model findings correspond to the same local disease association, five existing public summary-statistic sources were aligned in GRCh38 chr3:49,008,613–51,008,613, centered on model SNP rs2353586. After excluding indels, palindromic SNPs, ambiguous rsIDs, invalid statistics, duplicates and nonunique or negative-strand liftovers, **2,256 SNPs** had matching position, rsID and allele pair across all five inputs. FinnGen R13 insomnia supplied the reference/alternate allele orientation. The aligned table is `stage6_rbm6_five_gwas_common_region.csv.gz`; individual filtering counts and lead comparisons are in `stage6_rbm6_five_gwas_region_summary.json`, produced by `stage6_rbm6_shared_region_screen.py`. The FinnGen R12 hospital-frailty source was re-read and verified against its published whole-file MD5; `stage6_rbm6_hfrs_region_receipt.json` records the extraction.

Within this *restricted five-study common set*, the smallest Jansen-insomnia p was 2.65e-6 at rs10865954; its FinnGen hospital-frailty p was 0.217. The smallest FinnGen hospital-frailty p was 3.85e-5 at rs2282749; its Jansen-insomnia p was 0.00664. These are postselected regional summaries, not necessarily either study's genome-wide or full-region lead, and the nominal p values are not corrected for the search. Differences in p values can reflect power, phenotype definitions and measurement; they do not prove different causal variants.

To check whether those two observed peak markers are close proxies, their exact alleles were retrieved from the public 1000 Genomes Phase 3 reference after GRCh38-to-GRCh37 position matching. The pair's dosage r² was 0.0010 in 503 European-ancestry reference samples and 0.000051 in the subset of 99 Finnish samples. The Finnish subset is included in the European group, so these are not independent reference replications. This small reference comparison suggests the two selected markers themselves are poorly correlated in these groups; it does **not** show whether the full underlying insomnia and frailty signals colocalize, how many independent signals exist, or whether any gene mediates them. Reference LD can differ from the study cohorts, and the selection of markers depends on the common-SNP filter.

For additional context, the influential RBM6 model variant rs2353586 had reference r²=0.886 with the strongest Mak FI marker rs7428430, and r²=0.406 with rs2282749 in the European panel. These are descriptive reference LD values, not proof of causation or common disease effects. All six selected markers had unique exact allele matches in the reference; `stage6_rbm6_lead_reference_coverage.csv`, `stage6_rbm6_lead_reference_ld.csv`, and `stage6_rbm6_lead_reference_receipt.json` retain the matching and pairwise results. The local index and panel were hashed; the remote genome-wide reference VCF was not downloaded and its whole-file checksum was not verified. `stage6_rbm6_lead_reference_ld.py` reproduces the reference check using `work/stage5_env` with pysam.

The regional signed-Z correlations in the JSON are descriptive across SNPs in LD, not estimates of local genetic correlation, independent observations, p values or colocalization. The five source cohorts overlap or may overlap, FinnGen insomnia and hospital-frailty samples may overlap, and the sources assess different sleep and frailty constructs. Multi-signal colocalization with appropriately matched LD, full regional variant coverage and verified sample overlap remains needed to assess shared causal signals. Source provenance and the exploratory RBM6 TWAS are described in `stage6_psychad_rbm6_twas_review.md` and the earlier project GWAS cache receipts.
