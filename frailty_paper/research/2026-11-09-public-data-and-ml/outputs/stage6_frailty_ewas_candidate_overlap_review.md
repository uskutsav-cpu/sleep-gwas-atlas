# Public frailty methylation results near project variants

[Mak et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11166364/) studied whole-blood methylation and frailty indices in Swedish and Danish twin-cohort samples, with 829 individuals in their meta-analysis. The authors released selected significant-CpG results in supplementary tables. This source is molecular phenotype evidence, not a genotype GWAS, sleep study or independent prediction test for our scores. Some cohort relationships with the Atkins/TwinGene discovery and other project sources remain to be checked.

We compared the 33 distinct project marker positions (GRCh37) with the source's 129 selected continuous-FI CpGs and 538 selected frail-versus-non-frail CpGs, using a ±100-kb coordinate window. One of these selected CpGs fell within the window: cg12594244, annotated to LOXL1, was 83,871 bp from rs11636381 (in the 22-marker list). It appears in the frail-versus-non-frail table, with meta-analysis β = −0.01466, standard error 0.00362, p = 5.15×10⁻⁵ and FDR = 0.0428; the reported I² is 48.2%. In this EWAS, methylation is the outcome and the comparison is frail versus non-frail, so the sign describes methylation differences, not a genotype effect. Counts and table columns were checked against the authors' source workbook.

**This is proximity only.** It does not demonstrate that rs11636381 changes methylation at this CpG, regulates LOXL1, is causally related to frailty, or affects insomnia. The marker is multiallelic in the reference, and an allele-specific mQTL comparison and suitable regional colocalization would be needed to evaluate a proposed mechanism. Whole blood does not resolve a causal cell type. Different index definitions, potential sample/relative overlap, heterogeneous component results, and post-selection limit interpretation. Conversely, no nearby selected CpG for the other markers does not mean absence of methylation associations; the source tables report only FDR-selected results, not the complete tested universe.

Next, use an appropriate ancestry- and tissue-matched, full mQTL dataset to look up the exact allele pair and genomic build, then compare full regional frailty and methylation-QTL signals with appropriately matched LD. Treat any result as a hypothesis until independently replicated. An mQTL would still not establish predictive score performance.
