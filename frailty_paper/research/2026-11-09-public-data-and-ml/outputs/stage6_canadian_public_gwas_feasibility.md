# Canadian frailty and sleep GWAS: public-data lead

**Update, 6 November 2026:** Both browsers are online. The actual CLSA catalog API returned 4,260 stratified entries for 745 phenotype codes, including measured grip strength and four-metre walking time, but no code/description containing `frail` or `insomn`. We completed exact coordinate/allele-pair/rsID lookups for all 33 distinct project candidates against three physical-component outcomes: all 99 pairs were returned, five displayed p values were below 0.05, and none survived Holm correction across 99 tests. These are exploratory rounded marginal associations, not independent score or composite-frailty validation; see [the completed screen](clsa_public_followup_20261106/README.md). The CARTaGENE catalog has 701 entries and likewise no `frail`/`insomn` label. Its sleep-duration and apnea phenotypes are distinct outcomes. The historical maintenance observations below are retained as provenance, not a statement of present service status.

A potentially useful new outcome source is [Borhan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00363-z), who report an ordinal Fried-type frailty phenotype GWAS in 23,105 Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging (CLSA) participants. Their outcome has three frailty categories, not the project's 0–5 Ye FFS or Atkins FI, and their model includes sex, chronic-condition count and ancestry PCs. The paper reports 1,547 frail, 10,261 pre-frail and 11,297 non-frail people. Cohort names differ from the main project discovery sources, but participant/relative overlap and all selection histories remain unverified. Even a valid summary association cannot measure individual score discrimination or calibration.

The paper's [data statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00363-z) directs qualified researchers to CLSA; it does not identify a full open GWAS file. Its supplementary table contains only selected loci, which cannot be treated as a full candidate or score test. CLSA's [GWAS-summary publication category](https://www.clsa-elcv.ca/our-publication-type/gwas-summary-data/) lists the paper, but the listing links to the publication, not a verified downloadable GWAS file.

Separately, the [PheWeb2 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02469-8) says its presented CLSA summary associations can be queried and downloaded free through [CLSA PheWeb2](https://clsa-pheweb.cerc-genomic-medicine.ca/). It is **not yet established** whether that browser includes Borhan's ordinal frailty GWAS, another usable FI/FFS-like phenotype, insomnia, or exact project variants. The live CARTaGENE endpoint returned a maintenance notice covering the CLSA and CARTaGENE PheWeb sites until **November 1**, without a time or timezone; this was checked on October 28, 2026. Cached search results describe the normal browser, not present live availability. When service returns, inspect the actual phenotype catalog and associated study metadata before querying candidates. Check file availability, build, allele orientation, sample size, outcome coding, QC, study and relative overlap, and source restrictions. A summary-based weighted association using appropriate LD remains exploratory and does not validate predictive performance.

The [CARTaGENE PheWeb](https://cartagene-pheweb.cerc-genomic-medicine.ca/) is a separate Quebec cohort browser; its public about page describes up to 29,337 genotyped participants and GRCh38-based processing, but the site explicitly warns its association results are not fully curated. Neither relevant phenotype coverage nor exact access to full statistics was verified during maintenance. The [CLSA data-access guidance](https://www.clsa-elcv.ca/data-availability/) describes a separate process for individual genotypes and phenotypes; public summary data must not be conflated with unrestricted linked individual records. No access application or external communication was made.
