# Harvard Personal Genome Project: public validation feasibility

The [Harvard Personal Genome Project](https://pgp.med.harvard.edu/data) is a promising public source because it describes participant-linked genomic and trait data, shared with explicit open consent. The project describes voluntary submissions and different genomic data types; genome, survey and medical-record availability are not guaranteed for the same participants. This is a potential resource, not evidence that the study has an adequate frailty-validation cohort.

I obtained an older public [Untap snapshot](https://download.ac2it.arvadosapi.com/c=3ccedff14c04af4d96e41e96648c55e2+3173/hu-pgp.sqlite3.gz) identified through the project's [researcher FAQ](https://github.com/PGPHarvard/FAQ/wiki/Researcher-FAQ). The snapshot date is not established. In that snapshot, **nine distinct people** have a condition name containing “insom”, a demographics record, and a nonempty listed download URL for at least one of the 23andMe, Complete Genomics or Illumina data types. This is an exploratory inventory, not a case count: entries have not been clinically validated, links and exact genotype coverage were not checked, and lack of a recorded condition must not be treated as absence of insomnia. Download labels do not prove a usable genotype file. Independence from discovery participants or relatives is unverified.

The snapshot has 2,890 distinct people with any survey row, 1,055 with a condition row, and 866 with an uploaded-data row. These groups overlap and have different levels of information. A targeted keyword scan of the 29 PDF survey forms listed in the project's [survey repository](https://github.com/PGPHarvard/pgp-surveys/tree/master/Surveys) found lines mentioning sleep apnea and recurrent sleep paralysis, but no exact matches for the scanned insomnia, frailty and Fried-component terms. All 29 yielded extractable text; the scan is not a complete semantic review and does not rule out relevant questions, uploaded records, or phenotype data elsewhere. In particular it does not establish that FI, five-component FFS, or suitable negative insomnia controls can be constructed.

The snapshot's 23andMe, Complete Genomics and Illumina metadata list URLs for 606, 220 and 10 people respectively; the categories can overlap. Only six, six and zero respectively also have an insomnia-labeled condition. No conclusions about other variants or phenotype availability follow from these counts. A 2016 FAQ describes genotype formats that may require conversion; present file access and current sharing status remain to be verified. Historical snapshots may not reflect later withdrawal or changes to what participants choose to share.

The project portal's survey and genetic-data endpoints returned TLS errors from this environment when checked; a separate snapshot was directly accessible. No individual-level associations were computed or participant records published. The source archive was hashed locally, without an independently verified publisher checksum. Scripts `stage6_pgp_survey_inventory.py` and `stage6_pgp_snapshot_feasibility.py` generate the aggregated inventories and receipts; the latter requires redownloading the public snapshot to `work/stage6_pgp_untap_snapshot.sqlite3.gz`.

**Assessment:** this particular snapshot does not support a credible, adequately powered independent evaluation of the proposed insomnia–frailty scores. It does not prove that the complete or current PGP cannot support an exploratory analysis. A viable test still needs verified accessible genotype files, exact score-allele coverage, a prespecified frailty or insomnia outcome and controls, sufficient outcomes, and assessed overlap. Public summary data and molecular results cannot substitute for such a test.

## 2026 public-directory recheck

On 29 October 2026, the [public genetic-data directory](https://my.pgp-hms.org/public_genetic_data) appeared in a web search index with entries dated into 2026, indicating the historical snapshot used above should not be treated as a complete current inventory. However, direct HTTPS requests from this environment failed certificate hostname verification, so the directory's live contents, current individual file access, consent status, candidate allele coverage and outcome linkage were not verified. A search-indexed individual insomnia entry is not an adequate case-control cohort, and an unrecorded condition cannot be assumed absent. No participant identifiers or records were downloaded in this recheck. The genuine open-consent model makes PGP a continuing lead, but its current fitness for the project remains unestablished.

## PGP Canada

The [PGP Canada site](https://personalgenomes.ca/) states that its former genomic-data application has been retired and that a replacement public repository is being prepared. The inaugural publication described 56 participants; neither that count nor the project description establishes linked insomnia and FI/FFS outcomes or current score-variant access. The published site did not identify a usable replacement file at the time checked. No contact was made and no participant data were obtained; this is a possible future lead, not a presently established validation cohort.
