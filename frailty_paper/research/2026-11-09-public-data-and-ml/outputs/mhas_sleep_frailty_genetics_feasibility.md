# Mexican Health and Aging Study: conditional genetic-data lead

Checked 29 October 2026. MHAS is a potentially relevant longitudinal cohort, but **no freely usable linked genetic dataset has been established for this project, and no participant records were accessed**.

The [MHAS genetic data profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11653003/) describes free survey databases and documentation for registered users; venous-blood genomic data via NIAGADS, and saliva genomic data under a separate restricted-use agreement with MHAS. An outside [dataset directory](https://atlaslongitudinaldatasets.ac.uk/datasets/mhas) reports no fee, but that does not by itself verify the cost, infrastructure or eligibility of either specific genetic-data route. The [NIAGADS cohort record](https://dss.niagads.org/cohorts/mexican-health-and-aging-study-mhas/) identifies the cohort; verify the exact released dataset, sample, data-use restrictions and linked phenotype access before treating it as usable.

Crucial use restriction: the live [MHAS linkage page](https://www.mhasweb.org/DataProducts/Linkages.aspx) states that accessing the distributed data constitutes agreement to its terms and that LLMs or other AI tools may not manage, process or analyze MHAS-distributed data, including public and restricted microdata. We have not accessed such data or accepted those terms. This note uses public documentation and papers only. A human-led analysis may be a possible route subject to the current terms and required agreements; the rules for any data obtained directly from another distributor must be checked separately. No claim is made that an AI-assisted microdata analysis is permissible.

[Published work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7493650/) constructed a 35-deficit FI from the 2012 survey; it includes fatigue and effort-related questions. A separate published [FRAIL-scale study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4899317/) uses fatigue, stair difficulty, walking, weight loss and illness burden. The FRAIL scale is not the five-component Fried phenotype. The [public constructed-variable guide](https://enasem.org/Resources/DATA/Constructed_Imputed/Created_Variables/MHAS_Created_Variables_Descriptive_Document.pdf) documents difficulty falling asleep, a symptom rather than a formal insomnia diagnosis. The [NIA Biomarker Network](https://biomarker-network.isr.umich.edu/studies-with-biomarkers/mhas/) lists timed walk and grip and describes genetic collections from 2012 blood and 2018 saliva. These reports establish measures in the study, not that the same people have all requested outcomes and genotypes or that all measures are in the released genetic data.

For feasibility, determine exact linkage keys and whether a specific approved genetic release includes sleep, FI/FRAIL and physical measures for the same participants at appropriate waves; examine the FI item list for explicit sleep overlap, fatigue and missingness; confirm number of usable subjects and cases, target SNP contrasts/imputation quality, data restrictions and fees. The Mexican population also provides a potential cross-population transportability evaluation, requiring appropriate local ancestry, kinship and sample-overlap handling. A no-fee option remains conditional rather than established, and score performance has not been evaluated.
