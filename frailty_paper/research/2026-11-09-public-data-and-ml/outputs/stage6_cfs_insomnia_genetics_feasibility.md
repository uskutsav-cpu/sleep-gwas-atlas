# Cleveland Family Study: insomnia and genomics feasibility

The [Cleveland Family Study (CFS)](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/cfs) is a family-based cohort initially selected around sleep-apnea cases and neighborhood control families. The NIH [TOPMed CFS release phs000954.v5.p2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000954.v5.p2) describes whole-genome-sequenced participants, GRCh38 Freeze 9b and 10b call sets, and points to phs000284 for comprehensive phenotypic and pedigree data. The two call sets have largely overlapping samples and should not be counted independently. This is a controlled-access genomic route, not freely downloadable participant data; the exact project cost, eligibility and permission to link releases were not established.

The public [NSRR variable page](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/cfs/variables/insodiag) lists a field labeled physician-diagnosed insomnia and a visit-5 distribution of **35 yes**, **690 no**, **8 don't know**, and **2 unknown**, total 735. This is a catalog distribution, not a genotype-linked case count; diagnosis assessment and coding should be checked in the original instrument. An age breakdown on the same page lists 32 of the 35 positive responses in known age categories, with only three in ages 65–74 and none shown in 75+; three positive responses have age outside that breakdown. The sample was selected for genetic informativeness of sleep-apnea pedigrees, not to represent the general older population. Even before genotype or frailty restrictions, the small recorded case count and much smaller known older subset limit potential score evaluation. The 735 rows are the selected clinical-visit group, not the 1,293 consented TOPMed subjects or the entire 2,284-person cohort.

Public variable search lists a physician-diagnosed insomnia field and sleep symptoms, exercise, fatigue and self-reported walking limitations. These descriptions do not establish a complete FI or five-component Fried score, nor that the relevant fields and exact candidate genotypes are present for the same people. Family relationships, ancestry, sleep-apnea ascertainment, discovery-sample overlap, age and survey selection require appropriate design. Given the case counts and absent established frailty measure, CFS is a secondary sleep-phenotype lead, not a high-confidence joint score-validation dataset. No patient-level data or scores were accessed.

A future feasible analysis would first request non-identifying aggregate counts by age, insomnia field, genotype completeness, study consent and family, then assess variant-level quality and an appropriately independent matched outcome. No = physician-diagnosis field absent is not equivalent to clinically confirmed absence of insomnia; don't-know/unknown should not be coded as controls.
