# Harmonized frailty index across eight aging surveys

[Seligman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glaf217) published a 30-item deficit-accumulation frailty index (FI) using common variables across CHARLS, ELSA, ELSI, HRS, LASI, MHAS, SHARE and TILDA. They use LASI wave 1 (2017–18). This provides a published starting point for comparing frailty across cohorts, including some of our candidate genetic-validation studies, but **the index includes a sleep item**. [Supplementary Table S2](https://academic.oup.com/biomedgerontology/article/81/3/glaf217/8280509) labels it `Restless Sleep`; Table S3 labels it `Difficult Sleeping`. For LASI, S3 describes sleep in the past week recoded to more than occasionally. This timeframe differs from the prior-month `HT219`–`HT222` questions in the official [LASI instrument](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_W1_Individual_Questionnaire.pdf); the precise LASI field and actual identity of the two sleep constructs are **not established**. The main article says a four-level LASI item was recoded with only the lowest category as zero and all other symptom categories as one, while S3 describes a more-than-occasional cutoff. Do not silently choose between these descriptions; obtain the exact field and reproducible scoring code or clarify the discrepancy before reconstruction.

Thus an association between an insomnia-symptom measure and the published 30-item FI can involve directly overlapping content if the same sleep field is used; related sleep constructs can also correlate even without exact item reuse. A sensitivity that removes the sleep item would create a **new 29-item adaptation**, whose missing-data rules, denominator, properties and thresholds require prespecification; it is not automatically the published or validated index. Related symptoms, measurement overlap and cohort-specific wording remain. The index differs from both the published LASI 40-item FI and the adapted five-component physical phenotype, and its use does not establish that either FI or physical frailty is more genetically related to insomnia.

The study used mortality follow-up in **HRS, MHAS, SHARE and TILDA**, not LASI, and reports associations for those surveys; do not attribute those validation results to LASI. It reports a total of 184,715 respondents across all eight surveys, not genotyped, independent or complete-case counts for this project. Applicable access agreements and linkage permissions differ by cohort; in particular, existing [MHAS restrictions](mhas_sleep_frailty_genetics_feasibility.md) must be observed. No source establishes that participant genotypes or project score effects are publicly available. A comparison of the adapted indices against the same outcomes across approved independent cohorts would require actual data, participant and relative overlap checks, allele QC and uncertainty, and is not yet performed.

## Source-field cross-check

The Gateway's [cross-study questionnaire](https://g2aging.org/cms/download/user-guide/Gateway_Questionnaire.pdf) describes a past-week CES-D `sleep was restless` question in a four-category format and lists LASI among comparable studies. An older [LASI pilot instrument](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/IIPS-LASI_Pilot_Instrument_2010.pdf) also includes restless sleep, but it is the **2010 pilot**, not proof of a field in the 2017–19 wave-1 release. The official wave-1 instrument already inspected contains the prior-month `HT219`–`HT222` sleep questions and a past-week ten-question CES-D block, but the latter does not explicitly list restless sleep. A separate [cross-national CES-D study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12509702/) reports no restless-sleep entry for LASI-DAD in its item table, although LASI-DAD and parent LASI are different instruments and that omission cannot determine the parent dataset's content. Together these are reasons to check the **actual harmonized LASI wave-1 codebook and creation code** rather than assigning a pilot question or interpreting the Gateway's comparative label as an authenticated raw field. The paper's recoding discrepancy also remains unresolved. No variable was imputed or substituted.
