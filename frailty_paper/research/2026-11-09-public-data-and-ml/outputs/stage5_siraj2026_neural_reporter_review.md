# Neuronal-cell-line reporter follow-up

The public processed MPRA results from [Siraj et al. (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10121-6) include a human SK-N-SH neuroblastoma cell line. The authors deposited their processed files in [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15297965). We retrieved all nine SK-N-SH allele-differential result files from the public archive using byte-range reads, with ZIP CRC checks, and retained the source filenames and SHA-256 hashes in `stage5_siraj2026_neural_source_receipt.json`.

Of 488 queue variants, 433 had a previously validated reciprocal GRCh37 mapping with reference checked (405 SNVs and 28 normalized indels). Exact chromosome, position, REF and ALT matching against the original GRCh37 assay labels yielded 66 rows representing **52 distinct variants**. The other 55 queue variants were not evaluated in this cross-build screen. Fourteen additional rows arise from variants assayed in more than one library; they are not independent variants. See `stage5_siraj2026_neural_coverage.csv` and `stage5_siraj2026_neural_mpra_exact_alleles.csv`.

A transparent source-row screen approximating the paper's stated criteria requires an allele's absolute log2 activity at least 1 with -log10 Bonferroni-adjusted p at least 2 and the active-set allelic-skew -log10 FDR at least 1. Seven rows for **six distinct variants** pass that screen. This is an exploratory reclassification of source rows. A subsequent check of the authors' two processed analysis files applied their `best_library == 1` and `type != other_test` filters: 129 selected rows cover 42 of these variants, and the published-code activity and skew rules identify five of the six preliminary candidates. The sixth, the SMAD5-region alternative discussed below, is not present in that selected subset; its raw reporter result remains a separate exploratory observation, not a selected study hit. Repeated rows reflect traits and tissues, not independent experiments. All thresholds and original columns are retained in `stage5_siraj2026_neural_mpra_model_comparison.csv`. The selected rows are in `stage5_siraj2026_selected_library_records.csv`, and both original processed-file hashes are in `stage5_siraj2026_best_library_source_receipt.json`. The paper's [public analysis code](https://github.com/julirsch/finemapped_mpra/blob/main/code/preprocess/read_in_functions.R) documents the activity and FDR definitions.

**SMAD5-region alternative chr5:136207208:G>A** passes the source-row screen in SK-N-SH: the reported log2 skew is -1.0048, and active-set -log10 FDR is 21.1766. It belongs to the project's naïve CD4 SMAD5 molecular credible set (PIP 0.0148); Jansen insomnia p = 3.10 × 10⁻⁶, FinnGen insomnia p = 0.0091 and Mak FI p = 0.000986 are postselected associations on different scales, not evidence of colocalization or causality. The AlphaGenome SK-N-SH DNase score for this allele is -0.02156. The reporter uses a short cloned sequence; DNase predicts native chromatin accessibility. Despite the matched cell-line label, these are different assays and the original source's A/B-to-REF/ALT direction and final library-selection rules need verification before interpreting signs as concordant. The assay does not prove a SMAD5 target, a healthy neuronal effect, or a shared sleep–frailty mechanism.

**rs323509** was also tested in SK-N-SH; its source row does not pass this active-and-skew screen (reported log2 skew 0.208, active-set -log10 FDR 0.358). This is not proof of a zero effect in other cell types, a direct test of the previously reported testis splice event, or a causal assessment. The primary FOXP2 insertion and original SMAD5 marker did not yield matched SK-N-SH rows in the 433-variant reference-checked screen.

The SK-N-SH cell line is a neuroblastoma-derived model, not normal brain tissue. The study's reporter measurements are a distinct experimental assay; we have not established that the particular variants, cell-line data or genomic regions were excluded from AlphaGenome training or benchmarking. In particular, `ALL_FOLDS` model predictions are not held-out sequence evaluation. The results are postselected and repeated libraries and related datasets must not be counted as independent samples or used to estimate model accuracy without a prespecified design.

To reproduce, from the project root run `python3 outputs/stage5_siraj_fetch_neural.py PATH_TO_EMPTY_DIRECTORY`, then `python3 outputs/stage5_siraj_neural_reporter_matches.py --directory PATH_TO_EMPTY_DIRECTORY` and `python3 outputs/stage5_compare_siraj_neural_alphagenome.py --store PATH_TO_LIVE_ALPHAGENOME_RESULTS`. The first command uses public byte-range access to the ZIP archive and checks ZIP integrity; an empty destination avoids treating stale files as current. The original GRCh37 matching and reference-check files are retained in `outputs/`; selected data files and source URLs are recorded in the receipt.

For the selected-library cross-check, obtain the two processed RDS files recorded in `stage5_siraj2026_best_library_source_receipt.json`, then run `Rscript outputs/stage5_siraj_best_library_extract.R PATH_TO_GTEX_RDS GTEX_OUTPUT.csv` and the same command with the trait RDS and a distinct output filename. Combine with `python3 outputs/stage5_siraj_combine_selected.py --gtex GTEX_OUTPUT.csv --traits TRAITS_OUTPUT.csv`. The phenotype and gene rows are retained as reported; deduplicate by variant when counting variants. The selected-library annotation in the comparison table is a variant-level summary, not a claim that each raw library row was selected.
