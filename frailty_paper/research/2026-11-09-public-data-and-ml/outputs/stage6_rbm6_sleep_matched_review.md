# RBM6: insomnia studies compared on matched variants

## Result

Using the same 960 variants, GTEx cortex RBM6 expression and one Jansen insomnia signal yielded PP.H4=0.922 and PP.H3=0.078 at p1=p2=0.0001 and p12=0.00001. The cortex expression and MVP clinical-insomnia pair yielded PP.H4=0.090 and PP.H3=0.884 at the same prior. With p12 from 0.000001 to 0.00005, the respective PP.H4 ranges are 0.542–0.983 and 0.0098–0.330. A second Jansen signal favors distinct variants from the cortex signal under this model.

The difference in pairwise posterior persists when the two comparisons use the same variants, cortex data and external NFE LD. It is **not a formal test of heterogeneity**, nor evidence of why the results differ: the insomnia definitions, participants, effective sample sizes, effect-statistic construction and other study properties differ. The two comparisons also share the same cortex data, and the cohorts' complete participant and relatedness non-overlap has not been established. A direct Jansen–MVP signal pair gives PP.H4=0.448 and PP.H3=0.535 at the illustrated prior, with PP.H4 0.075–0.802 over the three priors; this is inconclusive and prior-sensitive.

The matched set **omits rs10049477**, the published RBM6 cortex eQTL credible set's top variant (PIP 0.503), because it is absent from the cached Jansen regional data. It retains 39 of 49 unique variants in that published expression set, whose reported PIPs sum to 0.387 of 0.953 in the set. Consequently, neither a high Jansen posterior nor a low MVP posterior here resolves whether the full expression signal is shared. The separately analyzed MVP set includes the leading variant and still favors distinct expression and insomnia signals at the illustrated prior; its different support should not be interpreted as a controlled effect of adding one SNP. See [the expanded MVP analysis](stage6_rbm6_gtex_mvp_coloc_review.md).

The signed marginal Z scores for Jansen and MVP have Pearson correlation 0.842 over the 960 matched variants. This is descriptive: nearby variants are correlated by LD and do not represent 960 independent observations. It does not test replication or establish a shared causal variant. These analyses do not assess whether the 15- or 22-marker scores predict individual outcomes.

## Method and reproducibility

The intersection is drawn from the previously aligned 985-variant Jansen–cortex input and 1,000-variant MVP–cortex–frailty input. Exact GRCh38 variant, effect-allele and NFE reference-index agreement was checked. Maximum study/reference allele-frequency differences among retained variants were 0.0285 for Jansen and 0.0262 for MVP. QTL, both sleep studies and the signed gnomAD NFE LD matrix have the same ordering; external LD and frequency agreement do not ensure study-matched LD. The Jansen sample size is effective N=313,750 and MVP effective N≈253,769; MVP signed Z was reconstructed from p and the sign of a rounded odds ratio because usable standard errors were unavailable. QTL N=205. See the separate source reviews for ascertainment and provenance.

SuSiE-RSS used L=10, 95% credible sets and fixed residual variance; all three fits converged and yielded one cortex, two Jansen and one MVP credible set. Pairwise coloc was repeated at the three stated priors. The original published cortex top variant and other alternatives remain missing; analysis was postselected; the custom Hail-index decoder was not independently verified with Hail; bulk cortex does not locate the relevant cell type. Comparison does not establish a causal RBM6 mechanism or replace independent patient-level validation.

`stage6_rbm6_sleep_matched_prepare.py` produces the aligned input and coverage receipt; `stage6_rbm6_sleep_matched_coloc.R` writes diagnostics, set members and all prior-sensitive pairwise posteriors. It subsets the previously saved 985-variant Jansen reference matrix using checked row IDs. Earlier extraction and source audits are documented in the [Jansen review](stage6_rbm6_gtex_jansen_coloc_review.md) and [MVP review](stage6_rbm6_gtex_mvp_coloc_review.md).
