# Genes & Health: free summary-data lead and individual-level access

29 October 2026. [Genes & Health](https://www.genesandhealth.org/researchers/data/) describes a community-based cohort with genome-wide and linked health data and says precomputed GWAS results are freely downloadable under a CC BY-SA licence from `gs://genesandhealth_publicdatasets/`. The [GWASStudio project documentation](https://ht-diva.github.io/gwasstudio/projects/) describes 762 three-character ICD-10 binary-trait and 107 median quantitative-trait summaries from its 2025 release, based on British Bangladeshi and British Pakistani participants and GRCh38. Its deployment is described as on-premises; the documentation does not itself provide public access to those data. These are leads for ancestry-aware **variant-level** follow-up and are not individual-level score validation.

The source lists three-character ICD-10 traits in general. It is not established here that the cohort's release contains G47 sleep disorders, F51 nonorganic sleep disorders, R53 malaise/fatigue or R54 senility, that any particular code has an adequate case count, or that records are available for our variants. Even if present, broad sleep-disorder and symptom codes are not equivalent to insomnia, FI, Fried frailty or a joint outcome. Determine exact phenotype composition, case/control counts, model, sample overlap, access conditions, cohort ancestry, allele and build before any comparison; do not treat a code name as a validated phenotype.

An anonymous Google Cloud Storage API listing of the named public bucket returned HTTP 403 with reason `vpcServiceControls` from this environment. No file list or candidate association was retrieved. This does not prove the advertised resource is generally unavailable or that all individual objects are inaccessible; access through an authorized published path or another legitimate public distribution should be investigated. Do not bypass the organization's access controls. Published [Genes & Health blood-trait GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-53091-x) are separately reported as downloadable from GWAS Catalog and a paper-specific repository, but blood traits are not the requested sleep or frailty validation outcomes.

The [provider's access page](https://www.genesandhealth.org/researchers/apply-for-access/) describes participant-level research in a controlled Trusted Research Environment, subject to an application, institutional agreement and fees. The published fee schedule updated 10 November 2025 states £5,000 plus VAT setup per institution and £1,000 plus VAT per user per year for academic-only data access, with a reduced repeat-applicant setup fee. These are provider-listed prices, not a project quote, and may change. The [volunteer data page](https://www.genesandhealth.org/volunteer/your-data/) says researchers cannot download participant-level records. Therefore, this does not meet the user's current free open individual-level validation requirement despite the provider's use of “open access” in some genetic-data descriptions.

**Next actionable check:** find a provider-supported publicly accessible phenotype manifest or published equivalent for the 2025 release, then check candidate outcome availability and exact alleles. This could supplement existing summary-level analyses; it would not establish score calibration, discrimination, or independent predictive accuracy.
