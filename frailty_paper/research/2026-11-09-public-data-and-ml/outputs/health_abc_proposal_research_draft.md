# Draft research proposal: insomnia symptoms, physical frailty and genetic scores in Health ABC

**Working draft only; not submitted.** Prepared against the [NIA Health ABC proposal requirements](https://www.nia.nih.gov/healthabc-study). Sponsor, applicant details, release availability, institutional approvals and costs remain unconfirmed. An outside first author needs a Health ABC sponsor under the posted process. The Health ABC sponsor has not been selected or contacted.

## Research question

Are fixed research genetic scores developed from insomnia and frailty GWAS associated with (1) insomnia symptoms and (2) a prespecified physical-frailty measure in Health ABC? How sensitive are frailty results to the definition used, and do score associations differ by ancestry-related context where sample size permits? This is exploratory external evaluation, not clinical risk prediction or causal inference.

## Background and rationale

Sleep difficulties and physical frailty frequently coexist, and large GWAS show genetic correlation. Some frailty indices include sleep or exhaustion questions, complicating interpretation. Health ABC has reported insomnia symptoms and physical frailty in older adults and offers a genotyping release under general research-use conditions. Its initial cohort was selected to be free of mobility limitation, and its sample includes Black and White participants from two U.S. sites, so its findings may not generalize to all older adults. Phenotypic and genomic data are separately governed; a permissible linkage and actual usable intersection have not been confirmed.

## Proposed variables and design

Request documentation and authorized access to the genotype release, the published year-5 insomnia-symptom questions, demographic variables, physical-frailty components and their dates, ancestry and technical covariates, and information needed to assess overlapping discovery samples. Verify whether matching participants can be joined using authorized project identifiers; do not seek identifying information. Check whether the phenotypes occur at the same visit or whether sleep precedes a later frailty assessment. Do not claim a prospective analysis if the frailty measure precedes sleep.

The proposed exposures are the frozen 15-marker weighted sums and 22-marker equal-weight count or separate weighted sums; they have different allele and weight conventions. The team must select one primary score, one primary endpoint and a multiple-testing strategy before examining Health ABC outcomes. Confirm source effect-allele orientation, genome build, the exact multiallelic contrast, participant ploidy, genotype and imputation quality, marker missingness and discovery/selection/relative overlap. Exclude a comparison from independent-validation claims where overlap cannot be ruled out. Report any partial score separately from the full prototype.

Use a published reproducible frailty construction if inputs exist, noting that the 10-point SAVE and two-component mobility definitions are not identical to the original five-component Fried score. Assess insomnia symptoms separately from sleep duration. If an FI is included, inspect it for sleep and exhaustion items and prespecify item-exclusion sensitivities with recalculated denominators. Adjust for age, sex, site, genetic ancestry PCs and genotyping factors as appropriate; report ancestry-specific estimates only where sample sizes support them and without equating self-identified race to genetic ancestry. Compare a baseline model with and without the same fixed score on the same participants and report uncertainty, discrimination and calibration where outcome type and events allow. Account for missingness, survivorship and study selection; avoid causal or clinical claims.
