# MrOS score evaluation: access and analysis brief

**Status:** Draft for an eligible institutional investigator. No access request has been submitted, no individual data have been obtained, and neither research score has been validated.

## Research question and data

Evaluate prespecified insomnia and physical-frailty outcomes against the project's frozen 15-marker weighted sums and 22-marker count in older men, accounting for ancestry and relevant clinical variables. MrOS is a selected male cohort; its results would not establish performance in women or other populations. An independent FI comparison is not established for this cohort and would require separate deficit data and definitions.

The candidate controlled-access genetic source is [dbGaP phs000373](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs000373.v1.p1), which describes QC-cleaned PLINK files. [NCBI](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/ncbi/workshops/2023-05_genetic-disorder-mechanisms/supplemental-material/Flyer_Pharmocogenetics_CaseStudy.pdf) describes dbGaP data as no-cost; local computing and additional releases may have costs. [NIH eligibility rules](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/accessing-data/dbgap) require an eligible investigator, a signing official and approval. Review the [consent-group addendum](https://dbgap.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/aa/wga.cgi?stacc=phs000373.v1.p1&view_pdf=): groups 1 and 3 list relevant domains, while group 2 is restricted to other conditions. Applicability is determined through the approval process.

## Files and checks to resolve before committing to analysis

1. Identify current approved genotype releases, sample and variant metadata, build, alleles, imputation/INFO, missingness and consent groups. Direct genotypes alone might not cover every candidate; do not assume imputation is included in the listed PLINK release.
2. Confirm permitted same-person identifiers and availability for sleep visit 2 (2009–2012), including `SLISISCR` and its components, and visit 4 (2014–2016) physical-frailty components. The [analyst guide](https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/studies/mros/documents/download/Visit_Information.pdf/) says its calculated CHS-like frailty variables are not released and directs investigators to the coordinating center. Determine whether a later derived phenotype or all necessary raw items and definitions are accessible, and whether a separate charge applies. NSRR's MrOS release does not include actigraphy results.
3. Before accessing outcome data, audit discovery and selection overlap, close relatives and prior project analyses. MrOS contributed to the post-selection CHARGE gait screen; determine whether any decisions used those results. Do not represent a tuned evaluation as untouched.
4. Reconcile the score's counted alleles, multiallelic contrasts and FFS weight direction using primary source data. The [shared-marker crosswalk](score_15_22_shared_marker_crosswalk.md) documents discrepancies; the scripts cannot infer biallelic genotype representation from two labels.
5. Obtain aggregate counts after consent, genotype QC, outcome and visit linkage: enrolled, genotyped, sleep visit 2, visit 4, eligible events and missingness, with ancestry and relatedness summaries where permitted. Existing published sample counts refer to different analyses and cannot be intersected by assumption.

## Proposed evaluation after approval

Define separate endpoints: insomnia severity at sleep visit 2 and a visit-4 physical frailty phenotype with exact components, time window and cutpoints documented. Prespecify the treatment of baseline frailty, death before visit 4, inability to complete performance tests, missing data and date ordering; a survivor-only analysis answers a narrower question. If data permit, examine sleep symptoms and the MEIS1 marker with sensitivity to restless-legs/limb-movement measures without treating adjustment as proof of mechanism. The [detailed feasibility review](independent_outcome_validation_cohort_feasibility.md) describes visit-specific limitations and published precedent.

On the same eligible participants, compare a prespecified clinical/demographic baseline with the baseline plus each frozen score. Report effect estimates, uncertainty, model fit, discrimination and calibration as appropriate for each outcome; use paired uncertainty for comparisons, and avoid combining weights across different trait scales. Define a holdout before any tuning and acknowledge that actual power depends on the unknown sample and events. No existing result in this workspace establishes this performance.

## Array information

A [genetics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8807892/) describes the MrOS array as Illumina HumanOmni1-Quad v1.0 H; a [different study's supplement](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/22165296/GEFOS_Seq_SupplText.pdf) specifies v1.0 B. These descriptions may refer to different manifest revisions or analyses; neither establishes which manifest and post-QC marker set applies to the requested dbGaP release. Published MrOS analyses also report imputation, but that does not establish availability or quality in a particular release. The [manufacturer product page](https://emea.support.illumina.com/downloads/humanomni1-quadv1_product_files.html) lists a CSV manifest; its broader [download terms](https://support.illumina.com/array/downloads.html) restrict product files to customers and use with Illumina products and services. We did not assume eligibility under those terms or scan the file. A public variant-level manifest for the exact requested release remains unverified; ask the approved data custodian for its marker inventory and contrast-level QC rather than inferring full coverage from the chip name.

## Public dbGaP release audit (29 October 2026)

The [2017 study configuration report](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000373/phs000373.v1.p1/manifest/Study_Report.phs000373.OsteoporoticFractures_MrOS.v1.p1.MULTI.pdf) lists a 115 MB genotype marker-information archive `phg000979.v1.NIAMS_OsteoporoticFrctrs_MrOS.marker-info.MULTI.tar`, plus a QC archive and consent-specific genotype files. These are named in the report's distribution inventory; this audit did not establish public download access to their contents or inspect variant rows. The public [release directory](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000373/phs000373.v1.p1/) lists reports, phenotype summaries and documents, but no directly listed marker archive. Request the marker-information archive through the authorized route if permitted; do not assume its existence establishes coverage.

The public [subject-phenotype dictionary](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000373/phs000373.v1.p1/pheno_variable_summaries/phs000373.v1.pht007129.v1.MrOS_Subject_Phenotypes.data_dict.xml) contains 120 variables. An XML exact-name check found `GSGRPAVG`, `NFWLKSPD`, and `VSAGE1`, but **not** `SLISISCR`, `SLISICAT`, or `PASCORE`. It also lists sleep-efficiency and other sleep variables. This is a single 2017 dictionary, not an exhaustive audit of other repositories, possible aliases, current releases, or individual completeness; a missing exact name does not prove the phenotype was never collected. It does show that the planned visit-2 ISI and PASE should not be assumed present in this named phenotype table. Confirm access to the exact later-visit datasets and permitted linkage before promising the proposed evaluation.

The matching public [variable report](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/dbgap/studies/phs000373/phs000373.v1.p1/pheno_variable_summaries/phs000373.v1.pht007129.v1.p1.MrOS_Subject_Phenotypes.var_report.xml) records 2,543 non-null sleep-efficiency observations among 5,130 report subjects: 2,120 in c1 and 423 in c3, and zero in c2. Sleep-visit age is reported for 2,609 subjects overall. These are separate marginal counts, not a count with both measures or with usable genotypes, required SNPs or frailty outcomes; the zero for c2 is not evidence of an outcome result. Consent and release-specific rules still control use. For `NFWLKSPD`, the report says the original type is decimal but its calculated summary type is integer, so do not use that report's rounded distribution as individual walking speeds. The [aggregate extraction](mros_dbgap_public_variable_counts.csv) preserves reported and calculated types and counts for seven sleep and physical-function variables across the total and consent subsets. No participant-level data were downloaded.

The marginal counts do permit a **mathematical bound**, assuming the report's non-null counts use the same subject universe within each consent set. In c1, sleep efficiency is non-null for 2,120 and grip for 4,199 of 4,295 subjects, so at least 2,024 and at most 2,120 have both. In c3, the corresponding bound is 417–423 of 431. Since c2 has no reported sleep-efficiency values, summing gives **2,441–2,543** people with both variables in this public phenotype report. This is not the observed overlap, a sleep–frailty sample, or a genotype-qualified sample: grip alone is not Fried frailty, the measurement visits and imputed-variant availability are unverified, and project-specific consent eligibility and identity linkage still require approval.
