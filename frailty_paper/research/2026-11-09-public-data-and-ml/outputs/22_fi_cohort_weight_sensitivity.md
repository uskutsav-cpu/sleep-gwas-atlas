# FI score weights: UK Biobank versus TwinGene sensitivity

The same 22 candidate genotypes give **very different constructed FI sums** depending on whether the weights come from the UK Biobank or TwinGene component of the FI study. In 503 European-labeled 1000 Genomes reference individuals, the correlation between the UKB-weighted and TwinGene-weighted sums is **−0.455**. The correlation between the UKB-weighted and published meta-analysis-weighted sums is **0.996**; that similarity is expected in part because UKB contributes much more precision to the meta-analysis. The meta- and TwinGene-weighted sums correlate at −0.379.

These are correlations of numerical genotype sums in a reference panel with **no frailty outcomes**. They are not genetic correlations, predictive performance, proof of opposite biological effects, or an individual risk estimate. The 22 candidates were selected using results connected to the FI meta-analysis and later follow-up, so UKB and TwinGene weights are not equally independent of selection. TwinGene is much smaller and its effects are noisier; effect estimates can differ due to chance, phenotype differences, selection and technical factors. The sample contains multiple EUR subgroups and potentially related people; the correlation is descriptive and not a population-wide estimate.

## Why this matters

After aligning the candidate allele pairs and applying the project's inferred TwinGene sign reversal, **16 of 22** UKB and TwinGene FI coefficients have opposite point-estimate signs. A sign count alone is not a heterogeneity test, and many TwinGene estimates are imprecise. In an exploratory calculation using the difference between cohort effects and assuming independent estimates, 5 of 22 comparisons have nominal p < 0.05, and 4 have p < 0.05/22: rs12536335, rs28528997, rs17312861 and rs11636381. These are post-selection numerical checks, not confirmatory heterogeneity findings; sample overlap, scale equivalence, multiple analysis choices and the sign adapter remain unresolved. A large p-value is not evidence that effects are equal.

**The TwinGene sign convention is essential.** Its reversal is supported by reconstruction of the published meta-analysis at these 22 variants and by a prior 97,901-variant audit, but conflicts with the archive README and is not confirmed by the authors. If this adapter is wrong, the TwinGene directions and score correlation change. Original cohort-level effects, SEs, aligned meta effects, and reconstruction results are in [the table](22_fi_cohort_weight_sensitivity.csv); [the earlier audit](twingene_direction_genomewide_audit.md) explains the coding conflict. Original FI cohorts differ in items and separately normalized FI scales; the UKB FI contains insomnia and tiredness, and this analysis did not remove them.

The UKB-only, TwinGene-only and meta FI sums use exactly the same 22 selected alleles and marginal coefficients from the respective FI source. They are not LD-adjusted, calibrated, independently trained, or applied to a frailty endpoint. Matching a biallelic record in the reference panel does not settle multiallelic coding in the original studies. Do not select one of these sums for clinical or individual use; resolve the source allele convention and evaluate a frozen score against outcomes in an independent cohort first.

## Methods and receipt

We scanned all **16,557,800** records in the original Atkins cohort summary-statistics archive. All 22 candidates had a unique match on rsID, assumed GRCh37 chromosome and position, and unordered allele pair; all 22 inverse-variance reconstructions matched the existing meta beta within 0.0001. We then matched each same allele contrast to a biallelic record in 1000 Genomes phase 3 and obtained complete calls in 503 EUR-labeled samples. We calculated each score as the additive sum of allele dosage times the cohort-specific coefficient and took Pearson correlations over individuals. No genotypes or individual scores were retained. [Machine-readable receipt](22_fi_cohort_weight_sensitivity.json); script: `work/score22_cohort_weight_sensitivity.py`. Source archive and provenance are documented in [the six-variant cohort audit](six_leads_fi_cohort_comparison.md).
