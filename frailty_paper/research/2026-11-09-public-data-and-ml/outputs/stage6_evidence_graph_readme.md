# Insomnia–frailty evidence graph: first reproducible artifact

This graph turns the existing, postselected 15- and 22-candidate cross-trait tables and a public skeletal-muscle molecular screen into machine-readable nodes and edges. It contains 33 unique variant nodes, seven study/phenotype nodes, 36 variant-to-nearest-gene links, 180 marginal association links, and 61 links to cell-resolved molecular credible sets. The two candidate lists overlap at four identifiers. A variant may have multiple equally close genes. Inputs are [the 15-candidate table](integrated_15_candidate_review.csv) and [the 22-candidate table](expanded_22_candidate_risk_gene_table.csv); neither is an exhaustive genomic dataset. For four variants in both lists, the graph preserves the 15-table counted allele and does not duplicate 22-table association edges. For other 22-list variants it uses the 22 evidence table's counted allele, which may differ from the 22-marker score count allele; the graph must not be used as a score file.

A direct file audit matched all 22 shared-count rsIDs and assumed GRCh37 coordinates to graph nodes: **14 counted-allele pairs agree and eight are reversed**, with no other pair mismatch. The [crosswalk](graph_vs_shared22_allele_crosswalk.csv) records all pairs and graph identifiers. This compares existing project files, not original study strand, multiallelic representation or participant genotypes. For a verified same biallelic contrast, association-effect signs must be reversed when changing effect allele; genotype dosage conversion has additional biallelic and ploidy requirements. Never infer this solely from an rsID or matching two letters.

* [Nodes](stage6_evidence_graph_nodes.jsonl) and [edges](stage6_evidence_graph_edges.jsonl) are newline-delimited JSON. Each edge records its local source table or molecular-screen receipt and SHA-256; GWAS edges include beta on the stated counted allele, p-value and study caveat. Gene links are explicitly positional and are **not** causal gene assignments. Gene identifiers here are labels, not disambiguated canonical IDs; versions and reference annotations require further work before genome-wide use.
* [Build script](../work/build_insomnia_frailty_evidence_graph.py) checks unique variants, p-value ranges and edge endpoint integrity; [receipt](stage6_evidence_graph_receipt.json) records input hash and counts. Run `python3 work/build_insomnia_frailty_evidence_graph.py` from the project directory.
* The graph separates UKB FI and FFS, UKB insomnia, FinnGen insomnia, MVP insomnia and FinnGen hospital frailty by source and phenotype. FinnGen-insomnia edges are present only for the original 15 variants; their absence for the 18 additional variants reflects input coverage and does not indicate no association. Study samples overlap or overlap is uncertain. Betas have different scales and must not be summed or directly compared; the FFS convention remains inferred and TwinGene coding needs caution. Missing inputs are not evidence of no association. Candidate selection was based on related studies and cannot supply independent outcome labels.
* The skeletal-muscle molecular edges come from the CC BY 4.0 [Varshney skeletal-muscle multiome](https://zenodo.org/records/19670721), using the locally audited expression and chromatin credible-set screens. Three are expression-QTL set memberships, and 58 are chromatin-QTL memberships. Each molecular set links to its reported cell context; expression sets also link to the gene used as the molecular phenotype. Molecular PIP is a conditional within-set probability, not a disease-colocalization probability, and the same signal can appear in multiple sets. A missing edge is not a negative finding. See the [source review](stage6_varshney_skeletal_muscle_qtl_review.md). No AlphaGenome outputs or their derivatives or independent experimental outcome labels are in these files. Their absence must not be interpreted as negative evidence. The graph is a descriptive foundation, not a trained embedding, causal network, risk score or independent validation.

The graph also includes a [TenK10K](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anglixue/TenK10K_multiome) CD4-naïve chromatin association for rs7734045 and the source's **molecular–molecular** colocalization of that peak with SMAD5 expression. These are separate edges: the graph does not assert that the candidate causes a disease or that the disease signals colocalize. The candidate lies outside the peak, the remote full cis-QTL file was not fully checksum-verified, and the molecular-colocalization source's corrected-version status is provisional. The graph also records a separate [disease–molecular sensitivity](stage6_tenk10k_expanded_fi_susie_review.md): under all three tested priors, the expanded SuSiE-coloc model favored distinct FI and chromatin signals, subject to substantial reference-LD mismatch and other limitations. This prevents interpreting the molecular link alone as evidence of a frailty mechanism. The source is CC BY 4.0. See [variant-level evidence](stage6_tenk10k_smad5_variant_caqtl_review.md) and [molecular colocalization caveats](stage6_tenk10k_smad5_molecular_review.md).

## Next step toward an AI benchmark

Extend the graph with further documented, permitted, non-AlphaGenome variant-to-gene and cell-type evidence, retaining genome build, allele, tissue/cell state, assay, donor cohort, signal coverage, direction and missingness. Use a separately collected experimental dataset to define labels, select appropriate matched negative/background examples and avoid incorporating test labels as features. Establish nearest-gene and marginal GWAS baselines; then evaluate any embedding using held-out genomic regions or chromosomes and, where feasible, independent experiments. Compare precision/recall and uncertainty, accounting for LD and study overlap. Separately review AlphaGenome predictions under the applicable terms; do not use its covered outputs for training without resolving those terms. Patient-level predictive validation still requires an independent cohort with appropriate linked genotype and sleep/frailty outcomes.

A [reporter-label feasibility check](stage6_33_candidate_reporter_benchmark_feasibility.md) matched the 33 graph variants against the selected Siraj cell-line reporter subset. Only five variants had selected observations, with no active-and-skew calls; 173 of 198 variant–cell entries were unassessed. This cannot support a supervised benchmark and unassessed entries must not be coded as negatives.

A [newer neuronal reporter resource](stage6_retallick_neuronal_reporter_review.md) provides a potential broader benchmark across baseline and inflammatory conditions. One project variant appears in its design library, but none of the 33 identifiers appeared in the searched published result sheets; that absence is not treated as a negative or a graph edge. Source reuse terms and the full tested universe need review before any benchmark.

The graph also stores the [FOXP2-region disease–disease sensitivity](stage6_foxp2_gnomad_matched_coloc_review.md) as a regional study-to-study relationship, with all three tested priors. This is distinct from molecular colocalization: the fitted insomnia and hospital-frailty signals favored sharing under the stated model, but this does not prove a causal variant, FOXP2 gene, cell type, or individual risk. The selected window, external LD, uncertain cohort overlap and the FI/FFS-versus-HFRS phenotype difference remain material. The region label is descriptive and does not assert regulation of FOXP2.

The graph now includes three postselected rs7734045–SMAD5 single-nucleus eQTL associations from the open [NIAGADS aging-brain atlas](stage6_niagads_xqtl_candidate_review.md): excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons and oligodendrocytes. Each points to a separate source-study/assay context and cell label, with the source GRCh38 contrast and ALT-effect convention retained. It also includes candidate membership in the excitatory and inhibitory molecular credible sets and three prior-specific FI–excitatory-eQTL SuSiE-coloc sensitivity edges. The lack of a candidate oligodendrocyte credible-set edge is **not** a negative label. ROSMAP mega-analysis and component datasets overlap; these edges must not be counted as independent experiments. The FI sensitivity favors distinct signals under its assumptions and is included so the association is not mistaken for disease colocalization. LD mismatch and incomplete coverage limit that analysis; see the [colocalization review](stage6_niagads_smad5_exc_coloc_review.md).

The current graph has 154 nodes and 368 edges; its [receipt](stage6_evidence_graph_receipt.json) records counts. These new molecular links do not establish causality, predict patient outcomes, validate a genetic score, or provide independent ML labels. The graph remains postselected and should be split by locus and source when constructing any later benchmark.

A [Matoba 2026 neural-progenitor reporter audit](stage6_matoba_neural_reporter_benchmark_feasibility.md) identifies a possible broader independent-experiment benchmark with hundreds of reported allelic tests, but no direct project-rsID match in the examined cortical-variant tables. Tested-universe selection, model/sequence overlap, assay comparability and reuse terms remain to be resolved before training or evaluation; missing entries are not negative labels.

A fixed, non-AlphaGenome sequence baseline was subsequently tested on 108 reference-aligned chromosome-1/2 Matoba reporter pairs. The two orientation sensitivities gave AUROC 0.533 and 0.547, respectively; these are post hoc detected-call rankings from a selected small assay set, not reliable validation of disease or score prediction. [External assay review](stage6_matoba_external_baseline_transfer_review.md).

A [split-feasibility audit](stage6_evidence_graph_split_audit.md) identifies 15 represented chromosomes and 25 descriptive 1-Mb proximity groups, and documents how shared study nodes and observed edges could expose information across model splits. It is not an LD partition or a model evaluation; independently ascertained labels and a tested universe are still needed.

A [new excitatory-neuron reporter resource](stage6_salomon_neuronal_reporter_feasibility.md) supplies a larger cross-assay benchmark with accessible processed readouts. An exact coordinate/allele search found no match among the 33 candidate contrasts; untested entries are not negatives. The existing fixed strand-invariant baseline gave AUROC 0.501 on 6,573 selected chromosome 1–2 reporter pairs. This is an exploratory assay-label test, not score validation; data-reuse terms, test-set independence and label design remain material limitations.

## Separate MEIS1 evidence extension

A reproducible [extension builder](../work/build_meis1_graph_extension.py) adds four nodes and 18 edges in separate [node](stage6_meis1_graph_extension_nodes.jsonl) and [edge](stage6_meis1_graph_extension_edges.jsonl) files. It leaves the 33-candidate graph and frozen scores unchanged. The extension represents the insomnia proxy rs13033745 and its sentinel as **additional, non-score variants**, a human neural-progenitor chromatin-contact assay, and a zebrafish ortholog perturbation assay. It preserves raw source-specific effect alleles, scales and p values for six GWAS sources and EUR reference LD r²; the build checks unique nodes, endpoint integrity and p-value ranges. Each GWAS and LD edge cites its local source receipt and SHA-256. Human region–promoter contact and zebrafish gene perturbation are separate relations and do not assert that either score marker was edited or causes disease. See the [MEIS1 source review](stage6_meis1_2026_cross_species_experimental_data.md) for multiallelic caveats and source overlap.

This extension is **descriptive, postselected and not an ML benchmark**. Its small graph is missing a defined tested universe and independent labels; many edges derive from overlapping studies, and the new proxy and sentinel are highly correlated. It must not be randomly split across training and test or used to infer patient risk. External experimental labels, leakage-safe locus-level splits and independent outcome testing remain necessary.
