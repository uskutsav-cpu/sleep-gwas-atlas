# NCDS precomputed scores: two separate products

The [updated Polygenic Index Repository paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12633171/) lists insomnia as a new score phenotype and reports **6,267 NCDS participants** with repository PGIs in its cohort table. It says the N column counts participants for whom table-1 PGIs were supplied after subject filtering. This makes an insomnia-score–frailty analysis a plausible future **background comparison**, but the exact NCDS insomnia score file, participating subset, release-specific weights, phenotype linkage, current access terms and any costs for this particular product were not verified. The paper's insomnia discovery sample includes UK Biobank and 23andMe; check target overlap, relatives and score version before evaluation. That count is not the number with usable age-50 FI and sleep outcomes.

**Supplementary-table follow-up:** the authors' [Supplementary Tables workbook](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-7828579/v1/fbf19035b70b8fb58b216e7e.xlsx), sheet `11. Input GWAS meta-analyses`, specifically lists NCDS (1958BC) among the cohorts assigned `INSOMNIA1`. That row identifies a 23andMe/Jansen-excluding-23andMe meta-analysis of 944,460 and 386,533 participants, respectively, and lists the three UKB partitions as overlapping cohorts. NCDS is not listed in that row's overlap column; this is the authors' listed overlap assessment, not independent proof that no individual or relative overlaps the final analysis. The row is stronger evidence of intended availability of the specific NCDS score, but it does not provide the participant-level file, permissions, joint FI sample or a validation result. The checked workbook was downloaded from the preprint's linked supplementary file, SHA-256 `4106dda76dc2e8dd8d278c0fa5bce795ac57bd71036f1d6f643853cc53c5441c`; the paper describes the meaning of the table's cohort columns. Use and distribution of 23andMe-derived source statistics have separate restrictions as described in the paper; do not assume raw weights can be redistributed.

This repository should not be conflated with the [CLS PGI v1 product](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/PRS.html), which is separately described as TOPMed-based clumping-and-thresholding scores, distributed through the UK Data Service under a Special Licence. Its currently published trait list includes grip strength and longevity but does **not** list an insomnia or frailty score. The [updated repository paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12633171/) describes a distinct SBayesR score-generation approach and a broader trait table including insomnia. CLS's [access documentation](https://cls-genetics.github.io/docs/access.html) identifies application-based access for genotype and Special Licence access for its PGIs; it does not by itself establish how the updated SSGAC insomnia product is distributed for NCDS. The [SSGAC procedure page](https://www.thessgac.org/data-access-procedures) says datasets are distributed by their respective providers but does not list NCDS in the checked cohort-specific sections. Do not infer a freely downloadable linked insomnia-score dataset from either listing.

Even if the broad published insomnia score is obtained, it is **not** either project's 15- or 22-variant prototype and cannot reconstruct them or directly validate their performance. Its use would answer a different question: whether a previously developed insomnia score associates with a carefully defined FI or physical phenotype, preferably including sleep- and fatigue-excluded FI sensitivities. Source definitions, score selection, overlap, covariates and access conditions would have to be fixed and checked first. No data access request, participant data download, or analysis was performed.
