# LBC1936: sleep, physical frailty and genetic validation lead

Checked 29 October 2026. The Lothian Birth Cohort 1936 is a strong **conditional** lead for testing the project's scores because separate published analyses describe genotype data, sleep measurements and an observed Fried physical-frailty phenotype. It is not an openly downloadable linked genotype–phenotype dataset, and the current cost, eligibility and permission for this particular linkage are unverified. No data-access request or contact was sent.

## Why it is relevant

* A [published sleep study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/42/4/zsz019/5298134) describes an adapted Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and Illumina whole-genome SNP data in the cohort. A [cohort profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6124629/) reports the adapted questionnaire at Waves 3 and 4. The questionnaires measure symptoms and sleep patterns, not automatically clinical insomnia.
* A [published longitudinal study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5482391/) reports an adapted five-component Fried phenotype at Waves 1 and 3, and says 697 participants attended Wave 3. This is evidence of the phenotype and wave, **not** a count with complete sleep, genotypes and all our candidate variants. The related published [score comparison](fi_ffs_pgs_published_validation_audit.md) found no explicit sleep/fatigue label in its LBC1936 deficit-FI item inventory, though indirect relationships with sleep remain possible.
* The cohort's official [public data dictionary](https://lothian-birth-cohorts.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2023-11/Lothian%20Birth%20Cohort%201936%20Data%20Dictionary%20Waves%201%20to%204.xls) lists, for Wave 3, sleep quality (`sqquality_w3`), weeknight sleep-onset time (`sqquicklywknights_w3`), awakenings (`sqawakening_w3`), 6-metre walk time (`sixmwk_w3`), and six hand-grip attempts. The extracted [16-field crosswalk](lbc1936_dictionary_wave_crosswalk.csv) gives exact source rows and labels. `phyactiv_w4` is described as “Wave 3” in the dictionary; this apparent label inconsistency requires clarification before use. Dictionary labels alone do not provide response codes, missingness, dates or permission to derive a phenotype.
* Sleep and physical frailty are thus both *described at Wave 3*, but the actual same-person intersection, FI/FFS coding, visit dates and genetic sample remain unknown. Also check whether sleep and fatigue overlap the chosen FI items, whether the published physical-frailty variables can be reconstructed, and whether earlier sleep predicts later rather than concurrently measured frailty. Selection into later waves can bias results.

## Access, independence and analysis constraints

The official [access page](https://lothian-birth-cohorts.ed.ac.uk/data-access-collaboration) calls for a data request identifying variables, a principal researcher and institution, and proposed analysis. Its page did not establish a fee or fee exemption, access to the exact imputed genotypes, allowed linkage, data-use terms or turnaround time. The genome-wide genotyping, WGS, score files and covariates are distinct resources; availability and variant-level imputation/quality for all 33 distinct variants must be confirmed. This is not an anonymous public download and is **not a verified free route**.

LBC1936 contributed to the CHARGE gait GWAS already inspected during this project. This creates a potential selection-history issue but does not prove participants were in the score-discovery GWAS or the final target sample. Check participant and relative overlap across all discovery, selection and follow-up sources; document any prior use of these data before claiming a held-out analysis. Sample size, frail event counts, ancestry PCs, kinship and actual score coverage need verification before choosing models. The participants were born in 1936 and recruited in and around Edinburgh; age, geographic and survival selection limit generalization.

If access and linkage are approved, prespecify a Wave 3 physical-frailty outcome and, where feasible, an FI with explicit sleep/fatigue-item sensitivity analyses. Freeze the existing allele/weight definitions, check coding and marker quality, and compare the scores on the same participants against an appropriate age/sex/ancestry baseline. Prediction, discrimination, calibration and incremental performance require adequate events and uncertainty; association alone is insufficient.

The dictionary extraction used the public XLS, not participant data. SHA-256 `e395e159b071bfaf33017a1c2166476cfbe45c30320f977ceb45d8265193cf00`; script `work/lbc_dictionary_crosswalk.py` reads it using `xlrd`. Exact item phrasing and source row numbers are preserved in the crosswalk. No score has been calculated here.

## Specific whole-genome release and linkage restriction

The EGA study [EGAS00001003819](https://www.ega-archive.org/studies/EGAS00001003819) reports sequencing 1,075 LBC1936 members. Its controlled [joint VCF release EGAD00001005478](https://www.ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00001005478) describes 1,068 LBC1936 samples after joint calling and variant quality recalibration. This is a concrete genetic resource, not a count of Wave 3 sleep/frailty participants and not proof that the 33 variants or allelic contrasts are usable. Genome build, genotype callability, missingness, relative/participant overlap, costs and the ability to extract only project markers have not been verified. The 1,075 sequenced, 1,068 joint-VCF and 697 Wave 3 attendance figures describe different sets and must not be added or treated as the overlap.

The public EGA dataset page shows a **data-access agreement for genetic and phenotypic data**. Its displayed section 2.3 requires prior written consent from Edinburgh before linking to other datasets, including some datasets from the same cohort held by different recipients or under different projects. Its displayed use conditions also restrict access to approved users, institutions and projects and include a return-to-resource condition. These are the public terms for the referenced release; the current executed agreement and any project-specific approvals govern future access. Specifically request approved genotype-to-Wave 3 phenotype linkage rather than assuming the cohort name or an EGA approval makes that linkage permissible. The site does not establish a zero fee for the full linked project. No agreement was signed, data downloaded or application sent.

## Published Wave 3 frailty events and prior genetic sleep work

A [2017 LBC1936 longitudinal frailty paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5482391/) reports that its own complete-case analysis had **594 participants**: 230 non-frail, 279 pre-frail and 85 frail at Wave 3. The study selected participants with the variables required for its cognitive analysis; these counts are **not** the available number for our genotype–sleep–frailty analysis, and the paper does not establish whether these 594 overlap the released WGS sample or have complete sleep data. The 85 frail individuals illustrate why the number of events—not just total genotyped participants—could constrain a score-validation model. This does not establish actual power or a minimum event count for our proposed sample. The authors also reported that people lost to follow-up differed in health and other characteristics, underscoring survivor/attrition selection.

A separate [2019 LBC1936 sleep study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6448287/) used Wave 3 sleep questionnaires and tested pre-existing chronotype and sleep-duration polygenic scores based on genotyping. This demonstrates that a prior sleep–genotype analysis was feasible in some cohort members, but those are different scores and do not establish the usable linked frailty set, project-marker calls or predictive performance. The study reported that its nominal genetic score–sleep associations did not survive its FDR correction. Its sleep data were self-reported and analyzed at one time point. No causal or joint sleep–frailty score conclusion follows.

An [application-ready research outline](lbc1936_validation_research_request_draft.md) specifies the Wave 3 phenotype, marker, cost, linkage and overlap questions. It is an unsent draft without investigator or institutional attestations.

The [published FI item audit](lbc1936_published_fi_item_audit.md) finds no explicit sleep/fatigue term among 30 top-level labels, notes shared physical-function items, and identifies source-table coding ambiguities to resolve before reconstruction.
