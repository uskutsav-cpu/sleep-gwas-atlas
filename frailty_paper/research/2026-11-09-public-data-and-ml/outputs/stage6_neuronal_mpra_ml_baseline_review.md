# Sequence-only baseline on a neuronal reporter assay

This exploratory analysis addresses the mentor's request for an AI direction, not participant-level insomnia–frailty prediction. We used baseline-condition results and paired library sequences from [Retallick-Townsley et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77114-x). The model predicts the author's *detected versus nonsignificant statistical call* in this assay; an undetected call is not proof that a sequence is biologically inactive. These variants were selected for psychiatric-risk and brain-expression evidence, not as a representative genome-wide or insomnia–frailty sample.

The fixed baseline is L2 logistic regression using absolute differences in 1-, 2- and 3-mer counts between reference and alternate 199-base sequences. Training used 801 paired variants from all autosomes except 1 and 2. Chromosomes 1 and 2 were held out entirely: 161 variants, including 32 detected calls. No AlphaGenome outputs, project GWAS results, or pre-trained sequence representations were included. The held-out **AUROC was 0.517** and average precision was **0.298**, compared with detected-call prevalence of **0.199**. The near-chance AUROC and small, selected test set give no compelling basis to deploy or generalize the model; average precision is sensitive to prevalence, and there is no independent cohort test. A constant-score classifier has AUROC 0.5 and average precision equal to prevalence.

Of 2,370 groups with reference/alternate-type rows, 1,357 were excluded for not having exactly one row per allele and 51 for uncertain or missing detection labels; 962 complete groups remained. Duplicate or incomplete observations were **not** collapsed into independent examples. Training-only feature standardization and chromosome-held-out evaluation reduce some leakage, but the same study, assay, donors, and genomic selection inform both sets; chromosome split does not establish independence from unobserved model resources or remove study-specific selection. FDR-based labels can reflect statistical power, not only sequence biology. There was no tuning or comparison to a stronger model, no validation in another study or cell type, and no test of the 33 project variants.

The [analysis plan](stage6_neuronal_mpra_ml_baseline_plan.md) was written before model fitting; [results](stage6_neuronal_mpra_kmer_baseline.json) and [code](../work/neuronal_mpra_kmer_baseline.py) record the fixed method, source hash and exclusions. A future model needs a broader, suitably licensed and independently tested assay set, explicit positive/unlabeled treatment and region-held-out evaluation. Clinical prediction requires separately approved participant-level genotype and outcome data; this analysis does not provide it.

## Conditional test-set uncertainty

A 5,000-resample stratified bootstrap of the same held-out variants gives AUROC 0.517 with a 2.5th–97.5th percentile range of 0.396–0.638, and average precision 0.298 with a range of 0.198–0.441. The model was fixed and the 32/161 detected-call prevalence was held constant in every resample. These ranges describe only sampling variability under exchangeable variant resampling: they do not include training uncertainty, selection of the chromosome split, regional dependence, assay effects, or uncertainty about the labels. With only two held-out chromosomes, reliable chromosome-cluster uncertainty cannot be estimated from this split. The result is consistent with a wide range of performance and does not establish useful discrimination or any relationship to the genetic scores.
