# SLEEP editorial novelty assessment

Date:2026-10-08. Automated evidence-based assessment; not a journal decision, acceptance probability or substitute for human review.

**Current judgment: NO_GO_FOR_SLEEP_AS_CURRENTLY_SUPPORTED on a first-discovery/phenome-screening novelty argument.** An association study may remain scientifically useful, but sufficient novelty for SLEEP is not established by this evidence. It would be misleading to advertise 1,200 tests or23 replicated pairs as23 newly discovered diseases, loci, or mechanisms.

The official [SLEEP author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/pages/author-guidelines), accessed2026-10-08, identify novelty, methodological rigor and sleep-measurement depth as editorial considerations. Comparable broad screens already exist in SLEEP2021 and2024, Communications Biology2025 and2026 primary studies. The2024 SLEEP supplement's1,403 external traits per construct exceed this extension's100 selected outcomes; its single-indicator insomnia/duration comparisons overlap the current analysis more than the historical registry acknowledged. Nature2026 includes prior Dashti–FinnGen disease correlations, and Sun2022 includes prior GERD outcome-side estimates including napping. These concrete table comparisons support concern about incremental science; a negative title search cannot rebut them.

The defensible added value is a versioned, source-audited numerical resource with transparent external-outcome validation, nonidentical-definition sensitivity, heterogeneity diagnostics, null/failed outcomes and reusable provenance. Its editorial value depends on a clearly documented scientific question and insight that changes interpretation. Updated releases alone, a larger formatted table, software test counts and publication-quality graphics do not establish that insight.

Priority cannot be certified for unresolved snoring–cholelithiasis or sleep-apnea–abdominal-pain pairs. New cohort validation of a known relationship can be useful, but its independence and contrast with previous FinnGen/MVP data need explicit evidence. Local or causal analyses should be added only when source-valid and scientifically useful; prestige is not justification.

A conditional human manuscript handoff may be appropriate for transparently scoped association/resource claims after source/statistical qualifications are reconciled. A SLEEP-specific publication GO requires human investigators to decide that the incremental resource merits the target journal and to articulate a substantive advance supported by validated findings. This audit does not invent such an advance.
