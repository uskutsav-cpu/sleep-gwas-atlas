# Fried frailty score effect-allele adapter audit

All **37 of 37** Ye supplementary COJO discovery rows matched unique chromosome, position and variant ID rows in the archived FFS summary file; each had identical `refA`/`A1`, marginal `b`/`BETA` and `se`/`SE` values. The [GCTA COJO documentation](https://yanglab.westlake.edu.cn/software/gcta) identifies `refA` as the effect allele and `b` as the original marginal effect. The [Ye paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/) says the GWAS used BOLT-LMM. The downloaded supplement matches the prior workbook hash; the archived FFS file matches the previous project hash. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [37-row comparison](comparisons.tsv), [code](audit.py) and [receipt](receipt.json) record the audit.

The 37 checks give strong empirical support that the FFS source uses A1 as its effect allele. They do not independently verify the adapter for every row, and the archived file has not been authenticated against a publisher checksum. Under this supported source-wide convention, **rs12659965 A versus G has FFS beta −0.0121556 (SE 0.00183091, p=2.6×10⁻¹¹)**, matching the direction of the separately analyzed FinnGen R13 insomnia A-allele estimate (−0.0280273, SE 0.00666856). These estimates describe different phenotypes and scales. Same-direction associations at a post-selected marker do not establish a shared causal allele, independent replication, clinical ADHD association, or a gene mechanism. The FFS beta is a linear-model estimate; the exponentiated `OR` in the archived file is not established as a meaningful odds ratio.

At the independently published FFS lead rs7703746, the workbook's `refA=G` and `b=+0.0131507` agree with the FFS source. This agrees in sign with the separate project FI and FinnGen-insomnia estimates after those were oriented to G; different cohorts and trait definitions still preclude causal or replication claims. The 37 rows include rs7703746 but **not** rs12659965, so the candidate-specific orientation is inferred from the observed file-wide convention rather than directly cross-checked in the COJO table.
