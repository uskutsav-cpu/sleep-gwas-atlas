# Adult ADHD symptom GWAS: preliminary independent-context lead

A 2026 [Varvas et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10457433/v1), available in an [author-posted readable manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/411896630_Genome-wide_meta-analysis_of_ADHD_symptoms_in_the_adult_general_population_reveals_distinct_genetics_of_inattention_and_hyperactivity-impulsivity), reports genome-wide meta-analyses of self-reported adult ADHD symptoms in **141,505** Estonian Biobank and Norwegian MoBa participants. It reports a moderate genetic correlation between inattention and hyperactivity–impulsivity (r_g=0.35, 95% CI 0.26–0.43), with stronger ADHD-diagnosis genetic correlation for the latter. The authors describe a stronger insomnia genetic correlation for hyperactivity–impulsivity than inattention, but exact insomnia estimates and the full comparison statistics were not recovered in this check. This is **unreviewed preprint evidence**, not a result produced by Brain6.

The authors report a hyperactivity–impulsivity signal annotated to **FOXP2**, and a symptom signal near **ARNTL/BMAL1**. ARNTL participates in a molecular circadian clock with CLOCK, as described in [NCBI's gene annotation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/9575); this makes circadian regulation a plausible hypothesis to evaluate, not proof of an ADHD–sleep mechanism or a variant effect. Brain6's chromosome 7 region also overlaps FOXP2, but a shared gene label is not a shared variant, molecular target, or colocalized causal signal. We did not identify an exact lead rsID, full region statistics, alleles or LD from the available manuscript text, so **no claim of overlap with rs1476535 or other Brain6 markers is made**. The study phenotype is a self-report symptom score rather than a clinical diagnostic GWAS. Its polygenic-score association in an external cohort validates a score's predictive association, not individual sleep–ADHD loci.

The authors used EstBB and MoBa adult participants; participant and relative overlap with the Brain6 discovery sources and the separate childhood-symptom analysis have not been established. The [published childhood-symptom study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40962958/) and this adult preprint use different instruments and ages. Even without identical individuals, relatedness, data reuse and ascertainment need evaluation before claiming independent replication. The preprint's available data statement describes controlled **individual-level** data access; it does not identify an openly downloadable full GWAS summary-statistic file in the checked text. This is not proof that no public summary release exists. The publisher Research Square page could not be opened by the current web tool, so the review used the DOI metadata and readable author-posted manuscript; full supplementary tables were not verified.

## Implications for Brain6

A predeclared adult symptom-domain analysis could complement diagnostic ADHD and childhood symptoms, and a circadian hypothesis could be evaluated separately from LSAMP/LINC03051 and FOXP2. Obtain a public, versioned summary-statistic release with genome build, effect alleles, phenotype coding, sample sizes, QC, ancestry and cohort-overlap documentation. Freeze marker families and test sleep and ADHD-side associations independently before interpreting effect directions or colocalization. Do not treat gene labels, aggregate genetic correlation, PGS results or an abstract as evidence that a particular Brain6 variant is causal.

The paper's table lists 77,276 EstBB and 64,229 MoBa participants, adding to 141,505. Its earlier [conference abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2025.08.418) reports 77,726 EstBB participants, which does not sum to the stated meta-analysis total. This review follows the manuscript table and flags the discrepancy rather than resolving it. No query, application, or communication with study authors was sent.
