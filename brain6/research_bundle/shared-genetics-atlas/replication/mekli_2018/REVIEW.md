# Earlier frailty-index GWAS in HRS and ELSA

[Mekli et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258126/) analyzed the square-root-transformed frailty index in older adults in the US Health and Retirement Study (HRS) and the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA). Their top discovery association, rs6765037, reached p = 2.14 × 10⁻⁸ in HRS but was not confirmed in ELSA (p = 0.204). A second variant, rs7134291 in the GRIN2B region, was associated in HRS (p = 1.85 × 10⁻⁶) and nominally in ELSA (p = 0.0343), with the same reported A-allele direction. The latter was not a genome-wide-significant discovery, and its nominal replication is not a corrected, independently established sleep–frailty locus. GRIN2B is biological context, not a demonstrated effector of a Brain6 variant.

A later [MVP insomnia lookup](sleep_followup/RESULTS.md) found neither of the two paper-highlighted variants nominally associated; it is a post-selected rsID comparison with unresolved cross-study strand and allele mapping.

The authors' public [phenotype supplement](../../PUBLICATION.md#external-dependencies) (external target: `source_S1.xlsx`) lists a question about **restless sleep in the past week in both cohorts**. Thus, neither index is a sleep-item-excluded FI, and this study cannot answer whether the genetic sleep–FI relationship persists after removing sleep-related components. Their comparative pathway results did not survive Bonferroni correction. Different ages, samples, component definitions, ancestry and the transformed outcome also limit direct comparison with the project's FI, FFS and HFRS analyses. Cohort independence from every project source and participant relatedness were not individually verified.

The public [association supplement](../../PUBLICATION.md#external-dependencies) (external target: `source_S3.xlsx`) has 32 selected discovery rows. A [literal ID comparison](compare_ids.py) found none of its 13 `rs` identifiers among the 425 Brain6 candidate variants or the 82 PLACO diagnostic variants. The remaining 19 supplement identifiers use `kgp` names, which were not converted; aliases, genomic intervals and LD were not evaluated. This is **not evidence that the studies have no overlapping regions or shared causal variants**, and the study did not release a full per-SNP replication dataset in the supplement examined. Results were examined before this comparison; it is descriptive rather than a prespecified replication test. [Machine-readable receipt](literal_id_check.json) records the scope and input hashes.

The phenotype supplement SHA-256 is `0a499803e13c7adca7da6acf274d2921a84b020a0b2ae8e57f295a9a39408f46`; the association supplement hash is in the receipt. These are [authors' public supporting files](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0207824), not individual-level data.
