# Published falls follow-up at the chromosome 5 lead

An earlier [falls GWAS by Trajanoska and colleagues (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7527955/) reports the same identifier as Brain6's chromosome 5 lead, rs2431108. This is prior evidence for a **related physical outcome**, not a newly discovered sleep–frailty locus or independent replication of an insomnia–ADHD association. Falls are not the FI, Fried frailty phenotype or hospital frailty risk score.

The paper's table places the variant at chromosome 5 position 103,947,968 in the build-37 study, with C as effect allele and T as the other allele. The C allele is also the orientation of the [Brain6 follow-up](../six_lead_joint/RESULTS.md), though matching identifiers and alleles does not establish a shared causal variant. The published estimates are:

| Falls sample | C-allele odds ratio (95% CI) | p |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| UK Biobank discovery, 89,076 cases and 362,103 controls | 1.03 (1.02–1.04) | 9.9 × 10⁻⁸ |
| Rotterdam Study, 1,009 cases and 4,925 controls | 1.13 (1.03–1.25) | 0.01 |
| B-PROOF, 1,206 cases and 1,364 controls | 0.98 (0.88–1.10) | 0.77 |
| Combined, 91,219 cases and 368,392 controls | 1.03 (1.02–1.04) | 4.20 × 10⁻⁸ |

The Rotterdam result is nominal and in the same direction as the UK Biobank estimate; B-PROOF is imprecise and in the opposite direction. The authors carried 17 suggestive discovery variants forward and used nominal p<0.05 or combined genome-wide significance in their replication definition. A simple illustrative 17-test Bonferroni multiplication of the rounded Rotterdam p is 0.17; it is not a complete correction for the two follow-up cohorts, selection, or the wider Brain6 analysis. The combined result includes the discovery sample and is not an independent replication statistic. Differences in retrospective questionnaire and prospective calendar ascertainment, cohort composition and power complicate comparison. The paper itself cautions that additional evidence is needed for this locus.

For context only, the C-allele directions of the FinnGen insomnia and childhood ADHD-symptom follow-ups are positive in our earlier analysis. Their effect scales and outcomes differ from the falls odds ratios; sleep discovery contains UK Biobank participants, so the falls discovery and Brain6 evidence cannot be treated as independent. Exact cross-study participant and family overlap has not been audited. There is no joint three-phenotype analysis, effect-size harmonization beyond the published allele labels, locus colocalization with falls, or molecular demonstration that C affects a gene. The existing chromosome 5 insomnia–ADHD single-signal coloc and failed local-QC gates remain unchanged. Prior paper maps the lead to RP11-6N13.1; location is not evidence that this transcript mediates any phenotype.

## Source and reproducibility

The study table and methods are transcribed from the linked open full text (DOI 10.1038/s42003-020-01256-x, PMID 32999390), checked 28 October 2026. The retrospective Rotterdam and prospective B-PROOF phenotype definitions are described in the methods. These are published rounded summary values, not a new analysis of individual data or a full-file validation; no additional cohort independence has been verified. The multiplicity illustration is 0.01 × 17 = 0.17, capped at 1 if needed.
