# rs62264764: planning sensitivity using public FinnGen browser counts

On 27 October 2026 the [FinnGen R13 public GWAS browser](https://r13.finngen.fi/) phenotype table displayed **5,559 cases and 489,493 controls** for “Disturbance of activity and attention” (`F5_ADHD`), with two genome-wide significant loci. This confirms the previously unverified *phenotype-table* case and control counts; it does not establish the per-variant sample size, imputation quality, variant association, or study independence. The endpoint page did not load in this check, and no variant-level result was obtained. The [FinnGen results page](https://www.finngen.fi/en/access_results) distinguishes its public browser from the summary-statistics access procedure.

A revised illustrative calculation uses those displayed counts, the Brain6 discovery ADHD absolute log-odds estimate 0.06470, and the earlier 503-person European reference A-allele frequency 0.1640. Effective sample size under the simple case–control approximation is 21,986. Under a two-sided test, approximate marginal ADHD power is **71.0%** at α=0.05 and **45.0%** at α=0.05/6 if the true effect equals the discovery estimate. If it is half as large, the corresponding powers are **24.1%** and **8.3%**. The six-test threshold is illustrative and does not define a preregistered or comprehensive candidate family.

This is not a study-specific measured power calculation: the actual Finnish allele frequency, per-variant information, age and case ascertainment, covariate losses, relatedness and overlap with discovery cohorts are unverified. Winner's curse may affect the selected effect. Different disease definitions and genetic backgrounds can change effects, and the normal approximation assumes homogeneous additive effects and independent samples. A joint sleep–ADHD replication requires valid evidence for both traits and cannot have greater power than the ADHD marginal test; no independent replication, shared causal-variant conclusion, or LAVA QC repair follows from these calculations.
