# MVP insomnia and FinnGen clinical ADHD: genome-wide LDSC sensitivity

A post-hoc LDSC comparison of the existing MVP European-ancestry insomnia GWAS and FinnGen R13 clinical ADHD input estimated **r_g = 0.4729 (SE 0.0674, p = 2.24×10⁻¹²)** on 1,124,041 matched SNPs. This is a genome-wide genetic-effect correlation, not evidence of causation, individual prediction or a shared causal variant at a locus. The differently ascertained sources are separately named cohorts, but exact participant and relative overlap with each other and with discovery studies is not established. This is a source sensitivity, not a confirmed independent two-trait replication.

The diagnostics limit interpretation. The MVP observed-scale h² estimate was 0.0489 (SE 0.0032), intercept **1.1356 (0.0094)**; the ADHD observed-scale h² was 0.1908 (0.0233), intercept **1.0464 (0.0077)**. The cross-trait intercept was −0.0034 (0.0061). Elevated univariate intercepts can have several causes and should not be assigned a specific cause from these results alone. The LD reference is general-European and may not fit Finnish LD, and the original ADHD input's complete processing lineage is unresolved despite its [sampled source-concordance check](../finngen_adhd_archived_munged/source_concordance/RESULTS.md). LDSC logged a compression warning while reading the archived ADHD file; the file's compressed hash and earlier source audit provide separate but not exhaustive checks.

The MVP file does not contain standard errors. We reconstructed a signed normal Z from each reported p-value and the direction of its rounded odds ratio, rather than treating the published confidence limits as Wald SEs. Earlier checks found that those limits and p-values were not consistently related by a simple Wald formula; the reason is unknown. We excluded **27,416** eligible-position rows whose rounded odds ratio was exactly one and hence had no recoverable direction, and excluded all **140** rsIDs with more than one eligible source row. Each retained MVP row used the working effective N **253,768.615**, calculated from 78,566 cases and 329,572 controls. This constant approximation is not independently verified per-variant effective sample size for the SAIGE model. Effect estimates and p-values should not be treated as proof of model calibration.

All 19,703,815 MVP rows were read to gzip completion. The compressed MVP and ADHD inputs matched the previously recorded SHA-256 hashes. Eligible variants were matched by unique rsID, GRCh37 reference position lifted to GRCh38, chromosome and non-palindromic allele pair; 563 reference positions could not be uniquely mapped and 2,135 source rows failed position or allele matching. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [stream code](stream.py), [run script](run.sh), [complete stream receipt](stream_receipt.json), [LDSC log](mvp_adhd_clean.log) and [arithmetic check](verification.json) document the run. This matching and checksum verify the performed computation, not participant independence, original source precision or LD-score suitability.

The recorded [run script](run.sh) now uses a unique output and receipt for each rerun so an old receipt cannot mask a failed stream. The first attempt (`mvp_adhd.log`) failed its end-of-stream duplicate assertion even though LDSC printed a numerical estimate. It was **not accepted**. The rerun excluded all duplicate eligible rsIDs before emitting data, passed the completion receipt check and produced the result above. These estimates were tested after the project's prior genetic analyses and should be viewed in the context of its broader, uncorrected research search. They do not repair the failed local-correlation gates or establish a sleep-independent physical-frailty association. No AlphaGenome output was used for training.
