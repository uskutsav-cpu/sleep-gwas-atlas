# Original Brain6 insomnia: FI versus FFS on common support

In a post-hoc comparison using **1,099,360 shared SNPs**, the estimated genetic correlation with original Brain6 insomnia was **0.6404 (SE 0.0236) for FI** and **0.4840 (SE 0.0269) for FFS**. Their FI-minus-FFS difference was **0.1564**, with a paired 200-block jackknife SE of **0.0281**, an unadjusted 95% interval of **0.1013 to 0.2115**, and an unadjusted two-sided normal p of **2.60×10⁻⁸**. The paired method accounts for covariance captured by the common delete blocks, rather than treating the two estimates as independent. The difference uses the four-decimal r_g values printed by LDSC, so the test and interval are approximate.

This is one post-hoc contrast; the p-value and interval are **not adjusted across the atlas's analyses**. A positive difference does not show that FI is a better frailty measure, that insomnia causes frailty, or that the difference reflects physical frailty biology. FI contains insomnia and tiredness, and FFS includes exhaustion; cohort, phenotype, sample overlap and model differences also matter. This is a same-data analysis, not independent replication or the requested sleep-item-excluded GWAS.

## Support and checks

We matched the original insomnia munged input to the already harmonized FI/FFS support using unique IDs and compatible nonpalindromic allele pairs. Of 1,134,953 previously selected FI/FFS rows, 1,099,360 matched; 35,593 lacked a usable insomnia row. There were 19,476 effect-allele reversals, with insomnia Z signs aligned accordingly, and no incompatible retained pairs. The insomnia source has 1,217,311 unique SNP IDs and no duplicates, including rows unusable for analysis. The selected FI/FFS support was originally filtered for other insomnia and HFRS analyses, and the matched set is not exhaustive genome-wide coverage.

Both LDSC runs retained all 1,099,360 input SNPs, and their insomnia h² delete vectors were exactly equal. Each reconstructed r_g jackknife SE matched its LDSC log to printed precision; an independent R calculation reproduced the paired SE. These checks support the paired arithmetic and shared-block implementation, not full validity of cohort overlap or LD-reference assumptions. On their broader native supports, the earlier [Brain6–frailty runs](../RESULTS.md) gave 0.6405 for FI and 0.4839 for FFS; agreement is a support sensitivity, not independent replication. Those original runs did not establish a paired difference.
