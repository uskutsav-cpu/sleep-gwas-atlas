# Random marker-support stability at locus 464

With FI and insomnia summary statistics and other inputs fixed, we sampled 20 random 2,452-marker subsets of the original 2,741-marker insomnia support and reran the same one-locus LAVA calculation. **One of 20** subsets had a negative FI variance estimate; 19 had positive estimates. FI variance diagonals ranged from −2.21×10⁻⁸ to +2.90×10⁻⁷, with median +1.31×10⁻⁷. The full original support produced −4.19×10⁻⁹.

The previously inspected sleep-apnea-derived support gave +4.89×10⁻⁷, above all 20 random subsets, and retained 185 LD components versus 208–214 in the random subsets. This is a descriptive comparison after selecting the region and the apnea-derived support, **not a calibrated significance test**. Only 20 masks were tried; the random masks overlap and preserve the original data, and they do not mimic the actual missingness, allele-frequency or LD structure of sleep apnea. The distinct component counts show that deleting an equal number of markers need not preserve the LD decomposition. The experiment does not identify individual causal markers, establish whether any estimated variance is reliable, or quantify a genome-wide failure rate.

The result reinforces that this selected locus is sensitive to support and LD decomposition, even when the GWAS values are fixed. It does not justify choosing a positive subset after seeing the data. No bivariate test was run, and the original failed QC gates remain unchanged.
