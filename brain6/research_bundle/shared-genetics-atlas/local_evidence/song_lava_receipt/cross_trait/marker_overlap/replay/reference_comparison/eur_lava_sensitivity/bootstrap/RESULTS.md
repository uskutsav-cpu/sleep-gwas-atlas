# Resampling the small LD reference

We reconstructed the 503-person 1000 Genomes European-labeled panel and reproduced the prior FI variance diagonal of **+1.881×10⁻⁶** with 183 retained components. In 30 bootstrap resamples of those people, all 2,738 markers remained variable. The FI variance diagonals were positive in all 30 and ranged from **+2.693×10⁻⁶ to +5.186×10⁻⁶** (median +3.734×10⁻⁶); the 99% rule retained 136–148 components (median 140.5).

These resamples contain repeated people: reconstructing the draws gives 308–329 distinct individuals per replicate. The resulting change in effective reference rank and LD eigenstructure is a plausible reason for the lower component counts and systematic shift, but this diagnostic does not establish the cause. **Do not treat the resample range as a confidence interval or the 30 positive results as 30 independent replications.** The experiment keeps the same GWAS and a post-selected locus fixed, and the source panel is small, potentially related and not study-matched. It does not determine which reference is correct, validate the absolute genetic variance, or repair the failed LAVA family gates. No bivariate correlation was calculated.

[Protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [reconstruction and resampling code](run.py), [all results](results.json), and [verification](verify.py) retain the seed, individual-replicate values and arithmetic. The code reuses the public-reference retrieval portion of the parent script, stops before its output-writing section and does not save participant genotypes or sampled identities. The VCF/index are not fully hashed; the verification regenerates counts and checks saved arithmetic, not independent source retrieval, reference calibration or participant independence.
