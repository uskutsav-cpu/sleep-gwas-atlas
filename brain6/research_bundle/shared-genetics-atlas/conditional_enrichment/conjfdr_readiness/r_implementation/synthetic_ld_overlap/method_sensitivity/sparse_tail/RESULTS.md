# Tail-count inspection of one simulated conjunction-FDR crossing

For the previously selected false crossing, the corrected marginal p-values were approximately **0.00763** for trait 1 and **0.000443** for trait 2. In each of the five pruned samples, 4,400 markers were retained. Only **0–2 markers per sample** were at least as significant as the selected marker on trait 2, and **none** met both of the selected marker's exact corrected p-value thresholds. The selected marker itself was not retained in any of those five pruning draws; the fitted lookup is nevertheless applied to the full marker set.

At that marker, the default smoothed directional estimates were 0.04222 and 0.04247; without smoothing they were 0.06350 and 0.42624. The conjunction value is the larger directional estimate, so it changed from **0.04247** to **0.42624**. The extremely sparse conditional tails and the dependence on smoothing provide a concrete reason to scrutinize the fitted lookup in this example. They do not prove that smoothing is incorrectly implemented, establish why the estimate differs, or demonstrate calibration of the unsmoothed option.

Counts use **exact target thresholds**, whereas the package bins p-values and then smooths, adjusts and interpolates a lookup grid. They are therefore not claimed to be the precise input cell counts of the algorithm. The marker was selected because it was a false crossing in a simulation where all trait-2 effects are null; this one case is not independent validation or a real-data association. Five correlated pruning samples from one simulation do not provide five independent cohort replicates. The original simulated-FDR findings and failed LAVA gates remain unresolved.
