# Max-p boundary-null stress

Before observing results, generate 1,000 new seeds (82001–83000) with the parent 44,000-marker design: 4,400 independent blocks of 10, within-block normal correlation 0.5, and residual cross-trait correlations 0 and 0.3. Set the first trait's mean Z to 12 at **every** marker; set the second trait's mean Z to zero at every marker. This deliberately extreme composite-null configuration makes the max-p resemble a test of the second trait and probes a less conservative boundary than the sparse-signal null simulation. Both settings share draws within each seed. Calculate two-sided normal p-values, their maximum, and Bonferroni, BY and descriptive BH adjustments across all markers; preserve counts, minimum adjusted p, and 95% exact binomial intervals for the number of runs with any rejection.

All conjunction nulls are true; run-level any-rejection equals false-discovery proportion in each simulated run. Verify sorted-threshold counts for the first seed and range/family bookkeeping independently. This is a deliberately artificial null stress, not a realistic genetic architecture, a power test, or a validation of conjunction-FDR, PLACO or real GWAS. Do not interpret BH as generally valid under arbitrary marker dependence. Do not assume a zero or small difference between two estimated rates proves equivalence, or that selected results supply a calibrated real-data discovery.
