The false-discovery proportion is the fraction of selected markers that are false under this simulation's truth labels; the Monte Carlo SE describes variation of its mean over just ten datasets. It is not the real-data FDR or a confidence interval. Each block contains ten markers with the same assigned effect status; blocks recovered are descriptive, not independent causal loci or calibrated block-level tests. The runs share seed-wise generated effects and residual components between correlation settings. The small simulated family, post-hoc choice of scenario, artificial LD, equal positive effects, fixed reference and unimplemented overlap correction limit generalization. No false crossings among ten datasets does not establish control for either max-p procedure; their validity requires valid marginal p-values. The two controls are more conservative tests of marginal joint association, not equivalent implementations of conjunction-FDR.

A [paired uncertainty analysis](uncertainty/RESULTS.md) quantifies simulation error: the approximate interval for mean marker FDP at rho=0 includes 0.05, while the rho=0.3 interval is above it under the small-sample t approximation. Ten zero-false control runs do not establish zero false-selection risk.

Unlike the earlier pure-null test, **disabling smoothing did not remove the mixed-signal false crossings**. Both tested settings remain unsuitable for an unqualified real-data conjunction-FDR discovery claim without further calibration and review. The original local LAVA gates remain failed.

The planned 20-dataset test stopped after **five completed datasets**: three seeds with zero residual correlation and two with correlation 0.3. The run failed while reading a newly copied reference file, and the SSD reported zero available bytes. An initial attempt also stopped immediately because the R temporary-directory path contained a space; the successful retry and failure are retained in [the logs](retry.log). In this deliberately simple synthetic model, each dataset had 22 shared-effect blocks, 198 trait-1-only blocks and 22 trait-2-only blocks. A marker was counted as a true shared association if it lay in a simulated shared block. All markers in a block were assigned the same synthetic effect status; this is not a causal-variant simulation. Both the smoothed and unsmoothed conjunction-FDR settings produced false marker-level crossings in **all five completed datasets**. Their observed marker false-discovery proportions ranged from approximately **0.045 to 0.106** and **0.045 to 0.102**, respectively. Both recovered at least one marker in all 22 shared blocks in these runs. False block counts ranged from 6 to 21, reflecting clusters of correlated marker crossings rather than independent tests.

For the same datasets, the conservative Bonferroni and BY procedures on marginal max-p values returned **no false crossings** under these synthetic truth labels, but recovered fewer shared blocks: **5–11** and **5–15**, respectively. These numerical contrasts describe this incomplete, pre-specified simulation only. In particular, the two residual-correlation settings have only three and two replicates and cannot be compared reliably; no observed false crossings in a few datasets is not proof of error control. Selection of this stress model followed earlier results. Fixed effect sizes, equal signs, artificial LD and reference, assumed residual correlation, pruning and package implementation may not resemble real GWAS.

The important practical result is that **disabling smoothing did not remove false crossings in these mixed-effect simulations**, even though it removed them in the earlier pure-null sensitivity. Neither setting should be treated as calibrated for the real sleep–frailty scan on this basis. The max-p procedures are different, more conservative tests; they require valid marginal p-values and do not establish shared causal variants. The failed LAVA gates are unaffected.
