# LSAMP: prior insomnia association and experimental context

The human insomnia connection predates the 2026 sleep–ADHD study. [Watanabe et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01124-w) analyzed 593,724 insomnia cases and 1,771,286 controls and reported LSAMP as the second-most significant gene in their gene-based analysis (p=2.8×10⁻³⁶). This is a **gene-level association from aggregated GWAS information**, not evidence that rs62264764 is causal, that LSAMP expression mediates insomnia, or that the same signal is shared with ADHD. The study includes UK Biobank data used in related work, so source overlap must be assessed before treating any apparent consistency as independent replication. See the [author-hosted version of the published paper](https://research.vu.nl/files/170385253/Genome_wide_meta_analysis_of_insomnia_prioritizes_genes_associated_with_metabolic_and_psychiatric_pathways.pdf).

For biological context, [Qiu et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2900390/) compared male Lsamp knockout mice with littermate controls and reported altered hippocampal long-term potentiation, spatial-memory performance and mineralocorticoid-receptor expression. This supports a hypothesis involving neuronal adhesion, circuit physiology and stress responses; it does **not** directly test sleep, ADHD, the human variant, or the specific variant's gene target. The knockout changes the gene much more broadly than a common noncoding allele, and the experiment's male mouse model limits direct translation.

For the project's selected variant, evidence remains mixed: the [brain-cell LSAMP expression–GWAS comparisons](../rs62264764_brain_xqtl/lsamp_finemapping/RESULTS.md) favor distinct dominant expression and trait signals in the specified single-signal fits; they cannot exclude secondary sharing. The nearby LINC03051 expression associations are measured but do not prove mediation. AlphaGenome's frontal-cortex accessibility change is a prediction, not a measured gene-specific effect. Together these results motivate an experimentally testable gene hypothesis while preventing a claim that LSAMP has been established as the shared effector.

A useful follow-up would compare allele-specific effects of rs62264764 and linked variants on LSAMP and LINC03051 in relevant human cell types, alongside controls and study-matched multi-signal molecular colocalization. A connection between those molecular effects and both sleep and ADHD would still require independent phenotype evidence.
