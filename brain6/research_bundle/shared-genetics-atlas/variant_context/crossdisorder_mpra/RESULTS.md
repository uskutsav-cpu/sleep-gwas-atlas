# Public cross-disorder human neural progenitor MPRA: coverage screen

A [cross-assay comparison](CROSS_ASSAY_COMPARISON.md) joins four of these candidates to the public Siraj neural-derived cell-line results, including rs62369151; the Lee model and quality-control limitations remain unresolved.

A subsequent [published-table comparison](published_table/RESULTS.md) recovered the authors' results for 27 of the 42 candidates. It identifies two chromosome 5 `emVar` candidates and reports the author's nonsignificant allele FDR for rs4916723. The published results supersede the provisional interpretation below; pipeline reproduction and the noted barcode discrepancy remain unresolved.

[Lee et al. (Cell, 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.12.022) measured regulatory activity of psychiatric GWAS variants using human neural progenitor cells from one fetal donor. The study reports a 150-base hg19-centered MPRA design and releases DNA and RNA count matrices through [GEO GSE244011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE244011). This is independent functional-assay material, but it is not an independent sleep–ADHD GWAS replication.

The earlier screen converted 425 Brain6 ranked variants from GRCh38 to GRCh37 using a recorded chain. Matching chromosome, position, reference and alternate alleles to both matrices found **42 candidates**, including **rs4916723** (GRCh37 chr5:87854395 A>C); none was rs62264764. The matches comprise 4 in the chr3 region, 25 in the chr5 103-Mb region, 2 in the chr5 87-Mb region, and 11 in the chr7 region. One of the 42 also occurs in the accompanying exclusion list. All 425 entries and the exact source keys are recorded in [coverage](ranked_coverage.tsv); the input was selected from prior Brain6 rankings rather than genome-wide.

A [reference-LD check](chr3_candidate_ld/RESULTS.md) found that the four chromosome 3 matrix candidates have low pairwise LD with rs62264764 in the 503-person EUR reference. Their matrix presence and provisional allele models therefore do not directly validate the prioritized marker.

**Matrix presence is not a result.** It does not establish that a variant passed all study quality controls, has a significant allele effect, or has any particular direction. The matrix and exclusion list alone do not identify expression-modulating variants; use the authors' complete analysis and QC or a verified published results table before making such claims. The authors' supplementary table containing allelic statistics was identified, but the attempted download returned an HTML access challenge rather than the spreadsheet, so it was not analyzed. The recorded source count data are public; [receipt](receipt.json) lists SHA-256 hashes and [code](screen.py) reproduces coverage. The public source files are on the SSD under `/Volumes/Extreme SSD/brain6-crossdisorder-mpra/`. The study is restricted to one donor and a reporter assay outside endogenous chromatin; even a verified effect would not establish sleep or ADHD causality, a target gene, or resolve failed LAVA QC.

An attempt to install the authors' `mpra` analysis dependency to the SSD was stopped on 2026-10-27 when temporary installation space grew quickly. The process exited with code 143 and no analysis was run. Partially installed dependencies and temporary files were left in place in the SSD folder; they are not valid results. ## Provisional allele-model reconstruction and version sensitivity

A direct matrix implementation using `limma` now approximates the published `mpra::mpralm` correlated-groups steps for unique element IDs, on all **14,494** rows of the public count matrices. It uses the authors' 22 paired replicates and batch design, an alternate-allele coefficient, weighted linear modeling, empirical Bayes, and BH adjustment over the 14,494 analyzed rows. This implementation has not been compared with the actual `mpra` package output or the authors' results table. The paper reports **13,311** variants passing allelic QC and **4,472** with significant allelic activity; our version-1.24-like reconstruction returns 5,021 FDR<0.05 among 14,494 rows. The different tested sets and other unverified details prevent calling this a replication of their results. The one candidate listed in the supplied exclusion file is still in the provisional fit; absence from that list does not certify full QC.

Inspection of the package source revealed a material version difference: in version **1.24.0**, the correlated model normalizes each RNA and DNA column separately, whereas in **1.34.0** it combines library sizes within replicate blocks before normalizing. We ran both forms as a sensitivity analysis. Across 42 matched candidates, 12 versus 13 pass FDR<0.05 in these two provisional models, and 7 change threshold status. For **rs4916723**, the 1.24-like model gives log2 ALT/REF coefficient +0.130, p=0.0695, BH-adjusted p=0.146; the 1.34-like model gives +0.243, p=0.000905, BH-adjusted p=0.00644. These are **our exploratory model outputs, not validated published effects**. In particular, a BH-adjusted allele-effect test is not sufficient to label an element an emVar: the paper also requires an active enhancer context. We cannot assert that rs4916723 passes the authors' QC or active-element criteria, or use either result to establish a sleep–ADHD association or endogenous gene mechanism.

## Exclusion-list sensitivity

The public `excludelist.csv.gz` contains 772 distinct variants that match matrix entries, of which 402 have at least one listed row with `prop > 0.30`. The study describes a 30% mismatch-related read-count filter, but the exact relationship of every row in this file to the full published QC procedure is not established here. Removing all listed variants leaves **13,722** matrix variants, still 411 more than the paper's 13,311; removing only the 402 above that numerical cutoff leaves **14,092**. These are two explicit sensitivity scenarios, not reconstructed publication QC. The available barcode mapping gives many raw candidate barcode labels but does not itself confirm which barcodes passed all filters.

We reran the version-1.24-like provisional model under each scenario, recalculating normalization, weights and BH across the remaining family. For rs4916723, adjusted p-values were 0.146 without either filter, 0.162 after excluding all listed variants, and 0.155 after excluding only variants with a listed `prop > 0.30`; it remained nonsignificant in these three versions. Among the 41 candidate variants present in all three fits, none changed its FDR<0.05 threshold status. This does **not** resolve the much larger change under version-1.34-like normalization, demonstrate all authors' QC, validate the direct reconstruction, or establish enhancer activity. The excluded candidate rs11242522 has a listed `prop` of 0.608 and is absent from both filtered sensitivity fits. [Candidate table](qc_sensitivity_42.tsv), [code](qc_sensitivity.py), and [receipt](qc_sensitivity_receipt.json) preserve the scenarios and hashes.

A [targeted barcode reconciliation](barcode_audit/RESULTS.md) found that the rs4916723 barcode labels in the released mapping do not occur in the released raw count files, despite nonzero aggregate matrix counts; a second variant also has differing raw and matrix totals. The cause and sample crosswalk remain unresolved, so these files cannot yet reconstruct the publication QC.
