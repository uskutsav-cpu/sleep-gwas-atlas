# rs62264764: decision-ready evidence and follow-up

## What can be claimed now

**rs62264764 is a previously reported insomnia–ADHD locus and an experimental priority, not an established causal variant or gene.** The Brain6 pilot's strongest local frontal-cortex DNase prediction within its selected chromosome 3 set is at rs62264764, GRCh38 chr3:117920728 G>A. The model's ALT-versus-REF 501-bp summary is −0.2734 on a log₂ ratio scale; it is not a measured effect or disease odds ratio. The published [Zu et al. study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-026-04166-4) already reported the marker and a model-dependent insomnia–ADHD colocalization. Brain6's possible contribution is a comparison of sequence predictions with independent molecular measurements and rigorous controls, not the first discovery of this overlap. The predictions are subject to the [AlphaGenome terms guidance](https://google-deepmind-alphagenome.readthedocs-hosted.com/faqs.html#what-terms-of-use-apply-to-alphagenome-outputs); the user authorized the specified public outputs for non-commercial research. Obtain and check current terms before any new use, especially model training or redistribution.

| Evidence | Finding | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence model | Predicted ALT-dependent local frontal-cortex DNase decrease in the selected set. | A measured chromatin effect, a causal variant or gene, or the direction of disease risk. |
| Molecular association | rs62264764 has reported LINC03051 expression associations in aging-brain oligodendrocytes and excitatory neurons, with opposite estimated signs. | Causal mediation, a statistically tested difference between cell types, or validation of predicted DNase change. |
| Disease–molecular single-signal comparisons | The selected LINC03051 and LSAMP brain-cell comparisons favor distinct dominant expression and GWAS signals under the tested one-signal model and priors. | Absence of a secondary shared signal; the selected region, LD fit, ancestry and model assumptions remain limitations. |
| Independent reporter context | rs62264764 was not in the searched Siraj design. The correlated rs1456193 had no detectable allele skew in the examined neural-derived reporter library. | A test of rs62264764 itself, zero effect in brain tissue, or an endogenous variant–gene link. |
| Developmental chromatin | A neural-cell ATAC peak overlaps part of the model's summary window; the variant itself lies outside that peak and no exact-marker caQTL row was found in the selected files. | A measured allele effect for rs62264764, or validation of the model's predicted change. |

The published [LaDDR RNA-coverage study](linc03051_laddr_2026/RESULTS.md) already highlighted LINC03051–insomnia, with linked rs1456193 as a best marker in its selected rows; its RNA features are not equivalent to total expression and it does not establish an ADHD mechanism.

## Competing hypotheses and decisive next observations

1. **LINC03051 regulation.** A risk-associated haplotype could alter a specific LINC03051 RNA feature in a relevant cell context. A compelling result would require a high-quality, multi-signal molecular–GWAS comparison with matching alleles and validated LD, followed by an endogenous allele-specific perturbation that changes the predeclared transcript readout. Evidence against the simple dominant-signal version is the existing single-signal H3 result; it does not test every RNA feature or secondary signal.
2. **LSAMP regulation.** An alternative is an effect on LSAMP or another neuronal adhesion-related process. The existing aging-brain LSAMP fine-mapping and single-signal comparisons favor distinct dominant expression and disease signals; the nearby gene name and plausible biology do not override that evidence. Test LSAMP separately with the same QC and experimental standards, including cell-state and developmental-context sensitivity.
3. **Another gene, transcript or context.** The active regulatory target could differ from either candidate, act during development, affect splicing or RNA processing, or involve several linked variants. Record all sufficiently powered genes and molecular modalities in the region, including negative and unavailable results, rather than selecting the favorable result after inspection.
4. **Non-regulatory or no detectable effect in the assayed context.** A null result with adequate assay sensitivity would limit the tested context and allelic construct; it would not exclude other cell types, time points, haplotypes or biological mechanisms.

These hypotheses concern molecular links. Synapse organization and neuronal pathway enrichment in prior studies are **gene-set-level** observations; they do not prove this variant acts through a specific synapse or cell type. Opposite LINC03051 eQTL signs do not establish a cell interaction without a formal test. Neither model predictions nor molecular QTL prove that sleep causes ADHD.

## Order of work

1. **Freeze a locus and comparator panel.** Define the GRCh37/GRCh38 builds and the original candidate family before new outcome access. Include rs62264764 and the separately identified linked variants rs62264767 and rs1456193; record their LD in the actual study population, not only the 503-person reference. Keep untested markers and both alleles. Include matched negative-control variants with comparable allele frequency, LD, local accessibility and assay coverage. Choose loci and controls independently of the new assay's outcomes.
2. **Repair the statistical inputs.** Obtain full, allele-harmonized GWAS and brain-cell eQTL/sQTL summary data, suitable per-variant sample sizes and study-matched LD. The available [LD–Z diagnostic](rs62264764_brain_xqtl/ldz_readiness/RESULTS.md) has substantial residuals, and the public reference is small and not study-matched. Predefine variant filtering, frequency/allele checks, LD consistency, missingness, multiple-signal model and a sensitivity grid of priors; report failed fits. Do not promote a colocalization based on a single posterior or rescue the failed chromosome 5 LAVA gate by analogy.
3. **Use an independent molecular assay.** First use predeclared, donor-replicated allele-specific accessibility and transcript readouts in a justified neuronal or glial model and, where feasible, developmental and adult contexts. Test the variant or haplotype with an endogenous edit and appropriate isogenic controls; verify editing, local copy number, off-targets and cell identity. Pair an activity assay with an explicit variant-to-gene readout; a reporter alone cannot assign a target. Specify biological replicates, assay sensitivity and an analysis family before measurement.
4. **Check clinical generalization separately.** Use source-verified, appropriately independent insomnia and clinical ADHD cohorts; account for differing phenotype definitions, overlap, LD and power. Predefine the locus family and correction. The archived file labeled FinnGen ADHD has incomplete source provenance and missing chr3 candidate rows, so it is not sufficient for confirmation.
5. **Evaluate prediction utility without prohibited training.** Compare precomputed, permitted AlphaGenome priorities against association-only, fine-mapping-only and distance baselines on truly held-out loci and experimental datasets. Predefine the endpoint, matched variant set, locus-blocked splits, precision/recall and uncertainty. Review output terms first; this is an evaluation proposal, not permission to train a variant-effect model on AlphaGenome outputs.

The principal outcomes are: a replicated endogenous allele effect, a defensible molecular target, and separately replicated disease associations. Each requires distinct evidence. This plan is prospective and does not claim those outcomes have been achieved.
