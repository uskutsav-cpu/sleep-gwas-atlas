# Developmental neuronal chromatin-accessibility lookup

A published ATAC-seq dataset contains an accessibility peak near rs62264764 that overlaps the **501-base window used to summarize the AlphaGenome prediction**, under the stated coordinate convention. This provides developmental-cell context for the predicted region; it does **not** measure the effect of rs62264764 on that peak or validate the predicted allele difference.

The [study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-023-02959-0) used primary human cortical neural progenitors and their differentiated neurons; its paired-modality subset had 75 progenitor and 57 neuron donors. These developmental cultures differ from the adult frontal cortex represented by the AlphaGenome output. In the two extracted Model1A caQTL files, the peak labeled `chr3_117920181_117920630` is present in both cell types. Assuming the peak identifier uses zero-based half-open coordinates and the reported positions are GRCh38, it overlaps 153 bases of the rs62264764-centered 501-base window (chr3:117920477–117920978 in BED notation). The variant's 1-based position, 117920728, lies outside that peak. A one-base coordinate-convention change would not remove the overlap, but the source peak convention should be confirmed before base-level interpretation. The study states its ATAC reads were aligned to GRCh38; the exact release's full peak/variant-coordinate semantics have not been independently audited.

Neither rs62264764 nor rs62264767 appeared at their GRCh38 positions in the extracted neuronal or progenitor chromosome 3 Model1A files. The search checked all 4,919,123 neuronal and 5,247,010 progenitor source rows, and a separate shell scan also found zero rows at either position. **No caQTL p-value can be assigned to the two markers from these files.** Model1A is a selected set of candidate variant–peak tests for a mediation analysis, so absence does not establish that the variants were never tested in any dataset or that they have no effect. The overlapping peak's other variant associations do not establish a caQTL for either candidate; their uncorrected minima in [the peak table](overlap_peaks.tsv) are descriptive, post-selected and not discovery claims.

The peak is measured open chromatin in a developmental context; the AlphaGenome result is a predicted allele-dependent track change summarized across a broader 501-base window. Their overlap does not establish that the prediction localizes to that peak, has the correct direction, or explains insomnia, ADHD or LSAMP expression. The measured region and brain molecular QTLs motivate targeted, donor-replicated allele-specific accessibility and perturbation experiments; they do not resolve GWAS–QTL colocalization or the failed LAVA quality gates.

## Source and checks

The [Zenodo release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7820257) distributes these data under CC BY 4.0. The two chromosome 3 gzip members were recovered from the larger ZIP via HTTP range requests. ZIP-member CRCs, inner-file SHA-256 hashes and complete gzip reads were checked; the 6.3 GB complete archive was not downloaded or verified against its publisher MD5. [Retrieval code](download_zip_members.py), [receipt](retrieval_receipt.json), [candidate scan](scan.py), [scan receipt](scan_receipt.json), [peak scan](peak_scan.py), [peak receipt](peak_receipt.json), and [verification](verification.json) document these checks and the coordinate assumptions. Full source files remain on the SSD. These are data-integrity and arithmetic checks, not validation of statistical inference.

A [prediction-window decomposition](predicted_peak_overlap/RESULTS.md) finds predicted DNase decreases both within and outside the part of the developmental peak overlapping the 501-base window. It is a model-output summary, not a measured allele effect.
