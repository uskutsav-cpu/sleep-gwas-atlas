# LINC03051 and LSAMP: a testable connection, with a tissue caveat

An earlier experiment offers a **hypothesis** for how the two genes near rs62264764 might interact. [NCBI Gene](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/127138867) lists Lnc-LSAMP-1 as an alias of **LINC03051** (ENSG00000239268). In [Gong et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9074231/), overexpressing Lnc-LSAMP-1 in two human lung cancer cell lines was reported to increase LSAMP RNA and protein, and transcription-blocking experiments were interpreted as slower LSAMP RNA decay. The paper proposed that Lnc-LSAMP-1 helps stabilize LSAMP RNA. This is **prior experimental evidence in cancer cells**, not a Brain6 discovery or evidence that the same mechanism operates in neurons.

## What was actually studied

The authors reported Lnc-LSAMP-1 and LSAMP expression in non-small-cell lung cancer tissues; their article describes 170 paired tissue samples overall and gives 143 pairs for the correlation in the Figure 6 caption. They also used lentiviral overexpression in A549 and PC-9 cells, measured LSAMP expression, used actinomycin D to examine RNA decay, and reported an LSAMP knockdown rescue of a proliferation phenotype. Ectopic overexpression is not the same as a small, naturally occurring allelic change. The cancer tissue and cell lines do not establish the normal brain cell context or direction of any rs62264764 effect.

The paper's text describes direct binding and attributes it to Figure 6f, while the same figure caption identifies panel f as LSAMP siRNA efficiency; subsequent panel references also differ between text and caption. Its abstract mentions RNA pull-down, but this review does not resolve the apparent panel-reference discrepancy or independently verify the binding reagent and interaction. Treat **direct molecular binding as the authors' proposed interpretation, not a settled mechanism**. The exact sequence and isoform of the paper's overexpression construct were not independently matched to the transcript measured in the Brain6 molecular-QTL datasets; NCBI's alias is a gene-level mapping, not proof that every isoform or assay is identical.

## Relevance to Brain6 and what would test it

The existing [aging-brain xQTL lookup](../rs62264764_brain_xqtl/RESULTS.md) associates rs62264764 with LINC03051 expression in oligodendrocytes and excitatory neurons, but these associations do not establish mediation. The [selected single-signal disease–QTL analyses](../rs62264764_brain_xqtl/dense_coloc/RESULTS.md) favor distinct dominant signals in oligodendrocytes; their assumptions leave secondary signals unresolved. The rs62264764 AlphaGenome frontal DNase signal remains a **model prediction**, not a measured effect on either transcript. Thus neither the cancer experiment nor the xQTL establishes that the variant changes LINC03051, changes LSAMP through it, or influences sleep or ADHD through this pathway.

A discriminating follow-up would first resolve relevant transcript isoforms and genomic LD in suitable brain-cell data. In independently derived human neurons, compare allele-edited isogenic lines and perturb LINC03051 with more than one RNA-targeting reagent, appropriate controls and rescue; measure LINC03051 and LSAMP RNA and protein, RNA stability, and neuronal phenotypes. An allele effect, a transcript perturbation effect and rescue would need separate replication and uncertainty estimates. Distinguish an RNA-mediated effect from a cis-DNA or transcriptional effect using complementary designs. Even a positive neuronal experiment would not alone establish mediation of either clinical phenotype.

This is a targeted literature check, not a systematic review or a claim that the interaction is unique. The cited study and NCBI record are publicly accessible. No clinical interpretation, independent two-trait replication or repair of failed LAVA gates follows from this evidence.
