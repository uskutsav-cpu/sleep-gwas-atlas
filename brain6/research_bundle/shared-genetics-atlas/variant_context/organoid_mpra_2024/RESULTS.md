# Public forebrain-organoid MPRA: candidate coverage check

The [2024 forebrain-organoid MPRA study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54302-2) made its supplemental workbooks available through [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10873509/supplementaryFiles), without a Synapse account. Its reporter assay tested approximately 7,000 previously selected 270-base enhancer fragments in iPSCs and differentiating forebrain organoids. This is a useful public functional resource, but the accessible tables did not yield a direct measurement for the selected chromosome 3 candidate under our coordinate lookup.

In the workbook's reporter-activity sheet, 6,737 of 6,892 data rows had parseable genomic interval labels; 388 were on chromosome 3. A separate external-dataset intersection sheet had 6,757 coordinate rows, including the same number on chromosome 3. **Zero rows** in either sheet overlapped the GRCh38 positions of rs62264764 or rs62264767, or the rs62264764-centered 501-base AlphaGenome summary window, when treating source coordinates as GRCh38 zero-based half-open intervals. A subsequent sensitivity also found zero rows overlapping either candidate’s GRCh37 position or a wider 1,001-base interval around rs62264764 (chr3:117639074–117640075, BED), assuming that coordinate convention. The wider GRCh37 interval is a proximity check, not a verified exact liftover of the 501-base AlphaGenome window. The two-build checks both yielded no matches, but the exact reporter-coordinate assembly and interval convention have not been independently verified; the paper's explicit GRCh38 reference for a separate scATAC analysis is insufficient to establish the reporter table's build. Thus these are conditional coordinate non-matches, not an authoritative determination that the variants were not tested. The 155 activity-sheet rows without parseable genomic intervals include controls and were not independently mapped to genomic sequences.

Even a confirmed absence in this deliberately selected reporter library would not show that the region is inactive, that the alleles have no regulatory effect, or that the AlphaGenome prediction is incorrect. This study does not provide the needed allele-specific comparison at these candidates. The developmental organoid experiment also differs from the adult frontal-cortex track used in the AlphaGenome prediction. No gene–sleep–ADHD causal mechanism or local genetic correlation follows from this lookup.

[Protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [scan code](scan.py), [receipt with source archive and member hashes, row counts and column names](receipt.json), and [matched rows](matched_rows.json) record the search. The 30 MB supplemental archive was held in memory and not retained; two retrievals produced different archive-level SHA-256 values despite identical hashes for both examined workbook members, so the whole-archive hash is a retrieval fingerprint rather than a stable release identifier. workbook members were read through ZIP CRC checks, and the generated hashes document retrieved bytes rather than matching an independently published checksum. The lookup uses a post-selected region and is not a multiple-testing discovery analysis.
