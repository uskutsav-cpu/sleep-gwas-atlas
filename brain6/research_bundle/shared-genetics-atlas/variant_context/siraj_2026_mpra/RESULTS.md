# Public large-scale reporter assay: selected Brain6 variants

See also the [cross-assay candidate comparison](../crossdisorder_mpra/CROSS_ASSAY_COMPARISON.md), which identifies four variants appearing in this screen and the Lee neural-progenitor matrices without claiming validated replication.

[Siraj et al., Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10121-6) tested hundreds of thousands of variants in several reporter-assay cell lines. This is measured transcriptional activity of short inserted constructs, not AlphaGenome prediction or a direct assay of a variant in its endogenous chromatin. The neural-derived **SK-N-SH** cell line is a limited model for mature human brain cells.

We matched the existing 425 ranked Brain6 variants, converted by the previously documented GRCh38-to-GRCh37 chain, to the authors' released variant/oligonucleotide design by chromosome, position, reference and alternate allele. **33** candidate variants matched the design; among them **32** matched one or more of the nine SK-N-SH allele-comparison files (38 library-specific rows). The design file uses coordinates consistent with the GRCh37 matches, supported by the paper's hg19-to-hg38 descriptions; this is not an independent full source-assembly audit. The rs62264764 variant itself was not among the matched design rows. Failure to find it is not evidence of absence of functional effect.

The insomnia-associated marker **rs1456193**, linked to rs62264764 at reference r²≈0.821 in the earlier 503-person European 1000 Genomes analysis, was tested in one SK-N-SH library. Its reported log2 allelic skew was **0.00094** (SE 0.12882), nominal p≈0.994 and source BH-adjusted FDR≈0.999. Neither allele met the paper's stated activity threshold in this row. This is a measured, nonsignificant reporter comparison at a linked marker in one context; it does not directly test rs62264764, distinguish linked causal variants, prove a zero effect, assign LSAMP, or replicate the sleep–ADHD association. The allele contrast and source estimates are preserved in [all rows](sknsh_results.tsv); no disease-risk direction is inferred from reporter skew.

A [four-cell-line OL33 follow-up](rs1456193_other_cells/RESULTS.md) likewise found no source-FDR significant allele skew for the linked marker in those non-neural cell contexts. This does not rule out effects in other tissues or directly test rs62264764.

A [second linked reporter marker](chr3_candidate_ld/RESULTS.md), rs9815484, also has r²≈0.821 with rs62264764 in the European reference. Its neural-derived and four non-neural OL33 allele-skew rows are not significant under the source FDR; these are context-limited assays of a different variant.

For the chromosome 5 shared-trait leads, rs2431108 and rs77960 appeared in two SK-N-SH libraries each; the source allele-skew FDR values for all four comparisons exceed 0.05. The **post-selected** rs12658032 has an OL33 source allele-skew FDR≈0.017 and OL32 FDR≈0.756; however, the two library rows differ in estimated uncertainty and activity, and its OL33 row does not meet the stated per-row activity threshold. We do not label this variant an author-defined emVar or infer independent confirmation without checking the full authors' aggregation rules and additional library context. The analysis involves multiple cell types, libraries, candidates and earlier selection steps; source FDR is not a project-wide correction for all the explorations.

The authors define expression-modulating variants using an activity condition and allele-effect FDR, and define a stricter trait-associated regulatory variant class by additional criteria. A nominal or adjusted skew alone does not establish either classification. Results in different sequence windows, cells, cohorts or sources can differ; the activity assessment and allele effects do not identify an endogenous gene target. The SK-N-SH results are functional context only, not independent GWAS replication and not a repair of the failed LAVA QC.

The [design matches](design_matches.tsv), [cell-specific table matches](cell_specific_matches.tsv), [SK-N-SH results](sknsh_results.tsv), source [table receipt](table_receipt.json), [range-fetch receipt](sknsh_receipt.json), and [scripts](scan_de.py) document the lookup. Selected ZIP members were range-fetched from the authors' [Zenodo processed-data archive](https://zenodo.org/records/15297965); each member passed its ZIP CRC and gzip read, but the 929-MB whole archive was not downloaded or independently hashed. The source design archive and published Table 7 are stored on the SSD and individually SHA-256 hashed in the receipt. This focused lookup did not search the other assay cell types for allelic results.

A [reference LD follow-up](chr5_lead_ld/RESULTS.md) finds rs12658032 linked to rs2431108 and rs77960 at r²≈0.77 in 503 European reference individuals. Its allele-skew results differ across assay libraries, so the LD supports prioritization but does not establish an effector variant.
