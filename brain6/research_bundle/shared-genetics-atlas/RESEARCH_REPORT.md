# Brain6: sleep, ADHD and frailty — research report

## Research question and interpretation

This project asks whether sleep and neuropsychiatric traits share genetic signals, how those signals relate to distinct frailty measures, and whether existing functional data and machine learning help prioritize follow-up. The evidence supports **genome-wide association patterns and testable biological hypotheses**, not causation, clinical prediction or a confirmed gene linking sleep, ADHD and frailty. Unless otherwise noted, results reuse existing datasets and are exploratory.

## What the genetics show

The same-input insomnia–ADHD LDSC recheck estimated **r_g = 0.4033 (SE 0.0318)**. The FI–Fried score (FFS) recheck estimated **r_g = 0.7534 (SE 0.0199)**. These are genetic correlations, not within-person score correlations, and repeating the same inputs is a computational recheck rather than independent replication. See the [global audits](global_audit/GLOBAL_RECHECK.md) and [phenotypic comparison](phenotype_comparison/PHENOTYPIC_VS_GENETIC.md).

A [MVP-insomnia–FinnGen-clinical-ADHD LDSC sensitivity](replication/mvp_finngen_genomewide/RESULTS.md) estimated r_g 0.4729 (SE 0.0674) using source p-values and signed rounded odds ratios. Elevated univariate intercepts, approximate effective N, source precision and unresolved overlap limit interpretation; it is not confirmed independent replication.

On the previously analyzed common SNP support, questionnaire insomnia had estimated r_g **0.6404 with FI, 0.4840 with FFS and 0.4355 with hospital frailty risk score (HFRS)**. These scores measure different constructs: the FI accumulates health deficits and includes insomnia and tiredness; FFS includes exhaustion; HFRS uses hospital diagnosis codes and includes a somnolence-related category. Measurement overlap, cohort differences and ascertainment prevent attributing the differences to physical frailty alone. Source-specific estimates and paired-comparison limitations are documented in the [three-frailty comparison](global_audit/brain6_frailty_covariance/three_frailty_common_support/RESULTS.md).

A post-hoc [MVP clinical-insomnia sensitivity](replication/mvp_frailty_genomewide/RESULTS.md) estimated genetic correlations of **0.5531 (SE 0.0390)** with FI, **0.3997 (0.0352)** with FFS and **0.5697 (0.0314)** with HFRS on 1,103,239 common markers. MVP and HFRS univariate intercepts were elevated, and unknown overlap, ascertainment, reference mismatch and item content limit interpretation; no formal contrast or sleep-independent frailty claim follows.

On a smaller common set assembled for the ML analysis, [paired clinical-insomnia contrasts](frailty_sensitivity/mvp_three_frailty_paired/RESULTS.md) found FI−FFS = 0.1476 (SE 0.0355) and FFS−HFRS = −0.1817 (SE 0.0407); both pass a post-hoc three-contrast correction. FI−HFRS did not. Different constructs, symptom content and cohorts prevent interpreting these differences as the effect of removing exhaustion.

A post-hoc [joint genetic analysis](global_audit/mvp_adhd_frailty_partial/RESULTS.md) estimated insomnia–frailty partial genetic correlations given the ADHD genetic component of 0.378 (FI), 0.165 (FFS), and 0.426 (HFRS). These are linear projections of genetic effects, not individual-level ADHD adjustment or causal interactions; symptom overlap, source dependence and elevated intercepts remain limitations.

A further [two-component projection](global_audit/mvp_adhd_mdd_frailty_partial/RESULTS.md) estimated partial insomnia–frailty genetic correlations given both ADHD and depression of 0.200 (FI), 0.073 (FFS), and 0.328 (HFRS); the FFS interval included zero. This is not diagnosis adjustment or mediation, and shared symptoms and source overlap limit interpretation. A later [paired contrast](global_audit/mvp_adhd_mdd_frailty_partial/paired_projection_change/RESULTS.md) found lower two-component estimates for all three measures; the mathematical contrast is not a mediation test.

A [clinical-insomnia physical-function sensitivity](component_exclusion/mvp_physical_components/RESULTS.md) estimated r_g **−0.2169 (SE 0.0308)** for walking pace and **−0.1174 (0.0335)** for left grip strength. Both are individual physical measures rather than item-excluded frailty; the elevated insomnia intercept, coding assumptions and unknown overlap remain limitations.

A [prior UK Biobank study](component_exclusion/prior_frailty_neuropsychiatric/REVIEW.md) already combined physical frailty and its components with sleep-disorder outcomes, genetics, brain imaging and biomarkers. Its five-component phenotype includes exhaustion, and its data statement does not identify an anonymous full frailty-component GWAS download; it narrows the project’s novelty claim.

The [older-adult low-grip sensitivity](component_exclusion/mvp_older_weakness/RESULTS.md) estimated a clinical-insomnia genetic correlation of **0.1747 (SE 0.0451, logged p = 0.0001)** on 992,358 markers. This is a single physical component, not an exhaustion-excluded frailty score; cohort overlap, source sample-size approximations and the elevated insomnia intercept constrain interpretation.

To assess physical components separately, older-adult low grip strength yielded questionnaire and registry insomnia correlations of **0.1693 (SE 0.0379)** and **0.1708 (SE 0.0482)**. The public moderate-activity GWAS gave 0.0625 (0.0355, p=0.0785) and 0.0041 (0.0430, p=0.9236). These are separate physical traits, not sleep- or exhaustion-excluded frailty scores; their estimates differ in design and have overlap, LD-reference and sample-size limitations. See [grip](component_exclusion/older_weakness/RESULTS.md) and [activity](component_exclusion/moderate_activity_ldsc/RESULTS.md). A [2026 Canadian component GWAS](component_exclusion/CLSA_2026_SEARCH.md) analyzed individual physical components, but its stated data access requires eligibility and an anonymous full-statistics download was not established. Full GWAS for matched FI without sleep/fatigue or FFS without exhaustion were not located; the [construction protocol](component_exclusion/CONSTRUCTION_PROTOCOL.md) describes what would be needed.

A post-hoc [explicit genetic physical-function index](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/INDEX_RESULTS.md) equally weights standardized genetic components of walking, grip and moderate activity, with no explicit sleep or exhaustion item. It gives correlations of −0.0736 (block-jackknife SE 0.0350) with registry insomnia and −0.2309 (0.0260) with questionnaire insomnia. The registry result does not meet a limited two-source correction (adjusted p=0.0711); the questionnaire result shares UK Biobank with the indicators. The index avoids the zero indicator-residual covariance restriction in the earlier latent-factor analysis. A [joint factor fit](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/JOINT_RESULTS.md) and [residual-covariance sensitivity](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/RESIDUAL_RESULTS.md) show that latent-factor estimates depend on that restriction and are not identified if all indicator-specific links are free. The explicit index remains a distinct, post-hoc genetic construct rather than a person-level frailty score or matched item-deletion GWAS. Source overlap, differing definitions, reference fit, exploratory selection and lack of independent validation limit interpretation.

A [weight-definition sensitivity](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/WEIGHT_RESULTS.md) finds that weighting activity more heavily shifts both source associations toward zero. Ranges over all nonnegative weights are conditional point-estimate ranges, not confidence intervals or validated weights; the equal-weight index is retained. The [scientific figure](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/explicit_index.png) shows individual-measure and fixed-index estimates with unadjusted intervals.

An earlier [MacArthur cohort analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2754409/) identified phenotypic frailty subdimensions involving gait, grip and activity and exhaustion-related traits. Exhaustion also loaded on the physical component, so this is not evidence that the traits are independent, nor a genetic analysis.

A prior [ELSA-Brasil observational study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9824948/) removed exhaustion from both an FI and physical frailty phenotype in analyses of common mental disorders. This is precedent for the measurement question, not a GWAS, a sleep-specific analysis, or direct evidence about genetic correlation.

A newly identified [public physical-function composite](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/SOURCE.md) combines lung function, grip strength and height without explicit sleep or exhaustion items. It is not an item-excluded frailty score. The [completed comparison](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/RESULTS.md) found genetic correlations of −0.0900 (SE 0.0274) with clinical insomnia, −0.1909 (0.0232) with FI, −0.4123 (0.0189) with FFS, and −0.0979 (0.0189) with HFRS. All four pass a post-hoc four-test threshold, but possible cohort overlap, intercepts and source assumptions limit interpretation. An additional [post-hoc conservative contrast](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/conservative_contrasts/RESULTS.md) finds differences involving FFS even under an approximate maximal-SE bound; these are not matched exhaustion-removal effects. The source paper already assessed insomnia and this composite using Mendelian randomization, which addresses a different estimand.

A [selected chromosome 3 lookup](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/brain6_loci/RESULTS.md) found physical-function p≈0.276 for each of rs62264764 and rs62264767. These two post-selected rows are not a regional shared-causal-variant test or proof of no effect.

A [post-selected chromosome 5 lookup](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/brain6_chr5/RESULTS.md) found nominal physical-function associations for rs2431108 and rs77960 (p≈9.6×10⁻⁵ and 8.9×10⁻⁵). Neither is genome-wide significant, and the selected marginal associations do not establish a shared causal variant or validate sequence-model predictions.

A [regional physical-function colocalization sensitivity](component_exclusion/schoeler_physical_function/chr5_coloc/RESULTS.md) at the selected chromosome 5 sleep–ADHD region was prior-sensitive and did not establish a shared causal variant. The physical-function signal was modest and the single-signal and overlap assumptions remain unresolved.

A newly located [2026 sarcopenia-index GWAS](component_exclusion/SARCOPENIA_INDEX_2026.md) has an anonymous catalog file, but its phenotype is a creatinine-to-cystatin-C ratio and can reflect kidney and biomarker biology. It does not provide an exhaustion-removed frailty phenotype and was not used for a physical-frailty claim.

A [2026 multivariate depression–physical-function factor study](component_exclusion/depression_physical_factor_2026/REVIEW.md) is additional prior work on joint psychiatric and physical genetic architecture. Its factor includes depression traits and is not an exhaustion-excluded frailty score; an anonymous complete factor-GWAS download was not established in this check.

## Candidate biology

A [clinical ADHD–insomnia–hospital-frailty follow-up](replication/clinical_three_trait/RESULTS.md) combines seven selected markers across existing sources; none of six complete markers crosses its descriptive three-trait benchmark. This retrospective result does not establish or rule out shared biology.

A post-hoc [31-variant evidence crosswalk](biological_mechanisms/three_trait_bridge/RESULTS.md) joins existing frailty and sleep results with selected reporter and molecular evidence. In a chromosome 5 region, rs325485 and rs6874138 were allele-dependent active elements in a neural-progenitor reporter study and were associated with FI and FFS in the selected screen. A computational link nominates **NUDT12**, but available astrocyte expression associations were weak; the target and effect in human neurons are unproven. On chromosome 7, rs1476535 has sleep–hospital-frailty associations in the follow-up, and **FOXP2** offers a neuronal-development hypothesis, but gene-level experiments did not test the variant. On chromosome 3, rs62264764 has a predicted frontal accessibility change and separate **LINC03051** expression associations in oligodendrocytes and excitatory neurons; prior single-signal analyses did not establish shared dominant molecular and disease signals. These results are not a shared three-trait mechanism.

A [chromosome 7 allele-aligned comparison](biological_mechanisms/chr7_haplotype_prediction/RESULTS.md) finds that two linked, conditional FI–insomnia candidates have opposite local predicted accessibility changes. They lie in different windows, and this does not establish opposing measured effects or identify a causal gene.

A [FOXP2 tissue crosswalk](biological_mechanisms/foxp2_tissue_bridge/RESULTS.md) aligns the selected allele with expression records in pituitary, adrenal and adipose tissue. These associations do not establish brain mediation or a causal pathway.

A [425-candidate clinical ADHD follow-up](replication/clinical_three_trait_425/RESULTS.md) matched 419 candidates across insomnia, ADHD and hospital-frailty sources, with no selected-set three-trait benchmark crossings. The retrospective screen does not test a shared causal variant or rule out shared biology.

The AlphaGenome/JBrowse tracks are sequence-model **predictions**, not measured regulatory effects; the public reporter assays are measured in particular cell models and do not by themselves identify endogenous targets. The [assay–prediction comparison](biological_mechanisms/cross_assay_prediction/RESULTS.md) shows that some predictions vary in sign across summary windows. No AlphaGenome output was used to train a model. The local-correlation quality gates failed, and prior diagnostic PLACO+ crossings remain unvalidated; see the [local audit](local_evidence/LOCAL_FRAILTY_AUDIT.md) and [PLACO audit](placo_diagnostic/completed_audit/RESULTS.md).

An additional [2026 Flint et al. preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.17.25336027v2.full) uses Foote's published frailty-domain genetic factors to study Alzheimer's disease with univariable and multivariable Mendelian randomization. The authors report that unhealthy-lifestyle estimates attenuated after adjustment for socioeconomic traits, and a disability-domain estimate changed direction across models. These are model-dependent, assumption-dependent findings, not validation of a sleep-independent physical-frailty effect. The factors reuse a joint model containing insomnia and tiredness, and the preprint is not a matched item-deletion GWAS. It is relevant prior work when framing disease-specific novelty.

## ML analysis

The project built an exploratory sleep–brain–frailty embedding from genetic-correlation estimates. It predicts correlations between traits, not an individual's risk. Its initial rank-two masked-cell model did not outperform the outcome-mean baseline; later multi-start optimization improved reconstruction on already inspected data. See the [embedding report](multiscale_embedding/REPORT.md).

A subsequent [group-held-out ridge model](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/RESULTS.md) uses six sleep–brain correlations to predict a sleep–frailty correlation. Its 12 sleep measures and their source cohorts are related, so the holdouts are not independent cohorts. The [bounded version](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/RESULTS.md) keeps outputs within the correlation range and achieved post-hoc pooled RMSE 0.1031 versus 0.1108 for unbounded ridge on the same 36 cells. An [equal-group evaluation](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/equal_group_evaluation/RESULTS.md) gives the eight groups equal weight without creating independent validation. [Feature ablations](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/feature_ablation/RESULTS.md) and [source-exclusion tests](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/source_exclusion/RESULTS.md) show that results depend on inputs and training sources.

The runnable prototype now reports each input's range in the remaining training rows. For the clinical-insomnia example, ADHD, depression and bipolar correlations exceed the respective training maxima after excluding the insomnia group: 0.4857 versus 0.3728, 0.5623 versus 0.3114, and 0.2507 versus 0.2304. Its predictions therefore involve marginal extrapolation on three inputs. These ranges are descriptive, not confidence intervals; being inside each range would not establish joint support or reliable prediction.

The strongest current check is the [clinical-insomnia feature transfer](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_feature_transfer/common_support_run/RESULTS.md), which re-estimated six disease features and three frailty targets on the same 991,707 SNPs. With the model fixed, predictions versus observed genetic correlations were **0.654 versus 0.543 for FI**, **0.608 versus 0.395 for FFS**, and **0.535 versus 0.577 for HFRS**. The FFS residual was 0.213 with [conditional paired block-jackknife SE 0.039](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_feature_transfer/common_support_run/jackknife_uncertainty/RESULTS.md). That SE captures shared LDSC-block variation conditional on the fitted model; it excludes training and model-selection uncertainty. Outcomes were already inspected, disease and frailty sources were reused, cohort overlap is uncertain, and LDSC intercepts for MVP and HFRS were elevated. This is a post-hoc source-transfer sensitivity, not independent validation. The model's training correlations still use other marker sets.

Earlier [different-support MVP features](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_feature_transfer/RESULTS.md) and a [questionnaire-feature proxy](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_proxy_check/RESULTS.md) are preserved as distinct experiments. A [fixed-model one-SE input stress test](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_feature_transfer/input_stress/RESULTS.md) for the different-support MVP fit gave a Fried-score prediction range of 0.545–0.662. This hypothetical range is not a confidence interval and excludes target and training uncertainty.

A [clinical-insomnia training-group deletion check](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/mvp_feature_transfer/common_support_run/training_group_deletion/RESULTS.md) found FFS predictions ranging 0.498–0.625 and HFRS 0.419–0.599 across seven overlapping refits. These are stability ranges, not prediction intervals or independent tests.

Training stability is a further limitation. In the [unbounded group-deletion sensitivity](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/training_group_sensitivity/RESULTS.md), the largest sleep-efficiency–FFS shift was from +0.041 to −0.543 after dropping a training group and retuning; one prediction left the valid correlation range. The 252 overlapping fits are not independent. A [bounded deletion check](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/training_group_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) still found a maximum shift of 0.566. In a separate [input-uncertainty stress test](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/bounded_model/input_uncertainty/RESULTS.md), 11 of 36 hypothetical one-SE boxes spanned zero; those boxes are not confidence intervals. The prototype needs larger source-audited data, uncertainty-aware modeling, frozen baseline comparisons and genuinely independent cohorts before predictive utility can be assessed.

## What can be claimed and what remains

1. **Supported:** several same-data genetic-correlation estimates can be reproduced; physical components and frailty definitions show source-sensitive associations; selected reporter and brain-cell data support follow-up hypotheses.
2. **Not established:** causal interactions, a shared causal variant or gene across sleep, ADHD and frailty, a validated item-excluded frailty GWAS, independent joint locus replication, or a passing local-correlation family.
3. **Priority next work:** obtain approved matched item-excluded GWAS; use study-matched LD and overlap information to rerun local QC; evaluate selected loci in independent cohorts; conduct multi-signal QTL colocalization and endogenous allele-specific experiments; evaluate ML with independent data and appropriate terms.

The [scope audit](SCOPE_AUDIT.md), [literature comparison](INTRODUCTIONS_AND_COMPARISON.md) and [reproducibility inventory](BUNDLE_INTEGRITY.md) give the detailed source trail. Existing literature already reports insomnia–ADHD overlap, sleep–frailty genetics and multivariate frailty modeling; this report does not assert first discovery.

## Summary-statistics reconstruction feasibility

The [6 November algebra and computational audit](component_exclusion/identifiability/LINEAR_ROUTE.md) establishes a narrow alternative to participant-level access: coefficients of an untransformed additive score can be reconstructed from compatible same-sample component GWAS, provided scales and joint sampling covariance are available. This does not apply automatically to the current transformed FI, binary frailty outcomes or incompletely documented component releases. An exact counterexample shows that identical marginal item and full-score distributions can coexist with different exhaustion-deleted binary associations. The explicit physical-function genetic index remains the available distinct-construct sensitivity.

## Retained-input LDSC reproduction

With the SSD reconnected on 6 November, an [isolated rerun](component_exclusion/physical_factor_workaround/VERIFICATION.md) reproduced all nine physical-index input correlations and SEs and all 27 vectors of 200 block-delete values exactly. All ten retained input hashes matched the archived receipts, and the rerun recorded and rechecked input, reference and code hashes. This verifies the LDSC stage from retained munged common-support inputs using the same reference; original GWAS generation, initial munging, overlap uncertainty and independent validation remain outside this check.
