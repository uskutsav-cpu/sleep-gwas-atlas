# A conservative product-bound check cannot rescue the PLACO candidates

Under the scan's cap |Z|≤√80, any selected product T=|Z₁Z₂| at least t implies that the null trait's |Z| is at least t/√80, whichever trait is null. If its marginal null Z is standard normal, this gives the unconditional bound **p_cap=2Φ(−T/√80)** for the capped product event, without assuming independence or a particular non-null effect. Markers failing the cap would receive p=1. This is a deliberately conservative mathematical benchmark, not the published PLACO+ statistic or an established replacement analysis.

For any marker passing the cap, T/√80 ≤ min(|Z₁|,|Z₂|). Consequently **p_cap is always at least the ordinary max-p conjunction value** derived from those same Zs. The bound cannot rescue a marker that fails max-p. At the atlas's two-pair Bonferroni threshold of 2.1963×10⁻⁸, the product would need to reach approximately 50.05. None of the 82 previously selected FI/FFS PLACO+ crossings passes either this bound or max-p. The smallest cap bound is 0.00161 at rs6596578 (max-p 2.50×10⁻⁵), compared with its much smaller diagnostic PLACO+ value. The full selected-row comparison is [here](candidates.tsv).

This result is a logical property of the bound and capped statistics, not a new estimate of PLACO's real-data false-discovery rate. The normal-null requirement and source p-value validity still matter, and the 82 candidates were chosen after observing the PLACO results. It neither evaluates unselected markers nor proves that other calibrated approaches cannot yield discoveries. It does explain why simply guarding PLACO's product with the cap and a worst-case null cannot add power beyond the already reported max-p check.
