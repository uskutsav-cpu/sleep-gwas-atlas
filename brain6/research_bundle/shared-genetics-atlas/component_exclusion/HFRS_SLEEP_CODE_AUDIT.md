# Hospital frailty score: sleep-related code audit

Checked 28 October 2026. The [Mak et al. HFRS GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y) says its continuous score sums the original 109 weighted ICD-10 codes, using all available codes from age 30 in the main analysis. The original score is a diagnosis-based hospital-risk measure, not a four-component physical Fried score.

An independently published [109-code HFRS table](https://storage.imrpress.com/journal/RCM/25/2/10.31083/j.rcm2502052/attachment/3d5b86b30d84f7c46af9ef94a87fd1b0.pdf), supplementary table 2, lists **R40, somnolence, stupor and coma, weight 2.5**. Thus the original HFRS has a sleepiness-related diagnostic category. This is not an insomnia questionnaire item and R40 also includes conditions other than somnolence; the presence or frequency of its subcodes in the two GWAS cohorts has not been verified here. The paper describes applying all 109 codes, but its study-specific supplementary table was not independently retrieved in this check. Do not label these HFRS GWAS results as sleep-free, exhaustion-free physical frailty, or as an analysis from which an item was removed.

The existing [four-way insomnia-source comparison](../global_audit/ukb_hfrs_public/RESULTS.md) remains useful as an observational genetic comparison of differently defined traits: UKB HFRS–questionnaire insomnia r_g 0.5288 (SE 0.0334); UKB HFRS–registry insomnia 0.3755 (0.0416); FinnGen HFRS–questionnaire insomnia 0.4363 (0.0253); FinnGen HFRS–registry insomnia 0.6132 (0.0326). These are earlier analyses, not a newly run item-exclusion GWAS. Cohort overlap, ancestry, ascertainment, source sample-size uncertainty and phenotype differences prevent attributing the pattern to R40 or to sleep-item overlap. The existing dementia-excluded HFRS sensitivity does not establish that R40 was removed.

The [physical-function and falls comparisons](falls_sleep_ldsc/RESULTS.md) supply distinct proxies; they are not composite frailty scores. A genuine within-cohort exclusion analysis requires the modified phenotype and its signed genome-wide statistics or appropriate individual-level data. This audit neither recovers those statistics nor establishes how much R40 contributed to any correlation.
