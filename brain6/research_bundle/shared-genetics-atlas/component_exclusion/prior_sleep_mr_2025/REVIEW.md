# Prior multi-sleep frailty MR: scope and correction audit

[Gao et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12708208/) applied bidirectional Mendelian randomization to six sleep traits and the Atkins FI meta-analysis. The sleep traits were duration, short sleep, long sleep, daytime naps, morning preference and chronotype. This is prior work on **multiple sleep phenotypes and FI**, so that broad framing alone is not novel. It is a directional MR study, not the requested unified comparison across FI, FFS and HFRS, local correlation and functional annotations. The FI source includes sleep-related items and is the same UK Biobank/TwinGene FI source used elsewhere in this atlas; it is not independent two-trait replication.

The article reports short sleep → FI p=1.24×10⁻¹⁰ and FI → short sleep p=0.015 in its main MR analyses. Its methods state a Bonferroni threshold of **0.05/6 = 0.00833** for six sleep traits. The forward p is below that threshold; the reverse p is **nominal only** and does not meet the stated six-test threshold (0.015×6=0.09, using the printed rounded p). This is an arithmetic comparison to the paper's stated family, not a reanalysis or a determination of any broader family across directions. The paper describes the relationship as bidirectional despite this distinction. Morning preference → FI is also nominal at p=0.033, above 0.00833.

The reported MR estimates depend on instrument validity, study overlap and phenotype definitions; filtering variants associated with the outcome and nonsignificant pleiotropy tests do not prove the exclusion restriction. The paper's instruments, method choices, sensitivity tests and any overlap require independent assessment. The study does not supply a matched sleep/fatigue-excluded FI GWAS. Its directional results are not evidence that insomnia causes frailty or that a particular Brain6 locus acts through a causal gene.

The source article's Methods §§2.2–2.4 document FI ancestry/source, the six traits, instrument filtering and stated correction; Results §§3.2–3.3 give the cited p-values. The arithmetic uses rounded published values and should be rechecked against underlying estimates if available.
