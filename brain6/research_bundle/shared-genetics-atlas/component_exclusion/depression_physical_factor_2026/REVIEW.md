# Prior multivariate depression–physical-function genetics

[Zeng, Yang and Xu (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13409734/) used Genomic SEM on five GWAS traits: depressive symptoms, depression diagnosis, grip strength, appendicular lean mass and walking pace. Their factor represents a genetic covariance dimension, not an individual clinical frailty score. The authors report opposite factor-loading directions for depression and the physical traits, and evaluate a factor GWAS and downstream annotations. This prior work narrows any claim that multivariate modeling of psychiatric and physical-function genetic associations is new.

It does **not** provide the requested four-component Fried score without exhaustion, a matched FI without sleep/fatigue, or a validated sleep-independent frailty phenotype. The factor deliberately includes depression-related traits; the label of each physical input alone cannot establish independence from sleep, fatigue or shared cohorts. The authors report a constrained residual variance after a negative unconstrained estimate, an LDSC intercept below one, and substantial SNP-level heterogeneity among tested significant signals. Those features warrant careful model and variant-level evaluation; they do not alone invalidate the study or demonstrate a causal mechanism. The paper notes its use of summary data and predominantly European-ancestry sources, and describes genetic covariance rather than within-person co-occurrence or causal direction.

The article's data statement, as reproduced in the [Open Science Code Review record](https://openscicode.org/paper/doi_10.3390_genes17070813/), identifies public input GWAS and supplementary post-GWAS results, and says custom scripts are available on request. It does not establish an anonymous download of complete signed factor-GWAS statistics. The publisher and PMC pages were rate-limited during direct access in this check; the scientific descriptions above were obtained from the indexed primary article text, and the availability statement should be rechecked at the publisher when accessible. No factor file was downloaded or analyzed, and this limited check cannot rule out another release. Supplementary selected results should not be treated as a complete genome-wide dataset.

A defensible follow-up would first locate the complete factor output and its provenance, effect-allele/build conventions, effective-N and sample overlap, and evaluate heterogeneity and LD-score suitability. Even then, an association with insomnia would be a comparison with a mixed depression–physical-function factor, not a test of an exhaustion-excluded frailty score. For the user's requested question, appropriately documented matched component-excluded GWAS remain necessary.
