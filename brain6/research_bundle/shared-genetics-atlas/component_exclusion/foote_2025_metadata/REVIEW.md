# Foote et al. 2025: prior multivariate frailty genetics and reusable data

[Foote et al., Nature Genetics 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02269-0), published 4 August 2025, is important prior work for this project. The authors used genomic structural equation modeling of GWAS summary statistics for 30 frailty-related deficits, including insomnia and tiredness. Their fitted bifactor model includes one general genetic factor and six residual factors for limited social support, unhealthy lifestyle, multimorbidity, metabolic problems, poorer cognition, and disability. They report 408 genomic risk loci across the factors and evaluate genetic correlations, functional context and prediction in external cohorts. These are published findings; the external prediction analyses do not establish independent replication of our specific sleep–frailty candidate associations.

This study pre-empts any broad claim that *multivariate* genetic analysis of frailty deficits, or modeling frailty domains rather than only aggregate scores, is new. A defensible remaining hypothesis is whether a consistently quality-controlled framework connecting **multiple sleep phenotypes, ADHD or other diseases, several frailty definitions, local signals and independent functional evidence** adds reproducible value beyond this and other published work. Novelty still requires a systematic comparison and held-out validation. Foote's general factor includes an insomnia indicator, making analyses against insomnia susceptible to direct phenotype/summary-statistic reuse. A factor correlation or a selected regional match would not by itself establish a sleep-independent frailty signal.

## Why a residual factor is not an item-deleted frailty GWAS

The [published model diagram and caption](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02269-0) state that all 30 indicators load on the general factor, and factors 1–6 are residual factors orthogonal to that general factor. The model-wide indicators include insomnia and tiredness. Therefore even a residual factor with a physical-sounding label arises from a joint model containing those traits; orthogonality to the fitted general factor is not evidence of independence from sleep, and it does not recreate a participant-level frailty score with exhaustion removed. The six residual factors may also correlate with one another. Their Q-filtered releases and Nhat require separate suitability checks before ordinary LDSC.

## Public-release audit

The article names GWAS Catalog accessions GCST90624046–GCST90624053. We fetched all eight small publisher metadata YAML files, retained their bytes and SHA-256 hashes, and mapped the labels in `catalog_manifest.tsv`. The first seven are the general and six residual frailty factors in the order above; **GCST90624053 is pneumonia, not an eighth frailty factor**. The metadata state 1-based GRCh37 and European ancestry. They identify `n` as expected sample size (Nhat) for latent factors; the metadata's sample-size field must not be presented as a count of unique people. The publisher states that the factor files were filtered to remove significant factor-specific Q heterogeneity at p<7.14×10⁻⁹. This selection may affect downstream genome-wide statistics and must be assessed rather than assumed equivalent to an unfiltered conventional GWAS.

The [FTP directory for the general factor](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90624001-GCST90625000/GCST90624046/) exposes an approximately 401 MB uncompressed table and a metadata file. The complete original general-factor table was subsequently streamed and checked against its declared MD5; the other six full factor tables were not verified. The manifest records the publisher declarations, and the separate [verification receipt](variant_crosswalk/GCST90624046_receipt.json) records the completed check. The catalog web page displayed blank study fields in this check even though the FTP metadata and article identify the study, so factor mapping rests on the YAML and paper, not the blank web display. Access, licenses, exact contents, allele harmonization, Q-filter impact, summary-statistic overlap and suitability of a chosen analysis must be checked before use.

These are latent-factor results, not a re-estimation of the same people's FI without insomnia or FFS without exhaustion. Group factors are statistically orthogonal to the general factor by model construction; this does not make them independent of the original sleep-related input or equivalent to a clean physical-frailty score. The matched item-deletion analysis in [the construction protocol](../CONSTRUCTION_PROTOCOL.md) still requires approved individual-level data or validated equivalent summary statistics. The factor files are useful as a **distinct** sensitivity and prior-study comparator only after their source, overlap and model assumptions are reviewed.

A [provisional indexed lookup](variant_crosswalk/indexed/RESULTS.md) checks five previously selected anchors in the separate GRCh38 harmonized factor files. Two chr5 markers are below the paper’s factor-GWAS threshold for the general factor, which includes insomnia; the lookup is not independent replication or a component-excluded analysis. Full-file checksum validation was not performed for these indexed remote queries.

The [extended selected-variant crosswalk](variant_crosswalk/indexed/EXTENDED_RESULTS.md) covers 87 post-selected markers and seven factors; remote indexed results are provisional and many markers are absent in the filtered release.

The [published methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02269-0) describe pruning ±1 Mb around significant Q-heterogeneity sites in downstream analyses. A [selected-marker neighborhood check](variant_crosswalk/indexed/ABSENCE_RESULTS.md) found many empty windows in the harmonized release, but cannot attribute individual absences to Q filtering.

A [supplementary-table crosswalk](published_locus_crosswalk/RESULTS.md) records the authors’ own FI/FFS locus categories and documents the UK Biobank insomnia and tiredness inputs. These prior comparisons are not independent two-trait replication.
