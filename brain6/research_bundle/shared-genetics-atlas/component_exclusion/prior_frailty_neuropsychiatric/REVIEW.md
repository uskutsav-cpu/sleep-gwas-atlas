# Prior frailty–neuropsychiatric research: relevance and access

A [UK Biobank study of physical frailty and neuropsychiatric outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13000945/) already combined observational follow-up, component-specific analyses, GWAS, Mendelian randomization, brain imaging and biomarker analyses. Its analytic cohort included 316,905 participants, although component and other analytic sample sizes differ. Its physical frailty phenotype uses five Fried-style criteria, including self-reported exhaustion, along with grip, walking, activity and weight loss. The authors reported an observational association between frailty and later sleep-disorder diagnosis, and separately assessed the five components. These analyses predate the present project and constrain any claim that linking physical frailty, sleep disorders, genetics and brain biology is broadly new.

The observational association and the paper's MR or mediation interpretations rely on different assumptions and measurements; they do not establish that sleep causes frailty, or that a genetic signal specifically mediates a sleep–frailty interaction. Frailty includes exhaustion, and observational component analyses are not GWAS of the four-component phenotype with exhaustion removed. Population selection, self-reported traits, outcome definitions and possible sample overlap also limit direct comparison with the Brain6 results. The article's abstract and results contain differences in some reported MR estimates, so this review does not reproduce a specific MR effect without checking the underlying tables.

The paper says it performed GWAS on frailty and individual components. Its data-availability statement points to application-based UK Biobank data and lists public neuropsychiatric GWAS sources; it does not identify an anonymous full per-variant download for its own frailty/component GWAS in that statement. Its public supplementary materials may contain selected results, but that does not establish access to complete signed genome-wide statistics. Therefore it is useful prior-study context, not an identified account-free item-excluded frailty dataset or a completed replication analysis. The project should seek full signed statistics and source metadata separately and make no claim based on their absence from this one statement alone.
