# Same-sample mobility-skip sensitivity

Use the corrected 2011–2014 NHANES working-score code and the existing 32 hash-checked source files. For people with PFQ054=1 and missing PFQB or PFQC, compare two deterministic **hypothetical** imputations: assign each skipped item 0 (no difficulty) versus 1 (difficulty), retaining the same respondents eligible under both assumptions and the same survey design. PFQ054 asks about difficulty walking without equipment, so assigning 0 may be implausible for some respondents; it is an extreme stress case, not a recommended clinical score. Assignment 1 is the earlier mobility sensitivity. Other missing values are not imputed.

Reproduce four exploratory weighted Pearson correlations on identical respondents: FI vs adapted-Fried, FI vs sleep, FI minus sleep/fatigue vs sleep, and modified FI vs modified adapted-Fried. Calculate paired differences using the joint survey moment covariance and survey degrees of freedom. Check row identity, sample counts, direct weighted arithmetic and agreement of the 1-assignment with the saved corrected mobility-proxy results. Do not interpret the two extremes as formal bounds over all possible missing-data mechanisms: mixed assignments, different eligibility and nonrespondents could yield values outside the two observed endpoints. The working Fried exhaustion proxy, shared physical items, source coding, selection and sample generalizability remain unresolved. This is observational, not a GWAS, independent replication or causal attribution.

After inspecting the two saved paired FI–sleep contrasts, calculate their difference using the same respondents and their joint survey covariance. This second-order comparison is post hoc and its interval is conditional on the two hypothetical scores; it does not prove equivalence, isolate the contribution of a single item, or quantify genetic effects.
