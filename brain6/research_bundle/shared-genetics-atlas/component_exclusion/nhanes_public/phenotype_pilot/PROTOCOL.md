# NHANES within-person phenotype pilot: preregistered working choices

This is an exploratory secondary analysis of public NHANES 2011–2014 data, not a reanalysis of UK Biobank GWAS participants and not a genetic analysis. Before calculating correlations, construct an **approximate** 49-item FI from the identified published table, with the assumptions below, and the separate published adapted Fried five-domain score. Different source papers do not establish that these definitions were previously analyzed together. Report the complete item crosswalk, scoring, cohort counts and unresolved interpretation. If any component cannot be constructed defensibly, stop rather than mislabel a substitute as a published score.

Choose adults aged ≥60, both cycles, positive mobile-exam weights and valid design variables; form a 4-year mobile-exam weight by dividing the 2-year weight by two, use cycle-specific strata and nested PSU identifiers, and preserve the full eligible survey design before domain subsetting. Create the full FI as the mean of up to 49 coded deficits, requiring at least 40 observed items. For a fixed denominator of *available retained items*, form FI-minus-sleep and FI-minus-sleep-and-fatigue after requiring their original items observed; compare all scores on the **same** fixed participant set. This is not the original project FI49, whose missingness and transformation differ. Do not apply threshold-based frailty diagnosis to modified scores.

Predefine unknown responses, refused/do-not-know and `do not do` as missing unless a documented skip implies a specific value. Treat PFQ061B/C as missing when not asked due to PFQ054 by default; a separate sensitivity assigns a deficit in both for reported difficulty walking without equipment, an assumption that must not be called a measured answer. For 2011–2012 HUQ050 4–9 visits, primary coding sets the component missing; compare scenarios assigning 0.5 or 1 to that category. For 5% weight loss below 10 lb with unknown intentionality, primary coding leaves the weight component unknown unless BMI alone satisfies the criterion; report alternate assumptions if feasible. Keep full and modified scores on the common complete set within each scenario. Do not identify all excluded observations as healthy or frail.

FI scoring assumes binary functional difficulty from the table, maximum observed valid grip trial across both hands for its sex/BMI thresholds, 0/0.33/0.66/1 depression items, diagnosis and laboratory thresholds as documented in the crosswalk, and participant-level `RXDCOUNT` without summing repeated medication rows. The FI's BMI 25 and 30 boundaries will use ≤25 as zero, >25 and <30 as 0.5, and ≥30 as one; this resolves an ambiguity in the displayed ranges and is not authenticated as the original authors' code. The adapted Fried phenotype uses the paper's weight, room-walking, lifting, quarter-mile and GPAQ criteria; its fourth component is a walking proxy for exhaustion, not a direct fatigue report. FI and adapted Fried share at least two physical items.

Report weighted Pearson correlations for full FI–full adapted Fried, full FI–the sleep symptom, FI without sleep/fatigue–the sleep symptom, and FI without sleep/fatigue–adapted Fried without the quarter-mile component. Use survey-design linearization and 95% intervals; compute paired differences with the same design for the full versus excluded FI–sleep correlations. These are descriptive post-selected sensitivities, not causal effects, diagnostic validation, a genetic-correlation estimate, or independent replication. Report unweighted sample sizes, eligibility, missingness and alternative scores, not individual records. Do not treat threshold-crossing differences as formal interactions; do not correct exploratory intervals as if this were a frozen project-wide discovery family.
