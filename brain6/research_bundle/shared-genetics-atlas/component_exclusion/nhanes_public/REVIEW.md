# Public NHANES route for a phenotypic item-exclusion comparison

The CDC releases public NHANES data that support a **within-person observational** sensitivity analysis. We identified a [49-item NHANES FI scoring table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11869335/) and a separate [adapted five-domain Fried phenotype](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6440876/) used in NHANES 2011–2014. These are distinct published constructions from different studies, and neither is the project’s UK Biobank FI or FFS. A subsequent [exploratory phenotype pilot](phenotype_pilot/RESULTS.md) calculates correlations using approximate working scores; it is not a validated reproduction or genetic analysis.

## Important measurement overlap

The 49-item table includes self-reported difficulty lifting or carrying and difficulty walking a quarter-mile, plus separate depression-screen items for sleep and low energy. The adapted Fried study uses difficulty lifting or carrying as a weakness proxy and difficulty walking a quarter-mile as its **exhaustion proxy**. The same descriptions map to CDC's 2011–2012 [PFQ061E and PFQ061B](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/PFQ_G.htm), respectively. These are at least two **shared questions** across the proposed instruments; exact response coding, skip handling and participant eligibility must still be reconciled. Any FI–adapted-Fried correlation could be partly mechanical even after the FI's sleep and low-energy items are removed. The published Fried adaptation also uses reported difficulty walking between rooms as a slowness proxy rather than measured gait speed.

The 49-item FI's sleep and low-energy items correspond by wording to the adult depression-screen questions [DPQ030 and DPQ040](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/DPQ_G.htm). DPQ030 combines trouble sleeping **or sleeping too much** and is not a clinical insomnia diagnosis. In the adapted Fried score, exhaustion is a quarter-mile walking proxy, not DPQ040; dropping DPQ040 from the FI is therefore not the same operation as removing the adapted Fried exhaustion proxy. Sleep duration and trouble-sleeping responses from the sleep questionnaire have different definitions and should not be silently substituted for DPQ030.

## What a defensible analysis would require

1. Confirm each of the 49 scoring rules against the source table and relevant cycle-specific CDC variable documentation, including special response values, survey skip patterns and absent variables. The supplement has merged cells and some thresholds require careful interpretation; a text extraction alone is not an executable specification. Explicitly resolve whether the FI source's item construction can be reproduced in both 2011–2012 and 2013–2014.
2. Reproduce the adapted five-domain phenotype from its stated NHANES criteria, including physical-activity calculations and documented missingness. It uses self-reported functional proxies; do not call it the original measured-gait-and-grip Fried score or assume it is the UKB GWAS phenotype. The older-adult analysis and the 49-item source used different sampling and eligibility criteria.
3. On a frozen common sample, compare full FI with versions removing DPQ030 alone and both DPQ030/DPQ040, adjusting the denominator to the number of available retained deficits under a prespecified missingness rule. Separately compare the full adapted Fried score with a modified score excluding its quarter-mile proxy. Because PFQ061B and PFQ061E occur in both, also report a prespecified sensitivity removing these common physical questions from the FI. Label all altered scores as research constructs.
4. Use the appropriate NHANES survey weights, strata and clusters, and account for the domain and combined-cycle design when estimating uncertainty. Report exact denominators, missingness, score distributions, common-item counts and prespecified paired contrasts. Do not interpret changes across scores as proof of causal pathways or as solely due to sleep items.

These source-assessment proposals precede the later [exploratory pilot](phenotype_pilot/RESULTS.md), which uses documented working assumptions and still needs source-level validation. NHANES public data are observational; the comparison does not provide the item-excluded GWAS, an independent genetic replication, or a genetic FI–FFS correlation. See the main [construction protocol](../CONSTRUCTION_PROTOCOL.md) and [phenotype versus genetic comparison](../../phenotype_comparison/PHENOTYPIC_VS_GENETIC.md).

An [initial public-file audit](input_audit/RESULTS.md) confirms the relevant variables are present in two cycles and quantifies a walking-question skip pattern that must be handled explicitly. It does not construct either score or estimate correlations.

An [additional item-mapping audit](item_mapping/RESULTS.md) finds a healthcare-visit category that straddles an FI scoring boundary and weight-loss cases with unobserved intentionality. These require explicit handling; no complete FI or adapted Fried score has been calculated.
