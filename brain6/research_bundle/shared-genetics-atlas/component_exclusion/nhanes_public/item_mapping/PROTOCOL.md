# NHANES FI and adapted-Fried item mapping

Before inspecting the additional files, acquire CDC public NHANES 2011–2012 and 2013–2014 MCQ, BPQ, DIQ, KIQ_U, HUQ, RXQ_RX, BMX, GHB, CBC, PAQ, WHQ and SLQ SAS transport files, supplementing the previously retrieved DEMO, DPQ, PFQ and MGX files. Check HTTP success, transport headers, full hashes and parsing. Inventory variable availability for the published 49-item FI and adapted five-domain Fried definitions using the primary papers and CDC cycle-specific codebooks. Distinguish variables directly available, inferred only by description, multiple-row files and absent or unresolved items. The table is a mapping and readiness audit, not an assertion that every score has been correctly reconstructed.

Flag ambiguous merged FI-table cells and thresholds, sample skip rules, medication non-users, cycle-specific labels and questionnaire values before analysis. The adapted Fried paper uses quarter-mile walking difficulty as an exhaustion proxy; the CDC has a mobility-equipment skip rule. Do not silently count skips as nonfrail, and preserve a sensitivity option. Independently audit all five components, study sample restrictions, combined-cycle survey weights and missingness before reporting the within-person score correlations. No genetic inference, individual-level exports or causal conclusions are permitted by this readiness check.

Follow-up diagnostic, specified before counting: among participants aged 60 or older, count the 2011–2012 HUQ050 category "4 to 9" because it straddles the FI table's 5-visit threshold; separately count 2013–2014 HUQ051 "4 to 5" for context, while documenting the changed question wording. In each cycle, count participants with valid reported current and prior weights, measured BMI >22.5, weight loss ≥5% but <10 pounds, and missing intentionality response. This combination would leave the adapted Fried weight-loss criterion unresolved under its published definition; do not infer whether the loss was intentional. These are unweighted raw file counts and depend on the stated valid-code and BMI filters, not population estimates.
