# Public 2017–March 2020 NHANES: separating exhaustion from physical items

A newly published [Zhang et al. study](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14213580) used the public 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic NHANES release to construct a three-component partial Fried-style score and, separately, a **two-component score of weight loss and recreational inactivity without exhaustion**. Its primary outcome was multimorbidity, not frailty, and its sleep-duration models used frailty scores as covariates. Those results do not estimate an item-excluded sleep–frailty genetic correlation or establish a causal effect. The two-item score is not the full Fried phenotype and should not use Fried clinical thresholds.

We independently checked availability in five [CDC public files](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/search/DataPage.aspx?Cycle=2017-2020), joining by respondent ID. Among **3,422 respondents aged 60 or older**, 3,333 had usable current and prior-year weight values, 3,420 had usable answers to both recreational-activity questions, 2,870 had a usable low-energy answer, and 3,382 had a usable weekday sleep-duration value. These are **unweighted item counts**, not prevalence estimates; no common complete-case count, effect estimate or association test is reported. The data refer to later survey cycles than the atlas's 2011–2014 observational pilot and are not the UK Biobank genetic sample.

The CDC [weight-history codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/P_WHQ.htm) states that the question about intentional weight change is asked only when reported loss is at least **10 pounds**. Of 804 older respondents whose valid reported weights indicate a loss of at least 5%, **55** lost less than 10 pounds and had no usable intentionality response. The remaining 749 had an answer: 344 reported unintentional and 405 intentional loss. The 55 must not automatically be coded as unintentional: any two-component construction needs a prespecified missing-data or sensitivity rule. These counts concern the full age-eligible set, not the 2,998-person analysis population in the published paper; they do not demonstrate how that paper handled the skipped cases.

The public release thus offers a feasible **phenotypic** follow-up with an explicit skip-pattern limitation. Separate same-person full and modified scores, suitable survey weights and design-based uncertainty, careful exposure coding and missingness sensitivities would be needed. The CDC notes that weekday sleep duration is a derived main-sleep-period estimate and may omit naps. No individual genotype data or matched item-excluded GWAS is supplied by these five files.

[Protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [code](audit.py) and [receipt](receipt.json) record source URLs, hashes, row counts and coding. Input XPT files were held in memory; no participant-level data were saved. During validation, the local pandas XPT parser represented SAS zero in DPQ040 as approximately 5.4×10⁻⁷⁹; the script explicitly normalizes finite near-zero values to zero before counting. Without that correction, the energy-item count would be wrong. These checks establish data availability and the described skip pattern, not the validity of any reconstructed frailty instrument.

A subsequent [same-person partial physical-score pilot](physical_sleep_pilot/RESULTS.md) reports the requested score comparison, with explicit missing-intentionality sensitivity and survey-weighted uncertainty. It is observational and does not supply an item-excluded GWAS.
