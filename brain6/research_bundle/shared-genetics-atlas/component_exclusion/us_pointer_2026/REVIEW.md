# U.S. POINTER: sleep-component exclusion as phenotypic sensitivity

[U.S. POINTER frailty analysis (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glag094) compared two randomized multidomain lifestyle interventions among 2,111 adults aged 60–79 at elevated risk of cognitive decline. The interventions differed in structure, intensity and accountability; both involved lifestyle changes. The longitudinal modified frailty index (mFI) had 31 components, including four sleep components. The paper repeated the comparison after omitting those four components. It reports a structured-minus-self-guided mean relative difference in mFI of −0.014 with sleep components included and −0.015 after their removal. These are intervention-group differences in an FI outcome, not sleep–frailty genetic correlations or effects of an isolated sleep intervention. The authors state there was no usual-care control.

The source reports identical limits, −0.020 and −0.020, for the confidence interval around the sleep-excluded estimate. We have not resolved that apparent reporting inconsistency, so we do not infer or silently correct the interval. Its description of a baseline correlation between “the two FIs” does not clearly establish in the passage whether that statistic compares the full and sleep-excluded longitudinal scores, so we do not use it as such.

This trial establishes additional prior *phenotypic* precedent for deleting sleep components from a frailty outcome; the [Zhu observational cohort](../zhu_observational/REVIEW.md) also removed the insomnia item. Neither provides the matched full and sleep-excluded FI GWAS summary statistics required for the atlas's genetic comparison. The U.S. POINTER mFI and population also differ from the existing UKB/TwinGene FI and the five-component FFS, so trial findings do not validate the atlas's correlations or provide a sleep-excluded FFS. A future matched genetic analysis must specify the exact components, participants, covariates, genotype QC and trait scaling, then compare the paired estimates with appropriate uncertainty.
