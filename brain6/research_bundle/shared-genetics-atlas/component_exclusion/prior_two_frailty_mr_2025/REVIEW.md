# Prior FI–FFS and sleep MR: scope and multiplicity

[Che et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812435/), “Bidirectional causal associations between frailty measures and sleep disturbances,” already compared FI and Fried Frailty Score (FFS) against insomnia, short sleep, long sleep and sleep apnea with bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization. They used the Atkins UK Biobank–TwinGene FI meta-analysis (175,226 people), Ye UK Biobank FFS (386,565), and separate sleep GWAS. This is directly relevant prior work: comparing **both FI and FFS with multiple sleep traits using genetic summary statistics** is not itself a novel project claim. The study does not provide HFRS comparisons, a matched FI-minus-sleep or FFS-minus-exhaustion GWAS, the present local-correlation QC audit, or independent replication of the present sleep–ADHD candidate loci.

For insomnia → FFS, the paper reports an inverse-variance-weighted estimate β=0.14 (95% CI 0.02–0.25), nominal p=0.021. Its Table 1 reports a MR-PRESSO global p=0.016 and Cochran Q p=0.013 for that direction, so the study itself reports diagnostic evidence consistent with heterogeneity or pleiotropy. These are not proof of any particular mechanism, and the author-reported estimate is not our reanalysis. The paper states it applied no multiple-testing adjustment because variables were not independent. Dependence does not by itself remove multiplicity: as a **post-hoc illustrative**, conservative comparison across the 16 directional combinations in its table (2 frailty measures × 4 sleep phenotypes × 2 directions), 0.021×16=0.336. This is not the authors' stated correction, a preregistered family, or a reanalysis of their estimates. It does not prove a null effect; the design and chosen family affect the interpretation.

The paper uses the standard broad FI and five-component FFS, so insomnia/tiredness and exhaustion item overlap remain relevant. It reports no sample overlap with frailty outcomes for sleep measures except duration; the duration study contains UK Biobank participants. This is the authors' account, not an independent participant-level verification. Variant exclusion based on associations with an outcome and a nonsignificant pleiotropy test cannot establish MR instrument validity; directional causal claims need the full assumptions and sensitivity evidence. Reusing the same FI and FFS GWAS also means that this study is not independent replication of this atlas's corresponding frailty inputs.

The source paper’s abstract and Methods describe outcomes and samples; Results and Table 1 give the estimate and diagnostics, and Methods state the multiple-testing choice. The article is used as prior-study context, not as a new genetic correlation, clinical result, or substitute for item-excluded data.
