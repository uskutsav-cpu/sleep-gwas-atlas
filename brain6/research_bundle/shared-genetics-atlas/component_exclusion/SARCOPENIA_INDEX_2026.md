# Public sarcopenia-index GWAS: phenotype and suitability

[Mu et al. (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13293-026-00973-y) report GWAS for the sarcopenia index in UK Biobank, including sex-stratified analyses, and list accessions GCST90832978–GCST90832980. The [GCST90832978 public directory](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90832001-GCST90833000/GCST90832978/) displayed a full 750 MB TSV and metadata without login at the time of this check. Download integrity, the individual accession's sex coding, alleles and per-variant sample size were not assessed here.

Despite its name, the index is a **serum creatinine-to-cystatin-C ratio**, not a measured gait-and-grip or a Fried frailty phenotype, and its inputs contain no explicit sleep or exhaustion item. The authors caution that it is affected by diet, medication and biomarker-related genetic effects, and report a positive genetic correlation with kidney disease. These influences mean that a sleep correlation with this biomarker would not isolate physical frailty or muscle biology. It is therefore a poor substitute for the requested matched exhaustion-deleted frailty GWAS, and was not fitted or used for training. The existing [physical-function composite](schoeler_physical_function/RESULTS.md) is more directly tied to measured physical function, although it too is not an item-deleted Fried score. A biomarker-specific analysis could be considered only with an explicit kidney-function sensitivity and appropriate source and sample-overlap checks.
