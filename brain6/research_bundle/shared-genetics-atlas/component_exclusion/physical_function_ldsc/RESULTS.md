# Registry insomnia and physical-function genetic correlations

A later [common-marker comparison of two insomnia sources](insomnia_source_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) quantifies source sensitivity for walking pace and grip using paired jackknife contrasts; its four-input support and correction family differ from this initial pilot.

Two exploratory LDSC analyses pair the previously archived FinnGen R13 registry-insomnia statistics with public Neale Lab UK Biobank physical-function statistics. These outcomes do not themselves contain sleep or exhaustion items, but neither is a frailty score with an item removed.

| Physical outcome | r_g (SE) | Logged p | Bonferroni p for these two tests |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Self-reported usual walking pace | −0.0631 (0.0319) | 0.0480 | 0.0960 |
| Left hand grip strength, rank normalized | −0.0896 (0.0352) | 0.0110 | 0.0220 |

P-values and corrections use the rounded values printed by LDSC. The grip result meets the two-test threshold; walking pace does not. This small, exploratory family was specified before inspecting these runs and is not a correction for the broader atlas or all other studies reviewed. These correlations are not causal estimates, local signals, independent replication of sleep–frailty loci, or proof of a sleep-independent frailty mechanism. Negative estimates are consistent with lower physical function accompanying insomnia liability, assuming the source outcome coding and allele conventions; the exact source processing and sign coding have not been independently reconstructed from raw phenotypes.

Both physical sources contain 1,096,648 markers, and each shares 1,053,614 rsIDs with the archived insomnia input; all shared allele pairs matched directly or by strand and none was palindromic. Both LDSC runs retained 1,053,614 valid-allele SNPs. The source files were read through gzip completion and SHA-256 hashed; no publisher checksum was available in the selected download listing. Their constant N fields are 358,974 for walking and 359,704 for grip; the insomnia input uses 497,916, a study-level N rather than independently verified per-variant effective N for a binary phenotype. These N values do not establish participant independence. The general-European LD reference may not adequately capture Finnish LD, and source cohort definitions, participant overlap and relatedness require verification. The LDSC log prints a compression runtime warning while reading the insomnia file; that file was independently read through gzip completion, but a successful read does not validate the GWAS model.

The [Neale Lab LDSC downloads](https://nealelab.github.io/UKBB_ldsc/downloads.html) identify UKB field 924 as usual walking pace and field 46_irnt as left grip strength; [UKB's field description](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=924) notes that people unable to walk were not asked the walking-pace question. The [grip summary](https://nealelab.github.io/UKBB_ldsc/h2_summary_46_irnt.html) identifies the rank-normalized phenotype and European-ancestry sample. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [input audit](input_receipt.json), [audit code](audit_inputs.py), [run script](run.sh), and complete [walking](walking.log) and [grip](grip.log) logs retain methods and warnings. Inputs remain on the SSD. Source-cohort and phenotype differences preclude treating the difference between these estimates or the earlier [published walking-pace estimate](../walking_pace_published/RESULTS.md) as a tested contrast.
