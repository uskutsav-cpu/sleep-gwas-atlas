# Five-outcome comparison of insomnia sources

Written before preparing the additional inputs and before inspecting these new LDSC results. The earlier physical-function comparison uses the intersection of questionnaire insomnia, registry insomnia, walking pace and grip strength. Its questionnaire file is an ordered subset of the previously audited, allele-aligned FI, FFS and HFRS inputs. Verify all three frailty inputs match their frozen hashes and ordered questionnaire SNP/allele keys, then extract the exact four-source common-marker subset without changing their alleles or signed statistics. Abort on any mismatch, duplicate, missing marker or input hash change. Preserve all data.

Run the six new correlations of FI, FFS and HFRS with questionnaire and registry insomnia on exactly that same support, using the existing unconstrained LDSC setup and 200 delete blocks. Reuse the four previously completed walking/grip runs and deletion outputs. For each of the five outcomes calculate the questionnaire-minus-registry r_g contrast with the paired block jackknife; verify the same outcome's heritability deletion vector is identical across the insomnia sources, the same insomnia heritability deletion vector is identical across all outcomes, and each reconstructed jackknife SE matches its printed LDSC SE. Use a post-hoc Bonferroni family of five contrasts and a separate family of ten individual correlations; do not retroactively claim a project-wide correction. Confidence intervals are unadjusted, and differences use logged rounded point estimates. Record any errors or warnings.

The analysis addresses source sensitivity, not a within-person comparison or matched item exclusion. Cohort, phenotype, sample overlap, LD reference, sample-size semantics and ascertainment change together. HFRS has previously documented elevated LDSC intercept and uncertain effective N, while FI and FFS include sleep and exhaustion items. Reference and Finnish LD mismatch remain possible. A paired block jackknife captures estimated genomic-block covariance but does not eliminate upstream sample overlap or systematic bias. Neither a statistically different r_g nor its absence isolates a causal mechanism or one source of variation.
