# Canadian ordinal frailty GWAS: component and replication relevance

Reviewed 27 October 2026 from [Borhan et al., npj Aging, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00363-z). This is a literature review, not a reanalysis of the study or a claim of independent replication of a Brain6 locus.

The authors analyzed 23,105 European-ancestry Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging (CLSA) participants using a three-category frailty phenotype: zero, one or two, and at least three of five Fried criteria. Their ordinal model included sex, genetic principal components, batch and number of chronic conditions, and did not include age. The different score, cohort, adjustment and outcome model preclude treating its estimates as directly interchangeable with the 0–5 Ye FFS or FI analyses in this atlas. The article does not establish participant independence from every Brain6 source.

The authors reported rs147311617, effect allele C, minor allele frequency 0.02, p=4.98×10⁻⁸ for the ordinal phenotype. This is a published frailty finding, close to the conventional genome-wide threshold, and is not a sleep–frailty replication. The article title and results locate it at 12q22, while the abstract says 12p22; resolve position against a reference before any coordinate-based lookup. The authors describe PLXNC1 and RP11-1105G2.4 as nearby and report SOCS2 eQTL associations for other highly linked variants; none establishes the causal gene or a sleep mechanism. Their tissue-enrichment analysis did not yield a statistically significant tissue.

The study also performed separate GWAS for the five individual components, reporting genome-wide findings for grip strength and unintentional weight loss but none for exhaustion, low physical activity or slow walking speed. This is useful component-specific context, not a GWAS of the four-component composite after exhaustion is removed. The composite still includes self-reported exhaustion. Separate component GWAS cannot recover the genetic effects or uncertainty of an item-excluded composite without joint data and an appropriate model.

The article directs researchers to CLSA data access subject to eligibility; we did not identify a freely downloadable full summary-statistics file in its data-availability statement. The open article alone therefore does not provide the matched item-excluded GWAS or an allele-matched two-trait replication. Independent validation would require the relevant summary statistics, confirmed cohort independence, comparable definitions, variant harmonization, and a prespecified multiplicity correction.

On 28 October 2026 the study was found on the CLSA website's [page labeled “GWAS Summary Data Archives”](https://www.clsa-elcv.ca/our-publication-type/gwas-summary-data/). The visible entry is a **publication citation** whose “Learn More” link leads to PubMed; it does not itself provide a summary-statistics download. The label therefore does not establish that the component GWAS files are publicly downloadable. A targeted web search did not locate them. An initial GWAS Catalog v1 query returned HTTP 410; a subsequent [supported v2 publication query](../CATALOG_V2_AUDIT.md) returned zero records, with a positive control. This does not rule out data elsewhere or later catalog additions. The original article's own data statement refers to access-eligible researchers, without identifying a public per-variant download. The individual grip and weight-loss results remain potentially useful independent-cohort physical-component comparisons if their complete signed statistics and metadata can be located, but cannot reconstruct a four-component score.

A [post-selected source comparison](../../replication/borhan_2026_lookup/RESULTS.md) found weak marginal associations at the lead in UKB Fried score and Finnish registry insomnia. Its multiple documented alleles and an unverified Canadian comparison allele prevent a formal aligned replication claim.
