# Separating sleep items from frailty: analysis-ready protocol

**Current status: not estimated.** No authorized person-level item dataset or validated same-sample component-excluded GWAS was available. The project's component-exclusion result table is empty, so neither attenuation nor persistence of the genetic relationship has been demonstrated. The following constructs are a reproducible plan, not observed results or a validated replacement for the published instruments.

The [Atkins et al. FI GWAS](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/acel.13459) used 49 UKB self-reported items in a complete-case sample (164,610), quantile-normalized the FI for GWAS, and also used TwinGene, whose item inventory differs. The 49-item definition includes insomnia/sleeplessness (UKB [field 1200](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=1200)) and fatigue/tiredness (field [2080](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=2080)); see the source instrument [Williams et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6417451/) and published detailed coding in [Table S4](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/medrxiv/early/2023/09/13/2023.09.13.23295486/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true). Original FI sleep codes are 0, 0.5, 1 and fatigue codes 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1; verify source coding, missingness and instance independently before use. An observational FI excluding insomnia was already studied by [Zhu et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-022-02557-0); this does not provide a component-excluded GWAS.

The [Zhu study review](zhu_observational/REVIEW.md) records its observed association after item removal and distinguishes it from the still-unavailable genetic comparison.

A [worked identifiability example](identifiability/NOTE.md) shows why the overall sleep–frailty genetic correlation cannot determine the correlation after removing exhaustion, even under simplified assumptions.

The five-component Fried phenotype includes exhaustion, weakness, slow walking, weight loss and low activity. A [Chen et al. supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11357-025-01734-2/MediaObjects/11357_2025_1734_MOESM1_ESM.docx) defines exhaustion from field 2080 as a binary high-frequency tiredness response and details the remaining components. `published_component_crosswalk.tsv` reproduces a prior review of those definitions. This corroborates potential overlap with FI fatigue, but it does **not** establish that the exact FFS GWAS or the separately distributed component summary files used the same operational coding. A [whole-file exhaustion audit](zenodo_full_exhaustion_audit/RESULTS.md) supports GRCh37 coordinates for the matched HapMap rows and verifies its source checksum, but unmatched variants, other component archives, lineage, ancestry, QC and sample model remain unverified. Do not use these files for component-level genetic claims until resolved.

The published component crosswalk uses UK Biobank [field 2306](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=2306), which asks whether weight has changed compared with one year earlier. That question does not establish whether loss was intentional or quantify the amount. A GWAS using that field alone must be described as self-reported weight loss, not as verified unintentional loss meeting the original Fried threshold. This matters when interpreting physical-frailty components and comparing cohorts; the crosswalk does not establish that the released component file actually used this coding.

A [source availability review](SOURCE_AVAILABILITY.md) records the Zenodo metadata gap and the alternative EHR-based FI study.

A [published walking-pace comparison](walking_pace_published/RESULTS.md) provides prior genetic-correlation evidence for a sleep-free physical-function measure versus insomnia and ADHD, with and without BMI adjustment. It is not an exhaustion-excluded composite and does not replace the same-person item-deletion analysis below.

An [exploratory registry-insomnia LDSC comparison](physical_function_ldsc/RESULTS.md) examines walking pace and left grip strength separately, with a two-test correction and source limitations. Neither physical measure is an item-excluded FI or FFS.

## Same-person phenotype construction

On a fixed complete-case UKB sample, with validated 49 deficit scores D_j in [0,1], sleep score S and fatigue score T among them, and five validated binary Fried components E, W, L, A, G:

- FI49 = (sum D_j)/49.
- FI minus insomnia = (sum D_j − S)/48.
- FI minus insomnia and fatigue = (sum D_j − S − T)/47.
- FFS5 = E + W + L + A + G, where E is exhaustion.
- FFS minus exhaustion = W + L + A + G, a 0–4 *modified* score, not the original validated 0–5 Fried phenotype.

The denominator changes deliberately; simply subtracting a component divided by 49 produces a different scale. Keep the exact same people, visit, covariates, genetic QC, ancestry and sample filters for all scores, and specify eligibility and missingness before comparisons. FI49 complete-case construction follows Atkins, but requiring complete FFS components as well is an additional restriction; report its sample impact. Recompute each phenotype's rank-based inverse-normal transformation separately within the same analysis sample, following the project's selected GWAS protocol. Its coefficients and r_g are on transformed phenotype scales; do **not** subtract these component GWAS betas or correlations to simulate exclusion. A narrow exception exists for untransformed additive scores fitted to identical per-SNP samples with the same linear estimator: raw coefficients can then be subtracted, with sampling covariance required for SEs. Correlated items and common covariates do not themselves break that identity. Separate normalization, incompatible samples/models and binary thresholding remove the guarantee. The [summary-reconstruction feasibility check](identifiability/LINEAR_ROUTE.md) derives the conditions and supplies computational checks and an exact binary counterexample. Run independent complete GWAS for the new phenotypes and repeat LDSC with matching allele, build, LD reference, per-SNP N and overlap metadata.

For any binary frailty comparison, prespecify the threshold separately from the item removal and report the complete paired classification table. If the same cutoff of three is used for FFS5 and FFS4 in the same complete-case participants, every FFS4 case is necessarily an FFS5 case; participants switch from case to noncase precisely when they have exhaustion and exactly two other components. Consequently, a lower case count is partly a mathematical consequence of retaining the cutoff, not evidence that the removed item caused frailty. As observational precedent, [Flores-Flores et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13536796/) used at least three of four criteria after excluding exhaustion and at least three of five for the full phenotype; they reported 56 versus 86 cases among 1,029 participants. That study addressed depression and multimorbidity, not genetics or sleep, and its data statement specifies access on request. The case-count change cannot determine the genetic correlation after exclusion. Report case fractions and power for each binary definition, and treat any alternative cutoff as a separate, prespecified sensitivity rather than an equivalent phenotype.

`construct_scores.py` implements the raw arithmetic for a user-supplied, pre-coded item table and explicit JSON column mapping, with fixed complete-case filtering and strict value validation. It does not reproduce the cohort's raw response coding, generate inverse-normal phenotypes, run GWAS, or establish eligibility of any downloaded component GWAS. Test it with `python3 test_construct_scores.py`. The script requires a caller-selected new output path; avoid placing participant-level data in this delivery bundle. The caller is responsible for lawful approved access and for implementing the actual item coding.

## Estimands and analysis

1. Compare within-person Pearson and Spearman FI49 vs modified FI and FFS5 vs FFS4, with N and paired bootstrap intervals; also compare raw sleep association with all scores adjusting prespecified covariates. Insomnia and the FI sleep item share a measurement in the UKB analysis, so label this as partly mechanical.
2. Compare insomnia–FI49, insomnia–FI48 and insomnia–FI47 genetic correlations and insomnia–FFS5 versus insomnia–FFS4 using matched-support LDSC and paired block-jackknife covariance, not independent-SE subtraction. Define the correction family over all sleep phenotypes and planned contrasts before inspecting associations. Distinguish exact same-source and independent-sleep-cohort analyses.
3. For physical-component follow-up, separately analyze validated weakness, slow walking, weight loss and low activity GWAS; assess heterogeneity, power and inter-component effects. A score without exhaustion is a useful sensitivity, not a purified physical-frailty construct; other components can also be influenced by sleep. Insomnia-excluded FI still contains nonphysical symptoms and diagnoses.
4. Re-run regional analyses with dense signed statistics, ancestry-appropriate LD and frozen quality gates; replication requires independently ascertained sleep and frailty outcomes. Treat local non-estimability as unknown rather than zero.

A genetic association after exclusion would support persistence under that phenotype definition, not prove a direct biological mechanism; attenuation is compatible with item overlap and other changes in measurement or power. Predefine null controls and report all failed analyses.

[Mak et al. 2023](mak_category_fi/RESULTS.md) also performed a GWAS for an FI excluding eight mental-wellbeing items, including insomnia and fatigue. Its exact per-SNP statistics were not identified in the reviewed release; removing the whole category differs from the specific paired score constructions proposed above.
