# Prior insomnia–frailty and psychiatric MR with overlap sensitivity

[Zhou et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjo.2024.835), whose published title begins “Casual associations,” used bidirectional Mendelian randomization to investigate frailty and nine psychiatric or related traits including insomnia. The primary frailty measure was the Atkins FI meta-analysis; the Ye Fried phenotype was used in additional analyses. The same FI and FFS sources are used in parts of this atlas, so this is important prior work, not independent replication of our own shared loci. ADHD was not one of the nine selected traits; the study does not evaluate the current sleep–ADHD–frailty multiscale framework or HFRS.

The paper reported FI → insomnia IVW β=0.50, 95% CI 0.25–0.75, p=1.1×10⁻⁴ and insomnia → FI β=0.160, 95% CI 0.141–0.179, p=3.2×10⁻⁶¹. Both are below the authors' 0.0028 threshold for 18 directional primary comparisons; they are estimates of directional models, not genetic correlations. The study reports that results involving insomnia remained robust under MR-APSS, a model intended to address correlated pleiotropy and potential sample overlap. That is an author-reported sensitivity, not a guarantee that overlap, selection bias or all pleiotropy was removed; its detailed assumptions and actual cohort intersections remain relevant.

The paper explicitly notes that mental-health-related items are part of FI and that it could not conduct an item-excluded sensitivity analysis using the available summary statistics. A small number of questionnaire items alone does not quantify their effect on the observed genetic association. The Fried score also contains exhaustion. Neither the MR-APSS sensitivity nor use of a second frailty phenotype constitutes an FI-minus-insomnia or FFS-minus-exhaustion GWAS. The findings limit claims of novelty for multivariate psychiatric–frailty comparison or overlap-aware MR, while a matched component deletion, a harmonized HFRS comparison and independent locus replication remain testable unmet questions.

The quoted estimates, reported correction and MR-APSS description are from the paper’s Methods, Results and Table 2; the FI item limitation is in its Discussion. This is a source comparison, not an independent rerun, direct evidence of mediation or clinical advice.
