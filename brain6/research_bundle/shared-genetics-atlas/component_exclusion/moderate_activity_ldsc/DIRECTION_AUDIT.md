# Direction audit for physical activity GWAS

The [Neale Lab's public both-sexes phenotype annotation](https://broad-ukb-sumstats-us-east-1.s3.amazonaws.com/round2/annotations/phenotypes.both_sexes.v2.tsv.bgz) identifies phenotype 884, N=343,943, as an ordinal PHESANT variable. Its field-specific transformation states that -1 and -3 are missing and records the eight category levels in order **0|1|2|3|4|5|6|7**. [UK Biobank's field description](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=884) confirms that these are days per week of moderate activity, excluding walking. Thus increasing activity phenotype category means **more reported days**, not less activity.

The [Neale Lab methods discussion](https://nealelab.github.io/UKBB_ldsc/confidence.html) says these ordinal phenotypes were analyzed using linear regression. Its [published pipeline](https://github.com/Nealelab/UK_Biobank_GWAS/blob/68b6d4c40e3961655037ff4822fb7917904b3f58/0.1/22.run_regressions.py) fits phenotype against genotype dosage, and the [LDSC export code](https://github.com/Nealelab/UK_Biobank_GWAS/blob/68b6d4c40e3961655037ff4822fb7917904b3f58/0.1/27.export_ldsc_sumstats.py) assigns A1=alternate allele, A2=reference allele and Z=the regression t-statistic. The [PHESANT integer processing code](https://github.com/MRCIEU/PHESANT/blob/dfb82c124576312512bfad79c001129479db38c3/WAS/testInteger.r) describes using original integer levels as ordered categories when fewer than 20 distinct values occur. Together, this supports the interpretation of a positive activity effect as an association with a higher ordered category, or more days, under the published processing and export conventions. It is a source-code and metadata verification, not an independent reconstruction of participant-level phenotypes or rerun of the original GWAS; undocumented source changes would not be detected.

The LDSC input was transferred without a Z-sign reversal. The [SNP audit](audit_receipt.json) confirms compatible non-palindromic allele pairs with both insomnia inputs. Conditional on those insomnia inputs representing increasing insomnia liability as documented in the original source, the positive questionnaire correlation estimate corresponds to a tendency for genetic effects toward more activity days to align with insomnia liability. This is a weak, imprecise estimate (r_g=0.0625, p=0.0785), not evidence that activity increases insomnia or vice versa. The registry estimate is close to zero and imprecise. The two estimates cannot be declared different without paired uncertainty, and they do not test a sleep-excluded frailty score.
