# Observational evidence after removing the insomnia item

[Zhu et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9585775/) analyzed 23,847 China Kadoorie Biobank participants aged 30–79 who completed both baseline and a second survey. The researchers removed the insomnia-symptom item from their original 28-deficit index, constructing a 27-item FI at both visits. This is prior evidence for the *phenotypic* sleep–frailty question, and it shows that an observational association can be studied without directly including the insomnia item in the outcome.

Among 9,522 participants prefrail at baseline, the authors report an adjusted prevalence ratio of **1.53 (95% CI 1.23–1.89)** for becoming frail at the second survey among those with baseline insomnia disorder versus those without it. Median follow-up was eight years. The FI and insomnia definitions, population and outcome differ from the project's UK Biobank/TwinGene genetic analyses. This is an association in a baseline-prefrail stratum, not an estimate for everyone in the cohort, a genetic correlation, a locus replication, or proof that insomnia causes frailty.

The authors adjusted for age, sex, area, education, smoking and alcohol, with additional disease/medication sensitivity analyses. Only surviving participants could attend the second survey, baseline frail participants were excluded, and residual confounding, selection and measurement error remain possible. Unlike the baseline exposure analysis, their 'maintained healthy sleep' exposure uses sleep data at both baseline and follow-up; its association should not be read as a prospective effect estimated solely from baseline sleep. Removing an insomnia item also does not by itself prove the remaining index is free from fatigue, broader health overlap or nonphysical deficits; we have not independently audited all 27 item codings here.

This source does **not** provide the matched FI-minus-insomnia genetic association statistics needed for the project. The authors direct readers to the [China Kadoorie Biobank access policy](https://www.ckbiobank.org/) for underlying data rather than reporting a freely downloadable component-excluded GWAS. A same-person GWAS contrast and independent genetic replication remain open; the observed prevalence ratio cannot be compared numerically to LDSC genetic correlation.
