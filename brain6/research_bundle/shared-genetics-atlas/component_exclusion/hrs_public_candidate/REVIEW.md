# Health and Retirement Study: candidate for a sleep–physical frailty analysis

The [Health and Retirement Study (HRS) public survey data](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/public-survey-data) are a promising alternative to the existing NHANES observational pilot. Download is free but requires registration, login and agreement to the [conditions of use](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products/conditions-of-use); this is public-release data, not an anonymous open download. No account was created and no HRS data were downloaded or analyzed in this assessment.

A published [HRS frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5233918/) reports grip strength and gait assessments in an enhanced interview subsample beginning in 2006. Another [HRS sleep–frailty study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13032618/) used sleep-disturbance and frailty measures across waves from 2004 through 2020. Together these establish a credible starting point, not that every variable is available for every participant or in a single same-wave file. Confirm exact variables, eligibility, physical-measurement subsampling, timing, and missingness using the cycle-specific HRS codebooks after access. HRS participants were also used in other published frailty research; assess overlap before any replication claim.

A feasible observational analysis would define a common participant and wave sample, construct a physical score from weakness, slowness, physical activity and weight loss, and compare it with the corresponding full score including exhaustion. Treat the modified four-component score as an unvalidated research sensitivity; prespecify cutoffs and how missing values affect sample and denominator. Test associations with separately measured sleep questions, check whether the sleep questions appear anywhere in the outcome definition, and use the appropriate survey and physical-measure weights and design variables. This is a proposal, not an estimated result, and observational associations cannot establish causation. If the necessary weight-loss or activity variables cannot be confirmed, do not label a partial score as a full four-component score.

[HRS survey data access](https://hrsdata.isr.umich.edu/data-products) differs from access to the genetic linkage files and genotypes. The HRS portal says genetic cross-reference files require a supplemental agreement, with genetic data held in dbGaP or NIAGADS. Public survey registration alone does not authorize or supply an item-excluded GWAS. Therefore HRS could extend the **observational** sleep–frailty analysis after registration, but it does not immediately resolve the missing publicly downloadable item-excluded genetic summary statistics.
