# A deposited frailty–sleep cross-trait analysis: provenance and interpretation

A 2025 [Zenodo-deposited manuscript](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15449098) by Tian and colleagues describes a cross-trait analysis of the Atkins frailty index and FinnGen R11 sleep disorders, depression and anxiety. It claims an FI–sleep-disorder LDSC correlation of **0.5019 (SE 0.0289)** and an HDL correlation of **0.5692 (SE 0.0291)**. Its reported endpoint is `finngen_R11_SLEEP`, not an independently confirmed insomnia-specific definition. The manuscript also reports PLACO, colocalization and gene analyses. If verified, this would be an additional prior analysis relevant to the atlas's novelty discussion, not independent validation of its results.

**Publication and source provenance remain unresolved.** Zenodo labels the deposit a journal article, and the PDF labels itself *Machine Learning*, volume 26, issue 2, 2025. However, the [publisher's volume-26 listing](https://link.springer.com/journal/10994/volumes-and-issues/26-2) is from 1997, and its [2025 listing](https://link.springer.com/journal/10994/volumes-and-issues/114-1) is volume 114. A Crossref exact-title check found no exact match among the first ten returned records; that limited search does not prove the work was not published elsewhere. A university-hosted copy has the same title and study identifiers but different file bytes. These discrepancies require independent bibliographic verification; they do not by themselves establish the manuscript's origin, intent or the correctness of its numerical results. Treat the document as an **unverified deposited manuscript**, not established peer-reviewed evidence.

The reported FI input, `GCST90020053`, is the same UK Biobank/TwinGene frailty-index meta-analysis considered in this atlas and includes sleep and fatigue items. The manuscript's sleep-disorder input is FinnGen R11, whereas the atlas's registry-insomnia input is R13 and has a different endpoint. Its proposed genetic correlation, PLACO and colocalization findings therefore cannot be counted as an independent replication or a sleep/exhaustion-excluded frailty result. It calls an LDSC-intercept assessment confirmation of no population overlap; the intercept alone cannot establish zero participant or family overlap. Its brief description also refers to applying sample-level call-rate QC despite using GWAS summary data; which upstream checks were actually performed is not established by that statement. Its H4 posterior thresholds are conditional on the chosen model, region and priors and do not prove shared causal variants or mediation.

The document points to supplementary tables, but the examined Zenodo record contains a single PDF and we did not obtain the named source tables or original analysis code. This prevents an independent check of the lead-level analyses, calibration, sample overlap and model details. The source should be checked when assessing prior claims, but should not be used to upgrade the atlas's PLACO results, assign a causal gene, or resolve LAVA quality-control failures.
