# Genes and pathways connecting sleep and ADHD: evidence and hypotheses

This review links published biology to the existing Brain6 results. It does not establish that a variant regulates a named gene, that a pathway mediates either phenotype, or that a predicted molecular change is measured. Brain6's local-correlation family failed its prespecified QC gate. The selected variants were not independently replicated for both traits, and the AlphaGenome tissue scores are predictions, not empirical molecular assays.

A subsequent [dense GTEx–GWAS comparison](../variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/RESULTS.md) of the two chromosome 3 genes in adrenal and frontal cortex found low shared-signal support under the specified single-signal model, despite the exact-variant adrenal associations. That limits the gene-mediation hypothesis but does not rule out secondary signals.

## Interpreting the published chromosome 3 colocalization

[Zu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13493937/) reported rs62264764 at the *LSAMP*:RP11-384F7.2 locus and a trait–trait coloc PP4 of approximately 0.91. Their table describes PP4 as a probability for a particular SNP, but the standard single-signal coloc PP4 is the posterior for a **shared causal signal across the analyzed region**, conditional on the model and priors; it does not by itself give the posterior that the lead SNP is causal. Brain6's chr3 PP4 is also prior-sensitive (0.921 at p12=10⁻⁵ and 0.537 at 10⁻⁶). Neither analysis establishes that LSAMP or RP11-384F7.2 is the effector gene. For the underlying interpretation, see the [original coloc method](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004383).

The Brain6 [winner-removal sensitivity](../alphagenome_pip_weighted/RESULTS.md) further shows that the chromosome 3 weighted predicted-accessibility summary drops below the remaining variants' unweighted mean if rs62264764 is removed. This focuses experimental follow-up on the specific prediction; it does not demonstrate a broad pathway signal.

## How to turn this into a useful ML project

Represent each *variant, gene, trait, tissue and pathway* as a different node type, with typed edges for GWAS association, physical overlap, credible-set membership, measured eQTL, published experimental interaction and model prediction. Store genome build, alleles, phenotype, source, cohort overlap, experiment, statistical uncertainty, direction, and edge provenance. A nearest-gene edge is not interchangeable with an eQTL or experimentally established regulatory edge. Explicitly mark the AlphaGenome outputs as research-only annotations under the applicable terms; the present work does not use them to train a model.

First use transparent rules to retrieve evidence paths and compare them with simple baselines, including GWAS p-value and fine-mapping PIP. If suitably licensed independent experimental labels become available, train and evaluate an embedding on permitted features only. Hold out entire LD blocks and source studies (and related individuals for phenotype prediction); test against an independent functional or molecular dataset, include matched negatives, evaluate top-k retrieval and calibration with confidence intervals, and compare against each simple baseline. This avoids claiming that an embedding has learned causal biology merely because linked variants or repeated cohorts appear in both training and test sets. A prediction still requires direct biological testing.

A later [dense chromosome 5 RP11-6N13.1 expression–GWAS follow-up](../variant_context/chr5_dense_qtl/RESULTS.md) found high testis model-based H4 for each GWAS under tested priors, but no frontal-cortex rows were available. This adds tissue-specific molecular context; separate pairwise fits do not establish brain mediation or a shared causal variant across all three traits.

A [three-trait chromosome 3 sensitivity](../variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/hyprcoloc_pilot/RESULTS.md) did not return a cluster including adrenal LSAMP expression under the settings tested; this is model-dependent and does not exclude a secondary signal or a brain-specific mechanism.

A [direct rs62264764 MVP and reference-LD follow-up](../replication/rs62264764_mvp/RESULTS.md) finds same-direction MVP insomnia association and r²=1.0 with nearby rs62264767 in 503 EUR reference individuals. Their predicted frontal-DNase changes differ, but this does not establish which variant or gene is causal.

A separate [aging-brain single-nucleus xQTL lookup](../variant_context/rs62264764_brain_xqtl/RESULTS.md) reports associations for neighboring LINC03051 in oligodendrocytes and excitatory neurons. These are not LSAMP expression associations, measured accessibility effects, or disease–QTL colocalizations.

A [published LaDDR RNA-phenotype review](../variant_context/linc03051_laddr_2026/RESULTS.md) adds prior GTEx brain RNA–insomnia xTWAS and model-dependent colocalization for LINC03051. The lead rs1456193 is linked to rs62264764 in the small reference; shared UKB/GTEx sources and distinct latent RNA phenotypes preclude independent two-trait or causal-gene claims.

A [prior LINC03051–LSAMP experimental review](../variant_context/linc03051_lsamp_function/REVIEW.md) describes an RNA-stability hypothesis from lung cancer cells. The transcript-isoform mapping, direct interaction and relevance to neurons remain unverified; this is not evidence that rs62264764 mediates sleep or ADHD through either gene.

An [aging-brain xQTL follow-up for rs4916723](../variant_context/chr5_brain_xqtl/RESULTS.md) adds cell-specific expression associations for TMEM161B-AS1 and LINC02060 at the ADHD-prioritized chromosome 5 region. This is a different region from the high shared insomnia–ADHD coloc, and single-variant QTLs do not establish an effector gene or shared disease mechanism.

The [dense oligodendrocyte LINC03051–GWAS pilot](../variant_context/rs62264764_brain_xqtl/dense_coloc/RESULTS.md) favors different dominant expression and disease signals in a one-signal model; secondary sharing and tissue-specific mechanisms remain unresolved.

[NIAGADS LSAMP brain-cell credible sets](../variant_context/rs62264764_brain_xqtl/lsamp_finemapping/RESULTS.md) provide additional molecular context, while selected single-signal LSAMP expression–GWAS models favor distinct dominant signals. They do not identify a shared causal gene or exclude secondary associations.

A [developmental chromatin-accessibility lookup](../variant_context/rs62264764_developmental_caqtl/RESULTS.md) finds a peak overlapping the rs62264764 prediction window but no exact candidate rows in the selected caQTL files. Overlap is not a measured variant effect or proof of the predicted mechanism.

A [UKB hospital-frailty screen of the 425 Brain6 candidates](../variant_context/brain6_ukb_hfrs/RESULTS.md) gives the strongest candidate associations in the chromosome 7 region, including rs1476535, while rs62264764 does not meet the exploratory 850-slot benchmark. These same-cohort marginal associations do not validate a shared causal variant, FOXP2 or LSAMP as an effector, or AlphaGenome predictions.
