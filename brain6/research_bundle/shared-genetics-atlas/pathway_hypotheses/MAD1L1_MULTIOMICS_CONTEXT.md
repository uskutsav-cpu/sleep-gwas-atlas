# MAD1L1: a cross-project hypothesis, not a replicated mechanism

[Qian et al. (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-026-00660-2) used blood methylation and expression QTL data from adolescents together with psychiatric and insomnia GWAS. They report a Mendelian-randomization association between methylation at **cg19624444**, within *MAD1L1*, and ADHD (reported odds ratio 3.03, p=1×10⁻⁵), as well as associations with other psychiatric traits. Their discovery methylation QTL sample had 1,274 participants; they tested 1,267 ADHD CpGs and describe Bonferroni correction. The result is a **putative** methylation–ADHD hypothesis, dependent on instrument validity, LD, model specification and the authors' selection and multiple-testing procedures; it is not a directly measured causal effect of a particular disease variant.

Separately, the Brain6/frailty atlas lists **rs28528997** with a positional *MAD1L1* label among 124 published insomnia–FI candidates. In the project's post-selected cross-source lookup, it has MVP insomnia p=3.24×10⁻⁷ and FinnGen hospital-frailty p=2.16×10⁻⁶. The full [candidate screen](../evidence/song124_mvp_hfrs_followup.md) documents the assumed source build, multiallelic and overlap concerns, selection and numerical 124-row benchmark. These are marginal associations with different phenotypes. The gene label is positional; no causal connection between rs28528997 and cg19624444 or *MAD1L1* expression follows from the shared gene name. A subsequent [reference LD check](mad1l1_multiomics/RESULTS.md) found r²=0.914 between rs28528997 and one of Qian’s published methylation instruments. This is an informative tagging relationship, not proof of a shared causal variant or mediation.

Qian et al. found that blood methylation at cg19624444 was negatively correlated with methylation in several brain regions in their cross-tissue comparison (reported r≈−0.49 to −0.58). This is an important reason not to transfer the reported **blood** methylation direction directly to a brain-cell mechanism. Their ADHD GWAS inputs include published PGC datasets, including the 2023 release; their QTL cohort is a distinct molecular source, but reuse of an ADHD GWAS is not independent replication of Brain6's ADHD association. The authors also highlight age/tissue differences and possible pleiotropic instruments. Their *MAD1L1* methylation result does not itself demonstrate that insomnia and ADHD share a causal methylation signal or that a frailty locus regulates this CpG.

The three published instruments and rs28528997 have now been mapped to a common build and compared in a European reference. An [exact-variant GTEx lookup](mad1l1_multiomics/gtex/RESULTS.md) finds brain expression associations for an antisense transcript and *MRM2*, plus *MAD1L1* splice associations in non-brain tissues. These are molecular context, not GWAS–QTL colocalization. A useful next test would use dense GWAS and molecular summary data with multi-signal colocalization across independently ascertained sleep and ADHD outcomes. If those tests and sample-overlap checks pass, allele-specific perturbation in relevant human brain cell types can evaluate *MAD1L1* methylation and expression. An exploratory [single-signal disease–eQTL comparison](mad1l1_multiomics/dense_qtl/coloc/RESULTS.md) found low H4 for the two selected GTEx genes against R13 insomnia and HFRS. It is prior- and coverage-dependent and does not test the methylation QTL or exclude secondary sharing. No multi-signal colocalization or perturbation test has been performed here; this is an explicit hypothesis rather than a new gene discovery or treatment target.
