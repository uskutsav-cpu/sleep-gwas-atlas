# MAD1L1-region disease–eQTL colocalization: exploratory single-signal analysis

The dense regional data do **not** give strong support for a shared eQTL–disease causal variant in any of the four selected comparisons under the specified single-signal model. This is a post-hoc investigation of two gene/tissue pairs and two related FinnGen disease phenotypes, not an independent replication, multi-signal analysis, multiple-testing-controlled discovery, or resolution of failed local-correlation QC.

For the fixed GRCh38 chr7:1,534,487–2,534,487 interval, 3,267 or 3,268 SNPs matched per comparison, about **81.4% of eligible eQTL rows** in the selected interval. Source alleles, positions, minor allele frequencies and numerical validity were checked as described in the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md). The remaining eligible QTL rows are missing or excluded, and the interval can truncate each gene's full cis window. These limitations can materially affect colocalization.

The first H4 column uses p1=p2=10⁻⁴ and p12=10⁻⁵; the last uses p12=10⁻⁶. Both assume one causal variant per trait in the region, the default coloc effect priors and a standardized quantitative scale for the eQTL and HFRS. The precise HFRS beta scale and the appropriateness of those priors are not independently confirmed. Narrow and wide effect-prior results are in [posteriors.tsv](posteriors.tsv); none changes these four comparisons into strong shared-signal evidence. H3 is the model's posterior for two distinct regional signals, not proof of two causal variants. Low H4 does not rule out shared biology or a secondary shared signal that the single-signal model cannot represent. HFRS and R13 insomnia releases overlap, GTEx is not the same cohort and its participant overlap was not established, and population and reference LD differ. No posterior identifies an effector gene.

For the matched SNPs, the smallest R13 insomnia p-value in the region is **8.18×10⁻⁵**, whereas the smallest HFRS p-value is **1.15×10⁻⁹**. At the previously selected rs28528997 A>G, R13 insomnia G beta is −0.00516 (SE 0.00737; p=0.484); the previously reviewed MVP insomnia association is p=3.24×10⁻⁷. These are different insomnia definitions and source populations, and source overlap, phenotype and power differences matter. The weak R13 result is not proof of no effect, but it limits claims of consistency across sleep sources. This comparison does not estimate ADHD association or test the methylation QTL.

That checks arithmetic and source consistency, not biological validity. `extract_gwas.py`, `match.py`, `run.R`, and `verify.py` reproduce the analysis using the external source paths and the pinned coloc 5.2.3 functions in the atlas.

## Frequency-format correction before fitting

An initial alignment check assumed the eQTL Catalogue imported GTEx `ac/an` field was ALT frequency. In the selected region it equaled the listed minor-allele frequency for all 5,022 variable AC110781.3 rows and never exceeded 0.5. This differs from the catalogue's [general column description](https://github.com/eQTL-Catalogue/eQTL-Catalogue-resources/blob/master/tabix/Columns.md) for ALT count, and is consistent with GTEx's original minor-allele count. The matcher was corrected **before any coloc fit** to compare minor-allele frequencies without assigning REF or ALT as the minor allele. This increased the matched count from 2,824 to 3,267 for AC110781.3; the initial intermediate was not interpreted or fitted. Five AC110781.3 and four MRM2 rows had zero frequency and non-finite SE and were retained in extraction but excluded from fitting. A minor-frequency match does not independently validate effect direction.
