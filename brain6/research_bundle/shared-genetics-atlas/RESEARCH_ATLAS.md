## 1. Brain6 introduction draft

Sleep is a complex, polygenic phenotype associated with mental and neurological health. Large insomnia GWAS have identified many loci and enriched biological pathways, making it possible to ask whether associations with brain disorders reflect partly shared genetic architecture. The large [Jansen insomnia GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-018-0333-3), [Watanabe insomnia meta-analysis](variant_context/rs62264764_lsamp_prior/RESULTS.md), and the [ADHD GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914347/) provide relevant starting points. Genetic correlation describes aggregate overlap in common-variant effects; it does not establish that insomnia causes ADHD or identify the same causal variant at an individual locus.

The directly relevant [2026 insomnia–ADHD study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13493937/) already reports significant genome-wide correlation, pleiotropic loci and coloc evidence, including rs62264764 at the LSAMP region and a chromosome 5 locus. A second [2026 cross-disorder insomnia study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41065713/) also examines shared loci and pathways. Thus neither the existence of shared genetics nor the rs62264764 locus can be presented as a first discovery. The studies use related public GWAS data, so re-observing a result on overlapping inputs is a consistency check, not independent replication.

Brain6 can contribute a transparent comparison across multiple sleep and disease pairs, with explicit quality gates, prior sensitivity and reproducible variant-level functional hypotheses. [AlphaGenome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10014-0) predicts molecular readouts from long DNA sequences; comparing predictions for reference and alternate alleles can prioritize experiments in a specified tissue. Its scores are model predictions, not measured effects or probabilities of disease. Brain6's exploratory four-track frontal-cortex pilot covers 425 selected variants across six insomnia–ADHD regions; it extends the annotation of previously identified signals but has not established their causal variants, genes or cell types. The appropriate scientific question is whether such predictions add validated information beyond fine-mapping, measured QTL and existing regulatory annotations in held-out loci.

A further [PIP-weighted sensitivity](alphagenome_pip_weighted/RESULTS.md) finds that rs62264764 accounts for 40.6% and 38.2% of the selected chromosome 3 insomnia- and ADHD-weighted predicted accessibility sums, respectively. These shares are descriptive products of fine-mapping weights and model predictions, not probabilities of causation or evidence of disease-risk direction. Removing rs62264764 lowers both chromosome 3 weighted means below the unweighted mean of the remaining variants, showing that the apparent difference depends on this selected prediction.

## 2. Sleep and frailty introduction draft

A later [NHANES observational pilot](component_exclusion/nhanes_public/phenotype_pilot/RESULTS.md) compares approximate FI and adapted Fried scores with item-excluded versions in the same people. It is not a validated reconstruction or an item-excluded GWAS, and its physical questions overlap across the scores.

Frailty captures vulnerability across multiple dimensions, but its definition changes the scientific question. The frailty index (FI) is the proportion of accumulated health deficits; the [Atkins FI GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8441299/) uses a broad deficit-based measure. The [Fried Frailty Score GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651618/) uses five physical dimensions: weakness, exhaustion, weight loss, low activity and slow walking. The [hospital frailty risk score GWAS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00925-y) analyzes a weighted hospital-diagnosis-based construct. FI, FFS and HFRS capture related, nonidentical concepts and differ in measurement, ascertainment and populations.

Sleep disturbance and frailty frequently co-occur, but questionnaire items such as tiredness or sleep complaints can also appear within a frailty measure. A statistical relationship can consequently reflect shared measurement as well as biological and environmental pathways. The [2024 insomnia–frailty genetic study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2024.1358996/full) already reports genome-wide and local shared genetics and nominates loci; subsequent MR work also asks directional questions. Causal interpretation requires assumptions that shared genetic associations alone cannot verify.

[Che et al. 2025](component_exclusion/prior_two_frailty_mr_2025/REVIEW.md) already compared both FI and FFS with several sleep traits using bidirectional Mendelian randomization. Their insomnia–FFS result is nominal, with a pleiotropy diagnostic and no reported multiplicity correction; it does not resolve shared items or establish a causal mechanism. The novelty question therefore concerns what a harmonized FI–FFS–HFRS comparison, component-exclusion sensitivity, local QC and independent validation add beyond prior studies.

[Foote et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02269-0) already modeled 30 frailty deficits, including insomnia and tiredness, using genomic structural equation modeling. Their general and six residual genetic factors show that multivariate frailty-domain analysis is established prior work. The general factor includes an insomnia indicator and does not replace a matched, sleep-excluded frailty GWAS.

Before those studies, a [published walking-pace GWAS](component_exclusion/walking_pace_published/RESULTS.md) reported genetic correlations with insomnia and ADHD, including after BMI adjustment. Walking pace is relevant to physical slowness and does not itself contain a sleep item, but it is not a sleep-excluded FI or exhaustion-excluded FFS; UK Biobank overlap and differences in phenotype and adjustment prevent treating it as independent replication or a matched item-deletion test.

An [exploratory LDSC follow-up](component_exclusion/physical_function_ldsc/RESULTS.md) compares FinnGen registry insomnia with UK Biobank walking pace and left grip: r_g=−0.0631 (SE 0.0319) and −0.0896 (SE 0.0352), respectively. Only grip meets the pilot's Bonferroni correction for two tests. These sleep-free physical-function measures are not modified frailty composites; source coding, sample overlap and reference fit remain limitations.

A [common-marker comparison](component_exclusion/physical_function_ldsc/insomnia_source_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) estimates walking-pace correlations of −0.2900 for original questionnaire insomnia and −0.0608 for registry insomnia; the paired difference meets a two-contrast correction, whereas the analogous grip contrast does not. The sources differ in phenotype, participants, ancestry and other factors, so no particular cause of the difference is established.

A [five-outcome extension on the same markers](component_exclusion/physical_function_ldsc/insomnia_source_sensitivity/frailty_extension/RESULTS.md) finds questionnaire versus registry correlations of 0.6443 versus 0.4301 for FI, 0.4869 versus 0.2435 for FFS, and 0.4363 versus 0.6131 for HFRS. These three paired differences meet the post-hoc five-contrast correction. FI/FFS and questionnaire insomnia include UK Biobank, whereas HFRS and registry insomnia both use FinnGen; exact overlaps, source and reference differences remain unresolved, and none is evidence of causation or a component-excluded score.

An additional [public UKB versus Finnish HFRS comparison](global_audit/ukb_hfrs_public/RESULTS.md) estimates questionnaire/registry-insomnia r_g of 0.529/0.376 with UKB HFRS and 0.436/0.613 with Finnish HFRS on common markers. This source-sensitive pattern is consistent with several explanations, including cohort overlap and ascertainment, but does not isolate one. The released UKB file reports N=407,643 while its study description states 407,463; the discrepancy remains unresolved.

A [selected 425-variant MVP-insomnia/Finnish-HFRS screen](variant_context/brain6_mvp_finngen_hfrs/RESULTS.md) finds joint marginal evidence at rs62264764 and rs1476535 under a post-selected max-p benchmark. Their separate childhood ADHD-symptom p-values are 0.437 and 0.660; this is not independent three-trait replication, common-causal evidence, or a gene assignment.

An [expanded childhood-symptom follow-up](variant_context/brain6_three_trait_candidate/RESULTS.md) matched 418 of those candidates in all three datasets and obtained no crossing of the 425-slot three-phenotype max-p benchmark. It is a selected-family screen with limited power and does not establish that three-trait overlap is absent.

In a [UKB HFRS–registry-insomnia chromosome 7 coloc pilot](frailty_coloc/ukb_hfrs_chr7/RESULTS.md), H3≈0.633 and H4≈0.051 under an illustrative HFRS effect prior and p12=10⁻⁵; H4 falls at p12=10⁻⁶. The one-signal model favors distinct dominant signals but leaves substantial HFRS-only support, and cannot exclude secondary sharing. The unverified HFRS effect scale, sample overlap, ancestry and prior selection limit interpretation.

A [same-marker insomnia-source sensitivity](frailty_coloc/ukb_hfrs_chr7/insomnia_source_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) gives H4≈0.872 or 0.404 for questionnaire insomnia at the two p12 values, versus 0.051 or 0.005 for registry insomnia. Questionnaire insomnia and HFRS both use UK Biobank but the model assumes independent statistics; the result is therefore provisional and differences cannot be attributed to any single cause.

A [correlation stress test using HyPrColoc](frailty_coloc/ukb_hfrs_chr7/insomnia_source_sensitivity/overlap_stress/RESULTS.md) returns no two-trait cluster at tested positive effective correlations of 0.05 and higher. Those correlations are hypothetical, the method and priors differ from the preceding coloc, and the result does not identify the actual degree of sample dependence or rule out a shared signal.

In a [chromosome 5 HFRS–insomnia–ADHD common-support pilot](frailty_coloc/chr5_hfrs_three_trait/RESULTS.md), the pairwise insomnia–ADHD H4 remains 0.991/0.917 at p12=10⁻⁵/10⁻⁶. HFRS pairings are weaker and prior-sensitive, and the tested HyPrColoc cluster contains insomnia and ADHD but not HFRS. Same-cohort dependence, HFRS effect scale and one-signal assumptions prevent inferring that no three-trait signal exists; the original LAVA gate remains failed.

Near rs62264764, a [chromosome 3 common-support pilot](frailty_coloc/chr3_hfrs_three_trait/RESULTS.md) returns a three-trait cluster under independence, but HFRS is excluded from the returned cluster at tested hypothetical positive HFRS–insomnia correlations. Pairwise H4 values are prior-sensitive, and the actual overlap and HFRS scale are unknown. This is a hypothesis-generating result, not independent replication or a causal gene assignment.

A [selected-variant cross-source comparison](variant_context/hfrs_cross_source/RESULTS.md) finds HFRS associations in both UKB and FinnGen at correlated variants in the chromosome 3 and 7 regions. It is a post-hoc frailty-only check and does not demonstrate a replicated sleep–ADHD–frailty causal signal.

A [common-support conjunction screen](hfrs_conjunction/ukb_two_insomnia/RESULTS.md) tested UKB HFRS with questionnaire and registry insomnia in one two-pair correction family and found no corrected SNP–pair crossings. Its conservative max-p results do not imply no shared architecture or identify causal loci.

A [UKB HFRS dementia-weight sensitivity](global_audit/ukb_hfrs_public/dementia_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) finds similar insomnia correlations for original and modified HFRS on identical markers. The paired differences are small and do not meet their two-contrast correction; this does not exclude locus-specific changes or replace sleep/exhaustion-item deletion.

An observational precedent is [Zhu et al.](component_exclusion/zhu_observational/REVIEW.md): in 23,847 Chinese adults, the investigators removed insomnia symptoms from their 27-item FI and studied frailty transitions over a median eight years. Among participants prefrail at baseline, baseline insomnia was associated with subsequent frailty (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.53, 95% CI 1.23–1.89). This is a selected observational stratum in a different population, not a component-excluded GWAS or proof that sleep causes frailty.

[Mak et al. 2023](component_exclusion/mak_category_fi/RESULTS.md) also ran a GWAS of a broader category-excluded FI that removes insomnia and fatigue along with six other items. Its reviewed public release did not identify the modified-FI summary statistics by filename; no insomnia–modified-FI genetic correlation has been estimated here. This prior work limits broad claims of being first to perform an FI GWAS without sleep items.

A useful next study would compare the same sleep phenotypes against FI, FFS and HFRS within a harmonized framework, quantify uncertainty in their differences and test whether associations persist when sleep, fatigue and other overlapping items are removed. The present analysis finds strong but imperfect FI–FFS genetic correlation, and positive insomnia correlations across definitions. Independent phenotype measurements, component-excluded GWAS, locus replication and molecular experiments remain necessary before the observed overlap can be attributed to a physical-frailty-specific mechanism.

## 4. Loci and functional evidence

The [six-region AlphaGenome peak comparison](locus_atlas/alphagenome_peak_comparison/RESULTS.md) places each region’s strongest selected predicted DNase change beside credible-set membership and regional coloc, distinguishing predicted molecular effects from shared genetic evidence.


The consolidated [locus atlas](locus_atlas/LOCUS_ATLAS.md) separates the six Brain6 regions from the six post-selected frailty candidates, and provides machine-readable tables with evidence and QC status.

**rs62264764** is GRCh38 chr3:117920728 G>A. The A allele has beta −0.03999 (SE 0.00726) for insomnia and −0.06470 (SE 0.0128) for ADHD in the source inputs. Single-signal conditional PIPs are 0.0402 and 0.0312, respectively. It overlaps [LSAMP](https://rest.ensembl.org/overlap/region/human/3:117920728-117920728?feature=gene;content-type=application/json) and another annotated gene, LINC03051. Overlap is positional context and does not establish an effector gene. LSAMP's neuronal adhesion and circuit biology make it a testable candidate, supported by [mouse research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2275759/) and [synaptic work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2900390/); neither proves a human variant mechanism. The 2026 paper already reported this variant as an insomnia–ADHD lead, so the locus is not novel.

Among the 425 selected variants this variant has the largest absolute adult DNase local change: log₂[(ALT signal+1)/(REF signal+1)] = −0.2734 over a 501-bp window; AVI PHRED = 20.49. These are exploratory predictions, not p-values. The adult local RNA ratio is −0.0051 and is not a gene-level expression estimate. The chr3 single-signal coloc PP4 is 0.9207 at p12=10⁻⁵ but 0.5373 at p12=10⁻⁶; its interpretation is prior-sensitive. The stored lead eQTL result for rs62264767 cannot be attributed to rs62264764. A direct GTEx v8 exact-variant lookup subsequently found rs62264764–LSAMP and rs62264764–RP11-384F7.2 eQTL records in adrenal gland; cortex and frontal cortex dynamic LSAMP tests were not nominally significant. The frontal RP11-384F7.2 nominal p=0.032 is exploratory and did not appear in GTEx’s significant-pair query. See the [exact-variant QTL review](variant_context/gtex_v8/GTEX_QTL_REVIEW.md). A subsequent [dense GTEx–GWAS comparison](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/RESULTS.md) found low H4 for the selected adrenal and frontal-cortex gene–trait pairs under a single-signal model, with substantial coverage and model limitations; it does not rule out secondary sharing. A [reference LD comparison of regional leads](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/lead_ld/RESULTS.md) finds low linkage between the strongest adrenal eQTL marker and rs62264764 (r²≈0.061), which is consistent with but does not prove distinct causal signals. A [SuSiE multi-signal pilot](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/multisignal_pilot/RESULTS.md) yields low H4 for the qualifying eQTL–insomnia set pair, but LD–Z discrepancies, study-reference mismatch and no ADHD credible set leave it unvalidated. A [source-frequency check](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/multisignal_pilot/frequency_qc/RESULTS.md) found close reference frequencies and verified the ADHD effective-N arithmetic, but does not resolve the LD diagnostic. A [post-hoc removal sensitivity](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/multisignal_pilot/outlier_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) left eQTL–insomnia sets and H4 nearly unchanged, while some LD–Z outliers remained; it is not a validation.

**Chromosome 5, GRCh37 103.45–104.45 Mb** has trait–trait single-signal coloc PP4=0.9911 at p12=10⁻⁵ and 0.9174 at 10⁻⁶. This is encouraging but conditional on assumptions, and the region was also previously reported. rs2431108 (GRCh38 chr5:104612267 T>C) has conditional PIPs 0.1305 for insomnia and 0.2436 for ADHD; adult local DNase log₂ ratio +0.00053. rs77960 has ADHD PIP 0.2828 but a low overall AVI in this lookup. These metrics measure different things; none alone identifies a causal variant. A nearby-gene label is not gene assignment. Measured GTEx lead eQTL results for rs2431108 were nominally weak (cortex p=0.291; frontal cortex p=0.099), not evidence for a causal target. See the pilot molecular report for the exact molecular trait.

**Sleep–frailty examples** are post-selected candidates: rs12536335 lies within FOXP2 and has same estimated G-allele direction across reviewed FI, FFS, HFRS and insomnia inputs; the FI cohort estimates differ in sign under an unconfirmed TwinGene allele adapter. rs7734045 has same-direction point estimates in the reviewed FI, FFS and insomnia sources; TRPC7 is ~10.7 kb away, while AC009014.3 is closer. Neither gene is established as the causal target. rs10865954 was already reported in the 2024 shared-genetics paper. The evidence folder includes all 15 rows, source alleles, estimates and uncertainty; selected nominal p-values and overlapping sources are not replication.

### Gene and pathway hypotheses

A [gene and pathway review](pathway_hypotheses/GENE_PATHWAY_REVIEW.md) separates the published GABAergic and glutamatergic cell-type findings from the project's untested variant-to-gene hypotheses. One cross-region idea is FOXP2–MEF2C neuronal-circuit biology, supported by mouse experiments; however, the Brain6 variants have not been shown to regulate those genes and the MEF2C-containing region is ADHD-prioritized rather than a strong shared-signal result. The review gives the molecular experiments and independent-data checks needed to test each connection.

A 2026 [MAD1L1 multi-omics study](pathway_hypotheses/MAD1L1_MULTIOMICS_CONTEXT.md) provides prior blood methylation–ADHD evidence at a gene also named in the sleep–frailty candidate table. Variant-to-CpG colocalization and a shared sleep–ADHD–frailty mechanism remain untested; the paper uses public PGC ADHD inputs and is not independent locus replication.

### Exploratory candidate conjunction screen

A separate post-selected 15-marker analysis from the frailty project compared MVP insomnia with FinnGen HFRS using the larger of the two marginal p-values, followed by a 15-test Bonferroni calculation. rs12536335 has max-p 4.57×10⁻⁸ (×15 = 6.86×10⁻⁷), and rs1909650 has max-p 0.0003856 (×15 = 0.00578). Both estimated signs agree for the chosen counted allele. I independently checked all 15 arithmetic calculations in the [full candidate table](evidence/mvp_insomnia_hfrs_joint_candidate_screen.csv). This is an intersection–union test of marginal associations, not PLACO or conjFDR, and the post-selected set, unknown source independence and allele issues mean these numbers are exploratory rather than confirmatory. HFRS evidence is not an FFS association, and the same marker can tag different causal variants. The [source report](evidence/mvp_insomnia_hfrs_joint_candidate_screen.md) preserves the limits.

### Marginal association patterns

Within the shared HapMap3 support, a separate three-trait Bonferroni screen produced 30 insomnia-only, 21 FI-only, 313 FFS-only and four FI+FFS marker-level threshold-crossing patterns; no marker crossed the marginal threshold for insomnia and either frailty measure. These are **not** counts of independent loci and are not proof of trait-specific genetic effects. They are prioritization patterns affected by trait power and measurement. The [full table and protocol](marginal_priority/RESULTS.md) retain all three marginal associations and alleles.

The [HFRS follow-up](marginal_priority/hfrs_followup/RESULTS.md) matches all 368 selected markers to hospital-frailty statistics. None also crosses a stricter four-trait threshold for HFRS. This selected lookup does not include HFRS-only markers elsewhere and does not prove trait specificity.

A subsequent [four-trait marginal screen](four_trait_marginal/RESULTS.md) adds HFRS across the three-trait common support: 171 markers cross the HFRS threshold alone, in addition to 333 markers crossing for insomnia, FI or FFS. These are threshold patterns, not counts of independent loci or proof of trait specificity; 335 HFRS records were unavailable.

### Conservative conjunction check on common HapMap3 markers

An exploratory max-p intersection–union scan of FinnGen R13 insomnia against FI and FFS tested 1,138,262 common markers per pair. No SNP passed the two-pair Bonferroni threshold of 2.1963×10⁻⁸; the smallest max-p was 2.50×10⁻⁵. BH and dependence-robust BY FDR corrections of these max-p tests also return zero SNP–pair rejections at 0.05. These are not the distinct enrichment-based conjunctional FDR method or PLACO+, and the result does not rule out shared genetic architecture. It assumes valid marginal p-values, uses rounded p-derived Z scores and covers only shared HapMap3 markers. See [protocol, ranked markers and interpretation](conjunction_screen/RESULTS.md).

### FinnGen insomnia–hospital-frailty conjunction

A separate [HFRS conjunction screen](hfrs_conjunction/RESULTS.md) tested 1,158,555 matched HapMap3 SNPs, retaining all 1,158,908 insomnia rows in its one-pair correction family. No SNP passed Bonferroni, BH or BY correction. FinnGen R12 and R13 have overlapping participants, and this conservative test does not establish a shared causal variant or independent replication. It is a separate post-hoc analysis, not the enrichment-based conjFDR method.

### Descriptive conditional enrichment

The [conditional enrichment diagnostic](conditional_enrichment/RESULTS.md) finds that the proportion of markers with insomnia p≤0.01 rises from 2.33% overall to 6.14% among FI p≤0.001 markers and 5.64% among FFS p≤0.001 markers. A [physical-block sensitivity](conditional_enrichment/block_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) gives similar uncertainty estimates at 5 Mb and 10 Mb. These LD-dependent proportions, physical blocks and simplified Gaussian controls are descriptive; they are not a calibrated enrichment-based conjFDR or evidence of a particular shared causal locus.

A [common-support three-frailty comparison](conditional_enrichment/frailty_comparison/RESULTS.md) gives conditional insomnia p≤0.01 fractions of 6.13% for FI, 5.64% for FFS and 8.60% for HFRS, using frailty p≤0.001. A [regional sensitivity](conditional_enrichment/frailty_comparison/regional_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) finds that excluding a broad APOE-containing window has little effect on these fractions; it is not an APOE-specific causal test. Selection groups, power and ascertainment differ; FinnGen insomnia and HFRS overlap, and the paired block intervals are descriptive rather than calibrated biological contrasts.

### PLACO+ scan controls added 27 October

An exploratory PLACO+ scan of registry insomnia against FI and FFS on aligned HapMap3 markers completed for both pairs after an FFS output integrity failure and replacement run; it is not the dense whole-genome screen requested for final discovery. The completed results remain exploratory because of these unresolved calibration and source-model issues.

The [FI-only PLACO diagnostic](placo_diagnostic/completed_audit_fi_only/RESULTS.md) passed its row and control audit and has 16 numerical threshold crossings. ## 5. Quality control and missing analyses

Brain6's prespecified LAVA family has 17,465 trait–locus cells, of which 3,720 were NOT_RUN against an allowed maximum of 873. It failed the frozen family gate, so no primary local genetic correlation was promoted. Insomnia source repair did not meet the preset advancement criterion. Long-sleep N/model semantics remain unresolved; 0/32 reviewed LD matrices passed the separate fine-mapping QC gate, preventing the prespecified SuSiE route. An alternative same-source SUPERGNOVA method found no FWER-supported protected candidate (12 not supported, 13 method inapplicable). It is a method sensitivity, not independent cohort replication. The 25 candidates are screen candidates; none met the predeclared positive validation classes. Trait–trait ABF coloc remains exploratory. The four completed brain molecular QTL coloc tests concern one interval and one component trait, max PP4 0.0824; missing tests are not negative results. A subset positional enrichment analysis found no tissue or pathway at BH q<0.05 and cannot establish cell type.

A [local partition and LAVA-gate audit](local_evidence/partition_and_gate_audit/RESULTS.md) matches the previously published 3p21.31 interval to a standard European LD block and reconciles the saved insomnia failure categories. The exact published testing family and sample-overlap settings remain unknown; the prior result does not repair the failed gate.

A [selected chromosome 3 HDL-L feasibility pilot](local_evidence/hdll_pilot/RESULTS.md) returned local insomnia–FI correlation estimates of 0.871–0.900 under three hypothetical overlap settings. The p-values and intervals are uncorrected, the region was selected after prior work, and the same UKB-derived trait data are reused. HDL-L's target and partition differ from LAVA's; this result is not independent replication or a rescue of the failed local-analysis gates.

The [published-local frailty audit](local_evidence/LOCAL_FRAILTY_AUDIT.md) extracts Song et al.’s 3p21.31 supplementary row and flags that its `local_rhog` label corresponds to covariance in standard HESS output; the paper’s exact post-processing remains unverified. Sleep–frailty historical local LAVA gates also failed. The available shared-variant studies and candidate screens motivate follow-up, but no new reliable genome-wide PLACO/conjFDR run or validated local frailty atlas was executed in this synthesis. **Do not infer a null from non-estimability.** Do not run PLACO/conjFDR naively across overlapping cohorts: obtain signed harmonized dense statistics, frequencies, build, ancestry and per-SNP N; account for correlated Z statistics and assess null calibration; freeze all trait pairs and family corrections before screening, LD-clump and report both single-trait p-values. A cross-trait screen does not prove a shared causal variant; require credible sets, multi-signal coloc with valid ancestry-matched LD and independent cohorts. Failed input/QC stays visible in the denominator and status ledger.

## 6. Hypotheses and concrete next steps

1. **FI vs FFS:** their phenotypic correlation and r_g will be positive but less than 1. Obtain within-person FI/FFS measurements, report N and missingness, Pearson and Spearman estimates with bootstrap CI, and pre-specified age/sex strata. Independently evaluate r_g using matched reference LD and ascertainment. These are distinct estimands.
2. **Measure overlap:** insomnia–FI association will attenuate but remain positive after prespecified removal of insomnia, fatigue and mental-health items; compare separately with FFS minus exhaustion. Recompute phenotype and new GWAS on identical participants, pre-register denominator and exclusions, use covariance-aware paired inference. Status: untested.
3. **Shared loci:** fine-map the chr3 LSAMP and chr5 intervals using ancestry-matched, allele-verified LD and multi-signal methods; compare with independently ascertained sleep AND disease data. Test trait–QTL coloc with full summary statistics and tissue/sample provenance before choosing genes. Status: ABF only, no independent pairwise locus replication.
4. **Cell context:** overlay credible variants on measured single-cell ATAC and expression with appropriate cell-type matched background; test enrichment over all prespecified cell types, BH-correct, then use cell-type QTL/coloc where available. Adult frontal cortex is one tissue proxy, not evidence for a specific cell type. For frailty also test relevant muscle, immune and brain datasets; do not declare a tissue without a measured and corrected test.
5. **Sequence-function validation:** for rs62264764 and matched low-prediction, high-PIP and LD controls, evaluate the actual locus in relevant cell models by allele-specific reporter or CRISPR perturbation, measuring accessibility and expression of predeclared candidate genes; replicate donors and use negative controls. AlphaGenome's [variant-scoring documentation](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphagenome/blob/main/docs/source/variant_scoring.md) describes gene-aware RNA scoring; the current 501-bp RNA signal is not a substitute.
6. **Genetic risk and ML:** retain separate FI, FFS, HFRS, insomnia and ADHD weights; evaluate genome-wide PRS and a locked 15-marker prototype against held-out observed phenotypes, adjusting covariates and comparing to demographic and single-trait baselines. Use independent cohorts, ancestry-specific LD and calibration; report R²/AUC and uncertainty, not percentages from reference genotype variation. Split by individuals/families and independent loci as appropriate. Only with suitable licenses and adequate independent labeled data, develop variant/gene embeddings from public functional annotations and evaluate incremental retrieval against held-out loci, correcting for LD and train/test leakage. The current AlphaGenome output terms restrict use to train variant-effect or competing technologies; verify the exact governing terms before using outputs as ML training inputs. A rules-based research ranking and licensed features avoid assuming permission to train on these outputs. No 15-marker clinical score is supported.

A small [trait-embedding experiment](trait_embedding/EMBEDDING_REPORT.md) now applies matrix factorization to Brain6’s existing genetic correlations. Its held-out-cell diagnostic is exploratory and does not establish individual risk, causal interactions, or independent biological replication. An [exploratory extension adding FI and FFS](multiscale_embedding/REPORT.md) performs no better than a column-mean baseline in its pooled masked-cell diagnostic and worse than an additive baseline; a post-hoc multi-start sensitivity improves reconstruction but does not establish independent performance. Source harmonization remains incomplete, so the embedding should not be used for prediction.

[Tseng et al.](multiscale_embedding/TSENG_PRIOR_ML.md) previously combined frailty GWAS and machine-learning classification using clinical measurements and, in a separate FI setting, a genetic risk score. Their internal-split classification AUC and this project's trait-matrix reconstruction error measure different tasks and cannot be compared directly. Combining frailty genetics and ML alone is not a new contribution; added value in an independently evaluated sleep–disease framework remains to be tested.

### Additional exploratory FI–insomnia colocalization

Dense FI and FinnGen R13 insomnia summary statistics were matched at two previously selected windows. In the 1-Mb chr3 window, default H4 is 0.002 with H0 0.828, so the data are uninformative about a shared signal. In the chr7 window, H4 is 0.587 with p12=10⁻⁵ but falls to 0.125 with p12=10⁻⁶. The top chr7 conditional variants lie over 220 kb from the previously examined marker, and unmodeled multiple signals may affect the result. These exploratory, prior-sensitive findings do not establish a common causal variant or resolve the failed LAVA gates. See [methods and full results](frailty_coloc/RESULTS.md). A third, post-selected [chromosome 5 FI–registry-insomnia analysis](frailty_coloc_chr5/RESULTS.md) yields H4 0.512 and 0.095 under the same priors. Its top conditional candidate rs1363103 was already a published Atkins FI lead and uses the same FI source; see the [prior-discovery check](frailty_coloc_chr5/PRIOR_DISCOVERY_CHECK.md). Its top model-supported variants show only moderate EUR reference LD with rs2431108; pairwise insomnia–ADHD and FI–insomnia results do not establish a three-trait shared causal variant.

### FI and FFS diagnostic recovery

The replacement FFS scan passed gzip, row alignment and control checks. Across the two phenotype pairs, 16 FI and 66 FFS markers crossed the prespecified numerical PLACO+ threshold; these are exploratory and are not established independent loci. Sensitivity checks, positional groups and calibration limitations are in the [completed diagnostic](placo_diagnostic/completed_audit/RESULTS.md).

The [FFS reference-LD follow-up](placo_diagnostic/completed_audit/ffs_reference_ld/RESULTS.md) confirms substantial linkage among many selected markers and identifies a sensitivity-dependent chr22 overlap with FI markers; it does not establish independent signals or a shared causal variant.

A [chromosome 5 cross-project reference-LD follow-up](placo_diagnostic/completed_audit/ffs_reference_ld/chr5_cross_project/RESULTS.md) links the selected FFS markers to prior FI and Brain6 anchors, including exact variant overlap; the linked associations reuse data and cannot establish a common three-trait causal signal.

A [model-specific tail stress test](placo_diagnostic/tail_stress/RESULTS.md) found that the PLACO+ numerical screening rule can exceed its nominal per-marker threshold under fixed one-trait-only effects. The real-data error rate is unknown, and the resulting candidates remain exploratory.

### Prior multivariate frailty genetics

[Foote et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02269-0) already applied genomic SEM to 30 frailty deficits, including insomnia and tiredness, and reported a general and six residual factors. This limits any broad novelty claim for multivariate frailty genetics. The [release review](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/REVIEW.md) maps seven factor accessions and a separate pneumonia accession; the general factor includes insomnia, and none substitutes for matched component-excluded FI or FFS GWAS.

A [published-factor anchor lookup](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/variant_crosswalk/indexed/RESULTS.md) places rs2431108 and rs7703746 below Foote et al.’s published seven-factor GWAS threshold for their general frailty factor. Because that factor includes insomnia and contributing cohorts overlap, this is prior context, not independent sleep–frailty replication or an item-exclusion result.

The [extended published-factor crosswalk](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/variant_crosswalk/indexed/EXTENDED_RESULTS.md) matches 87 post-selected markers against seven prior frailty-factor files. Twenty-five selected chr5 markers meet the published general-factor threshold, while many queried variants are absent from the filtered release; neither count represents independent loci or establishes sleep-independent frailty.

The [Foote supplementary-table crosswalk](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/published_locus_crosswalk/RESULTS.md) records earlier factor-locus comparisons for rs1363103, rs2396766 and rs7703746 and identifies UK Biobank insomnia and tiredness inputs. The authors’ locus labels do not independently replicate a joint sleep–frailty association.

Foote et al.’s [published locus table](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/published_locus_crosswalk/RESULTS.md) already names rs2431108 as the general-frailty locus lead, with rs1363103 among its listed independently significant markers. This is prior frailty-factor discovery, not proof of a shared sleep–ADHD–frailty causal variant.

For phenotype context, an independent [Berlin Aging Study II comparison](phenotype_comparison/berlin_base_ii/RESULTS.md) gives rounded within-person Pearson r=0.38 at baseline and 0.53 at follow-up for adapted deficit FI and Fried scores in the first imputed dataset. These are not the project’s exact phenotypes or genetic correlation.

A [fresh same-data LDSC re-execution](global_audit/insomnia_adhd_rerun/RESULTS.md) reproduces insomnia–ADHD r_g=0.4033 (SE 0.0318), p=5.836×10⁻³⁷ to reported precision with archived inputs and European LD scores. It is not independent replication; the original atlas log remains unavailable and LAVA QC remains failed.

The [FI–FFS LDSC re-execution](global_audit/fi_ffs_rerun/RESULTS.md) reproduces r_g=0.7534 (SE 0.0199), z=37.8187 and 1,157,249 valid-allele markers to the logged precision. It reuses the named inputs and does not measure the correlation of participants’ phenotype scores.

A [shared-support registry-insomnia comparison](global_audit/insomnia_frailty_same_support/RESULTS.md) re-executes FI, FFS and HFRS LDSC on 1,134,953 aligned SNPs and computes paired jackknife contrasts. HFRS shows the highest point estimate, but its elevated intercept and shared FinnGen ascertainment limit interpretation; the comparisons do not establish a sleep-independent or causal frailty mechanism.

The [published general-frailty source](component_exclusion/foote_2025_metadata/variant_crosswalk/VERIFIED_GENERAL_FACTOR.md) has since been streamed in full and matched to its publisher checksum. Its selected-marker results agree with the harmonized indexed lookup after effect-allele alignment; missing records remain uninformative about unfiltered associations.

A [coordinate-restricted sensitivity](global_audit/insomnia_frailty_same_support/coordinate_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) excludes 537 markers unresolved by the selected HFRS liftover check from all three registry-insomnia comparisons; each displayed genetic correlation changes by at most 0.0003. The exclusions do not prove those markers invalid or resolve model and cohort limitations.

A [second frailty-pair sensitivity](frailty_sensitivity/mak2023_cross_study/RESULTS.md) independently checks saved paired-jackknife comparisons using Mak’s FI and modified physical phenotype alongside Atkins FI and Ye FFS, across three insomnia sources. Both frailty pairs retain sleep/fatigue content and may share UK Biobank participants; this is not a component-exclusion test or independent replication.

The [Mak construct-transfer stress test](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/mak_transfer/RESULTS.md) compares the saved held-out insomnia embedding predictions with a second FI and physical-frailty GWAS. The UKB-insomnia representation transfers poorly to the FinnGen registry outcome in this small proxy test; it is not an independent-cohort evaluation.

The [cross-source PLACO candidate follow-up](placo_diagnostic/cross_source_followup/RESULTS.md) found no 82-row benchmark crossing in the 80 candidates matched to both MVP insomnia and hospital frailty. These differ from the discovery phenotypes and the result neither validates nor disproves the original PLACO diagnostic.

A post-selected [chromosome 3 common-support analysis](frailty_coloc/adhd_same_support/RESULTS.md) compares FI with ADHD and both insomnia sources on the same 1,496 variants. ADHD-only dominates the FI–ADHD model, making it uninformative about sharing; it is not three-trait colocalization or independent replication.

The [chromosome 5 RP11-6N13.1 dense eQTL follow-up](variant_context/chr5_dense_qtl/RESULTS.md) shows high model-based shared-signal support in testis with each existing GWAS; frontal cortex was not estimable. This is tissue-specific exploratory evidence, not proof of brain mediation, three-trait colocalization or independent replication.

A [common-support chromosome 5 comparison](variant_context/chr5_dense_qtl/common_support/RESULTS.md) finds different conditional SNP sets for expression–GWAS and trait–trait comparisons; their selected marker groups have reference r² about 0.70. Regional pairwise H4 values do not establish a single three-trait causal variant.

A [three-trait chromosome 5 HyPrColoc pilot](variant_context/chr5_dense_qtl/hyprcoloc_pilot/RESULTS.md) gives default cluster posterior 0.7312 for testis expression, insomnia and ADHD under an unverified independence assumption; a stricter prior or threshold returns the two GWAS traits only. It is exploratory and does not establish brain mediation or repair LAVA QC.

A [chromosome 3 three-trait pilot](variant_context/rs62264764_dense_qtl/hyprcoloc_pilot/RESULTS.md) did not return an adrenal LSAMP–insomnia–ADHD cluster under four settings; the default insomnia–ADHD cluster favored rs62264767, with rs62264764 also receiving a conditional score. LD and source uncertainties remain and this does not establish absence of secondary sharing.

A post-selected [chromosome 7 FI–insomnia–ADHD pilot](frailty_coloc/chr7_three_trait/RESULTS.md) returned an FI–insomnia cluster on 1,055 matched variants under its default independent-statistics model, but not a three-trait cluster. Its marker is only modestly linked to the earlier Brain6 and frailty anchors in the European reference; sample and phenotype overlap and single-signal assumptions remain material.

In a [chromosome 7 FI precision stress test](frailty_coloc/chr7_three_trait/precision_sensitivity/RESULTS.md), the default FI–insomnia cluster persisted across 200 illustrative rounding perturbations but the leading marker changed in 81. This is a numerical sensitivity, not evidence of biological validation.

A post-selected [three-marker childhood ADHD-symptom lookup](replication/childhood_symptoms/RESULTS.md) found nominal same-direction rs77960 evidence in a symptom cohort reported nonoverlapping with the diagnostic ADHD source; rs62264764 and rs1476535 were weak. Sleep-side independence and a full candidate-family correction remain unresolved, so this is not independent two-trait replication.

A [retrospective two-source rs77960 comparison](replication/symptom_r13_joint/RESULTS.md) joins the previously examined FinnGen registry-insomnia result with the childhood ADHD-symptom follow-up; both directions agree with discovery. The max-p×3 result is descriptive after selection and exact independence across all cohorts remains unverified, so it is not confirmatory two-trait replication.

The [retrospective six-original-lead comparison](replication/six_lead_joint/RESULTS.md) matches five leads in both follow-up sources. The chr5 lead rs2431108 has joint max-p 0.0060 (six-lead numerical benchmark 0.036), but earlier marker inspection and unverified independence prevent treating it as confirmatory replication; one lead lacked a FinnGen record.

A [separate MVP insomnia follow-up](replication/mvp_six_leads/RESULTS.md) found a same-direction but weak rs2431108 association (p=0.0895), compared with its stronger FinnGen result. No original lead crossed the retrospective MVP–childhood-symptom six-test numerical benchmark; different phenotype, sample and power limit interpretation.

The [rs62264764 MVP and nearby-lead comparison](replication/rs62264764_mvp/RESULTS.md) finds same-direction insomnia association across the MVP and FinnGen sources and r²=1.0 with rs62264767 in 503 EUR reference people. Their AlphaGenome DNase predictions differ, motivating experimental follow-up without establishing causal variant or gene identity.

The exploratory trait-level ML model was further checked with a [related-measure group holdout](multiscale_embedding/hfrs_extension/heldout_sleep/group_holdout/RESULTS.md). Error increased relative to leaving individual traits out; the analysis still relies on overlapping cohorts and six known brain-correlation features and is not clinical prediction.

An independent neuronal reporter-assay resource was identified, but its public experiment file uses oligo identifiers and its variant-effect file was not released and returned HTTP 403. We cannot yet determine whether the selected variants were tested or infer allelic effects; see the [access and candidate lookup](variant_context/neuronal_mpra_2026/RESULTS.md). This does not replicate the sleep–ADHD association.

A [second neuronal reporter-assay lookup](variant_context/zeng_neuronal_mpra/RESULTS.md) identified a named allelic-effects table from Zeng and colleagues, but anonymous download is refused. No result for the selected variants is available from it yet.

A [public neural-progenitor reporter-assay lookup](variant_context/matoba_2026_mpra/RESULTS.md) did not find the seven selected rsIDs in the examined supplementary sheets; a limited coordinate lookup also had no matches. Build and coverage limitations prevent interpreting these as negative functional results.

A published [neuronal reporter-assay follow-up for rs40465](variant_context/rs40465_mpra/RESULTS.md) found no FDR-significant allele effect in 13 matched cell contexts. This chromosome 5 candidate is distinct from rs62264764, and the result neither excludes weaker effects nor demonstrates target-gene mediation.

A [public psychiatric MPRA coverage screen](variant_context/crossdisorder_mpra/RESULTS.md) matches 42 ranked variants, including rs4916723, in neuronal-progenitor count matrices. Presence in counts does not establish a significant allelic effect; the study results and full QC still need verification.

An [exploratory allele-model reconstruction](variant_context/crossdisorder_mpra/RESULTS.md) reveals substantial normalization-version sensitivity: rs4916723 changes significance across two provisional models. The published QC family and enhancer activity remain unverified, so neither result is admitted as the published finding.

A [quality-control sensitivity](variant_context/crossdisorder_mpra/RESULTS.md) removes variants named in the public exclusion list and refits the exploratory neuronal model. This explains part of the discrepancy with the paper but does not reconstruct its published test family.

A [raw barcode audit](variant_context/crossdisorder_mpra/barcode_audit/RESULTS.md) found unresolved discrepancies between the public barcode mapping, count files and aggregated matrices for two candidates. The provisional neuronal assay analysis remains unvalidated.

The [expanded barcode audit](variant_context/crossdisorder_mpra/barcode_audit/RESULTS.md) found limited source-file reconciliation across 42 candidates; lack of a barcode match is not a negative functional result.

A [FinnGen ADHD browser-count update](replication/rs62264764_power/finngen_browser_counts/RESULTS.md) supports an improved illustrative power calculation for rs62264764 but did not yield a variant association or establish cohort independence.

A [public reporter-assay follow-up](variant_context/siraj_2026_mpra/RESULTS.md) measured a linked rs1456193 marker in SK-N-SH cells with no detected allele skew; rs62264764 itself was not among the matched design variants. The assay is context-limited and does not establish absence of a functional effect or a causal gene.

A [chromosome 5 reference-LD follow-up](variant_context/siraj_2026_mpra/chr5_lead_ld/RESULTS.md) places the post-selected reporter candidate rs12658032 in substantial LD with both shared-trait leads. Its assay results remain library-dependent and exploratory.

A [developmental chromatin lookup for rs12658032](variant_context/siraj_2026_mpra/chr5_developmental_lookup/RESULTS.md) found no nominal association across 14 selected variant–peak rows. Its available expression association is testis-specific context and does not establish a brain target.
