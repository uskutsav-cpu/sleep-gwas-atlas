# FI and Fried frailty: phenotypic comparison and genetic correlation

The published evidence supplies a within-person comparison, though not the exact continuous FI–FFS correlation for the project's GWAS participants. In [Petermann-Rocha et al.](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12916-020-01822-4), 383,845 UK Biobank participants had a 49-item self-reported FI and an adapted five-criterion Fried phenotype, both assessed at baseline. The authors report the full 3×3 count table. We independently calculated **Spearman's rho = 0.2845** from the *ordinal categories* in that table: Fried robust / prefrail / frail and FI robust / mild / moderate-or-severe. This is a new descriptive reconstruction from published aggregated counts, not a value claimed to have been reported by the authors. It is a category-level correlation, not the correlation of the underlying continuous FI and 0–5 FFS. Ties use standard average ranks; `calculate.R` expands counts and checks the result. A 10,000-replicate paired-category multinomial bootstrap gives a descriptive 95% percentile interval of 0.2813–0.2876, assuming independent sampling from the observed 3×3 distribution. This interval does not recover confounding, age distributions, participant selection, missingness, family clustering or continuous scores; it is not a genetic-correlation interval. See `BOOTSTRAP_PROTOCOL.md`, `bootstrap_categories.py` and `bootstrap_summary.json`.

Of 11,836 people classified as frail by the adapted Fried phenotype, 3,986 (33.7%) were in the FI moderate-or-severe category. Of 15,958 people in the FI moderate-or-severe category, 3,986 (25.0%) were classified as frail by the Fried phenotype. Cutoffs and prevalence differ; these proportions are conditional on the specific definitions and are not sensitivities against a gold standard. Because the study uses UK Biobank, it may overlap with the FI and FFS discovery GWAS; it is not independent validation of their genetic findings.

Other contexts give different measures of agreement. [Welstead et al.](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/162825199/1_s2.0_S0531556520304034_main.pdf) reported rho=0.43 at baseline in the Lothian Birth Cohort 1936, with 1,091 baseline participants around age 70; their FI used 30 deficits, and Fried phenotype measures differed from the UKB study. They also reported 0.51 and 0.48 at later waves with fewer participants. The exact number used for each correlation was not stated in the located passage; the full cohort size is not necessarily the complete-case correlation N. [China Kadoorie Biobank research](https://www.ndph.ox.ac.uk/publication/1212214) reported weighted kappa of 0.357 and 0.408 between Fried frailty categories and 28- and 40-item FIs among 23,615 people. Kappa assesses category agreement beyond chance; it is not a correlation and should not be directly compared numerically to rho.

The project's archived LDSC estimate is **genetic correlation r_g = 0.7534 (SE 0.0199)** for Atkins FI and Ye FFS using common variants. This estimates overlap in genome-wide genetic effects under LDSC assumptions; it is not the proportion of genetic causes shared, a correlation between people's scores, or a measure of individual prediction. It cannot be subtracted from the category rho or the Lothian rho: they describe different populations, measures and statistical quantities. FI and FFS GWAS both include UKB participants, with the exact overlap unknown.

The original UKB insomnia GWAS and a sleep/insomnia item in the FI both use UKB field 1200, a direct phenotype overlap. The 49-item FI also includes tiredness and depressed feelings, and FFS includes exhaustion. These overlaps make a stronger observed insomnia–FI relationship hard to assign uniquely to physical frailty biology. One [observational cohort](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-022-02557-0) removed insomnia symptoms from an FI, but no same-participant component-excluded FI or FFS GWAS was available for the project's requested genetic sensitivity. The strongest next comparison uses the identical people and model for FI, FI minus insomnia, FI minus insomnia and fatigue, FFS, and FFS minus exhaustion, followed by appropriately paired statistics and independent replication.

A separate [category-agreement sensitivity](category_agreement/RESULTS.md) yields unweighted κ=0.207, linear-weighted κ=0.251 and quadratic-weighted κ=0.321 for the published table. The two category systems use different thresholds, the weights impose different disagreement penalties, and none of these is a continuous-score correlation or clinical accuracy measure.

A separate [BASE-II within-person comparison](berlin_base_ii/RESULTS.md) reports Pearson r=0.38 at baseline and 0.53 at follow-up between an adapted deficit FI and Fried score in the first imputed dataset (n=1,083). These are not the project GWAS participants, pooled multiple-imputation estimates, or genetic correlations.
