# Leave-one-sleep-trait-out frailty prediction

Prepared before running this follow-up, after earlier cell-mask experiments. Use the existing 12×9 table: the six Brain6 genetic correlations for a sleep trait are its input features, and its FI, FFS and HFRS correlations are three separate regression targets. For each of the 12 sleep traits, hold out all three of its frailty targets simultaneously and train separate ridge regressions for those targets on the other 11 sleep traits. The held-out trait's six already-observed brain correlations are deliberately available as inputs, so this is not prediction without phenotype data. The six feature/target source values were derived from related or overlapping GWAS and are not independent cohorts.

Standardize each feature using training rows only, with population standard deviation and zero-variance safeguards. Fit an unpenalized intercept and penalized standardized slopes. Select λ separately per frailty target from {0.1, 1, 10, 100} by an inner leave-one-training-row-out RMSE; refit preprocessing inside each inner fold and break exact ties toward the larger λ. Compare with a training-target mean baseline. Record all 36 predictions, targets, errors, fold-specific lambdas and nested scores, and count values outside [−1,1]. Report per-outcome and pooled RMSE, not a formal significance test. No model choice or AlphaGenome data are added from the outer held-out frailty values. SE and cross-estimate covariance are not modeled; the small sample makes estimates unstable. Both predictors share samples across traits, GWAS ascertainment and populations are heterogeneous, and exact source inputs/supports remain unresolved. This tests row holdout with six known brain associations, not independent two-cohort replication, causal biology, or person-level disease risk. Record all source hashes and model settings.

After inspecting the first ridge and mean results, an additional post-hoc three-nearest-neighbor baseline was added: Euclidean distance on the same six features standardized using only the 11 training sleep traits, with equal-weight average of the three training frailty targets. This is an additional baseline, not independent model selection.
