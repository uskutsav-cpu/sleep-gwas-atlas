# Training-group deletion: instability of the sleep–frailty ML prototype

Removing one entire remaining training group and reselecting ridge regularization changed some group-held-out predictions substantially. The largest change was for the **sleep-efficiency → Fried frailty** correlation: the original model predicted +0.041; excluding the three self-reported duration measures from training changed the prediction to −0.543, a shift of 0.584. The observed source estimate was −0.178. This is a post-selected worst case, not a causal effect or an estimate of a population-level loss.

Across the 36 held-out sleep–outcome cells, the median largest absolute shift per cell was 0.072 for FI, 0.155 for FFS and 0.102 for HFRS. Of 252 overlapping deletion predictions, 30 changed sign relative to the full-training prediction; 14 of the 36 cells had a deletion range spanning zero. One predicted correlation was 1.100, outside the mathematically possible [−1, 1] range. These counts are descriptive and correlated, not independent tests or uncertainty intervals. Removing a group also reduces training size and changes nested lambda selection, so the shift cannot be assigned to a specific feature or source alone.

The diagnostic follows the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), reproduces all 36 original group-held-out predictions and selects lambda again after each deletion. An independent augmented least-squares implementation reproduced all 252 results to a maximum absolute difference of 3.39×10⁻¹⁵; see [verification](verification.json). [All deletion fits](deletions.tsv), [per-cell ranges](summary.tsv), [input hashes](receipt.json), and [code](run.py) are available. The numerical check does not verify the source phenotypes or the model's validity.

This result strengthens the case that the prototype is **not ready for prediction**: the input matrix contains just 12 sleep traits, related cohorts and shared GWAS, and every prediction requires six already-estimated sleep–brain genetic correlations. The group splits and this sensitivity were chosen after prior exploration. No AlphaGenome outputs were used for training. Source uncertainty, dependence, ancestry differences and long-sleep coding remain unresolved; independent cohorts, more data, calibrated bounded models and prespecified comparisons are required.
