# Bounded model training-group sensitivity

Bounding predictions does **not** remove the model's training-data sensitivity. For sleep efficiency against the Fried frailty score, the full-training bounded model predicted +0.0474; removing the self-reported duration group from training and retuning changed it to −0.5181, a shift of 0.5655. The observed input estimate was −0.1783. This is the largest shift selected after inspection, not an independent test or a causal group effect.

Among the 252 overlapping deletion predictions, 29 changed sign relative to the corresponding full-training prediction; 11 of the 36 held-out cells had deletion ranges spanning zero. No prediction exceeded [−1, 1], as guaranteed by tanh. Median largest shifts per held-out cell were 0.0507 for FI, 0.1468 for FFS and 0.1236 for HFRS. These cells and deletion fits are strongly dependent; the numbers are descriptive, not confidence intervals. Deleting a group changes both training data and nested regularization selection, and groups have different sizes.

Using the same deletion cases, 128 of 252 bounded shifts were smaller in absolute value than the corresponding unbounded shifts. This is not evidence that the bounded model is reliably more stable: the model was selected post hoc, and the comparisons share all the same source data. The [original unbounded sensitivity](../../training_group_sensitivity/RESULTS.md) explains its setting.

All 36 stored bounded baseline predictions were reproduced before the deletions. The [252 deletion fits](deletions.tsv), [36 per-cell ranges](summary.tsv), [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [script](run.py), and [input receipt](receipt.json) preserve the calculation. An independent augmented least-squares implementation reproduced all fits to a maximum absolute difference of 4.50×10⁻¹⁵ ([verification](verification.json)). This checks arithmetic, not model validity or source calibration. The data contain just 12 related sleep traits, and cohorts overlap; all predictions require six already observed sleep–brain genetic correlations. There is no independent-cohort validation, individual-level risk prediction or use of AlphaGenome output for training.
