# Clinical-insomnia-specific features: exploratory model transfer

We calculated six clinical-insomnia–disease genetic correlations and supplied them to the previously specified bounded ridge model. The questionnaire-insomnia row was excluded from training and tuning; its saved group-held-out regularization choices were reused. All three frailty outcomes below were already available and inspected before this experiment.

| Frailty target | Prediction using MVP-specific features | Observed MVP genetic correlation (SE) | Absolute error | Three-neighbor prediction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frailty index | 0.659 | 0.553 (0.039) | 0.106 | 0.332 |
| Fried frailty score | 0.607 | 0.400 (0.035) | 0.207 | 0.325 |
| Hospital frailty risk score | 0.522 | 0.570 (0.031) | 0.048 | 0.272 |

The FFS difference is substantial compared with the reported uncertainty of the target estimate, but its error is not a significance test: feature and outcome estimates have uncertainty and unknown covariance, and prediction uncertainty was not estimated. Predictions and outcomes are genome-wide genetic correlations, not individual frailty scores. Three correlated, post-hoc outcomes cannot establish a general model ranking. For context, the prior [questionnaire-feature proxy](../mvp_proxy_check/RESULTS.md) produced bounded predictions 0.581, 0.536 and 0.469 on these same three targets; changing features also changes their source, SNP support and uncertainty, so this is not a controlled causal comparison of models or cohorts.

The [new features](features.tsv) were ADHD 0.4854 (SE 0.0362), depression 0.5609 (0.0307), schizophrenia 0.1724 (0.0287), bipolar disorder 0.2474 (0.0326), Alzheimer's 0.0161 (0.0469), and Parkinson's −0.0260 (0.0462). They used 1,043,509 to 1,112,104 SNPs depending on the disease; the frailty targets used 1,103,239. The MVP observed h² for the first feature fit was 0.0490 (0.0033), intercept 1.1361 (0.0102); LDSC reuses that first phenotype fit in the multipair run, even though the disease-specific support differs. Disease intercepts ranged from 0.9793 to 1.0850. Elevated or imprecisely estimated intercepts and different per-pair support limit interpretation. No feature estimate is a clinical effect or causal association.

This is a meaningful move beyond using questionnaire features as an MVP proxy, but it remains a post-hoc source-transfer check, not independent validation: the disease and frailty GWAS are reused, cohort and relative overlap is unknown, and full archived disease-input lineage relative to the original training matrix has not been independently established. The original FI contains insomnia and tiredness, FFS contains exhaustion and HFRS has a somnolence category. The model uses 11 related training rows, ignores uncertainty in the inputs, and is sensitive to training-group deletion. The MVP p-derived Z reconstruction, constant approximate effective N, and elevated insomnia intercept need caution; see the [input audit](../../../../../../replication/mvp_finngen_genomewide/RESULTS.md). None of this resolves the missing item-excluded frailty GWAS or failed local-correlation quality gates. No AlphaGenome output was used for model training.

The pre-result [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [LDSC script](run_ldsc.sh), [full log](ldsc-20261029T052652-387.log), [source hashes](input-hashes-20261029T052652-387.txt), and matching [stream receipt](stream-20261029T052652-387.json) document feature construction. The [prediction code](predict.py), [predictions](predictions.tsv), and [receipt](prediction_receipt.json) reproduce the prior questionnaire held-out bounded predictions and baselines to 1e-10 using augmented least squares before evaluating MVP. This checks the numerical implementation, not upstream model validity. The archived disease file hashes record the exact inputs used; they do not authenticate their upstream processing.

A later [common-support feasibility audit](common_support_feasibility/RESULTS.md) found 1,018,491 compatible markers across the nine disease and frailty inputs before intersecting MVP insomnia or the LD reference. This does not re-estimate the features or establish how marker differences affected the prediction.
