# Prior frailty machine learning: Tseng et al.

[Tseng et al. (2023 online; 2024 issue)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10828416/) already combined a frailty genetics study with machine-learning classification in UK Biobank. Broad claims to be first to apply AI, machine learning, polygenic scores, or multimodal data to frailty would therefore be unsupported.

The paper describes two distinct modeling settings. For the Fried phenotype (FP), it trained models using 24 serum markers, age, sex and BMI, and states that the FP-defining variables were excluded from training. For the separate FI setting, it included a polygenic risk score based on genome-wide significant FI variants with the listed clinical and demographic variables. The models classified frail versus robust participants; they were not sleep–ADHD interaction models or embeddings of genetic-correlation matrices. The article describes a random 60/40 training/test split and five-fold cross-validation for tuning. This is an internal split, not evidence of validation in an independently recruited cohort.

Table 4 reports an XGBoost AUC of 0.85 for the FP setting and 0.82 for the FI-plus-PRS setting. **These numbers compare different outcomes and populations, not a controlled measurement of the incremental benefit of PRS.** The authors state that adding genetic risk did not apparently improve performance, but the displayed cross-setting AUCs alone cannot quantify the incremental contribution. The article's results prose and Table 4 also give inconsistent AUCs for some algorithms; the XGBoost values cited here are the table entries. We have not independently reproduced the analysis or established how GWAS discovery participants overlapped with model testing participants; the methods and data statement do not resolve that question here. Do not infer leakage as a finding, or assume it was absent.

Brain6's current masked-cell experiment predicts missing entries in a trait-level genetic-correlation matrix, whereas Tseng's study classified individual frailty status. Its error metric and the published AUC are not comparable. A testable contribution would require a defined sleep–disease or sleep–frailty question, frozen cohort- and family-separated evaluation, appropriate baselines, and demonstrable added value of legally usable genetic or functional features. It is a proposal, not a validated clinical model. The paper's EHR FI is also not a matched deletion of insomnia or exhaustion from the present FI/FFS measures; its [availability review](../component_exclusion/SOURCE_AVAILABILITY.md) describes the remaining data limitations.
