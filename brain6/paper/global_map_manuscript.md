# Global genetic correlation map of sleep traits and six neurological and psychiatric disorders: a locked atlas reanalysis

**Manuscript state:** global-map working draft; not submission-ready. It reports the completed global extraction only. It does not imply local genetic correlation, pleiotropy, variant sharing, colocalization, a causal pathway, or a complete Brain6 analysis.

## Abstract

**Background.** Sleep traits are genetically correlated with psychiatric and neurological phenotypes, but the strength and direction of those relationships vary by phenotype and study definition. We summarized the relationships between 12 sleep and circadian traits and six brain-related disorders from a previously locked genetic-correlation atlas.

**Methods.** We extracted the 12-by-6 subset from the atlas containing 396 sleep-by-non-sleep pairs. We verified one-to-one pair coverage and inherited each pair's original 396-family false-discovery-rate value. We did not rerun the global genetic-correlation analyses or calculate a new 72-test correction. Disorder-profile similarities were computed as descriptive Pearson correlations across the 12 sleep-trait estimates. We assessed sensitivity to each sleep trait by recomputing each prespecified profile correlation after omitting that trait; this within-atlas analysis reports ranges only and does not estimate uncertainty or independent validation.

**Results.** The map contained 72 unique pairs. Thirty-five were significant under the original atlas's 396-pair FDR procedure: 9 for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), 10 for major depressive disorder (MDD), 7 for schizophrenia, 8 for bipolar disorder, 1 for Parkinson's disease, and 0 for Alzheimer's disease. The largest absolute estimate was for insomnia and MDD (genetic correlation, *r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.4425; SE = 0.0252; *P* = 9.079 × 10<sup>−69</sup>; original *q* = 8.988 × 10<sup>−67</sup>). Across sleep-trait profiles, ADHD and MDD had a descriptive Pearson correlation of 0.967; schizophrenia and bipolar disorder had a correlation of 0.880. One archived external estimate was available for insomnia–ADHD (FinnGen R13 *r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.3817; SE = 0.0521; *P* = 2.327 × 10<sup>−13</sup>), classified in the existing record as directional replication with population and phenotype limitations. A separate PGC MDD2025 no-UK-Biobank source sensitivity estimated insomnia–MDD LDSC *r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.4771 (SE = 0.0228; *P* = 4.65 × 10<sup>−97</sup>); this is not independent replication because cohort overlap with the locked MDD discovery source is documented and participant overlap remains unresolved.

**Conclusions.** The locked atlas shows broad but heterogeneous genetic correlations between sleep phenotypes and the six disorders. The map is descriptive: its inherited significance values do not remove post-screen selection, cohort overlap, or source-phenotype limitations. The present evidence supports no local, variant-level, molecular, or causal claim.

## Introduction

Sleep complaints, sleep duration, chronotype, and related circadian traits are heritable, and large genome-wide association studies have reported genetic overlap with psychiatric and neurological phenotypes. These source studies vary in phenotype definition, ascertainment, ancestry, cohort composition, and effective sample size. Insomnia GWAS, for example, combine self-reported and clinical measures across cohorts, while a long-sleep estimate can represent a specific tail contrast in a population biobank [@jansen2019insomnia; @dashti2019sleepduration]. Large association studies have also expanded the genetic evidence for ADHD, depression, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's disease [@demontis2023adhd; @howard2019mdd; @trubetskoy2022scz; @mullins2021bipolar; @nalls2019parkinson; @bellenguez2022alzheimer].

Genome-wide genetic correlation can summarize shared common-variant signal, but it is not evidence that one phenotype causes another, that a specific variant is shared, or that a common molecular mechanism has been identified. Linkage disequilibrium score regression estimates genome-wide genetic correlation from summary statistics while modeling the relationship between association statistics and linkage disequilibrium [@buliksullivan2015ldsc]. Interpretation still depends on the underlying GWAS, sample overlap, model fit, and phenotype compatibility.

Prior work has already compared seven sleep/circadian traits with bipolar disorder, MDD, and schizophrenia using LDSC, HDL, and pleiotropy methods [@jia2025sleeppsychiatricgenetics]. An insomnia-focused analysis also reports genetic correlations with ADHD, bipolar disorder, MDD, and schizophrenia, alongside cross-trait locus and cell-type analyses [@xue2026insomniapsychiatric]. Its abstract reports insomnia effective N=314,149, close to the effective N=313,750 computed from the Brain6 insomnia source-card case/control counts; the release identity and participant overlap are not established. These studies are prior context, not independent replication. A 2026 SleepChart study also reported positive LDSC correlations between long sleep and ADHD, MDD, schizophrenia, and bipolar disorder [@multiconsortium2026sleepchart]. Its exposure used UK Biobank field 1160 with a >8 h versus 6–8 h contrast (25,049 cases; 300,420 controls). Brain6 uses the same UK Biobank source population but a different ≥9 h versus 7–8 h contrast (34,184 cases; 305,742 controls). The shared cohort and different thresholds make these rounded, direction-concordant estimates prior context, not independent replication or an interchangeable replacement; outcome-release identity remains unresolved (Supplementary Table S40). We therefore do not frame those psychiatric correlations as novel. Here we provide a bounded reanalysis of the pre-existing sleep–trait genetic-correlation atlas: we extract the complete 12 sleep-trait by six disorder subset, preserve the original 396-pair multiple-testing decisions, and describe similarities between disorder profiles. The intended contribution is the broader locked six-disorder map, including ADHD and the neurodegenerative disorders, with transparent inherited significance and follow-up limitations. We do not present the selected pairs as prospective discoveries or as a substitute for local, variant-level, or independent-replication analyses.

## Methods

### Locked atlas and subset extraction

The source atlas contains 45 traits (12 sleep or circadian traits and 33 non-sleep traits) and 396 unique sleep-by-non-sleep genetic-correlation rows. The six target disorders were ADHD, MDD, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, Alzheimer's disease, and Parkinson's disease. We extracted the 72 combinations between those disorders and the 12 sleep traits from the stored atlas outputs. The extraction validator checks each estimate, standard error, *P* value, original false-discovery-rate value, and significance flag against the source table; duplicate or missing pairs cause validation to fail.

The reported significance status is inherited from the source atlas's 396-test FDR family. No new FDR correction was applied to the 72-row subset. The atlas estimates were not recomputed for this report.

### Descriptive disorder-profile comparison

For each pair of target disorders, we calculated the Pearson correlation between their vectors of 12 sleep-trait *r*<sub>g</sub> estimates. This comparison is descriptive: it has no inferential *P* value, confidence interval, or independent validation, and the vectors share the same sleep traits and source atlas. It is not interpreted as a disease taxonomy or as evidence of a shared mechanism.

For the two highlighted disorder pairs, we also recomputed the Pearson correlation 12 times, each time omitting one sleep trait according to the frozen sensitivity plan. We report the range across omissions and whether all estimates retained the same positive sign (Supplementary Table S19). This is a descriptive within-atlas robustness check, not a statistical uncertainty interval or independent validation.

### Pair selection and external replication record

The five primary downstream pairs are the existing reviewed post-atlas selection: insomnia–ADHD, insomnia–MDD, long sleep–schizophrenia, long sleep–bipolar disorder, and long sleep–Parkinson's disease. Alzheimer's disease remains `NO_ELIGIBLE_PAIR` because none of its 12 global pairs met the original atlas's FDR criterion. The selection is outcome-informed and does not correct selective-inference effects. FinnGen R13 supports a directional external replication for insomnia–ADHD. For insomnia–MDD, we computed a separate global LDSC sensitivity with PGC MDD2025 European summary statistics excluding UK Biobank; known cohort overlap with the locked Howard 2019 PGC component means it is not treated as independent replication.

For the insomnia–MDD source sensitivity, we converted the frozen Jansen 2019 insomnia input and the checksum-verified PGC MDD2025 EUR/no-UK-Biobank summary statistics [@adams2025pgcmdd] to signed Z-score format, used each variant's effective sample size for MDD, and munged both to the same HapMap3 allele list. We estimated genome-wide *r*<sub>g</sub> with LDSC and 1000 Genomes EUR LD scores and weights. The source PGC summary statistics and derived intermediate files remain on the external research drive; only the aggregate estimate and hashes are recorded in Supplementary Table S34 and its provenance. This single-pair sensitivity has no new multiple-testing correction and is not an independent replication.

### Source inputs, overlap, and evidence scope

The global table and figures are generated from the repository's locked atlas outputs. Seven dense GWAS source files named by the reviewed source cards have been verified against their expected source sizes and checksums on a mounted external research archive. Their normalization and pair preparation completed with 21 validated receipts (seven normalizations, five pair joins, five pair indexes, and four chunk-preparation artifacts); receipt and source audits are retained in the project manifests, QC records, and external scratch. These source and harmonized files are not distributed in the repository. The corrected canonical LAVA v3 family completed its full receipt audit but failed the frozen untested-cell limit, so it is not promoted; the separate roundoff run remains incomplete, and the protected Track B PLACO output is unavailable. This global-map manuscript does not report local-rg, PLACO, or other downstream results as final analyses.

Participant-level overlap is not resolved for all selected pairs. The insomnia–MDD sources both include UK Biobank components, and the long-sleep–Parkinson's sources include UK Biobank data and Parkinson's proxy cases. Other pairwise overlap is unknown where it is not documented in the source ledger. We do not assume zero overlap. This global-map manuscript does not report local-rg, PLACO, or other downstream results as final analyses. Partial PLACO candidate and LD-QC tables are included in Supplementary Tables S20–S23 for diagnostic transparency only; they do not establish an accepted five-track family or validated independent loci.

### Local-analysis feasibility and power-screen decision

We summarized canonical LAVA v3 receipt statuses and failure causes across the frozen 2,495-locus set, retaining the original 5% family-level `NOT_RUN` limit. A separate outcome-blind trait-level screen compared the locked long-sleep tail with continuous sleep duration using the same loci, LAVA reference, and univariate eligibility thresholds. Promotion to a bivariate sensitivity required a prespecified material technical improvement and passage of the family-level completeness gate. The screen improved continuous-duration processability, but the substituted seven-input family still exceeded the unchanged gate; therefore no bivariate sensitivity was run. Tables S25–S26 and S32–S33 and the source-bound candidate-screen audit provide the status totals and comparison. `NOT_RUN` is interpreted only as lack of estimability under the frozen eligibility rules.

## Results

### Global genetic-correlation map

The extracted map contained all 72 expected pair rows. Thirty-five pairs were significant under the inherited 396-family FDR decision: ADHD, 9 of 12; MDD, 10 of 12; schizophrenia, 7 of 12; bipolar disorder, 8 of 12; Parkinson's disease, 1 of 12; and Alzheimer's disease, 0 of 12. Figure 1A displays the correlation estimates; Figure 1B displays effect magnitude; and Figure 1C displays the original atlas significance status. Figure 2 shows the one archived insomnia–ADHD directional replication estimate alongside discovery, with the reused sleep GWAS and external-cohort caveats made explicit. Full estimates and source QC fields are in Supplementary Table S2.

The largest absolute point estimate in the 72-pair subset was insomnia–MDD (*r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.4425, SE = 0.0252, *P* = 9.079 × 10<sup>−69</sup>, original *q* = 8.988 × 10<sup>−67</sup>). The estimate was positive. The map contains positive and negative estimates across the six disorders, and the effect sizes should be interpreted in the context of phenotype and source-study differences.

In a source sensitivity using PGC MDD2025 EUR summary statistics with UK Biobank excluded, insomnia–MDD LDSC *r*<sub>g</sub> was 0.4771 (SE = 0.0228, *P* = 4.65 × 10<sup>−97</sup>; Supplementary Table S34). The cross-trait intercept was 0.0087 (SE = 0.0069). Nine PGC cohort entries overlap the PGC component of the locked Howard 2019 MDD source; this is not an independent replication, and the intercept does not prove zero participant overlap.

### Published psychiatric-slice comparison

A published European-ancestry analysis reports LDSC estimates for the seven sleep traits and three psychiatric disorders that overlap this map [@jia2025sleeppsychiatricgenetics]. Across its 21 rows, rg direction agrees with the locked Brain6 estimate in all 21; 18 corresponding Brain6 rows are significant under the original 396-pair FDR family, while 17 of the published LDSC *P* values meet that article's 0.05/21 threshold (Supplementary Table S30). The estimates are closely similar, but exact GWAS-release identity and participant-level overlap were not verified. The published article marks six MDD-pair LDSC intercepts as consistent with sample overlap. We treat these data as prior published context, not independent replication or a new Brain6 testing family.

### Disorder-profile similarities

The highest descriptive profile similarities were ADHD–MDD (*r* = 0.967) and schizophrenia–bipolar disorder (*r* = 0.880). Figure 1E and the complete matrix in `disorder_profile_similarity.tsv` show all pairwise values. These similarities reflect the shape of estimates across a shared set of 12 sleep traits; they are not independent tests and have no inferential uncertainty estimates.

### Local-analysis feasibility

The separate, receipt-audited canonical LAVA v3 family covered two sleep inputs (insomnia and long sleep) and five disorders at 2,495 loci each. It classified 13,745 of 17,465 cells as `TESTED` and 3,720 as `NOT_RUN` (21.30%), exceeding the frozen 5% ceiling of 873; the immutable decision is `FAILED_QC_NOT_PROMOTED`. The `NOT_RUN` cells comprise 3,564 low-local-h² failures, 154 insufficient-shared-reference-variant failures, and 2 other minimum-*K* failures (Supplementary Tables S25–S26 and S32). These are eligibility outcomes, not evidence of null local genetic correlation.

An outcome-blind, trait-only screen of continuous self-reported sleep duration yielded finite local-h² estimates at 1,619 of 2,495 loci (64.89%), compared with 1,204 (48.26%) for the canonical long-sleep tail: an increase of 415 loci or 16.63 percentage points. Low-local-h² `NOT_RUN` cells decreased from 1,271 to 876 (31.08%). This improves technical processability for that sleep input; it does not establish shared loci. The substituted two-sleep/five-disorder family still had 3,305 `NOT_RUN` cells, above the same 873-cell ceiling. Even if both sleep inputs had complete coverage, the five disorder inputs alone leave a lower bound of 1,758 `NOT_RUN` cells. No bivariate LAVA result was generated or promoted (Supplementary Table S33 and the candidate-screen audit).

Leave-one-sleep-trait-out profile correlations ranged from 0.961 to 0.983 for ADHD–MDD and 0.830 to 0.917 for schizophrenia–bipolar disorder; all 12 omission estimates remained positive (Supplementary Table S19). This descriptive within-atlas sensitivity does not provide independent validation or sampling uncertainty.

### External replication coverage

The archived insomnia–ADHD estimate in FinnGen R13 is directionally concordant (*r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.3817, SE = 0.0521, *P* = 2.327 × 10<sup>−13</sup>) and is classified as `DIRECTIONAL_REPLICATION`. FinnGen's population, phenotype, power, and unverified participant-level overlap limit the strength of that interpretation. For insomnia–MDD, we computed a genome-wide LDSC sensitivity using the PGC MDD2025 EUR release excluding UK Biobank (357,636 cases; 1,281,936 controls; 74 cohorts). The estimate was *r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.4771 (SE = 0.0228, *P* = 4.65 × 10<sup>−97</sup>), with cross-trait intercept 0.0087 (SE = 0.0069) across 1,061,081 HapMap3 SNPs with valid alleles. This source excludes UK Biobank, which reduces direct sample overlap with the UKB-only insomnia GWAS; however, nine PGC cohort entries are shared with the PGC component of the locked Howard 2019 MDD discovery source, and exact participant overlap remains unverified. We therefore treat the estimate as a source sensitivity, not independent replication, and do not interpret the nonsignificant cross-trait intercept as proof of zero overlap (Supplementary Table S34). A separate published study reported a long-sleep–schizophrenia genetic correlation (*r*<sub>g</sub> = 0.30, SE = 0.05, *P* = 1.58 × 10<sup>−10</sup>) using a ≥10-hour sleep definition and UK Biobank plus Million Veteran Program participants [@austinzimmerman2023sleep]. The Brain6 long-sleep source uses a ≥9-hour contrast and UK Biobank; the published comparison therefore has possible sleep-sample overlap and a non-identical phenotype. We archive it as contextual, level-D evidence only, not as an independent replication or a new Brain6 analysis (Supplementary Table S18). Grover et al. report a pairwise cross-trait LDSC screen using the same Dashti 2019 long-sleep and Nalls 2019 Parkinson GWAS releases, with matching sample counts; their article says no neurodegenerative-disorder/sleep pair showed a genetic correlation but does not give a long-sleep–Parkinson numeric estimate in the text [@grover2022sleeppd]. We record this as prior negative context only and retain the `MR_ONLY` class for positive published direct-rg evidence (Supplementary Table S35). SleepChart 2026 reported rounded positive LDSC estimates for long sleep with ADHD, MDD, schizophrenia, and bipolar disorder. Its UK Biobank exposure has a different long-sleep threshold and fewer cases than Brain6, so it is prior context rather than independent replication or a power replacement (Supplementary Table S40). Four archived FinnGen R13 candidates were excluded because their source-object access and transformation receipts could not be verified.

## Discussion

This locked atlas subset provides a compact view of global common-variant correlation between 12 sleep or circadian phenotypes and six brain-related disorders. Thirty-five of the 72 extracted rows retain the original atlas's 396-family FDR significance. The profile similarities for ADHD with MDD and schizophrenia with bipolar disorder are visually consistent with partially similar correlation patterns across sleep phenotypes. All leave-one-sleep-trait-out correlations for these pairs remained positive, though the schizophrenia–bipolar range was wider (0.830–0.917) than the ADHD–MDD range (0.961–0.983). This descriptive within-atlas sensitivity does not supply sampling uncertainty or independent validation and should not be interpreted as evidence that either pair forms a common disease class.

The insomnia–MDD relationship had the largest absolute estimate in this subset. This result is consistent with substantial genome-wide overlap in these particular source GWAS, but it does not establish direction, mediation, shared causal variants, or a mechanism. The two source definitions include UK Biobank data, and individual-level overlap is not verified. Similar caution applies to the long-sleep–Parkinson's pair, whose Parkinson's source includes UK Biobank proxy cases. Sample overlap can affect the sampling covariance of summary-statistic estimates and therefore matters for downstream local tests.

The Alzheimer's row is a global null within this locked panel: none of the 12 pairs passed the original atlas FDR criterion. This should not be generalized to all sleep phenotypes, Alzheimer's GWAS, or analytical methods. It is a result about a particular set of source studies and the inherited threshold.

Most of the bipolar–MDD–schizophrenia slice is already represented in a published seven-trait comparison. The close, direction-concordant estimates support that these patterns are not first reported here, but unresolved study-source and participant overlap prevent treating the table as independent replication. Any contribution claim should focus on the full locked 12×6 coverage and its qualified extension to ADHD, Alzheimer's disease, and Parkinson's disease, not on rediscovery of the shared psychiatric subset.

The archived FinnGen R13 result provides a directionally concordant external global estimate for insomnia–ADHD. It is a single directional replication record and should not be described as full independent replication of the six-disorder map. The PGC MDD2025 result is a distinct source sensitivity for insomnia–MDD, but documented overlap with the earlier MDD discovery cohorts prevents an independent-replication claim. Four archived FinnGen candidate endpoints were excluded pending source-object access and transformation receipts; they are not failures to replicate.

## Limitations

First, the analysis is a subset extraction from a pre-existing atlas, not a fresh GWAS or a prospective hypothesis test. Because the five downstream pairs were selected after inspecting the global screen, their follow-up interpretation remains subject to selection effects. The inherited original FDR controls the source atlas family but does not provide selective-inference correction for the subsequent pair selection.

Second, source GWAS differ in phenotype definitions, ascertainment, ancestry, sample size, cohort composition, and reporting. These differences can change the estimand and may limit comparability across rows. Participant-level overlap is only partly characterized; unknown overlap is retained as unknown.

Third, the disorder-profile Pearson correlations are descriptive. The six 12-element profiles reuse sleep traits, GWAS sources, and the same atlas estimation process. We did not estimate uncertainty for these similarities or validate them in an independent set of sleep traits.

Fourth, the local-analysis family failed its prespecified completeness gate because many input-by-locus cells did not meet local-h² or shared-reference eligibility. A continuous-duration GWAS improved trait-level processability relative to the long-sleep tail, but the full substituted family remained above the frozen missingness limit and produced no bivariate local-rg estimates. `NOT_RUN` cells cannot be interpreted as negative local correlations, and the processability improvement alone is not evidence for biological sharing.

Fifth, insomnia–ADHD has one archived external directional replication estimate, with Finnish-founder-population and phenotype-comparability limitations. The PGC MDD2025 no-UK-Biobank estimate is a global source sensitivity, not independent replication: the MDD meta-analysis shares nine cohort entries with the earlier Howard 2019 PGC component, and exact participant overlap is unresolved. Its cross-trait LDSC intercept does not establish zero sample overlap. The published long-sleep–schizophrenia comparison is also not independent validation: it uses a different long-sleep threshold, includes UK Biobank participants, and its exact PGC release/cohort crosswalk is unresolved. The absence of admitted independent replication estimates for other pairs is not evidence that they fail to replicate. Four candidate FinnGen files were found on an external archive, but official object metadata returned HTTP 403 and the files lack source and transformation receipts; they were not used to compute replication estimates.

Finally, no accepted Brain6 shared-locus set, fine-mapping, colocalization, molecular, cell-type, pathway, or causal result is reported. Partial PLACO candidate results remain diagnostic because the five-track family is incomplete and LD-based locus grouping lacks genotype-level validation; they do not support independent-locus or functional claims. Separate post-gate tables record nearest-gene positional context for 21 partial PLACO lead signals (Table S36), three cross-pair candidate-region overlaps (Table S37), lead-set stability across LD-handling diagnostics (Table S38), and exact p-value replay for all 2,297 available candidate variants (Table S39). The baseline and factor-normalized analyses share all 21 lead IDs; the range-edge-excluded rebuild adds four method-sensitive leads (rs6430538, rs199535, rs199534, and rs4938023), while all three retain the same 19 merged intervals. All 2,297 reported PLACO+ p-values reproduce from the receipt-bound z-scores and pinned source implementation, with maximum relative error below 5.1×10⁻¹⁴; this numerical check does not resolve the incomplete family or LD-reference issue. These differences are diagnostic, not confirmed independent loci. The long-sleep–bipolar and long-sleep–SCZ outputs reuse rs1051168 at the same chr15 candidate interval, but this is not independent replication or colocalization; all 19 candidate regions remain `NOT_TIERED`. These diagnostics do not prioritize causal genes. QTL, regulatory, tissue, cell-type, and pathway evidence has not been evaluated. Such downstream methods require complete verified inputs, method-specific references, and reviewed participant-overlap handling. The present results support no mechanistic or causal claim.

## Conclusion

The 72-row locked map preserves the original 396-pair FDR decisions and shows heterogeneous genome-wide genetic correlation between sleep traits and six neurological or psychiatric disorders. Much of its bipolar–MDD–schizophrenia slice has prior published rg evidence; the broader locked 12×6 coverage and qualified ADHD/Alzheimer's/Parkinson's comparisons define the atlas-level contribution. The evidence includes one directionally concordant archived replication record and one non-independent MDD source sensitivity, both with source-overlap caveats. The package remains incomplete for local and variant-level follow-up, and this manuscript is not ready for submission as a mechanistic or causal study.
