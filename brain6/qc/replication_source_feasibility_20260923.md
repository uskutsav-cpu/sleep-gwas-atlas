### FinnGen access-page and registration-form recheck (2026-09-23 16:44 UTC)

The current official [Access results page](https://www.finngen.fi/en/access_results) states that Data Freeze 13 was publicly released on June 2, 2026, comprises 500,186 participants and 2,755 endpoints, and that summary statistics may be downloaded free of charge after its access workflow. That workflow requires an [online registration form](https://elomake.helsinki.fi/lomakkeet/124935/lomake.html) and emails download instructions. The form asks for first name, last name, organization, email address, country, and a personal certification that the submitter complies with applicable laws and institutional rules for human-subjects and genetics research. FinnGen's [Green Data guide](https://docs.finngen.fi/finngen-data-specifics/green-library-data-aggregate-data/how-can-you-download-green-data) separately says the aggregate Green library is accessed by signing in with a FinnGen account. The form's personal details and certification have not been supplied, so no registration was submitted. The four candidate files remain excluded until authorized official access yields a source-object generation/version, checksum, acquisition record, and transformation receipts.

## Eligibility conclusion

The verified insomnia–ADHD FinnGen R13 directional result remains the sole replication result in the current ledger. For MDD, an MVP-only European GWAS is a plausible independent outcome source, but full data access requires a dbGaP application; the public PGC 2025 meta-analysis could not be certified independent because its core builds on the earlier PGC samples. For SCZ, bipolar disorder and Parkinson's disease, no additional public outcome source was established here that is both scientifically compatible and demonstrably independent of the locked disorder GWAS. The inability to certify independence is not evidence that the underlying associations fail to replicate.

### PGC MDD Wave 3 public cohort-provenance follow-up (2026-09-25)

The exact public `pgc-mdd2025_no23andMe-noUKBB_eur_v3.49.24.11.txt` sidecar was retrieved from Figshare file 51486998 (7,981 bytes). Its observed MD5 `3367c2fd90cba302b1e59f06e1448927` matches Figshare metadata; SHA-256 is `4d75b066df33dd9b584eb289a3f7a2badeb6cf9c25817cc88a4d31b5d68769a1`. It has 74 cohort rows. Nine rows identify cohorts also described as members of the earlier PGC MDD29 anchor: COFAMS (`cof3`), DGN (`grdg`), NESDA/NTR (`nes1`), QIMR I317/I610/COEX (`qi3c`, `qi6c`, `qio2`), RADIANT-UK (`rad3`), SHIP-0/SHIP-LEGEND (`shp0`), and SHIP-TREND (`shpt`). The MDD29 membership of these source cohorts is documented in the earlier PGC MDD cohort reports, while the Figshare sidecar lists them in the exact no-UKB EUR meta-analysis. This establishes cohort-level overlap with the PGC component of locked Howard 2019, though not participant-level identity or an exact overlap count. [PGC MDD2025 summary-statistics archive](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/GWAS_summary_statistics_for_major_depression_PGC_MDD2025_/27061255), [PGC MDD29 cohort description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5862738/), [Wray et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934326/), [Howard et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6522363/).

The official PGC MDD rmUKBB project separately documents that Wray/Howard-era MDD summaries can retain overlapping participants when only a UK Biobank meta-analysis component is omitted; its overlap-removal procedure excluded 335 participants from 12 PGC cohorts and 622 Generation Scotland participants, while two SHIP participants could not be removed. This supports careful interpretation of overlap and why a no-UKB label is insufficient, but the direct reason to exclude MDD2025 here is the nine shared MDD29 cohort entries in its own no-UKB EUR sidecar. [PGC rmUKBB methods](https://psychiatric-genomics-consortium.github.io/mdd-rmUKBB/).

Decision: exclude MDD2025 EUR/no-UKB from independent replication of locked insomnia–MDD because its exact cohort sidecar documents nine entries from the PGC MDD29 anchor retained by the locked Howard 2019 PGC component. The precise individual overlap count is unknown. The summary-statistics file was not downloaded and no replication estimate was computed; existing replication results and classifications are unchanged. The source sidecar and machine-readable comparison are preserved in `qc/replication_sources/`.

The cited PGC pages note use conditions for consortium resources; the official PGC data-use terms must be checked before any download or analysis. No protected access was attempted, no access form was submitted, and no new source files were downloaded.

### Yale European long-sleep summary-statistics check (2026-09-25)

The official Gelernter Lab page links an “European ancestry Long Sleep” summary file at `https://hugesumstats.yale.edu/dl/eur_meta_long_dbgap.txt.gz` ([official statistics page](https://medicine.yale.edu/lab/gelernter/stats/)). The link label alone does not establish cohort independence. The paper describes this EUR result as combining UK Biobank and MVP; its Table 1 reports 278,003 UKB EUR participants (6,521 long sleepers) and 167,963 MVP EUR participants (9,441 long sleepers), for 445,966 EUR total. Its long-sleep definition is ≥10 h versus 7–8 h ([Austin-Zimmerman et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10539313/)).

The Brain6 long-sleep discovery uses UK Biobank, so the public EUR meta-analysis overlaps the discovery and is not eligible as independent replication. It also uses a narrower ≥10 h phenotype than the canonical ≥9 h definition. The public page does not label a standalone MVP-only long-sleep file; MVP-only output remains tied to the dbGaP/MVP access workflow (`phs001672.v1.p1`; [official MVP access guidance](https://www.mvp.va.gov/pwa/discover-mvp-data)). No application was submitted, no file was downloaded, and no checksum or harmonization was claimed. Decision: exclude the linked EUR meta-analysis from replication; reconsider an MVP-only source only after authorized access, exact phenotype and cohort confirmation, and source-byte provenance.
